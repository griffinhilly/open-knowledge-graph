# Stage Audit Criteria

For each topic, assess whether its current stage matches where the content is typically first encountered:

| Stage | Level | Examples |
|-------|-------|---------|
| pre-formal | Preschool/K (ages 2-5) | Counting, shapes, sensory exploration |
| concrete-operations | Elementary (ages 5-10) | Arithmetic, basic science observation, reading |
| abstract-reasoning | Middle school (ages 10-15) | Algebra, intro sciences, basic logic, AP-prep |
| formal-systems | HS through standard undergrad (ages 15-22) | Calculus, intro college courses (100-300 level), AP/IB |
| advanced | Upper-division undergrad (300-400 level) | Specialty courses requiring substantial prior training |
| expert | Graduate/professional (post-bachelor's) | Research methods, specialized theory, professional certification |

Key principles:
1. Stage = where content is TYPICALLY FIRST ENCOUNTERED, not the hardest level it could be taught at
2. A topic in an "Intro to X" college course is formal-systems, not advanced
3. A topic in a standard required undergrad course (bio majors take genetics, CS majors take algorithms) is formal-systems
4. A topic in an upper-division ELECTIVE that requires multiple prerequisites within the major is advanced
5. A topic that requires graduate training or professional certification is expert
6. Look at the core_idea text — does it read like intro material or specialized research?
7. Consider the course name — "intro", "fundamentals", "principles" suggest formal-systems; "advanced", "theoretical" suggest advanced/expert
