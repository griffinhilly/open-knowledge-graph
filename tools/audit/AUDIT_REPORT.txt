BIOLOGY AUDIT: 45 RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

All 45 recommendations are DOWNGRADES from advanced to formal-systems.

BIOCHEMISTRY (31 topics: advanced -> formal-systems)
------------------------------------------------------
These are fundamental biochemistry topics taught in standard undergraduate
intro courses, not upper-division specializations:

Core coverage patterns:
  - Amino acid structure, classification, properties
  - Protein structure, folding, chaperones
  - Enzyme kinetics (Michaelis-Menten, catalysis, cofactors)
  - Central metabolic pathways: glycolysis, citric acid cycle, electron
    transport, oxidative phosphorylation
  - Fatty acid/lipid metabolism and synthesis
  - Carbohydrate metabolism and homeostasis
  - Nucleotide structure and synthesis

Rationale: These are all covered in standard "Biochemistry I" or "Introduction
to Biochemistry" courses taken by biology majors as part of required curriculum.
They are formal-systems level (standard undergrad 100-300 level), not advanced
(upper-division 300-400 specialty).

MICROBIOLOGY (14 topics: advanced -> formal-systems)
-----------------------------------------------------
Intro-level microbiology topics taught in standard undergraduate courses:

Coverage patterns:
  - Bacterial structure (cell wall, flagella, pili)
  - Bacterial metabolism and growth
  - Gram staining and cell classification
  - Viral structure and replication
  - Fermentation pathways
  - Fungal spores and reproduction

Rationale: These are core topics in "Introduction to Microbiology" or
"General Microbiology" courses, standard undergrad requirements. Lab techniques
like Gram staining are foundational, not advanced.

NO UPGRADES IN BIOLOGY:
Evolutionary biology topics currently at formal-systems (e.g., natural
selection, allele frequency, Hardy-Weinberg) are correctly placed. Advanced
topics (Bayesian phylogenetics, coalescent theory, population genomics)
are correctly at advanced.

PHYSICS AUDIT: 30 RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

19 downgrades (advanced -> formal-systems)
11 upgrades (formal-systems -> advanced)

MODERN PHYSICS (19 topics: advanced -> formal-systems)
------------------------------------------------------
Canonical intro-level modern physics topics that belong at formal-systems:

Topics downgraded:
  - Photoelectric effect
  - Compton scattering (and wavelength shift)
  - Blackbody radiation (Planck's law)
  - Photon concept and quanta
  - Planck-Einstein relation
  - Special relativity and Lorentz transformations
  - Mass-energy equivalence (E=mc²)
  - Nuclear binding energy
  - Gamma decay

Rationale: These are the bread-and-butter topics of "Modern Physics" courses
(standard 200-300 level undergrad). They appear in every intro modern physics
textbook and are AP Physics C scope. Senior undergrad would encounter these
in their intro course, making them formal-systems, not advanced.

QUANTUM MECHANICS (11 topics: formal-systems -> advanced)
---------------------------------------------------------
Advanced quantum topics that require substantial QM prerequisite knowledge:

Topics upgraded:
  - Bell's theorem and Bell inequalities (foundation of quantum foundations)
  - Dirac notation (bra-ket formalism)
  - Hilbert space formalism (abstract mathematical structure)
  - Canonical commutation relations (formal operator algebra)
  - Fermions, bosons, and exchange symmetry
  - Slater determinants (many-body antisymmetrization)
  - Identical particle quantum mechanics

Rationale: These are taught in upper-division "Quantum Mechanics II" courses
(300-400 level) that require QM I as prerequisite. The formal mathematical
structure and many-body treatments are not intro-level. Most of these appear
after week 4-5 of QM I at the earliest, when students have solid foundation
in Schrodinger equation and basic applications.

NO UPGRADES NEEDED:
- Classical mechanics, E&M, thermodynamics, waves: correctly formal-systems
- Electrodynamics, statistical mechanics: correctly expert
- Most of QM is correctly advanced (senior undergrad level)
