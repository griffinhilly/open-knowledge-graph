# Developmental Stages

Topics can optionally be tagged with a developmental stage indicating the cognitive level required. These are approximate and based on developmental psychology research, not rigid age cutoffs.

## Stages

### `pre-formal` (roughly ages 2-5)
Experiential and sensory learning. No formal notation. Knowledge is built through physical manipulation, social interaction, and pattern observation. Counting, shape recognition, spatial reasoning, measurement intuition.

### `concrete-operations` (roughly ages 5-10)
Introduction of formal notation and standard procedures, but always grounded in physical or visual models. The learner can reason about concrete objects and their representations. Place value, arithmetic operations, basic geometry, data representation.

### `abstract-reasoning` (roughly ages 10-15)
Variables, generalization, and reasoning about relationships without concrete referents. The learner can manipulate symbols, follow multi-step logical arguments, and think hypothetically. Algebra, ratios, negative numbers, basic proof concepts.

### `formal-systems` (roughly ages 15-18)
Formal mathematical structures, rigorous proof, and multi-step abstraction. The learner can construct and evaluate arguments within axiomatic systems. Calculus, formal geometry, trigonometry, introductory linear algebra.

### `advanced` (roughly ages 18+)
University-level and beyond. Abstract structures, generalization across domains, original problem-solving. Analysis, abstract algebra, topology, measure theory.

## Usage Notes

- Stages are **metadata for filtering and display**, not constraints on the graph. A 12-year-old working through calculus topics tagged `formal-systems` is fine.
- The prerequisite graph, not the stage tag, determines what a learner is ready for.
- When in doubt, tag with the stage at which the topic is *typically first encountered* in a standard curriculum.
