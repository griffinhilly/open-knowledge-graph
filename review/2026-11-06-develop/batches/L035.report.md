## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. homology-with-coefficients.md, Q2 (true-false, answer true): "With field coefficients k ... H_n(X; k) ≅ H_n(X; Z) ⊗ k." False for k = Z/p: Z/p is not flat over Z, so Tor(H_{n-1}, Z/p) can be nonzero. Example: H_2(RP^2; Z/2) = Z/2, but H_2(RP^2; Z) ⊗ Z/2 = 0. It holds only for fields of characteristic 0. The Explainer, paragraph 4, makes the same error ("Tor vanishes for fields").
2. homology-with-coefficients.md, Q4 answer and the last Explainer paragraph: "By computing H_n(X; Z/pZ) for all primes p, one can recover the full torsion structure" / "integer homology is uniquely determined by the collection of all mod-p homologies together with the rational homology." False: Z/p and Z/p^2 cannot be told apart by mod-p homology, since Z/p^k ⊗ Z/p = Z/p.
3. hurewicz-theorem.md, Q3: the answer index is 0 ("X is contractible") for a CW complex with H_k = 0 for k ≥ 1. False: acyclic CW complexes need not be contractible (the Poincaré homology sphere minus a point, for example). The correct option is index 1. The question's own explanation concedes the point.
4. five-lemma.md, Core Idea: "if the first and third vertical maps are surjective and the second and fourth are injective, then the middle map is injective." Wrong: the correct hypothesis is β and δ injective and α surjective. The "third" map is the middle map itself.
5. five-lemma.md, Q3 (true-false, answer true): "The five lemma works only for abelian groups." False: the five lemma also holds for (non-abelian) groups. The explanation's claim that it "can fail" there is incorrect.
6. five-lemma.md, Explainer: "each using only two of the four assumed isomorphisms." The injectivity half uses three properties (α surjective, β and δ injective). The Q1 explanation also omits δ injective and β surjective.
7. five-lemma.md, Q2 answer: it assumes f_*: H_n(A) -> H_n(B) is an isomorphism "by homotopy invariance". That requires f|A : A -> B to be a homotopy equivalence too, which the question does not state.
8. chain-homotopy-equivalence.md, Explainer final paragraph: "the lens spaces L(7,1) and L(7,2) have isomorphic homology but are not homotopy equivalent." They are homotopy equivalent: 1·2 ≡ 3^2 mod 7, and this pair is the standard example of spaces that are homotopy equivalent but not homeomorphic. A correct example is L(5,1) and L(5,2). Also, the analogy in the Explainer and in Q3 ("just as spaces with the same homology need not be homotopy equivalent") misleads. Over Z, free chain complexes (such as singular complexes) with isomorphic homology are chain homotopy equivalent.
9. higher-homotopy-groups.md, Q4 explanation ("homotopy groups collectively form a complete invariant for CW complexes up to homotopy equivalence"), Q5 explanation, and the last Explainer paragraph ("they determine the homotopy type of CW complexes"). False: Whitehead's theorem needs a map that induces the isomorphisms. S^2 × RP^3 and RP^2 × S^3 have isomorphic homotopy groups but different H_2.
10. cochain-complexes-cohomology.md, Core Idea: "over the integers it carries strictly more information due to the universal coefficient theorem." The UCT shows that the integral cohomology groups are determined by the integral homology groups, so the groups alone carry no extra information. Explainer paragraph 3 ("extra information") repeats this.
11. homology-of-spheres.md, Q1 explanation: "The vanishing of H_1 means S^3 is simply connected." H_1 = 0 does not imply simple connectivity (the Poincaré homology sphere). Q5 answer: "H_1(S^2) ≅ H_0(S^1)/im(...)" is the wrong formula. H_1(S^2) is the kernel of H̃_0(S^1) -> H̃_0(U) ⊕ H̃_0(V), or 0 by the connecting map's injectivity argument.
12. cw-complexes.md, Q4 answer: "S^2 needs 2 cells (CW) vs 14 triangles (simplicial)." The minimal triangulation has 4 triangles, as Q1's explanation in the same file says. Q2 explanation: "closure-finite (each cell touches only finitely many other cells)" misstates the condition. The closure of each cell meets only finitely many cells, and a 0-cell can lie in the closure of infinitely many cells.
13. cup-product.md, Q4 explanation: "There is no ring homomorphism between Z[α]/(α^3) and Z[β, γ]/(β^2, βγ, γ^2) that preserves degree." Degree-preserving ring homomorphisms exist (α ↦ β, for example). It should say "no ring isomorphism".
14. brouwer-fixed-point-theorem-homological.md, Q3 explanation: "f(x) (which is in the interior since the map goes D^n → D^n)." f(x) can lie on the boundary. Q4 (true-false, answer false): the statement is arguably true as phrased, and the explanation argues against itself.
15. poincare-duality.md, Q3: the answer is true ("This violates Poincare duality"), but the explanation says it "does not 'violate' Poincare duality". These contradict each other, so the item needs a rewrite.
16. lefschetz-fixed-point-theorem.md, Q1: options 0 and 3 both say L(f) = 4 with a fixed point, so the item is ambiguous. Explainer paragraph 2: "the identity is the only fixed-point-free map with L = 0 up to homotopy considerations" is garbled or false.
17. kunneth-formula.md, Q3 explanation contains "let me recompute". The keyed option (index 2) credits Tor(Z/2, Z/2) for the torsion in H_2, but the explanation itself shows the torsion comes from the tensor term H_1 ⊗ H_1, with Tor contributing 0. Explainer: "torsion in adjacent dimensions" misdescribes when Tor terms appear (any p, q contribute in degree p + q + 1).
18. degree-theory-maps-spheres.md, Q1 explanation contains "Wait — actually". The Q3 explanation is muddled. The last sentence of the Q4 explanation ("f maps every point of S^n to at least one other point as well") is meaningless. The Explainer's Brouwer sentence ("a map S^{n-1} -> S^{n-1} of degree 1 that is also a retraction") is confused.
19. homotopy-exact-sequence-fibration.md, Q4 explanation: "a map S^n → B lifts to a map D^{n+1} → E whose boundary (a map S^n → F)" has the dimensions off by one. ∂ goes from π_n(B) to π_{n-1}(F).
20. jordan-curve-theorem-homological.md, Q3 explanation: "H_0(S^2 \ C) ≅ H̃^0(S^1) ≅ Z". By the stated formula it should be H̃^1(S^1), since H̃^0(S^1) = 0.
21. mayer-vietoris-sequence.md, Q1 explanation: injectivity of H_n(A∩B) -> H_n(A) ⊕ H_n(B) forces the connecting map H_{n+1}(X) -> H_n(A∩B) to be zero, not "H_n(X) → H_{n-1}(A ∩ B)".
22. excision-theorem.md, Q4 explanation: it attributes the failure of excision for homotopy groups to their being "non-abelian in general". π_n is abelian for n ≥ 2, so this reasoning is wrong.
23. cellular-homology.md, Explainer last sentence: attaching an n-cell "creates a new n-dimensional homology generator (if phi is null-homotopic) or kills an (n-1)-dimensional class". The relevant condition is whether φ_*[S^{n-1}] is zero in H_{n-1}, not null-homotopy. Example: the torus 2-cell's attaching map is not null-homotopic, yet it creates H_2. Partial killing (torsion) also occurs.
24. simplicial-complexes.md, Q3 explanation: it lists "CW complexes" among spaces that "can be triangulated". Not all CW complexes are triangulable.
25. simplicial-homology-groups.md, Q4 answer and Explainer paragraph 4: they tie torsion to non-orientability. Torsion also occurs in orientable spaces, for example RP^3 with H_1 = Z/2.
26. singular-homology-groups.md, Q3 explanation: "Only special maps (like homotopy equivalences, or maps with degree +/- 1 on spheres) induce surjections" is false as stated (any map with a right homotopy inverse, any retraction, and so on).
27. exact-sequences-homological-algebra.md, Explainer paragraph 4: "knowing two of the three families of groups determines the third, up to extension problems" overstates it. The maps are also needed.
28. borsuk-ulam-theorem.md, Explainer: Tucker's lemma is linked to "the PPAD complexity class". Low confidence: Tucker and Borsuk-Ulam search problems are usually associated with PPA. Worth a check.
29. euler-characteristic-homology.md, Explainer paragraph 2: the notation b_n is reused for the rank of boundaries, then "beta_n" appears. This is confusing, though not false.
