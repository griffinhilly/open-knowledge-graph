## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. electric-field-lines.md, question 2 (true-false, keyed true): "A positive charge released from rest in a static electric field will move along an electric field line." This is false in general. Once the charge has velocity, inertia carries it off a curved field line; it holds only where field lines are straight.
2. electric-field-lines.md, existing misconception bullet 1: "(charges move perpendicular to field lines)". Wrong. The force on a charge is along the field line (tangent to it), not perpendicular.
3. circuit-topology-and-elements.md, question 0 (keyed "In series"): identical current through three resistors does not prove they are in series. Three equal resistors in parallel also carry identical currents, so option C is defensible.
4. covariant-em.md, question 2 explanation and Explainer: "in the wire's rest frame this is a Coulomb force from charge density imbalances due to length contraction." This is reversed. In the wire's rest frame the wire is neutral and the force is magnetic; the force is electric in the moving charge's rest frame.
5. covariant-em.md, Explainer: "There is no physical distinction between electric and magnetic". Overstated. The invariants E² − c²B² and E·B are frame-independent and can distinguish field configurations.
6. covariant-em.md, Core Idea: "∂_μF^μν = J^ν" omits μ₀, which the questions and Explainer include (inconsistent units convention).
7. electric-field-superposition-principle.md, Explainer: "a positive charge at position A pulls a test charge toward A". A positive source pushes a positive test charge away from A.
8. ampere-law-applications.md, Core Idea: "Like Gauss's law for magnetism". The analogy is to Gauss's law for electric fields; Gauss's law for magnetism is ∮B·dA = 0.
9. ampere-law-applications.md, Explainer, right-hand-rule paragraph: "curl the fingers of your right hand in the direction of positive current flow around the Amperian loop". Garbled. The fingers follow the loop's traversal direction and the thumb gives the direction of positive enclosed current.
10. ampere-law-field.md, Explainer: calls ∇ × B = μ₀J "one of Maxwell's four fundamental equations". That is the magnetostatic form; Maxwell's equation includes the displacement-current term.
11. boundary-conditions-em-fields.md, Explainer: "(H₂ − H₁) × n̂ = K". Sign error: the standard form is n̂ × (H₂ − H₁) = K with n̂ pointing from region 1 to 2; the written form gives −K.
12. boundary-conditions-em-fields.md, Explainer: "At a perfect conductor ... normal B = 0 inside". True for time-varying fields (or superconductors), not for static B in a perfect conductor in general.
13. conservation-of-electric-charge.md, Explainer: charge conservation underpins "the derivation of Gauss's law from Maxwell's equations". Gauss's law is itself one of Maxwell's equations; the phrase does not make sense.
14. coulomb-force-superposition.md, question 4 explanation: "a 3 N force eastward and a 4 N force northward produce a 5 N force to the northeast". The resultant points about 53° north of east, not northeast.
15. dielectric-materials-polarization.md, Explainer: "P is proportional to the applied field: P = ε₀χ_e E". In P = ε₀χₑE, E is the total macroscopic field in the material, not the applied field.
16. dielectric-susceptibility-constant.md, Explainer: "related to the applied field E by P = ε₀χₑE". Same issue as flag 15.
17. displacement-current-and-maxwell.md, Explainer: "ε₀∂E/∂t acts as an effective current density equal to the conduction current density in the wires". The total displacement current equals the conduction current; the densities differ because the plate area differs from the wire's cross-section.
18. electric-flux-and-divergence.md, Core Idea: "The divergence theorem relates flux through a closed surface to charge enclosed: ∮E⋅dA = Q_enclosed/ε₀". That relation is Gauss's law; the divergence theorem is the mathematical identity ∮F·dA = ∫∇·F dV.
19. electric-dipole-moment.md, Explainer: "It is the first term in the multipole expansion". The dipole is the second term, after the monopole (the same sentence then lists the monopole first).
20. conductors-electrostatic-behavior.md, Explainer: "The only place charge can reside is on the outer surface". When a cavity holds a charge, induced charge also sits on the inner surface.
21. capacitor-networks.md, question 3: typo "they most have the same voltage" (should be "must").
22. electric-field-continuous-distributions.md, question 0: assumes P is on the rod's perpendicular bisector without saying the rod is centered on the origin.
