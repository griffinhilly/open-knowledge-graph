## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- atmospheric-circulation-planets.md, Q0 option/answer: "Jupiter rotates roughly 24 times faster than Venus". Venus's sidereal day is about 243 Earth days (~5,830 h) and Jupiter's is about 9.9 h, a ratio near 590, not 24.
- atmospheric-circulation-planets.md, Q4 explanation: "Earth (Ro ~ 1 in midlatitudes)". Synoptic-scale Rossby number at midlatitudes is about 0.1 (U ≈ 10 m/s, f ≈ 1e-4 s⁻¹, L ≈ 1,000 km).
- core-crystallization-and-reversals.md, Q0 explanation and Explainer last paragraph: "Mars ... its smaller core likely cooled and solidified" / "crystallized completely". InSight seismic data indicate Mars's core is largely liquid today. The dynamo is thought to have stopped because core heat flow dropped too low, not because the core froze solid.
- crater-scaling-laws-and-morphology.md, Core Idea: "simple craters (dome-shaped ...)". Simple craters are bowl-shaped (concave). A dome is convex.
- crater-scaling-laws-and-morphology.md, impact-basin-mechanics.md, crater Q explanations: central-peak and peak-ring formation is called "isostatic rebound" occurring "in minutes". Isostatic adjustment is slow viscous relaxation. The fast floor uplift is dynamic gravitational collapse. The term is misapplied.
- crater-morphology-and-degradation.md, Q3 explanation: "Crater size and degradation state are independent variables." Small craters are erased faster than large ones of the same age, so the two are not independent.
- impact-basin-mechanics.md, Core Idea ">100 km impactors" and Explainer "impactors hundreds of kilometers across". Lunar basins such as Orientale are usually modeled with impactors of a few tens of km (about 50–65 km for Orientale).
- impact-basin-mechanics.md, Explainer: maria filled basins because "the thermal pulse from the impact can trigger long-lived volcanic activity". Mare basalts mostly erupted hundreds of millions of years after their basins formed. Their link to impact heating is doubtful; thin crust and basin topography are the usual explanation.
- impact-cratering-mechanics.md, Q3 explanation: "rock under megapascals of shock pressure flows like a viscous fluid". The Explainer correctly says hundreds of gigapascals.
- impact-cratering-mechanics.md, Q1 and Explainer: transient crater "typically 20–30 times" the impactor diameter. Common estimates are about 10–20 times. Check the source.
- eruptive-styles-and-lava-rheology.md, Explainer: low-gravity lava "can travel enormous distances because gravitational resistance to flow is reduced", and the maria spread "precisely because low gravity allowed thin basaltic lava to spread far". Gravity drives lava flow; lower gravity slows it. Long lunar flows are attributed to very low-viscosity, iron-rich basalt and high eruption rates.
- eruptive-styles-and-lava-rheology.md, Core Idea: "explosive eruptions dominate small bodies". The Moon's maria (effusive flood basalts) contradict this, and the Explainer itself describes them.
- exoplanet-characterization-spectroscopy.md, Q2 explanation: the radius valley "was established" from "a bimodal density distribution". It was found in the radius distribution (Kepler planets with precise stellar radii), not from densities.
- exoplanet-atmospheric-composition-spectroscopy.md, Q1 explanation: "CH₄ is destroyed by O₂ on timescales of ~1,000 years". Earth's methane lifetime is about a decade (mostly through OH). atmospheric-chemistry-planets.md and biosignatures say "thousands of years". The files disagree, and neither matches the usual figure.
- exoplanet-atmospheric-composition-spectroscopy.md, Core Idea: "Hydrogen-dominated atmospheres show Rayleigh scattering; secondary atmospheres show molecular bands". H₂-rich atmospheres also show strong molecular bands (the water detections on hot Jupiters). This is a false dichotomy.
- exoplanet-mass-radius-relation.md, Explainer: "A planet twice Earth's mass is only about 1.25 times Earth's radius". 1.26 is the uncompressed constant-density value. With compression it is closer to about 1.2. The text claims compression and quotes the no-compression number.
- fluvial-processes-planetary-surfaces.md, Explainer: lower Martian gravity means water "carrying less sediment". Several studies argue the reverse, since lower gravity lowers settling velocity and keeps grains suspended. The Q1 claim about channels being wider and shallower for this reason is also uncertain.
- giant-impact-hypothesis.md, Q2 explanation: "The giant impact hypothesis actually predicts near-identical oxygen isotope ratios". Canonical simulations predicted a Theia-dominated Moon, so the match was a problem for the hypothesis. The file's own Explainer (last paragraph) says so.
- giant-impact-hypothesis.md, Q0/Q3 and Explainer: "Moon's iron depletion (~1–2% of mass vs. Earth's ~32%)". This compares core mass fractions, not iron content. The Moon's rocks contain substantial FeO.
- impact-induced-outgassing.md, Q1/Q4 and Explainer: the Moon-forming impact "stripped Earth of its primordial ... atmosphere entirely". Impact simulations generally find partial loss, depending on conditions. "Secondary atmosphere" mainly means outgassed rather than captured nebular gas, so it is not direct evidence of impact stripping.
- impact-induced-outgassing.md, Explainer: "Oblique impacts are less efficient at eroding atmosphere". Some modeling work finds oblique impacts erode as well as or better than vertical ones. Check the source.
- late-heavy-bombardment.md, Core Idea, Explainer, and Q0/Q2/Q3: the LHB is presented as an established spike at 3.8–4.1 Ga, and Q3 marks "continuation of debris clearing" as false. Current work questions the cataclysm. Arguments include Imbrium-ejecta bias in Apollo samples, sawtooth or smooth-decline models, and dynamical studies placing the giant-planet instability early (within ~100 Myr). This needs rewriting with hedges.
- late-heavy-bombardment.md, Q1 and Explainer: "a substantial fraction of Earth's ocean water arrived during this period". Most estimates put the LHB's water delivery at a small fraction of the oceans.
- mantle-convection-planets.md, Explainer and Q4: larger planets "generate more radiogenic heating per unit volume". Heating per unit volume depends on composition, not size. Size matters through the volume-to-surface ratio.
- mantle-convection-planets.md, Q2 (answer true): stagnant-lid "volcanic outgassing ceases". Stagnant-lid planets can stay volcanically active for billions of years (Mars's Tharsis). The statement should be false or reworded.
- mantle-convection-planets.md, Q0 and Explainer: Mars "transitioned" from mobile-lid, and convection "stalled". Most models put Mars in a stagnant-lid regime for nearly all of its history. Stagnant-lid is also a mode of active interior convection, not stalled convection. The mobile/stagnant-lid choice depends mainly on lithospheric strength and viscosity contrast, not on a Rayleigh-number threshold alone.
- mantle-convection-planets.md, Q1 explanation: "A small planet with high viscosity (option D)". Option D says low viscosity.
- comets-structure-and-composition.md, Explainer: Rosetta "detected amino acids". Rosetta reported glycine only, one amino acid.
- crustal-heat-flow-and-geotherms.md, Explainer: "30 km down, temperatures reach 750–900°C — hot enough to partially melt some rock types". This is a straight-line extrapolation. Real continental geotherms curve, and Moho temperatures in stable crust are usually much lower.
- atmospheric-escape-mechanisms.md, Q2 (answer false): "A planet with a strong global magnetic field is mostly protected from atmospheric escape." Whether magnetic fields protect atmospheres overall is debated. The statement is arguably true for ion pickup. The item is ambiguous.
- atmospheric-escape-mechanisms.md, Q1 explanation: farther from the Sun "reduces solar wind intensity slightly". At 1.52 AU the solar wind flux is about 43% of Earth's, which is not slight.
- exoplanet-transmission-spectroscopy.md, Explainer: "JWST's first transmission spectrum of the rocky exoplanet TRAPPIST-1b". The headline JWST TRAPPIST-1b result was thermal emission at 15 μm. The transmission data were dominated by stellar contamination. Wording needs care.
- meteorites-and-planetary-samples.md, Core Idea: meteorites come from "comets". No meteorite has been confirmed as cometary.
- atmospheric-dynamics-exoplanets.md, Q0/Q2 explanations: the superrotating jet is "driven by Coriolis deflection". Standard theory attributes it to standing planetary-scale (Rossby/Kelvin) waves that pump momentum to the equator. This is an oversimplification.
