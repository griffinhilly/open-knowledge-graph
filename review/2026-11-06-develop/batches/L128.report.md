## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:
- absolute-monarchy-divine-right.md, Core Idea: "elaborated by thinkers like Jean Bodin and Thomas Hobbes ... typically justified through divine right doctrine". Hobbes grounded absolute sovereignty in a social contract, not divine right. Explainer also calls Filmer "the English theologian"; he was a political writer, not a theologian.
- age-of-exploration-motivations.md, Explainer and Q1/Q2: "The papal Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)" and "through papal arbitration". Tordesillas was a bilateral Spain–Portugal treaty that revised the papal bulls of 1493. Papal confirmation came later (1506).
- encomienda-labor-colonial-system.md, Q3 explanation: "The Pope's grant (via the Treaty of Tordesillas)". Same error; the papal grant was the 1493 bulls (Inter caetera). Its short-answer also says the Valladolid debates "functioned to authorize conquest rather than question it", which overstates; Valladolid openly questioned the justice of conquest.
- atlantic-racialized-slavery.md, Explainer: "This 'one-drop' logic, developed formally in American law in the 19th century". Most formal one-drop statutes date to the early 20th century (e.g., Virginia 1924).
- atlantic-slave-trade-commerce.md, Q2 explanation: "no ship returned empty"; atlantic-triangular-trade.md, Q1: "no leg was a 'dead run'". Overstated; many slave ships returned to Europe with little cargo, and many voyages were bilateral (e.g., Brazil–Angola).
- baroque-sensuality-theology.md, Explainer: "This is why Baroque did not spread to Protestant England or the Dutch Republic". Contradicts baroque-art-and-culture.md (Rembrandt, Vermeer, Bach as Baroque) and ignores English Baroque architecture (Wren, Hawksmoor, Vanbrugh). Q2 and Q4 repeat this.
- british-east-india-company-colonialism.md, Core Idea: "The EIC pioneered the joint-stock company". Earlier English joint-stock companies existed (e.g., Muscovy Company), and the EIC first financed voyage by voyage; the VOC had permanent capital first. Q4: "Effective Crown oversight came only after the 1857 rebellion" understates the Regulating Act (1773) and India Act (1784).
- caravel-ship-design-maritime-technology.md, Q2 and Q4: return winds "blow from the west" causing headwinds for homeward ships. For Iberian ships the westerlies were tailwinds home; the headwinds were the northeast trades off West Africa (hence the volta do mar). Explainer also credits the caravel with introducing the stern rudder, which northern European cogs already used from about the 13th century. "20–30 meters long" seems high for typical caravels.
- columbian-exchange-ecology.md, Explainer: "Smallpox preceded Cortés into the Aztec capital". Cortés entered Tenochtitlan in November 1519; smallpox arrived in 1520 with the Narváez expedition. Q1 repeats "Smallpox reached Tenochtitlan before Cortés did".
- copernican-heliocentric-universe.md, Core Idea: "heretical to both Church and many scientists" conflicts with the file's own Explainer (no condemnation until 1616). Explainer: "By the sixteenth century the system had accumulated dozens of these corrections ... epicycles on epicycles" repeats a common myth; Copernicus's system used a comparable number of circles.
- diderot-encyclopedia-enlightenment.md, Explainer: the "cannibalism among Pacific islanders" example and the "ecclesiastical power" -> "tyranny"/"superstition" cross-reference could not be confirmed. The well-known example is "Anthropophages" -> Eucharistie/Communion/Autel. The Explainer also implies the work was arranged by subject; articles were alphabetical, with the tree of knowledge supplying categories.
- dutch-republic-and-commerce.md, Q4: "Amsterdam Stock Exchange (Beurs van Hendrick de Keyser) opened in 1602". Trading in VOC shares began in 1602, but de Keyser's exchange building opened in 1611.
- early-modern-anatomy-and-medical-thought.md, Explainer: Vesalius publishing the same year as Copernicus "was not accidental". Unsupported. "Medieval university medical training meant reading Galen, not cutting bodies" understates the university dissections held from about 1300.
- early-modern-navigation-and-cartography.md, Explainer: "The Portuguese systematically institutionalized this knowledge at Sagres". Most historians regard the "school of Sagres" as a later myth.
- early-modern-gender-and-family-relations.md, Explainer and Q2/Q3: coverture is presented as a doctrine European states "encoded ... into national legal codes". Coverture was English common law; continental systems differed (community property, dotal regimes).
- early-modern-witch-hunts-anxiety.md, Core Idea: "the largest instance of collective violence in early modern history" is doubtful (Thirty Years' War, conquest of the Americas). Its "roughly 1580-1630s" span for "Europe and North America" excludes Salem (1692). Explainer says women gained "new economic roles as the guild system weakened", which conflicts with early-modern-gender-and-family-relations.md (roles "narrowed").
- edict-of-nantes.md, Explainer: "the edict did not apply to Calvinist communities in other parts of the realm" is unclear or wrong. Q4: Locke's Letter (1689) "appeared the same year" as the 1685 revocation; it did not. *Cuius regio, eius religio* was the Augsburg (Empire) principle, not a French one.
- encomienda-colonial-labor.md, Explainer: encomenderos worked people to death "in silver mines (especially at Potosí in Peru)". Potosí labor came mainly through Toledo's mita, not the encomienda, and Potosí lay in Upper Peru (modern Bolivia).
- early-modern-expanding-literacy.md, Core Idea: "roughly 15% to 50%" is a single Europe-wide figure for rates that varied widely by region and sex. Treat as a rough average at most.
