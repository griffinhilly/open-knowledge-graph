## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:
- niche-concept-fundamental-realized.md, Q2 (true-false): "A species' realized niche is generally smaller than its fundamental niche." answer: false. The explanation itself says "In most cases the realized niche is a subset", and the Explainer says "always a subset (or, in rare cases involving mutualism...)". The statement as worded is true; the answer key looks wrong.
- population-bottleneck-drift-inbreeding.md, Q0: says one generation at Ne ≈ 50 causes "substantial diversity" loss. Expected heterozygosity loss in one generation is 1/(2Ne) = 1%; only rare-allele richness drops sharply. The answer conflates allelic richness with heterozygosity.
- population-bottleneck-drift-inbreeding.md, Q4 explanation: cheetahs "still show near-zero immunological diversity". Later MHC studies found low but not near-zero variation; overstated.
- population-bottleneck-drift-inbreeding.md, Q3 explanation: defines heterozygosity as "the frequency of the most common alleles". Imprecise.
- reproductive-isolation-accumulation.md, Q0 explanation: "neither 'a' (in AAbb) nor 'b' (in aaBB)". The labels are swapped: AAbb carries b, aaBB carries a. The short-answer Q4 also assigns 'a' to Population 1, opposite to Q0 and the Explainer.
- reproductive-isolation-accumulation.md, Explainer: order "postzygotic inviability, and finally hybrid sterility". Comparative data (e.g., Drosophila, Haldane's rule) generally show hybrid sterility evolving before inviability.
- net-primary-productivity-allocation.md, Explainer: tropical rainforest NPP "roughly 2,000 grams of carbon per square meter per year". About 2,000 g is the usual dry-matter figure; in carbon it is roughly half that. Temperate and desert figures may have the same unit mix-up.
- nitrogen-fixation-limitation-cycling.md, Explainer and Q3: "Humans now fix more nitrogen industrially than all natural processes combined." Industrial fixation alone is comparable to natural terrestrial fixation, not clearly larger than terrestrial plus marine; total human-caused fixation is the usual comparison. Overstated.
- nitrogen-fixation-limitation-cycling.md, Q4 answer: nitrogenase must "break all three bonds simultaneously". Reduction proceeds stepwise; wording is misleading.
- phosphorus-cycling-freshwater-marine.md, Core Idea, Explainer, Q3: upwelling returns "sediment-bound phosphorus". Upwelling returns phosphate dissolved in deep water (mostly regenerated from sinking organic matter), not phosphorus locked in sediments.
- nutrient-cycling-phosphorus-sulfur.md, Q2: phosphorus "unavailable through atmospheric deposition in most ecosystems". Dust deposition is a meaningful P input in some systems (e.g., old tropical soils); "most" may be defensible but the explanation says every step is solid/dissolved.
- phenotypic-variation-populations.md, Core Idea: environmental variation is "invisible to natural selection". Selection acts on phenotypes regardless of cause; environmental variation produces no evolutionary response. Imprecise wording.
- natural-selection-types-and-examples.md, Explainer and Q0/Q4: stabilizing selection "most common" and disruptive "rarest". Field surveys of selection gradients do not clearly support this ranking; contested.
- polyploidy-instant-speciation.md, Core Idea: triploid "cannot produce viable gametes" (too absolute; a few are viable) and polyploidy "has driven major radiations (wheat, cotton)" (these are single allopolyploid crops, not radiations). Q1 "6n = 42" should be 2n = 6x = 42. Q4 answer: "In a tetraploid, every chromosome... can form stable pairs" ignores multivalent pairing in autotetraploids; "steric incompatibility" is the wrong term.
- predator-prey-coevolution-mechanisms.md, Core Idea: "tetrodotoxin resistance in pufferfish and snake predators". The classic system is rough-skinned newts and garter snakes (as the Explainer says); pufferfish carry TTX themselves.
- predator-prey-coevolution-mechanisms.md, Q4 answer: Red Queen predicts "continuous escalation". Red Queen dynamics can also be fluctuating cycles without escalation.
- population-age-structure-life-history.md, Q1 explanation: "protecting nesting beaches (adult females) yields far more demographic return than protecting hatcheries". The well-known loggerhead matrix analysis found large juvenile/subadult survival most influential; nesting-beach protection mainly protects eggs. Also the answer option (reproductive adults) may not match that analysis.
- population-stochasticity-and-extinction.md, Q3: "cutting a population in half more than doubles its extinction risk" stated as a general true rule. Not general; depends on size and model.
- source-sink-population-models.md, Q0: losing 1 of 3 sources predicted to cause "gradual collapse across the whole network". Two sources remain; reduced sink sizes are expected, full collapse is overstated.
- trophic-cascades-in-food-webs.md, Explainer and Q0/Q1/Q2/Q4; trophic-cascade-top-down-control.md Explainer and Q3: Yellowstone willow/aspen recovery, the "landscape of fear" mechanism, and wolves altering stream courses are presented as settled. These are actively disputed in the literature (recovery is patchy; behavioral effects weakly supported in several studies; beaver return was aided by releases outside the park). Should be hedged.
- rainfall-productivity-biogeographic-gradients.md, Q2: true-false asks whether, "according to the species-energy hypothesis", the main reason is more solar energy; answer false. The hypothesis does say that, so the item is ambiguous. Also the hump-shaped productivity-diversity curve is contested (large multi-site studies found no general hump); text is mostly hedged with "many".
- sexual-selection-speciation-driver.md, Core Idea: sexual selection "a major mechanism for sympatric speciation". Theory and data suggest it rarely suffices alone without ecological divergence; contested. Explainer: females "mated indiscriminately" under altered light is overstated; and the species-richness/ornament correlation has mixed support in comparative studies.
- secondary-succession-post-disturbance.md, Explainer: crabgrass and ragweed described as producing "wind-dispersed seeds"; neither is wind-dispersed (horseweed is).
- trophic-efficiency-energy-loss.md, Q3: 5-20% efficiency as a "hard energetic constraint" on chain length. Ecosystem size and disturbance also predict chain length; energy limitation alone has mixed support.
- plant-animal-coevolutionary-networks.md, Explainer: says this topic connects to "the trophic cascade concepts this topic builds toward", but builds-toward is empty. Q4 answer: "specialists interact only with generalists" is too absolute.
- mutation-as-evolutionary-force.md, Q2 explanation: equilibrium frequency "approximately μ/s" holds for dominant or additive alleles; for fully recessive deleterious alleles it is about the square root of μ/s.
