## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:

1. said-postcolonial-essays.md, Explainer: "Edward Said (1926-2003)". Said was born in 1935.
2. said-postcolonial-essays.md, Explainer: "his essay collections—particularly *Orientalism*, ... and *Culture and Imperialism*". Those two are book-length studies. Of the three titles, only *The World, the Text, and the Critic* is an essay collection.
3. polemic-and-manifestos.md, question 3 explanation: "Susan Sontag's polemics against camp". "Notes on Camp" describes camp and is partly sympathetic to it. It is not a polemic against camp. "Against Interpretation" would be the accurate example.
4. wolfe-new-journalism-techniques.md, Explainer: lists *Bonfire of the Vanities* among works showing that "reporting and literary sophistication could coexist". The Bonfire of the Vanities (1987) is a novel. "Pioneered new journalism" also overstates Wolfe's role. He named and anthologized the movement, but writers such as Talese and Capote worked in it at the same time.
5. temporal-montage-nonfiction.md, question 4 explanation: says the moments at ages 8, 25 and 35 "aren't in chronological order", but 8 → 25 → 35 is chronological. Also in the Explainer, Deborah Eisenberg is known mainly as a short-story writer, and *I Love Dick* is usually classed as a novel or autofiction. Both are doubtful examples of temporal montage in nonfiction.
6. scientific-observation.md, Explainer: "His scientific observation of finch beaks across the Galápagos was both systematic and aesthetic—he noticed small variations precisely and found them beautiful". This repeats a common myth. Darwin labeled his finch specimens poorly and saw their significance only later, after John Gould classified them.
7. scene-reconstruction-memory.md, Explainer: "Norman Mailer (In the Executioner's Song)". The title is *The Executioner's Song*.
8. reflective-nonfiction-introspection.md, Explainer: psychological realism "pioneered by writers like James Joyce and Virginia Woolf". Psychological realism comes before them (for example Dostoevsky and Henry James). Joyce and Woolf are linked with stream of consciousness.
9. place-based-essayistic.md, Explainer: lists Melissa Febos as a writer "who use[s] place as their primary subject". Febos is known mainly for memoir and essays on the body and desire, not for place-based writing. "thoreau" is also lowercase. Separately, question index 3 starts with "True:" but its answer is false.
10. research-visibility-process.md, Explainer: "Writers like David Foster Wallace pioneered this approach in literary essays, using extensive endnotes". Footnoted literary essays existed before Wallace, so "pioneered" overstates it. His notes are also mostly digressive, not about showing research.
11. unreliable-narrator-nonfiction.md, Explainer: "An unreliable fiction narrator often knows they are lying or distorting". Many well-known unreliable fiction narrators do not knowingly lie (for example the narrator of *The Remains of the Day*). The difference lies in the author's design, not in the narrator's awareness.
12. travel-writing-form.md, Explainer: travel writing "emerged in the Renaissance". Travel narratives go back to antiquity and the medieval period (for example Herodotus, Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta). The Explainer of travel-cultural-encounter.md itself mentions Marco Polo.
13. representation-responsibility.md, Explainer: presents composite characters and "reimagined scenes" as accepted practice "while maintaining commitment to emotional and thematic truth". This contradicts truth-fabrication-ethics.md, which calls undisclosed composites dishonest. The claim needs a disclosure qualifier.
14. thoreau-naturalist-philosopher.md, Explainer: says his journals "weren't intended for publication". Scholars dispute this, because Thoreau reworked the journal as a literary project. It should be softened.
15. profile-literary-portrait.md, Explainer: "they depict living people who did not consent to be subjects of a book or article in the same way a biography subject (often deceased) cannot object". The sentence is garbled. Profile subjects usually agree to be interviewed.
