## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- ampere-maxwell-law.md, Explainer: "The problem Maxwell discovered in 1865". Maxwell introduced displacement current in 1861-62 ("On Physical Lines of Force"). 1865 is the date of "A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field".
- ampere-maxwell-law.md, Explainer: "everything from radio transmitters to fiber-optic cables to the photoelectric effect traces back to this single correction". The photoelectric effect is a quantum phenomenon that classical Maxwell theory does not explain.
- cavity-resonator-solutions.md, Core Idea: "TMₙₘₚ modes have all three field components while TEₙₘₚ modes have zero Ez or Hz". This is garbled. TM modes have H_z = 0; TE modes have E_z = 0.
- cavity-resonator-solutions.md, Q2 (true-false, answer true) and Explainer: "dominant mode ... determined primarily by the cavity's largest dimension". At least two indices must be nonzero, so the lowest mode depends on the two largest dimensions.
- cavity-resonators.md, Explainer: "m, n, p are non-negative integers (not all zero)" and "Each distinct triple (m,n,p) corresponds to a unique standing-wave field pattern". At least two indices must be nonzero. When all three are nonzero, a TE and a TM mode share the same triple.
- cavity-resonators.md, Q1 explanation: "Q = (energy stored)/(energy lost per cycle)". The 2π factor is missing.
- cherenkov-radiation.md, Explainer: "As the particle speeds up, θ_c opens toward 90°." The maximum is arccos(1/n), about 41° in water, as the next sentence says.
- circular-waveguide-propagation.md, Core Idea: "cutoff frequencies given by jₙₘ = (λc/2πa)·(ωc/ω)" and "azimuthal symmetry is broken by propagation or mode numbers". Both are garbled. Should be f_c = c·x/(2πa), with x = j′ₙₘ for TE and jₙₘ for TM.
- circular-waveguide-propagation.md, Explainer: "f_c = c·j_{nm}/(2πa)" is stated for both TE and TM. TE should use j′_{nm}. Also, rotary joints usually use the azimuthally symmetric TM₀₁ mode, not the TE₁₁ degeneracy. And square rectangular guides do have degeneracies, against "a subtlety absent in rectangular guides".
- classical-electron-radius.md, Core Idea: "(re/r)·(a/c²)" is not dimensionless. Core Idea and Explainer present r_e as the scale where quantum corrections start. In fact quantum effects matter already at the reduced Compton wavelength, about 137 r_e.
- classical-electron-radius.md, Q2 stem calls Thomson scattering a "quantum-regime result". It is the classical, low-energy limit. Explainer: electron scattering is the "dominant opacity source in stellar interiors". That holds only in hot, massive-star interiors.
- conductivity-complex-dielectric.md, Explainer final sentence and Q3 explanation: "Kramers-Kronig relations guarantee there are corresponding features in ε' at those same frequencies". This is muddled. Absorption elsewhere gives smooth normal dispersion in the transparent window, not features at those frequencies.
- conformal-mapping-electrostatics.md, wedge question explanation: "Near the wedge tip (r → 0), this determines how field strength diverges". The field goes as r^(π/α − 1), so it diverges only for α > π. For α < π it vanishes.
- cylindrical-harmonics-em.md, Core Idea: "Bessel functions of integer and half-integer order". Half-integer orders belong to spherical problems (spherical Bessel functions), not cylindrical Laplace problems. Explainer: "J₁ and J₂ describe ... one and two lobes" is loose, since cos(nφ) has 2n lobes.
- dielectric-susceptibility-permittivity.md, Q1 explanation: "D is determined by free charge alone". This holds only with enough symmetry, because ∇×D = ∇×P.
- dispersion-relations-em-waves.md, Q1 correct option: broadening is attributed to "different phase velocities". It is caused by group velocity varying with frequency (GVD). Q4 explanation: group velocity "constrained to be ≤ c". That is false in anomalous dispersion; the signal front is the quantity limited to c.
- dispersion-relations.md, Q2 explanation: group velocity "in causal media is ≤ c". This is false, as above. Explainer runner analogy: "The 'group' moves at the average velocity of the cluster". Group velocity is dω/dk, not an average of phase velocities.
- drude-model-conductivity.md, Q1: X-rays "pass through" silver. That is overstated, since core-electron absorption is strong. Q2: ionospheric plasma frequency "tens of MHz" is high. Typical critical frequencies are up to about 10-15 MHz.
- electric-dipole-radiation.md, Q2 and Explainer: the half-wave dipole antenna pattern is called "exactly the sin²θ shape". A half-wave dipole is not short compared with the wavelength. Its pattern is [cos((π/2)cosθ)/sinθ]², which is close to sin²θ but more directive.
- electric-flux-and-gauss-law.md, Explainer: "Gauss's law in integral form is Coulomb's law plus the superposition principle" (mathematically equivalent). Gauss's law alone does not fix E; it also needs ∇×E = 0. Also: "differential form is more general than the integral form". The two are equivalent through the divergence theorem.
- electromagnetic-angular-momentum-radiation.md, Core Idea: "density l = ε₀(E × B)/c²". That expression is wrong; the angular momentum density is ε₀ r × (E × B). Explainer: "l = r × g = ε₀(r × (E × B))/c²" has an extra 1/c². Explainer: "carries Lℓ per photon" should be ℓℏ.
- electromagnetic-field-tensor.md, Explainer: "the angle between E and B is a relativistic invariant". Only E·B is invariant, so perpendicularity and the sign of E·B carry over, but the general angle does not. Q5 says the same thing ("relative magnitudes and angles ... frame-independent").
- electromagnetic-wave-equation.md, Q3: says the speed of light was known "from astronomical observations" through Rømer "to good accuracy". Rømer's estimate was roughly 25% off. Maxwell compared his result with terrestrial measurements by Fizeau (1849) and Foucault (1862).
- electromagnetic-waves-in-dielectrics.md, Core Idea: "frequency-dependent electric permittivity and permeability". But the wave equation given uses μ₀, and dielectrics normally have μ ≈ μ₀.
- em-angular-momentum-density.md, Core Idea and Explainer: say ∫ r × g gives the "orbital" angular momentum, as distinct from spin. In fact ∫ r × g is the total field angular momentum, spin included. Explainer: spin and orbital are "separately conserved". In general they are not, since focusing and scattering convert one into the other. Explainer: says EM field angular momentum "mediates" the Einstein-de Haas and Barnett effects. That claim is dubious; those effects involve exchange between electron spin and lattice rotation.
- em-field-momentum-density.md, Core Idea: "g = S/c² = (ε₀/c²)(E × B)". It should be ε₀(E × B). The Core Idea also says radiation pressure has "magnitude equal to energy density divided by c²". Pressure on an absorber equals u, and momentum density equals u/c. The Explainer has it right.
