## Flags

- spatial-geographic-methods.md, Explainer and Q0: "The 1884–85 Berlin Conference drew most of sub-Saharan Africa's borders". This is a common myth. The conference set rules for claiming territory; most borders were fixed later in bilateral treaties and boundary commissions.
- spatial-geographic-methods.md, Q1: "medieval Paris placed its cathedral at the highest, most central point". Notre-Dame stands on the Île de la Cité, a low river island, not the highest point of the city.
- spatial-geographic-methods.md, Explainer: Ottoman control of Constantinople "leading directly to the Atlantic voyages". Portuguese Atlantic exploration began before 1453; the direct-cause claim is an older, contested thesis.
- temporal-frameworks.md, Q0: "why wages declined for agricultural laborers in 14th-century England focuses on the effects of the Black Death". After the Black Death, English laborers' wages generally rose, not fell.
- temporal-frameworks.md, Explainer: "Julian and Gregorian calendars coexisted in Europe until the 18th century". Russia kept the Julian calendar until 1918 and Greece until 1923, so coexistence lasted into the 20th century.
- temporal-frameworks.md, Q1 explanation: textual sources "cannot reveal multi-century structural trends". Overstated; long textual series (harvest dates, price and tax records) are used for multi-century trends.
- time-consciousness-periodization.md, Explainer: humanists "wanted to mark off the classical past from what came before them". Garbled; they marked off the classical past from the period between it and their own time.
- time-consciousness-periodization.md, Q1 option C and explanation: calls the medieval Christian framework "cyclical" and ties "linear, progressive time" to the Sattelzeit. The Explainer itself lists Christian salvation as linear time; linear and progressive are conflated.
- time-consciousness-periodization.md, Q0: premise that medieval chronicles dated by regnal year "rather than by a continuous count from a fixed point". Anno Domini dating was widely used in medieval chronicles (for example after Bede), so the premise overgeneralizes.
- transnational-connected-history.md, Q0 and Explainer: Britain's growth and India's deindustrialization presented as "two sides of a single process" and tracing it as "the only way to understand it". This is a contested position in economic history and should be hedged.
- stratigraphic-interpretation-archaeology.md, Q3: statement mixes "any artifact" with "typically older", which makes the intended answer `false` arguable.
- Prerequisite: temporal-frameworks and temporal-scale-and-perspective cover nearly the same Braudel material; temporal-scale-and-perspective does not list temporal-frameworks (or vice versa for builds-toward). Possible duplicate topics.
