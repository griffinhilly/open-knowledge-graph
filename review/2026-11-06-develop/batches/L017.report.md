## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- wkb-quantization-rule.md, Core Idea: "∮ p(x) dx = (n + ½)πℏ". The closed (round-trip) integral equals (n + ½)h = (n + ½)2πℏ. (n + ½)πℏ is the one-way integral from x₁ to x₂. The Explainer states the correct form, so the Core Idea contradicts it. The same wrong form appears in Q0, Q1 and Q2 stems. Q0's explanation also says "½πℏ = ½h", which is false.
- wkb-quantization-rule.md, Explainer and Core Idea: says the WKB rule gives hydrogen's Bohr formula "to leading order, which is already exact". The radial WKB gives exact hydrogen energies only with the Langer replacement l(l+1) → (l + ½)². Without it the result is not exact. The claim needs that qualification.
- second-order-perturbation-theory.md, Core Idea: "E⁽²⁾ = ... always negative (energy is lowered)". That is true only for the ground state (or a lowest level). The file's own Explainer and Q3 say excited-state corrections can have either sign.
- second-order-perturbation-theory.md, Explainer: "States close in energy contribute more; states far away contribute negligibly." Distant states are suppressed but not negligible in general, since many of them or a continuum can add up.
- slater-determinant.md, Q0: "Two electrons are assigned to the same spatial orbital ... What is the value? answer: Zero". Wrong. Two electrons with opposite spins can share a spatial orbital, as in the helium ground state. The determinant vanishes only for identical spin-orbitals. The stem should say "spin-orbital".
- relativistic-quantum-mechanics.md, Explainer last paragraph and Q1 explanation: "the positive-energy modes are particle creation operators and the negative-energy modes are antiparticle annihilation operators". In the standard field expansion, positive-frequency modes multiply particle annihilation operators, and negative-frequency modes multiply antiparticle creation operators. Either the operators or the roles are swapped.
- spin-orbit-coupling.md, Explainer: "Because H_SO contains L² and S², the eigenstates of L_z and S_z ... are no longer the right basis". The reason is that L·S does not commute with L_z or S_z. Containing L² and S² is not the cause.
- spin-orbit-coupling.md, Explainer: "J itself precesses around the z-axis at rate m_j". With no external field, J is conserved and does not precess. m_j is a quantum number, not a rate.
- total-angular-momentum.md, Q1 explanation: "weak external magnetic field dominating — the Paschen-Back regime". Paschen-Back is the strong-field regime, where the field dominates spin-orbit coupling.
- total-angular-momentum.md, Explainer last paragraph: uses the uncoupled basis for "weak magnetic fields where spin-orbit coupling can be neglected". It should say strong fields. The sentence is also missing a verb ("when L and S independently conserved quantities").
- transition-probabilities-quantum.md, Explainer and Q4 answer: parity alone is said to give Δl = ±1, and Q4 says "the minimal such difference is 1, giving Δl = ±1". Parity gives only odd Δl. |Δl| ≥ 3 is excluded because r is a rank-1 (vector) operator, through the triangle rule. It is not excluded because 1 is the smallest odd number.
- time-independent-perturbation-theory.md, Q1 explanation: "(2) The energy denominator E_n − E_m must be large — states nearly degenerate ... mix in most strongly". This contradicts itself. Strong mixing needs a small gap.
- uncertainty-principle-canonical.md, Explainer: "Non-commuting operators cannot share eigenstates". This is false in general. For example, L_x and L_y share the l = 0 state. Non-commuting operators cannot share a complete eigenbasis.
- uncertainty-principle-canonical.md, Explainer last paragraph: "For energy eigenstates, ⟨[Ĥ, Â]⟩ = 0 ... so ... you can measure compatible observables with arbitrary precision in a stationary state". The conclusion does not follow. The vanishing commutator expectation gives a trivial bound on ΔE·ΔA because ΔE = 0. It says nothing about other observables being sharp.
- variational-principle-quantum.md, Q3 explanation: "A lower E_trial ... means the new trial wavefunction has higher overlap with the true ground state." Not guaranteed. A lower energy does not imply a higher overlap in general.
- variational-principle-quantum.md, Explainer: "For helium, the same form with independent exponents for each electron gives ∼2% error". The ~2% (−77.5 vs −79.0 eV) figure belongs to the single shared effective charge Z = 27/16. Independent exponents (the open-shell form) do better.
- variational-method-quantum.md, Explainer: "expanding your trial family ... always lowers the variational energy". It never raises it but can leave it unchanged. Q1 states this correctly.
- zero-point-energy-quantum.md, Q1 explanation: "Helium is uniquely light ... for all heavier elements". Hydrogen is lighter and still solidifies, because H₂ molecules bind more strongly. The helium argument needs both large zero-point energy and very weak attraction.
- scattering-theory-quantum.md, Q0: a sharp peak at a particular angle is explained as "a resonance in that direction". A resonance is a feature in energy, not in angle. An angular peak more often comes from diffraction or interference of partial waves.
- scattering-theory-quantum.md, Explainer: "For a hard sphere of radius R, σ = πR² classically; quantum interference modifies this at short wavelengths." The quantum hard-sphere cross section is 4πR² at long wavelength and 2πR² at short wavelength. The bigger change is at long wavelength.
- born-approximation.md (not in this batch, read as a register sample), Q3 explanation: "for the pure Coulomb potential, all higher-order Born terms vanish". False. The higher-order Coulomb Born terms diverge. The exact Coulomb cross section matches Rutherford, but the amplitude carries a logarithmic phase that the Born series does not reproduce term by term.
