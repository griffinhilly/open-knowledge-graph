## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- branching-processes.md, Explainer: "a continuous-state branching process (CSBP), which is a Lévy process with a specific branching structure". A CSBP is not itself a Lévy process; it is linked to a spectrally positive Lévy process through the Lamperti time change.
- branching-processes.md, Core Idea: "Extinction is certain (q = 1) if and only if μ ≤ 1". Needs the exclusion of the degenerate case P(X = 1) = 1 (the question explanation does state it).
- ergodic-theory-for-stochastic-processes.md, Explainer: "Ergodicity requires mixing". Mixing is strictly stronger than ergodicity; an ergodic process need not be mixing.
- ergodic-theory-for-stochastic-processes.md, Q2 explanation: "If the drift is zero or points outward ... the process escapes to infinity". With zero drift (Brownian motion in 1D) the process is null recurrent and does not escape to infinity; it just has no stationary distribution.
- feynman-kac-formula.md, Q3 explanation: "Each path is one-dimensional (a sequence of time steps), regardless of d". Each path is d-dimensional and its simulation cost grows with d; what is dimension-free is the Monte Carlo error rate.
- levy-processes.md, Explainer: "three defining properties" followed by a list of four.
- martingale-convergence-theorems.md, Explainer: "the posterior given n observations is a martingale that converges to the true parameter (on the support of the prior)". The posterior mean is the martingale; Doob's consistency holds for prior-almost every parameter under identifiability, not at every point of the support.
- martingale-representation-theorem.md, Explainer: "V(t) = E_Q[e^{-r(T-t)}C(S_T) | ℱ_t] that is a Q-martingale (after discounting). ... V(t) = V(0) + ∫H dW̃". The representation should be for the discounted price e^{-rt}V(t), and the share count H/(σS) should use the discounted stock price.
- martingale-representation-theorem.md, Q3 (true-false, answer false): the multidimensional representation theorem does hold in the filtration of (W₁, W₂) with two integrals. The question is ambiguous; it is "false" only if read as "with W₁ alone".
- optimal-stopping.md, Explainer: "The exercise boundary S*(t) is a decreasing function of time (as maturity approaches, the threshold for exercising drops ...)". For the American put the boundary rises toward K as t → T.
- optimal-stopping.md, Q2 explanation: "A ... increases precisely at times when continuation is strictly suboptimal but the optimal policy would not yet stop". Garbled; the increasing process grows when stopping is strictly better than continuing. Also the discrete-time result is the Doob decomposition, not Doob-Meyer.
- optimal-stopping.md, Explainer/Q3: "no closed-form formula exists for American options". True for the finite-maturity American put; the perpetual American put has a closed form and the American call on a non-dividend stock equals the European call.
- properties-of-brownian-motion.md, Explainer: "the first hitting time T_a ... has an inverse Gaussian distribution". For driftless Brownian motion T_a has the Lévy (stable index 1/2) distribution; inverse Gaussian applies with nonzero drift toward a.
- reflected-brownian-motion.md, Core Idea, Explainer and Q1 explanation: "|W(t)| ... equivalently X(t) = W(t) + L_t^0(W)". With the same W this is false. Tanaka gives |W| = β + L^0(W) with β = ∫sgn(W)dW, a different Brownian motion; Lévy's theorem says |W| has the law of W - min(W, running).
- stochastic-calculus-in-finance.md, Q4 (true-false): the stem "This empirical fact:" ends without a proposition, so "true" has nothing to attach to.
- stochastic-control-basics.md, Q1 explanation: "supermartingale for all controls and a martingale for the optimal control". For a minimization problem it is a submartingale under arbitrary controls (as the Explainer says).
- stochastic-control-basics.md, Q3 explanation: "the Sharpe ratio (μ-r)/σ²". The Sharpe ratio is (μ-r)/σ.
- stochastic-differential-equations.md, Q2 explanation and Explainer: "The Tanaka SDE dX = sgn(X)dW has a weak solution (|W(t)|, which is a reflected Brownian motion)". |W| is nonnegative and not a local martingale, so it cannot solve dX = sgn(X)dW; the weak solutions are Brownian motions B with W = ∫sgn(B)dB.
- stochastic-differential-equations.md, Q4 explanation: "it is unbounded almost surely at any finite time". At a fixed time X(t) is finite a.s.; what is unbounded is sup over t ≥ 0.
- stochastic-integration-semimartingales.md, Q1 and Explainer: semimartingale defined with A "predictable". The general definition only needs A adapted and of finite variation (as the Core Idea says); predictable A is the canonical decomposition of special semimartingales. Q1's "correct definition" is therefore not the general one.
- stochastic-integration-semimartingales.md, Q3 answer/explanation: claims the stochastic integral against fBM is "undefined" and that rough paths need H > 1/3. For H > 1/2 pathwise Young integrals exist; Gaussian rough path lifts of fBM exist for H > 1/4. The reason fBM with H > 1/2 is not a semimartingale is that it has zero quadratic variation but infinite variation.
- stochastic-integration-semimartingales.md, Explainer: "The three correction terms beyond the naive chain rule capture: (1) the stochastic integral (first-order)". The stochastic integral is the naive term, not a correction.
