## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- airy-isostasy-model.md, Explainer: "a 5 km mountain range might require a crustal root extending 30–35 km". The file's own formula with 2,700/3,300 kg/m³ gives r ≈ 4.5h ≈ 22.5 km.
- conduction-models-crustal-heat.md, Explainer: "If the curve bends — for instance, because heat-producing radioactive elements are concentrated in the upper crust — temperature evolves over time toward a new equilibrium." With heat production, the steady-state profile is itself curved. A curve means change only when heat sources do not balance it. This contradicts the next paragraph.
- conduction-models-crustal-heat.md, Explainer and Q0 explanation: "∂²T/∂z² = −A/κ". The heat-production term should divide by conductivity k, not diffusivity κ (Q4 uses k correctly).
- conduction-models-crustal-heat.md, Q1: "ocean floor ... deeper by a factor of 2". Only the subsidence below the ridge scales as √t, not the total depth.
- crustal-age-and-cooling-curves.md, Core Idea: "Crustal thickness ... change predictably with age". Oceanic crustal thickness stays roughly constant; the lithosphere is what thickens.
- crustal-age-and-cooling-curves.md, Q2 explanation: "Surface heat flow = κ × (temperature gradient)". This should be conductivity k.
- crustal-thickness-determination-gravity.md, Q4 explanation: "A 10 km variation in Moho depth produces a Bouguer anomaly difference of roughly 50–80 milligals". The slab estimate 2πGΔρΔh gives about 125–250 mGal for Δρ = 300–600 kg/m³.
- crustal-thickness-determination-gravity.md, Q2 explanation: "Continental cratons with ancient, thin crust". Cratons usually have thick crust.
- crustal-velocity-structure.md, Explainer: water-filled pores "can attenuate S-waves entirely if connected pore spaces contain free fluid". S-waves do travel through fluid-saturated porous rock.
- crustal-velocity-structure.md, Q0 explanation: it frames water saturation as what lowers Vp. Relative to dry rock, water saturation raises Vp; the low velocity comes from porosity and fractures.
- crustal-velocity-structure.md, Q3 explanation and Explainer: "denser minerals ... transmit waves faster" and "Higher mineral density ... translates to faster P-wave propagation". Density alone lowers velocity.
- earthquake-stress-inversion.md, Core Idea: "results reveal principal stress directions and magnitudes". Inversion of focal mechanisms recovers only orientations and the ratio R (the file's own Q2 says so).
- earthquake-stress-inversion.md, Explainer: σ₁ "parallel to a transform fault, or radial to a rift zone". On a strike-slip fault, σ₁ sits roughly 30–45° to the fault. In rifts, σ₁ is usually vertical.
- earths-magnetic-dipole-field-basics.md, Core Idea and Explainer: "tilted about 11°". This is an older value; the present dipole tilt is closer to 9–10°.
- elastic-plate-flexure.md, Q4 answer: "If Te = 0 (Airy isostasy) ... gravity anomalies closely mirror topography — every peak has a corresponding free-air gravity high". This is reversed for free-air gravity. Compensated (low-Te) topography gives a free-air anomaly near zero at long wavelengths. The Explainer's "gravity closely tracks topography = low Te" is correct only for the Bouguer anomaly, and the text never says which anomaly it means.
- elastic-plate-flexure.md, Core Idea: elastic support lasts "years to thousands of years ... before isostatic compensation occurs". Flexural support persists for millions of years (for example at Hawaii), and flexure is itself regional compensation.
- electrical-conductivity-crustal.md, Q2 explanation: "The Explainer explicitly states ...". This is a meta reference inside a question.
- electrical-resistivity-tomography.md, Explainer: "dipole-dipole (good lateral resolution, sensitive to horizontal boundaries)". Dipole-dipole is most sensitive to vertical structures (lateral changes); Wenner suits horizontal layering.
- focal-depth-classification-seismotectonics.md, Explainer: "Below about 700 km ... it has reached the transition zone where mineral phase changes absorb the strain". The transition zone is about 410–660 km. The text also omits transformational faulting as a candidate deep-focus mechanism. "Preserves brittle behavior" at 300–700 km is doubtful, because ordinary frictional failure is suppressed at those pressures.
- focal-mechanisms-and-stress-tensors.md, Explainer: "mostly black at the poles and white at the equator indicates thrust faulting". In lower-hemisphere beach balls, a thrust has a dark centre; "poles/equator" wording is confusing.
- geoid-determination-and-geodesy.md, Core Idea: "negative undulations over hot buoyant mantle plumes". The long-wavelength geoid is generally high over hotspot and plume regions (for example the African and Pacific large low-velocity provinces) because of dynamic topography.
- geomagnetic-dynamo-theory.md, Explainer: "These flowing currents of molten iron are the electrical currents that generate magnetic fields". Fluid flow is not electric current; currents are induced when the conductor moves through an existing field.
- geomagnetic-field-secular-variation.md, Explainer: north pole "recently accelerating to roughly 50 km per year". Its speed peaked near 50 km/yr around 2000–2005 and has since slowed. Westward drift is also presented as a uniform core rotation, while it is concentrated in the Atlantic hemisphere.
- geomagnetic-reversal-chronology.md, Explainer: "Earth's magnetic field periodically reverses polarity". Reversals are aperiodic. Also "Olduvai and Jaramillo subchrons ... lasting a few hundred thousand years each": Olduvai is about 170 kyr and Jaramillo about 70–80 kyr.
- geothermal-gradient-crustal-heat-flow.md, Core Idea: "radiogenic heat from the core and mantle". Core radiogenic heat is minor or uncertain; most radiogenic heat is in the mantle and crust.
- geothermal-gradient-crustal-heat-flow.md, Q4 answer: "quartzite conducts heat roughly 5–7 times better than shale". The file's own values (1–2 vs 5–7 W/m·K) imply about 3–5 times.
- gps-geodesy-and-crustal-deformation.md, Q1 explanation and Explainer: slow slip "without generating detectable seismic waves". Cascadia slow slip is accompanied by tectonic tremor that seismometers record (episodic tremor and slip).
- gravity-anomalies-and-interpretation.md, Core Idea: "International Gravity Reference Field for a spherical, non-rotating Earth". Normal gravity comes from a rotating reference ellipsoid (for example GRS80 / the International Gravity Formula).
- gravity-anomaly-separation-residual-regional.md, Q4 explanation: "the horizontal half-width ... equals approximately the source depth. So a 20 km-wide anomaly suggests a source at ~20 km". This mixes up full width with half-width; the half-width at half-maximum for a sphere is about 0.77z. Q0 option A, "broad gravity low caused by crustal thinning", is also wrong: thinning gives a high.
- gravity-potential-theory-earths-field.md, Core Idea: g = −∇U together with ∇²U = −4πGρ. This mixes sign conventions; with g = −∇U, Poisson's equation is ∇²U = +4πGρ.
- gravity-potential-theory-earths-field.md, Explainer: Laplace holds "in low-density rock". It does not, because any density gives Poisson. Also "If the crust is unusually thin ... gravity will fall below reference". Thin crust raises dense mantle and gives a positive anomaly.
- gravity-potential-theory-earths-field.md, Q2 explanation: "a shallow low-density body and a deeper high-density body can produce the same surface anomaly". These have opposite-sign contrasts. The intended pairing is a shallow, weak or small body versus a deep, strong or large one.
- coulomb-stress-transfer-faults.md, Q0 explanation and Explainer: "85% or more of aftershocks" is stated as a general figure. It is unsourced and varies by sequence.
