## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- trace-element-geochemistry.md, question 1 (true-false): statement "will be enriched by a factor of 100 in a 1% partial melt" is marked `answer: true`, but its own explanation computes about 50 for D = 0.01, F = 0.01. The answer should be false, or the statement should say "approaches 100 as F approaches 0".
- trace-element-geochemistry.md, Explainer: "A smooth, downward-sloping pattern indicates derivation from a depleted mantle source ... (like N-MORB)". On a primitive-mantle-normalized diagram ordered by decreasing incompatibility, N-MORB is depleted in the most incompatible elements, so its pattern rises toward the less incompatible end. A downward slope is the enriched (OIB-like) shape.
- trace-element-geochemistry.md, question 2 answer: calls the Nb-Ta trough "the definitive geochemical fingerprint of subduction-related magmatism". Crustal contamination produces the same anomaly. (The new misconception bullet says so.)
- mineral-stability-phase-diagrams.md, question 2 (short-answer): "diamond stability requires very high pressure (>4 GPa) regardless of temperature" and "diamonds form only at depths exceeding ~150 km". The graphite-diamond boundary has a positive Clapeyron slope, so the required pressure depends on temperature (much lower than 4 GPa at low T). "Regardless of temperature" is wrong.
- mineral-stability-phase-diagrams.md, Explainer: "counterclockwise paths characterize contact metamorphism". Anticlockwise P-T paths are usually linked to magmatic underplating or extension; contact metamorphism is closer to isobaric heating and cooling.
- organic-geochemistry.md, question 0: treats 2-alpha-methylhopanes as "diagnostic of cyanobacteria" and the 2.7 Ga biomarkers as valid. 2-methylhopanoids are also made by some other bacteria, and the late-Archean biomarker findings have since been attributed to contamination.
- organic-geochemistry.md, Explainer: "Sterols are produced exclusively by eukaryotes". Some bacteria also make sterols.
- organic-geochemistry.md, Explainer: "~0.5% of photosynthetically fixed carbon ... is buried" conflicts with biogeochemistry.md Explainer (~0.1 of ~120 Gt C/yr, about 0.08%). One of the two should be reconciled.
- stable-isotope-fractionation.md, question 1 (true-false): "Kinetic isotope fractionation always favors the lighter isotope in the product" is marked true. Inverse kinetic isotope effects exist, so "always" makes the statement false as written.
- stable-isotope-fractionation.md, question 0 explanation: quartz-magnetite fractionation of 6 per mil "corresponds to approximately 600 C". Common calibrations (about 5.6 to 6.3 x 10^6/T^2) give roughly 690 to 750 C. Please verify.
- mantle-geochemistry.md, question 0: implies Hawaiian basalts sample the HIMU component ("This is the basis of the HIMU mantle component"). Hawaii is not usually classed as HIMU; its Pb is only moderately radiogenic.
- mantle-geochemistry.md, question 1 (true-false): "mirror images" marked true is an oversimplification; complementarity is a mass-balance relation, not exact mirroring.
- cosmochemistry.md, Explainer: "Mercury and Venus are enriched in refractory elements". Venus is broadly Earth-like in bulk composition, and Mercury is metal-rich with surprisingly high volatile (S, K) contents. Also "metallic iron (~1100 K)": in standard nebular condensation calculations iron metal condenses near forsterite, around 1300 to 1350 K at 10^-4 bar.
- hydrogen-nitrogen-isotopes.md, Explainer: "kinetic effects during snow formation (deuterium excess > 10)". High d-excess mainly reflects kinetic evaporation at a low-humidity moisture source; supersaturation during snow formation tends to lower d-excess.
- oxygen-isotopes-geochemistry.md, Explainer: "Ocean water near the equator defines the standard (VSMOW)". VSMOW represents mean ocean water, not equatorial water.
- rb-sr-system.md: half-life 48.8 Gyr and lambda 1.42 x 10^-11/yr are the older convention; a revised value near 1.397 x 10^-11/yr (about 49.6 Gyr) is now widely used. Not wrong, but worth a note. Also its Explainer gives OIB 87Sr/86Sr as 0.703 to 0.706, while mantle-geochemistry.md says EM2 exceeds 0.707.
- ree-patterns-geochemistry.md, question 2 answer: "positive Ce anomalies in sediments can indicate incorporation of terrestrial material (which has no Ce anomaly...)". Terrestrial input with no anomaly would dilute a negative anomaly, not make a positive one.
- crustal-evolution-geochemistry.md, Core Idea: "bulk composition approximating andesite ... despite being generated primarily at subduction zones and hotspots". The "despite" is misplaced; arcs produce andesitic magma, and the real puzzle is that mantle melts are basaltic.
- cosmogenic-nuclides.md, Explainer: lists muons among particles causing "nuclear spallation"; muon production is by capture and fast-muon reactions, separate from neutron spallation. Minor.
