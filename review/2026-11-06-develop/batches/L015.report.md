## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text

1. clebsch-gordan-coefficients.md, question 3 (true-false): "the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition yields four total states, most of which are symmetric under exchange of particle labels" has answer `false`. Three of the four states (the triplet) are symmetric, so the statement is true as written. The answer key is wrong, or the question needs rewording.
2. clebsch-gordan-coefficients.md, Explainer: "J can take any integer value between |j₁ − j₂| and j₁ + j₂". J moves in integer steps and can be half-integer. Question 0's explanation says "with all values between the min and max allowed", which has the same problem.
3. atomic-orbitals-quantum.md, question 0 explanation: "|ψ₁s|² peaked near a₀". The 1s density |ψ|² is largest at r = 0. The radial probability r²|R|² is what peaks at a₀. The Explainer's line "highest probability of being found near the Bohr radius" is fine only if it means the radial probability.
4. atomic-orbitals-quantum.md, Explainer: the hydrogen orbitals "form a complete orthonormal basis for the electron's Hilbert space". The bound states alone are not complete; the scattering (continuum) states are also needed.
5. born-approximation.md, question 3 explanation: "for the pure Coulomb potential, all higher-order Born terms vanish". This is false. The higher-order Coulomb Born terms diverge. The first-order cross section happens to match the exact Rutherford result. The same explanation calls Rutherford's 1911 success "one of the first triumphs of quantum scattering theory", which is anachronistic.
6. coherent-states.md, Explainer: "Δx = Δp = √(ℏ/2mω)". Δp = √(mωℏ/2), and the two values do not even share units. The Explainer also says energy eigenstates have "wildly oscillating position and momentum uncertainties". Eigenstates are stationary, so their uncertainties are constant. The line "Energy eigenstates are also minimum-uncertainty states" contradicts the next clause.
7. dirac-equation.md, Core Idea, Explainer, and questions 1 and 4: these say half-integer spin is "an inevitable consequence of combining quantum mechanics with special relativity". This is false. Klein-Gordon (spin 0) and Proca (spin 1) equations are also relativistic. Spin-½ follows from Dirac's choice of a first-order equation with 4×4 matrices. Question 1's answer key rewards this claim.
8. dirac-equation.md, Explainer: it calls g = 2 the "electron's anomalous magnetic moment". The anomalous moment is the QED correction g − 2, not the Dirac value. The Explainer also says "Dirac predicted the positron in 1928". The equation dates from 1928, but the antiparticle reading came around 1931. "Confirmed for every known fermion" is an overclaim.
9. dirac-equation.md, question 3 explanation: this lists negative-energy solutions as a Klein-Gordon failure that Dirac fixed. The Dirac equation also has negative-energy solutions.
10. fermi-golden-rule.md, Explainer and question 1 explanation: "[sin(Δωt/2)/(Δω/2)]² → 2πt δ(E_f − E_i)". This is missing a factor of ℏ: the limit is 2πt δ(Δω) = 2πℏt δ(E_f − E_i). Question 1 also says the perturbation "does not supply energy", which is wrong for harmonic perturbations (absorption gives δ(E_f − E_i − ℏω)). The Explainer gets ω³ from "dipole moment" times ω², which skips the factor of ω from the photon field's coupling.
11. fermi-golden-rule.md, question 4 answer: it says the continuum oscillations "cancel by destructive interference". Probabilities to different final states add incoherently. The terms dephase; they do not interfere.
12. bell-theorem-inequalities.md, Core Idea: "This settles the foundational debate about completeness of quantum mechanics." Bell rules out local hidden variables only. Nonlocal completions such as Bohmian mechanics remain possible, and bell-theorem.md says so.
13. bell-inequalities.md, Explainer: "cannot be both local and deterministic". Bell inequalities also bind stochastic local hidden-variable models, so "deterministic" is too narrow.
14. entanglement-quantum.md, question 0 explanation, and entanglement.md, Explainer: these say Bell's theorem rules out "hidden variables" or "any pre-assigned values" without saying "local". entanglement.md question 2's explanation equates nonlocal with "signaling faster than light", which conflicts with its own no-signaling discussion.
15. entanglement.md, Core Idea: "Entangled states exhibit correlations stronger than any classical correlation." Some mixed entangled states (Werner states) admit local hidden-variable models. The claim holds for pure entangled states.
16. angular-momentum-quantum.md, Core Idea: it writes "L̂ₓ, L̂ᵧ, L̂ᵧ", "typically L̂ᵧ" and "Eigenvalues of L̂ᵧ" where L̂_z is meant. It also calls the angular momentum algebra "canonical commutation relations", which is the usual name for [x, p] = iℏ.
17. angular-momentum-addition.md, Explainer: "all states with a given M contribute to a specific total j". This is wrong. A given M appears in several j multiplets.
18. angular-momentum-coupling.md, Explainer: "the singlet combination ... has the antisymmetry required for identical fermions". The total wavefunction must be antisymmetric. A spin triplet with an antisymmetric spatial part is also allowed.
19. addition-of-angular-momenta.md, Explainer and question 4: "selection rules ... are written in terms of j, not the individual l and s separately". Electric-dipole rules also include Δl = ±1. The Explainer also writes "j = l + s" where j = l ± 1/2 is meant.
20. bose-einstein-statistics.md, Explainer: it says photons and phonons show a "tendency of bosons to condense into low-energy modes". With μ = 0, blackbody photons do not condense. The Core Idea uses g(E), the usual symbol for density of states, for the occupancy, and says the distribution "shows a singularity below T_c".
21. fermi-dirac-statistics.md, Explainer: "μ decreases very slightly as E_F is approached from different directions by thermal excitations and depletions". The mechanism is garbled. The sign of the shift depends on the density of states. Question 3's explanation compares to "Dulong-Petit", which is the lattice heat-capacity law; the classical electronic value is (3/2)Nk.
22. density-matrices.md, question 4 answer: "a pure state (superposition) has nonzero off-diagonal entries". This depends on the basis. |↑⟩ is pure and diagonal in the z basis.
23. dirac-notation.md, question 2: "The bra ⟨ψ| is simply the ket |ψ⟩ written in an alternative notation — they contain the same mathematical information" is keyed `false`. A bra is uniquely fixed by its ket, so they carry the same information. The explanation's claim that they differ in information for complex states is wrong. Only the "alternative notation" part is false.
24. born-rule-and-measurement.md, question 4 explanation: repeated identical results are called "direct experimental evidence for collapse". This depends on interpretation, since decoherence-based and many-worlds accounts predict the same thing. The Explainer's "most experimentally tested postulate in all of physics" is an unsourced superlative.
25. exchange-symmetry.md, question 1: in a "two-level system filled with particles", the keyed answer has fermions "placing one in each level". That holds only for two spinless (or same-spin) fermions; spin-½ fermions can put two per level. The Explainer's "only two possibilities are consistent with quantum mechanics" holds in 3D but not in 2D, where anyons exist.
26. expectation-values.md, question 3 (true-false, keyed true): it says Ehrenfest's theorem shows ⟨x⟩ and ⟨p⟩ "obey Newton's second law". They do so only when ⟨∂V/∂x⟩ ≈ ∂V/∂x at ⟨x⟩. The question's own explanation adds that condition.
27. fermions-and-bosons.md, Explainer: it lists the laser among phenomena produced by Bose-Einstein condensation. Lasers are not condensates. Question 0's explanation says "The amplitude-doubling error (option A) applies to bosons", which garbles the point.
28. cross-sections-scattering.md, question 4 explanation: "Quantum mechanics replaces R with the scattering amplitude f" is sloppy. The classical hard-sphere comparison also hides that the quantum low-energy hard sphere gives 4πR².
29. creation-annihilation-operators.md, question 4 answer: it says the algebra "applies to any system whose states are labeled by non-negative integers" and works "without specifying a potential". This overgeneralizes.
