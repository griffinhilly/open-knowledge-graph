## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- big-science-paradigm.md, Q1 explanation and Explainer: "Derek Price coined the term 'Big Science' in 1963". The term is usually credited to Alvin Weinberg (1961). Price's 1963 book "Little Science, Big Science" popularized it.
- climate-science-history.md, Core Idea: "carbon dating showed human civilization had occurred during an unusually stable climate". That evidence came mainly from ice cores and other paleoclimate records, not carbon dating.
- cold-war-science.md, Explainer: "the Cold War created modern research universities". Overstated. Research universities predate WWII (for example Johns Hopkins, 1876). The Cold War massively expanded the federal-grant model.
- computing-revolution-turing-von-neumann.md, Explainer: "Turing himself received no public recognition during his lifetime". He was appointed OBE (1946) and elected FRS (1951). Q5 explanation also links his death causally to the chemical castration, which is disputed. Explainer also states the Church–Turing thesis ("Any computation that can be performed at all can be described as a Turing machine computation") as proven fact. It is a thesis.
- copernican-revolution.md, Core Idea: the "core insight" is described as explaining "the apparent motion of stars". Earth's rotation explaining daily star motion was not new. The key Copernican payoff was explaining planetary retrograde motion. Explainer: Kuhn's 1957 book predates his 1962 "paradigm" framework, so "used it as his paradigm case for how scientific paradigm shifts occur" is anachronistic. Also, the file's claim that heliocentrism "displaced humanity from a privileged center" is contested by historians: in Aristotelian cosmology the center was the lowest, least noble place.
- darwin-evolution-reception.md, Q5: the universal genetic code supports common descent, not natural selection as a mechanism. The question says it "confirmed that ... Darwin's core mechanism was correct". Also, the code is nearly universal (minor variants exist, for example in mitochondria), not identical in all life.
- dna-discovery-molecular-biology.md, Q3: Crick's central dogma says sequence information cannot pass from protein back to nucleic acid. Reverse transcription does not violate Crick's own statement. Q5 and Explainer: ENCODE's broad "functional" claims for non-coding DNA are contested.
- electromagnetic-revolution-maxwell.md, Core Idea and Explainer: "Maxwell's four equations (1865)". Maxwell's 1865 paper had about twenty equations. The familiar four-equation vector form is Heaviside's reformulation from the 1880s.
- eugenics-history.md, Core Idea: "emerged in the early 20th century". This conflicts with the file's own Explainer and Q1 (Galton coined the term in 1883).
- galileo-and-church.md, Q5: De revolutionibus was suspended "until corrected" in 1616, not banned outright.
- germ-theory-revolution.md, Q5: "Pasteur had lost his family home in Alsace". Pasteur was from Dole and Arbois in the Jura, not Alsace. He did return his Bonn honorary degree. Q1: Koch's postulates are dated 1884 but are said to have been "used" for the 1882 tuberculosis discovery. The fourth postulate was added later by others.
- green-revolution-agriculture.md, Explainer: Borlaug "working at CIMMYT" in the 1940s–60s. CIMMYT was founded in 1966. Before that he worked in the Rockefeller-funded Mexican Agricultural Program.
- history-of-astronomy.md, Explainer: "William Fraunhofer" should be Joseph von Fraunhofer. Q2 explanation says Hubble's 1929 paper reported that spiral nebulae were galaxies. That result was 1924–25, as the file's own Explainer says.
- history-of-chemistry-alchemy-to-modern.md, Core Idea: "Boyle's experiments on air pressure established the concept of the chemical element". The element definition comes from The Sceptical Chymist, not the air-pressure work. Historians also note Boyle doubted that elements existed. The operational definition is more Lavoisier's.
- history-of-medicine.md: the Core Idea dates germ theory to the "late 19th century" and the Explainer to the "mid-19th century". These are inconsistent.
- history-of-psychology-science.md, Explainer: "roughly 40-60% of published findings cannot be reproduced". This conflicts with Q3 (36–39% reproduced, so about 61–64% did not).
- history-of-vaccination.md, Explainer: Lady Mary Wortley Montagu "introduced [variolation] to England in 1718". She had her son variolated in Constantinople in 1718. She promoted the practice in England from 1721.
- human-genome-project.md: the Core Idea says "By 2003, the draft sequence was complete". The Explainer has the draft in 2000 and a more complete reference in 2003.
- internet-origins-arpanet.md: the Core Idea says the architecture "reflected Cold War concerns about survivability". Q1 calls this largely a myth. Q5 and the Explainer call Mosaic "the first graphical Web browser" and say earlier browsers were text-only. Earlier graphical browsers existed (Berners-Lee's WorldWideWeb, ViolaWWW, Erwise). Q5 says users grew from 1 to 100 million over "roughly 7 years (1993-2000)". 100 million was reached around 1997–98.
- manhattan-project-science-policy.md, Core Idea: "theoretical physics (from Einstein to Bohr to Feynman)". Einstein did not work on the project. Q1 answer: "Szilard, Einstein, Russell, and others founded the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists (1945)". The Bulletin was founded by Eugene Rabinowitch and Hyman Goldsmith. Russell and Einstein are tied to the 1955 manifesto and Pugwash. Q5 lists Fermi among Jewish physicists who fled Germany. Fermi was Italian (his wife was Jewish). Q5 also credits "Allied bombing" for disrupting Norwegian heavy water, which was mainly commando sabotage.
- newtonian-synthesis.md, Q5: chaos undermines long-range predictability, not determinism (chaotic Newtonian systems remain deterministic). "Requires infinite computational resources" is overstated. The three-body problem has no general closed-form solution, but Sundman found a convergent series solution in 1912. Explainer: Halley's Comet returned "exactly where Newton's system predicted". Clairaut's prediction was off by about a month.
- nuclear-weapons-history.md, Explainer: fission discovered "by Hahn, Strassmann, and Meitner in Berlin". Meitner was in Sweden by then. She and Frisch interpreted Hahn and Strassmann's result.
- peer-review-history.md, Core Idea: the Royal Society is said to have relied on editors. The Society took over Philosophical Transactions in 1752 with a review committee and used referees from the 1830s.
- plate-tectonics-revolution.md, Core Idea: "Earth's crust is composed of mobile plates that move over a viscous mantle". The plates are lithosphere (crust plus uppermost mantle) moving over the asthenosphere. Explainer: Glossopteris was a seed fern, and coal is described as "a tropical product".
- quantum-revolution-origins.md, Q4: "Classical mechanics remains exactly correct" should say approximately. Q5 and Explainer: Bell and Aspect rule out local hidden variables, not all hidden variables. "Irreducibly probabilistic ... exactly as Bohr claimed" and "Einstein's intuitions ... empirically refuted" overstate the result.
- relativity-revolution.md: Q3 says gravitational lensing is "impossible in Newtonian gravity", but Q4 says Newtonian gravity predicts half the deflection. Formatting is garbled in Q2 and Q5 ("E=mc2", "9x1016 m2/s2", "10-18 meters") and should use exponents.
- science-and-empire.md, Q1 and Explainer: Morton's data being "selectively reported" rests on Gould (1981). A 2011 remeasurement (Lewis et al.) found Morton's measurements largely accurate and disputed Gould. Blumenbach also argued for human unity and against claims of African intellectual inferiority, so grouping him with ranking claims needs nuance.
