## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- greek-dramatic-structure-conventions.md, Core Idea: "The unities of action, time, and place, plus the requirement for catharsis, created systematic constraints". Greek playwrights followed no three-unities rule. Aristotle stressed unity of action only; the strict trio was codified by Renaissance critics (Castelvetro, 1570). Some Greek plays change location (Aeschylus' *Eumenides* moves from Delphi to Athens). Calling catharsis a "requirement" also overstates Aristotle. The Explainer and Q2/Q4 repeat the claim. The neoclassical-drama file's new misconception states the correct history, so the two files now disagree.
- greek-tragedy-structure-performance.md, Core Idea: "The form became a template for tragedy across all cultures." This is overstated. Sanskrit drama and Noh, for example, developed independently.
- greco-roman-epic-heroic-form.md, Explainer: "When Odysseus journeys home, his journey matters not merely to him but to Greek civilization itself." This is overstated. "National destiny" fits the Aeneid. It fits Homer poorly.
- greek-epic-heroic-tradition.md, Explainer: Achilles "chooses to fight anyway, seeking kleos". In the Iliad he returns to battle mainly to avenge Patroclus, so fame is not his main motive at that point.
- harlem-renaissance-african-american-literature.md, Explainer: "Vernacular speech, previously dismissed as literary". This is probably a typo for "dismissed as unliterary" or "dismissed as unfit for literature".
- magical-realist-aesthetics-form.md, Explainer: "A grandmother floats into heaven". This apparently refers to Remedios the Beauty in *One Hundred Years of Solitude*. She is a young woman, not a grandmother.
- kerouac-ginsberg-spontaneous-rebellion.md, Core Idea/Explainer: spontaneous prose "without revision". The published *On the Road* (1957) was revised for years after the 1951 scroll draft. Ginsberg also revised "Howl" through drafts. The method's ideal and the actual practice should be told apart.
- medieval-vernacular-literature-overview.md, Core Idea: "vernacular languages replacing Latin" over the 5th-15th centuries. Latin stayed dominant in learned, legal and church writing throughout the period. Old English and Irish vernacular literature also existed early in it.
- montaigne-essay-personal-reflection.md, Explainer: "short prose pieces". Some essays are very long, such as "Apology for Raymond Sebond".
- neoclassical-satire-pope-swift.md, Core Idea: it groups Pope and Swift under "heroic couplets, mock-epic". Swift's main satires are prose, and his verse is mostly octosyllabic, not heroic couplets.
- naturalism-scientific-determinism.md, Explainer: "heredity (genetic inheritance)". This is somewhat anachronistic. Zola's idea of heredity came before Mendelian genetics.
- postcolonial-rewriting-decolonization.md, Explainer: typo "reinterpre ting".
- lost-generation-post-wwi-disillusionment.md, Core Idea/Q2: it suggests the Lost Generation broadly used fragmentation and stream of consciousness. Hemingway and Fitzgerald mostly wrote ordered narratives, though Dos Passos (and Faulkner, if counted) fragmented theirs.
