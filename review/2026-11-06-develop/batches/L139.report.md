## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. motional-emf.md, Explainer para 2: "B⃗ pointing out of the page. The rod moves to the right ... F⃗ = qv⃗ × B⃗, which by the right-hand rule points upward along the rod." With v = +x and B = +z (out of page), v × B = x̂ × ẑ = −ŷ, so the force on a positive carrier points downward. Either the direction or the field orientation is wrong.
2. motional-emf.md, Q4 explanation: "Every power plant on Earth produces AC for this reason." and Explainer last para: "Every power plant on Earth ... converts that energy into electricity by using this same motional EMF." Solar photovoltaic plants produce DC without motional EMF. Should be "most power plants".
3. ohms-law-microscopic.md, Q5 answer: "In metals, n is approximately constant (the conduction band is full)". In metals the conduction band is partly filled; a full band would not conduct. Also Q5 explanation: "superconducting metals and cooling improve metallic conductivity" is garbled.
4. parallel-plate-capacitor.md, Explainer para 2: "Closer plates produce a larger field for the same surface charge density, meaning the same Q requires less voltage". At fixed σ, E = σ/ε₀ does not depend on d; closer plates need less voltage because V = Ed with the same E. Also para 3: "the electric potential decreases linearly with distance from the positive plate: V(x) = Ex" has a sign inconsistency (V(x) = Ex increases with x).
5. resistivity-and-conductivity.md, Q1 explanation ("~10²⁰ difference in resistivity") and Q3 question/explanation ("differ by a factor of roughly 10²⁰"). With the file's own values (copper 1.7×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, silicon 640 Ω·m), the ratio is about 4×10¹⁰. The 10²⁰ figure applies to copper vs. glass, as the Explainer says. Q3's true/false answer stays true, but the number is wrong. Explainer last para: "why chip designers shrink transistor dimensions" is a doubtful link to resistivity.
6. rl-transient-response.md, Explainer para 3: "large R means fast rise (the resistor dissipates energy quickly, reducing the current overshoot problem)". A first-order RL circuit has no overshoot. Q3 explanation's "reducing the inductor's ability to sustain its opposition" is also hand-waving.
7. rlc-resonance.md, Q5 answer and Explainer para 3: Q described as "the ratio of energy stored per cycle to energy dissipated per cycle". The standard definition is Q = 2π × (energy stored)/(energy dissipated per cycle). Core Idea's mention of "power transmission" as a resonance application is doubtful.
8. superposition-principle-electrostatics.md, Q3 explanation: "in a uniformly charged ring, the components along the ring's axis from opposite sides cancel, leaving only the axial component". This contradicts itself. The components perpendicular to the axis cancel; the axial components add.
9. transient-response-rc-circuits-electricity-and-magnetism.md, Q2 (true-false): the statement "double the time constant and therefore halve the initial charging current" is marked false, but both halves are true; the explanation admits this. The item needs rewriting. Q3 is typed true-false but asks "what percentage ... remains?". It needs a statement or a change to short-answer/multiple-choice.
10. toroid-magnetic-field-calculation.md, Q4 ("exactly zero everywhere, with no external flux leakage") and Explainer: true only for an ideal toroid. A real helical winding has a net one-turn current around the ring, so some external field remains. Consider "ideal" in the item.
11. solenoid-magnetic-field-properties.md, Explainer para 3: the fringe field of a finite solenoid is described as "identical to a bar magnet". It is similar in the far field, not identical. Minor.
12. potential-energy-systems.md, Explainer para 3: "This assembly energy equals the energy stored in the electric field throughout all space". For point charges the field energy also includes infinite self-energy terms that the pairwise sum leaves out. Minor wording issue.
13. parallel-circuits-conductance-current.md, Explainer para 3: "The branchiest path" is not a word and reads as a typo. "Current takes the path of least resistance" (in Q1 and the Explainer) feeds the misconception that other branches carry no current.
