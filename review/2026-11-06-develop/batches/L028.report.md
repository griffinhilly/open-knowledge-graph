## Flags
- algorithmic-information-theory.md, Q3 answer: "any computable predictor can be shown to be at most a constant factor worse." The direction is reversed. Solomonoff's mixture dominates every computable measure, so Solomonoff is at most a constant factor worse than any computable predictor.
- algorithmic-information-theory.md, Explainer: "Omega encodes the solution to every finite mathematical problem in its digits." Overstated. Omega's first n bits settle halting for programs up to length n, which covers Pi_1 statements, not every mathematical problem.
- arithmetic-coding.md, Q3 answer: "then C selects [0.462, 0.468). The final interval has width 0.006 = 0.6 * 0.3 * 0.6 * 0.1". Arithmetic error. Within [0.36, 0.468) (width 0.108), C selects [0.4572, 0.468), width 0.0108 = P(ABAC). Bits needed are about -log2(0.0108) = 6.5, so about 7 bits, not 7.38/8.
- arithmetic-coding.md, Core Idea: "Arithmetic coding ... is the basis of modern entropy coders like ANS". ANS is a separate family of entropy coders, not derived from arithmetic coding; it reaches similar compression.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Core Idea: "like successive interference cancellation at the transmitter side". SIC happens at the strong receiver, not the transmitter.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Q1 option 1 (the keyed answer): "R_2 ≈ (1/2)*log2(1 + alpha*P/N), roughly independent of the strong user's power allocation". Contradicts its own explanation and the Explainer, where R_2 = (1/2)log2(1 + alpha*P/((1-alpha)*P + N_2)).
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Q2 (true-false, keyed true): the Gaussian degraded BC region is NOT the convex hull of time-sharing between the two single-user extremes, and it is not a triangle. Superposition coding strictly beats time-sharing (the file's own broadcast-channel.md says so). Answer should be false.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Q4: explanation is a visible unfinished working ("Hmm, this doesn't match the options... There may be an error"). With the stated numbers, R_2 = 0.5*log2(1 + 4/(6+4)) ≈ 0.24 and R_1 = 0.5*log2(7) ≈ 1.4. No option matches. Question needs rewriting.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Explainer: "This duality was discovered by Bergmans and explained through water-filling arguments." Bergmans (and Cover) established the degraded Gaussian BC region; the BC-MAC duality is later work (Vishwanath, Jindal, Goldsmith, early 2000s). Also "cloud cloud" typo for "cloud point".
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Q3 answer: "The correlation between U_1 and U_2 is optimized via the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm". Doubtful; Marton's region is not computed by standard Blahut-Arimoto in general.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md and broadcast-channel.md: "5G NR uses NOMA-like strategies" / "basis for NOMA in 5G downlink". NOMA was studied for 5G (and MUST in LTE Rel-13), but it was not adopted in 5G NR releases as a core downlink scheme. Soften.
- broadcast-channel.md, Core Idea: "The Gaussian BC capacity region ... equals the MAC capacity region by duality." Not equal; the BC region equals a union of dual MAC regions under a sum-power constraint. The file's own Q2 explanation says they are not identical.
- broadcast-channel.md, Q1: premise "Why does user 2 get more power?" assumes alpha > 1/2, but the question defines 0 < alpha < 1. The power split is set by the target rate pair, not fixed in favor of the weak user.
- causal-information-theory.md, Q2: typed true-false but phrased as a "Why ...?" question. Should be short-answer, or rephrased as a statement.
- causal-information-theory.md, Explainer: "A path from X to Y represents a causal chain." Only directed paths do. Also the "Information Flow in Causal Systems" paragraph is muddled ("X does not need information from X's own past... to predict Y").
- channel-coding-theorem.md, Q4 answer: "Turbo codes, LDPC codes, and polar codes are structured codes that provably approach the random coding bound." Polar codes provably achieve capacity on binary-input symmetric channels; turbo and most LDPC results are empirical or ensemble-specific.
- channel-coding-theorem.md, Q2 explanation: "for any finite block length, the error probability is strictly positive". Not true for every channel; channels with positive zero-error capacity admit zero-error codes at low rates. The explanation itself mentions zero-error capacity.
- entropy-rate-stochastic-processes.md, Q1 explanation: "gzip achieves about 2 bits/character". gzip on English text is typically nearer 2.5-3 bits/character. Uncertain; check.
