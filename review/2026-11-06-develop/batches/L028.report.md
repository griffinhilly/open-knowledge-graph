## Flags
- algorithmic-information-theory.md, Q3 answer: "any computable predictor can be shown to be at most a constant factor worse." The direction is reversed. Solomonoff's mixture dominates every computable measure, so Solomonoff is at most a constant factor worse than any computable predictor.
- algorithmic-information-theory.md, Explainer: "Omega encodes the solution to every finite mathematical problem in its digits." Overstated. Omega's first n bits settle halting for programs up to length n, which covers Pi_1 statements, not every mathematical problem.
- arithmetic-coding.md, Q3 answer: "then C selects [0.462, 0.468). The final interval has width 0.006 = 0.6 * 0.3 * 0.6 * 0.1". Arithmetic error. Within [0.36, 0.468) (width 0.108), C selects [0.4572, 0.468), width 0.0108 = P(ABAC). Bits needed are about -log2(0.0108) = 6.5, so about 7 bits, not 7.38/8.
- arithmetic-coding.md, Core Idea: "Arithmetic coding ... is the basis of modern entropy coders like ANS". ANS is a separate family of entropy coders, not derived from arithmetic coding; it reaches similar compression.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Core Idea: "like successive interference cancellation at the transmitter side". SIC happens at the strong receiver, not the transmitter.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Q1 option 1 (the keyed answer): "R_2 ≈ (1/2)*log2(1 + alpha*P/N), roughly independent of the strong user's power allocation". Contradicts its own explanation and the Explainer, where R_2 = (1/2)log2(1 + alpha*P/((1-alpha)*P + N_2)).
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Q2 (true-false, keyed true): the Gaussian degraded BC region is NOT the convex hull of time-sharing between the two single-user extremes, and it is not a triangle. Superposition coding strictly beats time-sharing (the file's own broadcast-channel.md says so). Answer should be false.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Q4: explanation is a visible unfinished working ("Hmm, this doesn't match the options... There may be an error"). With the stated numbers, R_2 = 0.5*log2(1 + 4/(6+4)) ≈ 0.24 and R_1 = 0.5*log2(7) ≈ 1.4. No option matches. Question needs rewriting.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Explainer: "This duality was discovered by Bergmans and explained through water-filling arguments." Bergmans (and Cover) established the degraded Gaussian BC region; the BC-MAC duality is later work (Vishwanath, Jindal, Goldsmith, early 2000s). Also "cloud cloud" typo for "cloud point".
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md, Q3 answer: "The correlation between U_1 and U_2 is optimized via the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm". Doubtful; Marton's region is not computed by standard Blahut-Arimoto in general.
- broadcast-channel-capacity.md and broadcast-channel.md: "5G NR uses NOMA-like strategies" / "basis for NOMA in 5G downlink". NOMA was studied for 5G (and MUST in LTE Rel-13), but it was not adopted in 5G NR releases as a core downlink scheme. Soften.
- broadcast-channel.md, Core Idea: "The Gaussian BC capacity region ... equals the MAC capacity region by duality." Not equal; the BC region equals a union of dual MAC regions under a sum-power constraint. The file's own Q2 explanation says they are not identical.
- broadcast-channel.md, Q1: premise "Why does user 2 get more power?" assumes alpha > 1/2, but the question defines 0 < alpha < 1. The power split is set by the target rate pair, not fixed in favor of the weak user.
- causal-information-theory.md, Q2: typed true-false but phrased as a "Why ...?" question. Should be short-answer, or rephrased as a statement.
- causal-information-theory.md, Explainer: "A path from X to Y represents a causal chain." Only directed paths do. Also the "Information Flow in Causal Systems" paragraph is muddled ("X does not need information from X's own past... to predict Y").
- channel-coding-theorem.md, Q4 answer: "Turbo codes, LDPC codes, and polar codes are structured codes that provably approach the random coding bound." Polar codes provably achieve capacity on binary-input symmetric channels; turbo and most LDPC results are empirical or ensemble-specific.
- channel-coding-theorem.md, Q2 explanation: "for any finite block length, the error probability is strictly positive". Not true for every channel; channels with positive zero-error capacity admit zero-error codes at low rates. The explanation itself mentions zero-error capacity.
- entropy-rate-stochastic-processes.md, Q1 explanation: "gzip achieves about 2 bits/character". gzip on English text is typically nearer 2.5-3 bits/character. Uncertain; check.
- fisher-information-theory.md, Explainer: "the Fisher-efficient estimator is the one that maximizes entropy subject to the observed sufficient statistics". Not a standard result; appears garbled.
- gaussian-channel.md, Q2 explanation: "capacity approaches P/(N*ln2)" mixes total noise power N with spectral density N_0; the Explainer correctly uses P/(N_0 ln 2).
- huffman-coding.md, Explainer: "Arithmetic coding addresses all three limitations". Arithmetic coding alone does not exploit inter-symbol correlation or remove the need for a model; it needs a context or adaptive model for that.
- information-geometry-advanced.md, Core Idea / Q2 / Explainer: the Pythagorean theorem is stated as "m-projection onto an m-flat submanifold". The standard form (Amari) pairs the m-projection with an e-flat submanifold (and the e-projection with an m-flat one). Also: alpha ranges over all reals, not [-1, 1]; the Levi-Civita connection (alpha = 0) is not flat in general; Q2 is typed true-false but phrased as "Why ...?".
- information-geometry-advanced.md, Q3 answer: "natural gradient achieves faster convergence (linear in the dimension rather than quadratic)" is unsupported; "natural gradient follows geodesics" is imprecise (it is steepest descent under the Fisher metric).
- information-geometry-advanced.md, Q4 keyed option and Explainer: EM "brings the solution closer to the global optimum" / "The geometry guarantees it". EM is monotone but can stop at local maxima or saddle points. Explainer also says mean-field VI is "an additional m-projection", which is doubtful.
- information-geometry-basics.md, Q3 answer: Pythagorean relation stated for "a submanifold M" with no flatness condition; it needs M to be e-flat for the m-projection.
- information-theoretic-security.md, Q2 explanation and Explainer: "secrecy capacity C_s = C_main - C_eavesdropper". This equals the capacity difference for Gaussian and some symmetric degraded channels, not for every degraded wiretap channel (in general C_s = max over p(x) of I(X;Y) - I(X;Z)).
- information-theory-neuroscience.md, Q1: premise and keyed answer are wrong. I(S;R) = 0 is exactly statistical independence; the keyed option says it "is different from independence". The explanation contradicts the keyed option.
- information-theory-neuroscience.md, Core Idea: "The channel capacity of a single neuron ... explains why neurons use high rates" is doubtful; efficient-coding arguments under metabolic cost usually favour sparse, low-rate codes.
- information-theory-neuroscience.md, Q3 answer: "a neuron firing at 10 Hz with ±10 ms timing precision can encode roughly log_2(100) ≈ 6-7 bits" looks invented and is not a standard calculation. Explainer: "capacity is roughly log_2(f_max * delta_t) bits per spike" is wrong (f_max * delta_t is usually below 1, giving a negative value); "sensory neurons carry 1-10 bits per stimulus presentation", "10^9 ATP molecules" per spike, and "Animals in informatic-rich niches have larger brains and higher neural firing rates" need sources or removal.
- information-theory-statistical-inference.md, Q3 answer and Explainer: Chernoff information is defined as "min over beta of beta*D_KL(p||q) + (1-beta)*D_KL(q||p)". Wrong; that expression equals min(D(p||q), D(q||p)). Chernoff information is -min over lambda of log sum p^lambda q^(1-lambda). Also the Neyman-Pearson exponent (Stein's lemma) is D(p||q), while the Chernoff exponent belongs to the Bayesian error setting.
- information-theory-statistical-inference.md, Q2 explanation: "no estimator (biased or unbiased) can achieve variance smaller than 1/F(theta)". Wrong; the bound applies to unbiased estimators.
- information-theory-statistical-inference.md, Explainer "Convergence and Sample Complexity" and "Learning Theory Connection": "at least log(1/epsilon) samples ... each sample provides about 1 bit" and "distinguish at most ~sqrt(d) parameters ... information in n samples scales as sqrt(n)" look invented.
- kl-divergence.md, Explainer: calls D_KL(q||p) the "forward" KL and D_KL(p||q) the "reverse" KL. Usual ML usage is the opposite (D(p||q) forward/inclusive, D(q||p) reverse/exclusive). The exclusive/inclusive labels in the file are correct.
- kolmogorov-complexity-algorithmic.md, Q1 explanation: compressors "may underestimate K(x)". A compressor gives an upper bound, so it can only overestimate (up to a constant).
- kolmogorov-complexity-algorithmic.md, Q2 explanation: Omega's "value is independent of the choice of universal machine" is false (Omega depends on the machine); "knowing the first n bits of Omega tells you which programs halt in n steps" should be "which programs of length up to n halt".
- kolmogorov-complexity-algorithmic.md, Q4 explanation: "There is no shorter program" than about 30 bits is unprovable and machine-dependent; K(x) is about K(500) + O(1).
