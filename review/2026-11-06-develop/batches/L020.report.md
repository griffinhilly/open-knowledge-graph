## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- synthetic-gene-circuits.md (Explainer): "tetR represses lacI, lacI represses cI, cI represses tetR". In Elowitz and Leibler (2000) the ring runs the other way: LacI represses tetR, TetR represses cI, CI represses lacI.
- sensitivity-analysis.md (Q2, true-false, answer true): "This means control of a pathway is always distributed across multiple enzymes." The summation theorem allows one enzyme to hold nearly all control (the explanation itself says so), so "always distributed" is false. The answer should likely be false, or the statement reworded. The Explainer's "demolishing the concept of a single rate-limiting step" also overstates.
- stoichiometric-modeling.md (Core Idea): "The solution space (null space of S) defines all thermodynamically and stoichiometrically feasible flux distributions." The null space encodes only mass balance. It includes negative fluxes through irreversible reactions and thermodynamically infeasible loops; those need bounds and extra constraints.
- constraint-based-modeling-fba.md (Q3 answer): MOMA offered as an explanation for surviving a knockout that FBA predicts lethal. If FBA gives zero biomass, no feasible growth flux exists in the model, and MOMA searches the same feasible space, so it cannot rescue the prediction. Only model gaps (missing reactions, isozymes, medium) explain it.
- metabolic-engineering-strain-design.md (Explainer): OptKnock finds "sets where every growth-optimal solution necessitates product formation". OptKnock's bilevel form is optimistic: it maximizes product over the growth-optimal set, so alternative optima with no production can remain. This is why RobustKnock exists, as the same Explainer later says. Core Idea also says OptForce adds "kinetic constraints" and OptCouple addresses "cofactor coupling"; OptForce is flux-measurement/FVA based, and OptCouple is a growth-coupling design method (knockouts, insertions, medium changes). Please verify.
- metabolic-flux-analysis.md (Explainer): "In simple cases, the stoichiometric constraints alone can determine fluxes (this is the basis of flux balance analysis)." Determined systems are classical metabolite balancing; FBA handles underdetermined systems by optimization. Also Q2 (true-false, answer true) says "uniformly labeled" glucose, but position-specific tracers such as [1,2-13C]glucose are widely used, and the explanation itself says "some or all".
- parameter-estimation-in-biological-models.md (Explainer): "30 parameters fit to ... 50 data points ... The system is underdetermined." 50 > 30, so counting alone does not make it underdetermined; the problem is low information content and correlated parameters.
- circadian-clock-modeling.md (Explainer): "the doubletime kinase mutation that shortens the period in Drosophila and causes familial advanced sleep phase syndrome in humans". doubletime is the fly gene. FASPS in humans is linked to mutations in PER2 (a CK1 site) and in CK1 delta, not to doubletime itself. The sentence conflates the fly gene with the human condition.
- boolean-network-models.md (Q2 explanation): number of attractors "often on the order of sqrt(N) in random Boolean networks". Kauffman's sqrt(N) estimate was later shown to undercount; in critical random Boolean networks the attractor count grows faster than any power of N.
- biological-network-analysis.md (Core Idea): "Most biological networks are scale-free." This is contested; large-scale tests found strongly scale-free structure rare across real networks. Explainer also says Erdos-Renyi degree is "normally distributed"; it is binomial/Poisson. Q1 answer says removing one hub fragments the network into disconnected components, which overstates the effect of a single hub.
- network-motifs.md (Q3 explanation): motif enrichment/depletion pattern "remarkably consistent across different biological networks and organisms". The original cross-network work found different network types have different characteristic motif sets. Explainer also says the incoherent FFL pulse ends in "a return to baseline", but it typically settles to a new, lower steady state.
- signal-transduction-networks.md (Explainer): "Positive feedback through ERK-mediated stabilization of active Raf can create bistability". The best-known MAPK bistability (Xenopus oocytes) runs through Mos synthesis; ERK feedback on Raf in mammalian cells is mainly negative. Please verify.
- systems-pharmacology.md (Explainer and Q3 explanation): BRAF+MEK and EGFR+MET combinations described as "model-predicted" and "predicted from network models before clinical validation". These came mainly from experimental resistance studies, not network models. Q1 explanation calls network compensation "the primary mechanism of acquired resistance"; secondary mutations (e.g., EGFR T790M, NRAS) are also major causes.
- immune-system-modeling.md: Explainer calls model predictions "the foundation of HAART"; models supported the rationale, but combination therapy came from clinical trials. Q1 explanation says pre-existing memory cells "lower the effective threshold"; in the described model, a faster response raises the inoculum needed for the virus to win.
- gene-regulatory-network-modeling.md (Q3 explanation): directional constraints from perturbations "would be impossible to extract from observational data, regardless of the sample size". Time-series and causal-discovery methods can give partial directionality; overstated.
- ode-models-in-biology.md (Explainer): "Linear systems ... relax exponentially to a single steady state." Linear systems can show damped oscillations or be unstable.
- reaction-diffusion-spatial-patterning.md (Q2 explanation): attribution of a zebrafish-stripes vs related-species-spots comparison to Kondo and Miura is uncertain; the classic early Turing-in-fish paper (Kondo and Asai 1995) studied angelfish. Please verify.
- synthetic-gene-circuits.md (Q3 explanation): "The toggle switch revealed that host cell growth rate feeds back on circuit performance" is unsupported as an attribution.
