## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. hydrogen-energy-levels.md, Core Idea: "Each level has degeneracy (2n)²". Should be n² (orbital) or 2n² (with spin). The Explainer itself says 2n².
2. hydrogen-energy-levels.md, Explainer: "In any other central potential, l-degeneracy is broken." Q2 lists "a harmonic oscillator" and Q0 says the degeneracy "does not hold for other central potentials". This is false: the 3D isotropic harmonic oscillator also has degeneracy across different l at the same energy (its own hidden symmetry). The Q4 explanation correctly notes the oscillator as the other closed-orbit case.
3. hydrogen-energy-levels.md, Explainer: "The ground state energy −13.6 eV is also the ionization energy". The ionization energy is +13.6 eV. The sign is wrong.
4. quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md, Explainer: "An atom in its excited state that spontaneously emits has dropped from n = 1 to n = 0 in that field mode." Reversed. On emission the field mode goes from 0 to 1 photon. The atom is what drops.
5. harmonic-oscillator-energy-levels.md, Core Idea: "equally spaced—unique to quadratic potentials". Not strictly true. Other potentials have equally spaced spectra, for example the half-oscillator (wall at x = 0) and potentials isospectral to the oscillator. Also Q2 explanation: "zero-point motion prevents electrons from collapsing into the nucleus". That is not oscillator zero-point energy.
6. fine-structure-hydrogen.md, Q0 and Explainer ("Two physical effects contribute comparably"), and fine-structure-splitting.md, Q0 (which marks the Darwin term option as wrong): both leave out the Darwin term. The Darwin term contributes at the same order for l = 0 states, and the n,j-only result needs it. fine-structure-hydrogen.md, Explainer: "of order α² × 13.6 eV ≈ 10⁻³ eV" overstates the hydrogen n = 2 splitting, which is about 4.5×10⁻⁵ eV (about 10.9 GHz). Also "This degeneracy is an artifact of the ideal Bohr model" should refer to the nonrelativistic Coulomb Schrödinger problem.
7. fine-structure-splitting.md, Explainer and Q3: "The proton's magnetic moment is about 1/1836 times the electron's (mass ratio)". The proton moment is about 2.79 nuclear magnetons, so the ratio is about 1/658. Explainer also says "Because both effects mix orbital and spin degrees of freedom, neither L nor S is individually conserved". The kinetic correction does not involve spin, and L² and S² stay conserved. Only the components are not conserved.
8. first-order-perturbation-theory.md, Explainer: "if E⁽¹⁾ₙ ≪ Eₙ⁽⁰⁾, you're in the perturbative regime". The correct criterion is that matrix elements are small compared with level spacings. It also names the Stark effect as a first-order example. The ground-state hydrogen Stark shift is zero at first order, and n = 2 needs degenerate theory. Q3 has a typo: "you is expected to solve".
9. hydrogen-atom-quantum.md, Q4 answer: says the n² degeneracy "arises because the Coulomb potential energy depends only on r". That explains only the m-degeneracy, as the Q4 explanation itself says. The explanation also says the n² degeneracy "produces the structure of the periodic table through Aufbau filling". That is dubious, because the l-degeneracy is broken in multi-electron atoms.
10. hydrogen-atom-spectrum.md, Q2 (true) and Explainer: Δl = ±1 "follows from angular momentum conservation". Angular momentum addition with a spin-1 photon allows Δl = 0 for l ≥ 1. Parity is what forbids Δl = 0. Core Idea: "explains spectral line positions perfectly" overstates, because fine structure and the Lamb shift exist.
11. interpretations-quantum-mechanics.md, Core Idea ("All reproduce empirical predictions") and Q2 (answer true: "identical predictions for every possible experiment"). Objective collapse theories make different predictions in principle, which the Q2 explanation and Explainer both concede. Q2's answer should probably be false, or the stem should say "every experiment performed to date". The same tension appears in measurement-problem.md Q1 ("All four are empirically equivalent — they agree on every experimental prediction") and its Explainer, and in quantum-interpretations.md.
12. measurement-problem-quantum.md: Q0 explanation calls option D (choosing a basis for a calculation) "the preferred basis problem", which it is not. Q1 explanation calls "the electron was always spin-up" the Bohmian view. Bohmian spin is contextual, not pre-existing. Q2 explanation says "Schrödinger's equation correctly predicts the probabilities ... (via Born's rule)", but the Born rule is a separate postulate. Explainer and Q4 say the interpretations "make different empirical predictions in principle". That holds for collapse theories, not for Copenhagen, Many-Worlds or Bohm.
13. quantum-interpretations.md: Q4 answer says "Copenhagen: no collapse is real", which contradicts the Core Idea ("collapse is real"). Explainer says Bohmian mechanics is "satisfying the letter but not the spirit of locality". It is explicitly nonlocal, so it does not satisfy the letter of locality.
14. quantum-measurement-postulate.md: Q0, Q3 and Explainer state interpretive positions as settled fact ("genuinely indeterminate", "interference experiments prove the two paths are simultaneously real", Q3 answer false). Bohmian mechanics, which is not ruled out, has definite paths. Q1 explanation says option D (time elapsed) matters if the state evolves. An energy eigenstate only gains a phase, so it does not. Core Idea: "remains in the collapsed state until further interaction" ignores ordinary Hamiltonian evolution when Â does not commute with Ĥ. The Q4 explanation ("Copenhagen says collapse is not a physical process") conflicts with Q3 ("Collapse is ... a physical transition").
15. quantum-superposition.md: Q2 explanation writes the cross-term as "|c₁|²|c₂|²cos(phase)". It should be about 2|c₁||c₂||ψ₁||ψ₂|cos(phase). Q3 says hidden variables are "ruled out by experiment". Only local hidden variables are ruled out. The Explainer's last sentence ("occupy all 2ⁿ directions simultaneously — the basis of quantum computing") is misleading.
16. pure-and-mixed-states.md, Q2 and Q4 explanations: "purity is determined by whether coherences exist". Coherences depend on the basis. |↑⟩⟨↑| has no off-diagonal terms in the z-basis and is pure. Tr(ρ²) is the basis-independent test.
17. quantum-observables.md, Q3 explanation: says â† has complex eigenvalues with coherent-state eigenstates. It is â that has coherent states as eigenstates, and â† has no normalizable eigenstates. Explainer: "No other class of operators guarantees this [real eigenvalues]". Some non-Hermitian operators also have real spectra. Q1/Q4 also overstate "only Hermitian".
18. observables-and-operators.md, Explainer and Q2: "Two observables can be simultaneously well-defined only if their operators commute". Also "When the commutator is nonzero ... no state can have definite values of both". This is false in general. For example, L_x and L_y share the l = 0 eigenstate. It holds for x and p because their commutator is a nonzero multiple of the identity.
19. orbital-angular-momentum-quantum.md, Core Idea and Explainer: "only one component can have a definite value at a time". The file's own Q2 shows the l = 0 exception.
20. identical-particles-quantum.md, Explainer: "ψ(r₁, r₁) = −ψ(r₁, r₁) = 0" is given as the same-state Pauli argument, but it is the same-position argument. It also says you "cannot factorize ψ_B". For a = b, ψ_B is a product state. Core Idea: the symmetry constraint "emerging from spin-statistics theorem" blurs two things. The symmetrization postulate is separate, and spin-statistics fixes which sign applies.
21. quantum-operators.md, Q4 explanation: "generators of continuous symmetries always appear as differential operators". Spin rotation generators are matrices.
22. partial-wave-analysis.md, Q3: calls a resonance "a sharp peak in δ_l near π/2". A resonance is δ_l rising rapidly through π/2, not a peak in δ_l. Explainer: "The total cross section is the integral |f(θ)|² over all angles, giving the optical theorem". The optical theorem does not follow from that integral alone.
23. kets-and-bras.md, Explainer: defines a Hilbert space as "the complete, normed vector space". That definition describes a Banach space. A Hilbert space needs a complete inner-product space.
24. feynman-path-integral.md: the title "Feynman Diagrams and Perturbative Expansion" does not match the id feynman-path-integral. Minor.
