## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. sequential-consistency.md, Explainer last paragraph: "two clients might disagree about the order of concurrent writes from different sources". Under sequential consistency every process is explained by one total order, so clients agree on write order. Disagreement is allowed under causal consistency.
2. sequential-consistency.md, Explainer last paragraph: "at significantly lower latency and higher availability than linearizability". Sequential consistency also cannot stay available for all operations during a partition; the availability claim is doubtful.
3. sequential-consistency.md, question 4 answer: sequential consistency is "achievable with cheaper coordination mechanisms like causal ordering or vector clocks". Causal ordering and vector clocks give causal consistency, which lacks a single total order.
4. strong-eventual-consistency.md, question 1 explanation: "incrementing twice by the same operation gives the same result as once (idempotent)". Increments are not idempotent. Idempotence applies to the state merge (max per slot), not to the increment operation.
5. strong-eventual-consistency.md, Explainer: grow-only counter "the merged state is the sum of all counters". Merge takes the per-replica maximum; the sum is the counter's value, not the merge rule.
6. strong-eventual-consistency.md, Explainer: CRDT text editing "using techniques from the operational transformation family". Text CRDTs (RGA, LSEQ, etc.) are a separate approach from OT.
7. strong-eventual-consistency.md, Explainer: "CRDTs are data structures whose operations are designed to be commutative, associative, and idempotent". True of state-based merges; operation-based CRDTs usually need only commutativity of concurrent operations plus exactly-once causal delivery.
8. read-after-write-consistency.md, Explainer and question 4 explanation: DynamoDB offers read-after-write "as a default". DynamoDB reads are eventually consistent by default; strongly consistent reads are an option.
9. read-after-write-consistency.md, Core Idea: "also called session consistency". Session consistency usually names a bundle (read-your-writes plus monotonic reads and others), not read-your-writes alone.
10. replica-placement-strategies.md, question 2 (true-false): keyed false, but its own explanation says geographic distribution does reduce read latency for nearby clients. The item is ambiguous and arguably keyed wrong.
11. replica-placement-strategies.md, question 1 explanation: with all three replicas on different continents "every write must wait for at least 2 cross-region round trips". A 2-of-3 quorum with a coordinator co-located with one replica needs one remote acknowledgment, so typically one cross-region round trip.
12. replication-strategies-analysis.md, question 4 answer: writes must be "durably recorded everywhere before returning". Majority quorums are enough for strong consistency; "everywhere" overstates it. Explainer also says leaderless "requires W + R > N", which is a tuning choice, and "a banking ledger demands primary-backup", which is overstated.
13. state-machine-replication.md, Core Idea: "SMR achieves linearizability by having consensus order all operations". Holds only if reads are also ordered through the log or the leader confirms its leadership; local reads from replicas can be stale.
14. state-machine-replication.md, question 1: keyed answer is log replay; option B (fetch a snapshot) is how many real systems recover after log compaction, so the distractor is partly correct.
15. three-phase-commit-protocol.md, Core Idea: pre-commit "releasing read locks but keeping write locks". This lock detail is not part of the standard 3PC description.
16. three-phase-commit-protocol.md, Explainer: new coordinator "finds that everyone is in the preCommit state". Some participants may not have received preCommit yet; the rule is that any pre-committed survivor means commit is safe.
17. view-change-protocols.md, question 3 (true-false): "can never revert to a previous leader". View numbers never decrease, but the same node can lead again in a later view; the statement conflates view and leader.
18. synchronous-asynchronous-systems.md, Explainer: partial synchrony described as "asynchronous most of the time but eventually behaves synchronously". The usual definitions are unknown bounds, or bounds that hold after an unknown stabilization time.
19. timeout-retry-strategies.md, Explainer: "TCP itself uses this approach" right after describing a p99-based timeout. TCP's retransmission timeout uses smoothed RTT plus a variance term, not a percentile.
