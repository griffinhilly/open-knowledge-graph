## Flags
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/alphabet-primer-learning-text.md: Explainer "children benefit from encountering letters in a consistent sequence rather than random order" and MC item 1 option "mirrors how children naturally learn the sequence of letters" state a contested pedagogical claim as fact.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/author-illustrator-collaboration-integration.md: Explainer "Successful collaborations typically involve close, ongoing communication" and true-false item 3 (answer false to "Picture books function adequately even when author and illustrator work separately with minimal communication"). In much trade picture-book publishing the editor or art director chooses the illustrator and mediates, and author and illustrator may never communicate directly; many acclaimed books were made this way. The item's keyed answer is doubtful.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/chapter-book-episodic-form.md: Explainer "a 300-page uninterrupted narrative ... twenty chapters of 15 pages each" uses a length typical of middle-grade novels, not chapter books, which are usually much shorter.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/children-adaptation-film-television-translation.md: true-false item 3 asks whether TV faces "fewer time and budget constraints" but the explanation discusses only episodic structure, not budget. Item 2 cites *The Princess Bride*, which William Goldman wrote as an adult novel, as a children's-literature adaptation. Explainer calls *The Lord of the Rings* films an adaptation aimed at "adults who loved the books as children", which is loose.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/child-protagonist-agency-empowerment.md: Explainer states as fact that child readers "internalize the message that they, too, are capable of agency" from such books; this is a reader-effects claim that should be hedged.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/children-fantasy-secondary-worlds.md: Explainer lists "Ged arrives at Roke School" and Taran in Prydain as examples of protagonists who "enter the secondary world"; both are natives of their worlds, so these are newcomer-within-the-world devices, not entries from our world.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/children-graphic-novels-visual-literacy.md: Core Idea "works by Raina Telgemeier and Dog Man" pairs an author with a series title (Dog Man is by Dav Pilkey). Explainer "Contemporary research increasingly recognizes..." is an unsourced research claim.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/children-high-fantasy-epic-form.md: Core Idea and Explainer call *Harry Potter* and *Percy Jackson* high fantasy. Both are usually classed as contemporary or low fantasy because they are set in our world with hidden magic; high fantasy usually means a fully secondary world. True-false item 3 keys "High fantasy requires that characters from different races or species play meaningful roles" as true, but its own explanation says "typically"; "requires" is too strong.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/children-horror-scary-stories-age-calibration.md: true-false item 2 explanation lists *The Giving Tree* and *Hansel and Gretel* as "beloved picture books for young children" with disturbing elements; *Hansel and Gretel* is a folk tale, and *The Giving Tree* is not a scary book. MC item 0 explanation "Children's developmental psychologists recognize..." states a research consensus without support.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/children-implied-reader-development.md: The file defines the implied reader as "the idealized reader the author imagines." In Wolfgang Iser's original sense, and in Aidan Chambers's use for children's books, the implied reader is a role built into the text, not the author's private idea; the explainer blurs implied reader with intended reader.
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/children-literature-censorship-challenges-freedom.md: MC item 1 uses *The Handmaid's Tale*, *The Bluest Eye*, and *Beloved*, all adult novels, as examples for a children's-literature topic. This topic substantially overlaps children-literature-censorship-banning-intellectual-freedom (possible duplicate).
- domains/literature/childrens-and-ya-literature/children-literature-critical-theory-nodelman.md: Explainer says Nodelman's work operates through "what he calls the 'hidden curriculum'." The term "hidden curriculum" comes from education research (associated with Philip Jackson), and Nodelman is better known for "the hidden adult" and his colonialism analogy; the attribution looks wrong.
