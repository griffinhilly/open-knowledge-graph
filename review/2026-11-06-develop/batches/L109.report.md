## Flags
- rna-splicing-mechanisms.md, Core Idea: "the first cuts at the 5' splice site, releasing the intron lariat; the second ligates..." Step 1 forms the lariat intermediate; the lariat is released in step 2 (the Explainer states this correctly). Same sentence calls snRNPs "small nuclear RNAs"; snRNPs are ribonucleoprotein particles.
- rna-splicing-mechanisms.md, Q4 answer: "This almost certainly causes a frameshift". A retained intron causes a frameshift only if its length is not a multiple of 3; "almost certainly" overstates it. The "80,000–100,000 distinct proteins" and "15–50% of disease-causing mutations" figures are loose estimates stated as fact.
- second-messenger-systems.md, Q4 explanation: "Caffeine inhibits phosphodiesterase..." presented as caffeine's mechanism. At usual doses caffeine acts mainly as an adenosine receptor antagonist; PDE inhibition needs higher concentrations. The Explainer's "partly" is closer, but "which is why it makes you feel alert" still credits PDE.
- substrate-level-phosphorylation.md, Q3 explanation: "destabilization created by the double bond in pyruvate after dephosphorylation". The driving force is tautomerization of the released enolpyruvate to the more stable keto form; the wording is muddled. Q1 explanation's aside on option D is confusing.
- sulfur-amino-acid-metabolism.md, Explainer: "which is why these vitamins are so tightly linked to cardiovascular health". Homocysteine is associated with cardiovascular disease, but trials of B-vitamin homocysteine lowering largely did not reduce cardiovascular events; the causal framing is overstated. Q2 statement wording ("can seldom synthesize it under any circumstances") is awkward.
- translation-initiation-and-elongation.md, Explainer: "the amino acid in the P site is transferred onto the amino acid in the A site". After the first cycle it is the whole peptidyl chain on the P-site tRNA that is transferred.
- urea-cycle.md, Explainer: "operating exclusively in liver hepatocytes". The complete cycle is essentially hepatic, but some of its enzymes act in other tissues (for example intestine and kidney in arginine synthesis); "exclusively" is too strong.
