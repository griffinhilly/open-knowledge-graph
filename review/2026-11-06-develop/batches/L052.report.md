### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- monte-carlo-statistical-mechanics.md (Explainer): "For N = 100, that is more configurations than atoms in the observable universe." 2^100 ≈ 1.3 × 10^30. The observable universe holds roughly 10^80 atoms. The claim is false.
- ideal-fermi-gas-t-equals-zero.md (Explainer): "U₀ = (3/5)NE_F. This is roughly 60% of the classical equipartition expectation (3/2)Nk_BT_F". (3/5)/(3/2) = 0.4, so the ratio is 40%, not 60%.
- ideal-fermi-gas-t-equals-zero.md (Explainer): "g(ε) = (V/2π²)(2m/ℏ²)^{3/2} √ε. Setting the integral ... = N (with a factor of 2 for spin)". As written, g(ε) already includes the spin factor of 2. Adding another factor of 2 would give the wrong E_F.
- mean-field-theory.md (Core Idea): "overestimates order-parameter fluctuations and critical exponent values". Mean-field theory neglects (underestimates) fluctuations. Its exponents are not uniformly too large: β = 1/2 is above the 3D value (~0.33), but γ = 1 and ν = 1/2 are below the 3D values (~1.24, ~0.63).
- mean-field-theory-statmech.md (Q2 explanation and Explainer): "overestimates T_c by roughly 30%" / "T_c is reduced from the mean-field value by roughly 30%". On the square lattice, 4 vs 2.269 J/k is a 76% overestimate, or a 43% reduction from the mean-field value.
- mean-field-theory-statmech.md (Explainer): "the susceptibility diverges as χ ∝ |T − T_c|^{−1}, the correlation length exponent ν = 1/2". This sentence attaches the susceptibility exponent to ν. The χ exponent is γ = 1; ν is a separate exponent.
- linear-response-theory.md (Q1 explanation and Explainer): χ_AB(t) = −(i/ℏ)θ(t)⟨[Â(t), B̂(0)]⟩₀ with the perturbation −δB(t)B̂. With that coupling the standard result is +(i/ℏ)θ(t)⟨[A(t),B(0)]⟩. This also conflicts with linear-response-theory-statmech.md, which uses +i/ℏ. Separately, "Im[χ(ω)] ∝ S(ω)" omits a frequency-dependent factor: classically Im χ = (ω/2kT)S(ω).
- linear-response-theory.md (Core Idea): the notation "⟨δA⟩ = χ_{AB} δB of ... perturbation δB" uses δB for the field while B is also the observable. Confusing, though not strictly false.
- free-energy-thermodynamic-relations.md (Q3 explanation): "Energy minimization governs only an isolated system at absolute zero". An isolated system maximizes entropy. Energy minimization applies at T = 0 for a system in contact with a bath (where F = E).
- long-range-order.md (Q4 explanation and Explainer): this text puts superconductor ODLRO in the one-particle density matrix ⟨ψ†(r)ψ(0)⟩. For superconductors, ODLRO is in the two-particle (pair) density matrix. The one-particle form applies to Bose superfluids.
- liouville-theorem-statistical-mechanics.md (Q2 option B/explanation and Explainer): this says the Poisson bracket {ρ, H} "vanishes exactly when ρ depends only on H" and that ρ "must be a function of H alone". Depending only on H is sufficient, not necessary. A stationary ρ can depend on any conserved quantity, such as total momentum or angular momentum.
- goldstone-theorem.md (Explainer and Q5 explanation): "the number of Goldstone bosons equals the number of broken continuous symmetry generators". This fails for the Heisenberg ferromagnet: two broken generators, one magnon branch with ω ∝ k². It also fails for crystals, where broken rotations add no extra phonons. The Core Idea "every continuously broken symmetry produces a gapless ... mode" also has exceptions: gauge symmetries (the Anderson-Higgs mechanism) and long-range interactions.
- landau-theory-phase-transitions.md (Explainer): the Ginzburg criterion is given as "fluctuations are negligible when the correlation length is small compared to atomic scales". The actual criterion compares the order-parameter fluctuations within a correlation volume with m² itself. It is not a comparison of ξ with atomic scales.
- landau-theory.md (Explainer): "This simple linear form for a(T) is the mean-field assumption". The mean-field assumption is the neglect of fluctuations, not the linear form of a(T). Also, the Core Idea's ν = 1/2 needs a gradient term, which the stated expansion F_0 + a m² + b m⁴ lacks.
- fokker-planck-equation.md (Explainer): "ṁv = −γv + η(t)" is a typo for m v̇. "The stationary solution ... with thermal noise is always the Boltzmann distribution" fails for non-conservative forces. "The Black-Scholes equation" is a backward Kolmogorov equation, not a forward Fokker-Planck equation.
- fluctuation-dissipation-theorem.md (Q2 explanation): "jostles it less coherently (lower diffusion)" is muddled. It also conflicts with the Explainer's "jostles harder": the random force does grow with viscosity, while D falls. The Explainer's closing claim that the theorem "ultimately reflects the equipartition theorem" is loose.
- grand-partition-function.md (Q5 answer): "⟨n⟩ = −∂Ω/∂ε". For a single mode, Ω = −kT ln(1 + e^{β(μ−ε)}) gives ⟨n⟩ = +∂Ω/∂ε.
- langevin-equation-stochastic.md (Explainer and Q2): τ = m/γ for a micron bead in water "on the order of microseconds". It is closer to 0.05–0.2 µs (tens to hundreds of nanoseconds). Also, "a frictionless fluid would also be noiseless, which would allow the particle to cool below ambient temperature" is illogical. Cooling below ambient needs friction without noise, not neither.
- h-theorem-irreversibility.md: the Core Idea says the resolution "requires that initial conditions are non-generic", while the Q5 explanation says initial conditions must be "generic". The wording is inconsistent. Q4's statement ("can seldom spontaneously decrease ... absolutely forbidden") is garbled.
- h-theorem-reversibility.md (Explainer): "take a gas that has just equilibrated (H at its minimum) and reverse all velocities ... H must increase". This holds only for a gas that recently came from a low-entropy state, not for one at full equilibrium.
- microcanonical-ensemble.md (Explainer): "temperature is the rate at which entropy increases as you add energy" describes 1/T, not T. Q3's statement says "most accessible microstates are equally probable"; the postulate says all.
- metropolis-algorithm.md (Explainer): it does not mention that the plain Metropolis rule needs a symmetric proposal; without one, detailed balance fails.
- monte-carlo-methods-stat-mech.md (Q4 explanation and Explainer): "Any Markov chain satisfying detailed balance converges to its stationary distribution" and "detailed balance ensures ... unique stationary state". Both also need ergodicity (and aperiodicity).
- mean-field-theory-statmech.md (Q5 explanation): "In d = 3, [mean-field exponents] are approximately correct". This is loose: β is 0.5 vs ~0.33.
- maxwell-boltzmann-distribution.md (Core Idea): "the probability that a classical particle has energy E as P(E) ∝ exp(−E/kT)" omits the density-of-states factor. For a 3D gas, P(E) ∝ √E e^{−E/kT}. The Explainer's speed formula also omits the 4πv² factor, though it then describes the shape correctly.

- gibbs-free-energy.md (Explainer): "For a second-order transition, G is continuous through the crossing". A continuous transition has no crossing of two G branches. Minor.
- helmholtz-free-energy.md (Q2/Q5 and Explainer): treating TS as a fixed "unavailable energy" that "even a perfect engine cannot convert" is loose. Only changes (−ΔF) bound the work.
- information-theory-entropy.md (Explainer): it says isolated systems' Shannon entropy rises toward maximum, but Liouville's theorem keeps the fine-grained entropy constant.
