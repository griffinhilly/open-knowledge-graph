## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. industrial-mechanization-factories.md, Q4 explanation: "In cottage industry, workers owned their looms and materials". Under putting-out, merchants usually supplied the materials (the Explainer itself says so).
2. industrial-urbanization-cities.md, Explainer: Snow's 1854 work "established the relationship between contaminated water and cholera". His conclusion was not widely accepted at the time. Q2 explanation calls the fix "straightforward (remove the pump handle)"; the outbreak was already declining when the handle was removed.
3. industrial-urbanization-cities.md, Explainer and Q3/Q5: rural workers "could never achieve" organizing. Counterexamples: Swing Riots (1830), Tolpuddle labourers' union (1834).
4. international-organizations.md, Q2 explanation: "This trade-off is explicit and acknowledged in the UN Charter." The Charter sets the voting rule (Art. 27) but does not state a trade-off.
5. iron-curtain-cold-war-divide.md, Explainer and Q4: "The Potsdam Agreement ... left Eastern Europe in the Soviet occupation zone." Potsdam set occupation zones in Germany; Soviet control of Eastern Europe came from Red Army presence, not a Potsdam zone.
6. iron-curtain-cold-war-divide.md, Q2: presents Soviet security motives as "primarily" the answer and calls ideological expansion "a Western framing". This is a contested historiographical question presented as settled.
7. korean-war-proxy-conflict.md, Explainer and Q2/Q5: "The war's aim was ... to restore the status quo ante." In autumn 1950 UN forces crossed the 38th parallel aiming to unify Korea; the limited aim came after Chinese entry.
8. korean-war-proxy-conflict.md, Q4 explanation: "No U.S. and Soviet forces directly engaged each other." Soviet pilots fought US pilots in air combat over North Korea (covertly).
9. late-twentieth-century-globalization.md, Explainer: "the end of the Cold War opened ... China". China's opening began in 1978. Also lists WTO (1995) as a Bretton Woods institution. Q3 explanation states technology "was not what caused it", a contested causal claim stated as settled.
10. mass-media-propaganda.md, Q1 explanation ("propaganda works even when people 'know' it is propaganda ... processed below the level of rational critique") and Q5 (algorithms "systematically amplify" outrage) state contested empirical claims as settled.
11. militarism-alliance-system-prewar.md, Q4 explanation quotes "'always makes aggression irrational'" but the question says "usually". Q5 explanation calls alliance entanglement the "security dilemma"; that term means something else (arming for defense makes others insecure).
12. militarism-arms-race.md, Q1 explanation: "both sides genuinely believed they were building defensive fleets." Tirpitz's fleet was designed to pressure Britain. Q3: planners "genuinely believed" buildups would prevent war; some (e.g., Moltke the Younger) favored preventive war. Contested.
13. modernism-cultural-movement.md, Explainer and Q2/Q3: presents WWI as a main driver of the modernist rupture, but Cubism, Schoenberg's atonality and the Rite of Spring predate 1914. "Schoenberg's abandonment of traditional key signatures" should be abandonment of tonal centers. Q4 calls modernism and romantic nationalism "in fundamental tension"; many modernists (Bartók, Stravinsky) drew on folk material and some were nationalists.
14. nation-state-consolidation-19th-century.md, Explainer and Q3: Cavour "unified Italy between 1859 and 1871" and absorbed the Papal States. Cavour died in 1861; Venetia (1866) and Rome (1870) came later. Also "Unified Germany after 1871 was the largest industrial economy in Europe": Britain stayed larger for some decades.
15. national-self-determination.md, Explainer: Wilson championed self-determination "as one of his Fourteen Points". The phrase is not in the Fourteen Points; Wilson used it in a February 1918 speech. Yugoslavia is listed as a successor state of Austria-Hungary; it also absorbed Serbia and Montenegro.
16. nationalism-political-ideology.md, Q3 explanation: Herder's nationalism "developed in direct response to French occupation". Herder died in 1803, before the occupations. The Explainer's claim that Herder saw the Volk as "superior to" the civic definition misreads Herder, who opposed ranking peoples. Q1 says the revolutionaries "banned" regional languages; they campaigned against them.
17. nuclear-deterrence-strategy.md, Q1: option D (missile defense gives a first-strike advantage that tempts preemption) is close to correct and overlaps with keyed answer B. The question is ambiguous.
18. nuclear-weapons-and-deterrence.md, Explainer: Hiroshima and Nagasaki "each killed between 70,000 and 140,000". Nagasaki estimates are lower (roughly 40,000–80,000 by end of 1945).
19. operation-barbarossa-eastern-struggle.md, Explainer: 27 million Soviet dead were "the vast majority of WWII's dead". They were about a third of the total. "Soviet productive capacity actually grew during the war": output fell sharply in 1941–42 before recovering. The USSR "destroyed the German military almost single-handedly" overstates the case.
20. pan-africanism-black-unity.md, Explainer: Ghana was "the first sub-Saharan African country to gain independence (1957)". Sudan became independent in 1956. The Explainer also leaves out the 1900 London Pan-African Conference, which came before Du Bois's 1919 congress.
21. postcolonial-development-and-modernization.md, Explainer and Q4: Iran (1953) and Chile (1973) are cited as postcolonial new states punished for non-alignment. Neither was a newly decolonized state. Q1 states the path-dependency view as settled ("governance alone could not undo").
22. postcolonial-state-building-challenges.md, Explainer: "There were no factories, few universities". This is absolute; India, for example, had large textile and steel industries. "subcontition" is a typo. Botswana and Zimbabwe are described as having "similar structural deficits", but Zimbabwe had a large industrial base at independence. Q3 explanation says the Berlin Conference "produced African borders". The conference set rules for claims; most borders came from later bilateral treaties. "The answer is not that postcolonial leaders failed" is a contested claim stated flatly.
23. postwar-settlement-international-order.md, Explainer: Article 231 acknowledged "sole responsibility". Its text assigns responsibility to "Germany and her allies". Reparations were "a sum Germany could not pay"; historians debate this, since much of the 132 billion was notional (C bonds). "(the final figure came later)" is muddled, since 132 billion was the 1921 final figure.
24. potsdam-occupation-germany-reparations.md, Explainer: Potsdam came "three months later" than Yalta; the gap was about five months. The "5 D's" list omits decentralization and lists overlapping items (decartellization, deconcentration).
25. racial-genocide-holocaust-context.md, Q2 explanation: "industrialized death camps coordinated at Wannsee". Gassing at Chełmno began in December 1941, before Wannsee.
26. romantic-nationalism-ethnic-identity.md, Explainer: Latin American independence movements "celebrated indigenous and mestizo heritage". They were led mainly by creole elites, and indigenismo is largely later. Q1 and the Explainer say minorities are "permanent outsiders by definition". Several romantic-nationalist states pushed assimilation instead (e.g., Magyarization).
27. scramble-africa.md, Explainer and Q3/Q5: "European diplomats drew lines on maps in Berlin". The Berlin Conference did not partition Africa. Q2 explanation and the Explainer say "Belgian Congo" for Leopold's rubber regime; that was the Congo Free State (1885–1908).
28. second-industrial-revolution-electricity.md, Explainer: "Bell Labs, GE's Menlo Park". Menlo Park was Edison's lab. Bell Labs dates from 1925, after the period. The telegraph (1840s) predates the Second Industrial Revolution.
29. socialism-worker-state.md, Explainer and Q4: Owen "founded" New Lanark. David Dale founded it, and Owen took over its management. New Lanark was a reformed mill village, not a cooperative community. Q5 explanation treats the "utopian" socialists as the source of the durable reformist path. That conflates utopian socialism with democratic socialism.
30. soviet-union-collapse-communism.md, Explainer: American unipolarity was "already ending by the time of the 2003 Iraq War". This is a contested judgment stated as fact.
31. spanish-civil-war-ideological-preview.md, Explainer and Q1: Guernica was "the first deliberate aerial bombardment of a civilian population center in modern warfare". Earlier cases include WWI raids on London, the bombing of Madrid (1936) and Durango (March 1937). Hemingway is listed among those who "came to fight"; he was a war correspondent. The 1931 Republic is described as a coalition including anarchists and communists; anarchists entered government only in November 1936.
32. stalinism-totalitarian-state.md, Q4 presents the totalitarian model as settled. Many social historians contest it.
33. third-world-development-paths.md, Explainer: Cuba "achieved universal literacy and healthcare but stagnated economically". This is a contested evaluation stated flatly. ISI is credited to dependency theory, but it came from earlier ECLA structuralism, and Frank argued ISI could not end dependency.
