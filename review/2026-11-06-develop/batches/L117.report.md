## Flags

Factual errors in existing text:
- nonsense-verse-linguistic-play-subversion.md, Explainer: "Edward Lear's \"Jabberwocky\" and \"The Owl and the Pussycat\"" and "Lear's \"brillig\" and \"tumblebum\"". "Jabberwocky" and "brillig" are Lewis Carroll's (Through the Looking-Glass), not Lear's. "tumblebum" is not a known Lear coinage. The same paragraph says Jabberwocky's lines "disrupt normal word order", but the poem keeps ordinary English syntax and swaps in invented words.
- children-series-fiction-episodic-character.md, Explainer: "The Nancy Drew series ... was originally written with Nancy aging gradually through the books." Nancy stays a fixed teenager (16, later 18) and does not age book to book.
- newbery-caldecott-canon-and-awards.md, Core Idea "Newbery Medal (American fiction, 1921-present)" and Explainer "honors outstanding fiction". The Newbery goes to the most distinguished contribution to American literature for children, and nonfiction and poetry have won. It was established in 1921 but first awarded in 1922. The Caldecott was first awarded in 1938.
- roald-dahl-voice-irreverence-humor.md, Explainer: "The child protagonist (Matilda, Charlie, Hansel)". Hansel is not a Dahl character. Also "the parents in \"George's Marvelous Medicine\" depicted as cartoonish in their selfishness": the grotesque adult in that book is George's grandmother. The q4 explanation calls Trunchbull "the headmaster"; she is the headmistress.
- fairy-tale-fractured-perspectives.md, Explainer: "In the original \"Sleeping Beauty,\" Maleficent is a flat villain". The name Maleficent comes from Disney's 1959 film. In Perrault and the Grimms the villain is an unnamed wicked or thirteenth fairy.
- disney-adaptation-cultural-media-transformation.md, Explainer: the example of someone who "has seen a Disney or Pixar interpretation" of "Little Red Riding Hood" is weak. Disney has no major feature adaptation of that tale. The q4 explanation's "billions of people" is unsupported.
- early-reader-phonics-vocabulary-controlled.md, Explainer: "Level 1 Guided Reading range". Guided Reading (Fountas & Pinnell) uses letter levels A-Z, so "Level 1" mixes it up with publisher series labels. The example "'running' might be too complex ... so the author uses 'going' instead" does not hold up, since "going" also has an -ing ending and a long vowel. "Research supports that..." is an unhedged research claim.
- sendak-psychological-depth-picture-books.md, Explainer: Max "running through night and day". In the book Max sails. "The blank pages create psychological space" is doubtful, since the book has wordless full-spread pages, not blank pages.
- lewis-allegorical-fantasy-narnia.md, q2 (true-false): "deliberately designed to convey Christian theological concepts" is stated as settled. Lewis said the stories began with pictures in his mind and that the Christian element came in later. He also rejected the label "allegory" ("supposal"). The Core Idea's framing of Narnia as "allegorical" is likewise contested.
- representation-diverse-voices-children-literature.md, q2 (true-false) explanation: "Research shows children develop stronger self-esteem..." is unhedged. The Explainer's "documented developmental and psychological needs" is also stated more firmly than the evidence supports.
- ya-contemporary-realism-social-issues.md, Explainer: "Earlier YA sometimes avoided these topics" and the overall "increasingly" framing understate the 1960s-70s problem novel (e.g. The Outsiders, 1967).
- middle-grade-episodic-serial-narrative.md, Explainer: middle-grade readers are "not yet capable of sustaining engagement through 300 pages" is an overclaim. The q4 prompt calls "The Wild Robot" and "Pax" episodic series, which is a stretch.
- middle-grade-animal-protagonists-anthropomorphism.md: Watership Down is treated as middle-grade, but it was published as a general or adult novel. Also overclaims: animal protagonists "dominate" middle-grade publishing.
