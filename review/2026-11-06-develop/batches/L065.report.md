## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. agricultural-credit-markets.md (Explainer): "The farmer who could double her yield with a $100 investment will not borrow at 80% interest when the expected return is only 150%." The arithmetic fails. She repays $180 and earns $250, so she still clears $70. The example does not support the claim.
2. agricultural-productivity-development.md (Explainer): "urban wages stay depressed by food costs". This reverses the mechanism. High food costs push urban wages up, as the file's own MC question 0 says ("keeps urban wages high").
3. agricultural-productivity-development.md (Q3, true-false, answer false): this question says cheaper food is "not the primary mechanism". In Lewis/Ricardian dual-economy models, the food-price (terms-of-trade) channel to industrial wages and profits is central. Also, agriculture-and-development.md and dual-economy-agricultural-industrial.md treat food prices as a main channel ("identifying food prices as the hidden transmission mechanism"). The files are inconsistent with each other.
4. agriculture-and-development.md (Explainer): "agriculture's share of GDP and employment falls from 60-80% in the poorest economies". 60-80% fits the employment share. The GDP share in the poorest economies is typically much lower, around 20-40%.
5. agricultural-extension-and-information.md (Q1 explanation and Explainer): this text says Reuters Market Light and iCow raised incomes through price information. iCow is mainly a livestock and dairy advisory service. The best-known RML evaluation (Fafchamps and Minten) found little effect on prices received. The farmgate-price claim is overstated.
6. banking-services-and-development.md (Q2, true-false, answer false): the statement says the correlation "could simply mean" reverse causation. Predictive timing (King and Levine) does not rule that out, so "false" is a contestable key. The explanation itself concedes the relationship runs both ways.
7. conditional-cash-transfers-cct.md (Q2 explanation): "evaluations have confirmed better child outcomes under this design" (paying mothers). Progresa did not randomize which parent received the transfer. The mother-control evidence comes mostly from other studies, so "confirmed under this design" is overstated.
8. corruption-and-economic-development.md (Q0): "spends 20% of GDP on education — more than most OECD nations". This is an implausible figure; OECD education spending is roughly 5% of GDP. Suggest about 6-7%.
9. credit-constraints-and-borrowing.md (Q0 and Q2 explanations): "risky borrowers have higher expected returns (on average)". In Stiglitz-Weiss, the projects can have equal means. Risky borrowers stay at high rates because limited liability lets them keep the upside while the lender bears the downside. Also, "refuse all loans" overstates the model's prediction, which is credit rationing.
10. development-policy-evaluation.md (Q2, true-false, answer false): the statement is that a *well-designed* RCT typically gives more credible causal estimates than quasi-experiments. The explanation argues against *poorly implemented* RCTs, which the statement excludes. The key is arguably wrong. Separately, the Explainer says "World Bank and USAID now require impact evaluations for major programs", which overstates their policy.
11. disease-health-constraints.md (Q0 and Explainer): "increased school attendance by 25%". Miguel and Kremer reported that deworming reduced *absenteeism* by about one quarter. That is not a 25% rise in attendance. Also, "treating one person for tuberculosis prevents transmission to dozens" is likely overstated, and "market forces alone will always under-provide" is an absolute.
12. fertility-development-relationship.md (Q3 explanation): "This reinforcing cycle — sometimes called the demographic dividend". This is a mislabel. The demographic dividend is the growth effect of a favorable age structure, not the fertility, investment, and growth loop.
13. foreign-direct-investment.md (Explainer): "The 1997 Asian financial crisis illustrated how quickly capital can flee". This is used as an FDI sudden-stop example, but the 1997 reversal was mainly in bank lending and portfolio flows. FDI to the affected countries stayed relatively stable.
14. gdp-limitations-alternative-metrics.md (Q3, true-false, and Explainer): this text says GDP does not count subsistence farming ("entirely invisible to GDP"). Under SNA rules, own-account production of goods, including subsistence crops, is inside the production boundary and imputed. gross-domestic-product-development-measurement.md says so correctly. The Q3 statement as a whole is still false (household services and ecosystem services are excluded), but the explanation's subsistence claim is wrong. Also in this file: Q1's explanation says the HDI supplements income with "distribution-sensitive measures". The HDI is not distribution-sensitive; the inequality-adjusted IHDI is. And Q0's key (GDP rises after an oil spill) ignores lost fishing output, so the net effect is ambiguous.
15. geographic-determinants-development.md (Explainer): "landlocked developing countries (Chad, Nepal, Bolivia) consistently grow more slowly than their coastal neighbors" is overstated. "Countries with moderate, diversified resource bases tend to outperform both resource-poor and resource-rich extremes" has no support in the file and looks unsupported. The Q4 model answer has a garbled sentence: "if geography raises trade costs through landlockedness, public health investment can address the disease burden".
16. geographic-resource-endowments.md (Q1 explanation): "oil profits are boosted by a stronger currency (they earn foreign currency)". This is wrong. Appreciation lowers the local-currency value of foreign-currency earnings.
17. gross-domestic-product-development-measurement.md (Q4 answer): "The informal sector — often 50–80% of economic activity". The Explainer gives 50-80% as a share of *employment*. Informal output shares are usually lower than employment shares.
