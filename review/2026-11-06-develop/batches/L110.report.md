## Flags
Question numbers (Q0, Q1, ...) are 0-based item indexes in each file's Questions block.

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- adaptive-radiation-patterns.md, Q2 (true-false): "Adaptive radiations typically show an 'early burst' pattern" and the explanation's list of clades where it "has been documented". Large comparative analyses have found clear early bursts in only a minority of clades, so "typically" overstates it.
- antipredator-defenses-and-mimicry.md, Q2 explanation: "A naive predator must have one aversive experience to learn a warning pattern." Predators often need several encounters. This is a simplification of the fixed-number-of-attacks model.
- apparent-competition-indirect-effects.md, Q0 explanation: "Options A and B invoke mechanisms (shared resource, disease) that would require direct interaction." A shared parasite or pathogen is itself a shared natural enemy and a standard route to apparent competition. It does not need direct contact between the two hosts.
- bioaccumulation-and-ecological-toxicology.md, Q1 explanation: "High acute toxicity (option A) is a consequence of biomagnification, not a prerequisite." Toxicity is a property of the chemical. Biomagnification raises the dose, not the toxicity.
- bioaccumulation-and-ecological-toxicology.md, Explainer and Q4 explanation: "DDT at parts-per-trillion in lake water reached parts-per-thousand in eagle tissues". This looks doubtful. Classic estuary data show roughly a millionfold rise, to tens of ppm in fish-eating birds, not parts per thousand.
- biodiversity-ecosystem-function-relationships.md, Q4 explanation: "the variance of a sum of imperfectly correlated variables is less than the sum of their individual variances". This is false for positively correlated variables, where Var(sum) exceeds the sum of variances. The true statement concerns standard deviations or the coefficient of variation.
- biodiversity-patterns-richness-evenness.md, Core Idea ("Diversity varies predictably ... at intermediate disturbance levels"), Q1 and Explainer: these present the intermediate disturbance hypothesis as reliable. It is heavily contested, and many field studies do not find a mid-level peak. Q4 answer: "higher evenness in many groups" for tropical communities is unsupported. Tropical communities often have many rare species.
- biogeographic-patterns-and-realms.md, Q0 explanation: "Placentals introduced later (rats, bats, humans' dingoes)". Bats and native rodents reached Australia naturally (bats long ago). Of these, only the dingo was brought by humans, and rats only partly. Q3 explanation says South American taxa went extinct "in competition" with North American taxa. That cause is debated.
- community-assembly-rules-and-coexistence.md, Q0 and Q2 explanations: "competitive exclusion produces dispersion, not clustering". Competition can also produce trait clustering when it removes species whose traits carry low competitive ability (Mayfield & Levine 2010). community-assembly-rules.md Q1 has the same issue in milder form.
- community-stability-resistance-resilience.md, Core Idea: "Stability trades off with productivity—highly productive systems are often less resistant". This is contested. Grassland experiments found diversity raises both productivity and stability. Explainer: Cedar Creek is cited as showing diversity raises "both more resistant and more resilient". Later analyses found consistent gains in resistance but mixed effects on resilience. Q0 and Q2 infer "high resistance" from centuries of unchanged composition. That observation may only mean the forest was not disturbed (constancy), not that it is resistant.
- community-succession-primary-secondary.md, Core Idea: "later stages have higher diversity". Diversity often peaks at intermediate successional stages.
- comparative-phylogenetic-methods.md, Explainer: "random drift (Brownian motion)". A Brownian motion pattern can come from drift or from randomly fluctuating selection. Minor.
- conservation-genetics-and-population-recovery.md, Q1: in conservation the "living dead" usually means species or populations already committed to extinction (extinction debt), not genetically impoverished ones. Q4 answer: "a more distant felid subspecies could have disrupted Florida-adapted traits" is speculation stated as fact.
- conservation-genetics-effective-size.md, Q1: option C says "harmonic mean ≈ 70". The correct value is 4/0.05275 ≈ 76, and the explanation itself says ≈ 75. Q4 explanation: "A population that crashed to 30 individuals three generations ago and recovered to 3,000 has the genetic diversity of a population that never exceeded ~100". This is wrong. A short crash removes heterozygosity at about 1/(2N) per generation, so three generations near 30 cost only a few percent. Explainer: "In a population of Ne = 50, there is roughly a 1% chance per generation that any given allele is lost". Wrong: 1/(2Ne) = 1% is the expected loss of heterozygosity per generation, not the chance of losing an allele.
- decomposition-microbial-mineralization.md, Explainer: peat stores "roughly twice as much carbon as the entire atmosphere". Peatlands hold roughly 500–600 Gt C, which is less than the atmosphere holds. The common claim is about twice the carbon of all the world's forests. Q1 explanation: names "white rot and brown rot fungi" as efficient lignin degraders. Brown rot fungi mainly break down cellulose and only modify lignin. Q0, Q3, Q4 and Explainer: put tropical soil nutrient poverty down only to fast cycling. Heavy weathering and leaching of old tropical soils is also a major cause.
- density-dependence-mechanisms.md, Q1 explanation: "they kill a fixed proportion or absolute number regardless of how many organisms exist". A fixed absolute number killed is inversely density-dependent per individual. Minor.
- disturbance-ecology-and-succession-dynamics.md, Q1: two options are low-diversity extremes under the hypothesis: the intertidal zone disturbed every few days and the forest undisturbed for centuries. The explanation says both are low, yet only option 2 is keyed. Rewrite the item. Explainer: calls the hypothesis "a genuine and widely observed phenomenon". Reviews find the hump-shaped pattern in only a minority of studies. Q4 explanation: calls the high-disturbance case "competitive exclusion ... pioneers in the high-frequency case". That loss is caused by disturbance, not competition.
- ecological-niche-modeling-and-species-distribution.md, Q2 explanation: the realized niche is "always a subset" of the fundamental niche. Facilitation and sink populations can let a species occur outside its fundamental niche.
- ecological-speciation-sympatric-divergence.md, Q4 answer: "This fitness disadvantage of hybrids is called reinforcement". Wrong. Low hybrid fitness is extrinsic postzygotic isolation. Reinforcement is the evolution of stronger prezygotic barriers that this cost favors (the Explainer states it correctly). Core Idea and Q2 explanation: define ecological speciation as happening "without geographic separation". The standard definition is by mechanism (divergent natural selection), and it includes allopatric cases. Lake Victoria cichlids are also a weak example of sympatric ecological speciation, since sexual selection plays a large role.
- ecological-stoichiometry-element-ratios.md, Q3 explanation: "Productivity depends on the elemental ratio relative to organismal demand, not on absolute quantities." Wrong. The supply of the limiting element sets productivity. In a P-limited lake, doubling P can raise production a lot. Q4 explanation: calls the Redfield ratio one of the first demonstrations "that elemental ratios — not absolute concentrations — are the relevant quantity". This overstates the same point.
- ecosystem-productivity-gpp-npp.md, Core Idea: "NPP ... is the true measure of organic matter accumulation in an ecosystem". Accumulation at the ecosystem level is net ecosystem production, which is NPP minus heterotrophic respiration. Q1: "NPP is 90% of its GPP" is unrealistic, since NPP/GPP is typically about 0.3–0.6. Explainer: tropical rainforest NPP of "1,000–2,000 g C/m²/year" runs high. Usual values are about 800–1,200 g C, roughly 2,000 g dry matter.
- endemism-and-range-restriction.md, Q1 explanation: says the golden toad died out because its cloud forest "shifted upward". The cause is debated: climate anomalies, chytrid fungus, or both. It should be hedged.
- energy-pyramid-efficiency-trophic-transfer.md, Explainer and Q2: present "1–5%" for endotherms and "10–15%" for ectotherms as trophic transfer efficiencies. These are production efficiencies (production divided by assimilation), a different quantity. Core Idea: "progressively fewer organisms at higher trophic levels". Pyramids of numbers and biomass can be inverted. Q4 calls food chain length a "hard thermodynamic constraint", but other hypotheses (ecosystem size, dynamic stability) have support.
