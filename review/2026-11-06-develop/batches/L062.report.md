## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- line-integrals.md, Core Idea: "Both depend on the parametrization's orientation." The scalar integral ∫_C f ds does not depend on orientation. The file's own Explainer and questions 2 and 4 say so. My misconception bullet follows the Explainer.
- tangent-planes-surfaces.md, Core Idea "∇f lies in the plane", plus the same claim in the Explainer and the Q1/Q3 explanations. The vector ⟨f_x, f_y, 0⟩ dotted with the normal ⟨f_x, f_y, −1⟩ gives f_x² + f_y², which is nonzero unless ∇f = 0. So the vector is not parallel to the tangent plane. (The lifted vector ⟨f_x, f_y, f_x² + f_y²⟩ is.) The Q5 explanation also says ⟨f_x, f_y, −1⟩ points "upward" and in the same sentence gives it a negative z-component, which is self-contradictory.
- total-differential-approximation.md, Q1 explanation: "errors in r are amplified by 2πh = 62.8". The coefficient on dr is ∂V/∂r = 2πrh = 60π ≈ 188.5 at r = 3, h = 10. The final dV = 7.8π is correct.
- triple-integrals-cylindrical.md: the Q1 and Q4 explanations and the Explainer say that omitting r "systematically undercounts volume". Where r < 1, omitting r overcounts. Q5 answer: "error factor is exactly ... R/2, so you undercount by half the radius on average". Omitting r for a cylinder gives 2πRH against the true πR²H, a ratio of 2/R, so the result is too large when R < 2. Q5 explanation: "your integral gives RH". Omitting r gives 2πRH.
- unconstrained-optimization.md, Explainer: "If f is defined on all of ℝ² and f → ∞ in all directions, then a local minimum is a global minimum." This is false. Such a function can have several local minima, and only the lowest is global. (A global minimum does exist, and it lies at a critical point.)
- surface-integrals-scalar.md, Explainer: "The factor 1/z reflects how the hemisphere tilts away from vertical as you move toward the equator". This is reversed. Near the equator the surface tilts toward vertical, away from horizontal, which is why the area factor grows.
- stokes-theorem.md, Explainer last paragraph: it says Stokes "explains why the specific surface spanning C doesn't matter for conservative fields", and it defines conservative as ∇×F = 0. Surface independence of the curl flux holds for every smooth F; the file's own Q1 and Q3 say so. Equating "conservative" with "curl-free" also needs a simply connected domain.
- second-partials-test-hessian.md, Q2 explanation: it calls the origin for f = x³ − y³ "a saddle". It is a degenerate critical point that is not an extremum. "Saddle" is loose there, since the function has no up-in-one-direction, down-in-another quadratic shape.
- multivariable-functions-intro.md, Q4 wording: "along most straight-line path" is garbled. It probably means "every straight-line path".
