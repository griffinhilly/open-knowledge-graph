## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- umvue.md, Core Idea: "A necessary condition for a UMVUE is that it's a function of a complete sufficient statistic." Being an unbiased function of a complete sufficient statistic is a sufficient condition (Lehmann-Scheffé), not a necessary one. UMVUEs can exist when no complete sufficient statistic exists.
- umvue.md, Explainer: "for exponential family distributions ... the natural sufficient statistic is always complete." This needs a full-rank family (natural parameter space containing an open set). Curved exponential families can fail. Also "no linear combinations of T that are informationally empty" should say functions, not linear combinations.
- weak-law-of-large-numbers.md, Core Idea: "The key assumption is finite variance" conflicts with its own first sentence (finite mean suffices for i.i.d.). Finite variance is only needed for the Chebyshev proof.
- weak-law-of-large-numbers.md, Explainer: "The convergence rate is σ²/n". σ²/n is the variance of the average; the typical error shrinks like σ/√n.
- strong-law-of-large-numbers.md, Explainer: "For bounded variables, Chebyshev-like tail bounds give P(Aₙ) ≤ C/n²". Chebyshev (second moment) gives only C/n, which is not summable. The C/n² bound comes from the fourth-moment Markov bound.
- variance-higher-moments-rigorous.md, question 3 explanation: "A distribution is uniquely determined by its moments only when Carleman's condition is satisfied". Carleman's condition is sufficient, not necessary; "only when" is wrong (the Explainer states it correctly with "if").
- variance-higher-moments-rigorous.md, Explainer: "High kurtosis (leptokurtic) indicates heavy tails and a sharp peak". Kurtosis reflects tail weight; the link to peakedness is a known misconception (there are high-kurtosis distributions with flat centers). Same paragraph: "AM-GM inequality ... and the fact that the geometric mean never exceeds the arithmetic mean" lists the same fact twice; "concavity of entropy" is not a direct Jensen consequence (Gibbs' inequality is).
- variance-higher-moments-rigorous.md, Core Idea: μₖ is used for both raw moments E[Xᵏ] and central moments E[(X − E[X])ᵏ]. Notation clash.
- uniformly-most-powerful-tests.md, Core Idea: "A UMP test maximizes power (Type II error)". Power is 1 − P(Type II error). The sentence "For composite alternatives (e.g., H₁: θ > θ₀), UMP tests may not exist" uses the one-sided case as its example, where UMP tests do exist under MLR; the two-sided case is the usual example.
- uniformly-most-powerful-tests.md, question 4 explanation: "UMPU tests exist for all exponential family problems". Overstated; the standard result covers one-parameter (and certain multiparameter) exponential families with specific hypothesis forms.
- relationships-between-modes-of-convergence.md, question 0 explanation and question 3: says Xₙ converging in distribution to N(0,1) does not mean each Xₙ is approximately normal, and that they "could be wildly non-normal random variables whose distribution functions happen to converge to Φ". If the CDFs converge to Φ (uniformly, since Φ is continuous), the distributions of Xₙ are close to N(0,1) in Kolmogorov distance for large n. The intended point (no pathwise closeness to a particular normal variable) is correct, but option C as worded is arguably true, and the "truncated Cauchy" example in question 3 is muddled.
- sufficient-statistics.md, question 0: option D ("No other statistic derived from the data could provide additional information about μ beyond what X̄ already provides") is called "subtly wrong", but under sufficiency it is a true statement. The item may have two correct options.
- stationary-distributions.md, Explainer: "Since every row of P sums to 1 ... 1 is an eigenvalue — so a stationary distribution always exists (for finite chains)." The conclusion is true, but an eigenvalue of 1 alone does not give a nonnegative left eigenvector; that needs a further argument (Perron-Frobenius or a compactness argument). Also "infinitely many states (continuous state spaces)" conflates countable and continuous spaces, and the Core Idea's convergence claim needs positive recurrence for infinite chains.
- multivariate-normal-distribution.md, Core Idea: "the sample mean vector is MVN for large samples" should be approximately MVN (by the CLT); it is exactly MVN for normal data.
- sigma-algebras-and-measurable-sets.md, question 3 explanation: "The power set of ℝ ... Using the power set leads to contradictions." That holds for Lebesgue (translation-invariant) measure, not for every probability measure; point masses and discrete measures are defined on the power set. Question 3's statement text "the power set of most subsets of ℝ" is garbled.
