## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:

1. perihelion-precession-mercury.md, Q1 explanation: "about 5557 arcseconds per century — is due to Newtonian gravitational perturbations from other planets." About 5025"/cy of the ~5600"/cy observed is the precession of the equinoxes (Earth-based reference frame). Planetary perturbations give about 532"/cy. The Explainer's "Newtonian calculations accounting for the gravitational tugs ... explained all but about 43" carries the same blur.
2. perihelion-precession-mercury.md, Q2 explanation: "The factor a(1-e²) is the semi-latus rectum, which is the radius of the orbit at the endpoints of the semi-minor axis." The semi-latus rectum is the focus-to-orbit distance perpendicular to the major axis. The minor-axis endpoints lie a distance a from the focus.
3. perihelion-precession-mercury.md, Explainer and Q3 explanation: they credit the extra −1/r³ term only to spatial curvature (g_rr). In PPN terms the shift scales as (2 + 2γ − β)/3, so both g_tt (β) and g_rr (γ) contribute. This is oversimplified.
4. frame-dragging.md, Q4 answer: "(4.7×10²³)/(3.1×10²⁵) ≈ 1.5 × 10⁻² arcsec/year, or about 15 milliarcseconds per year." The denominator c²r³ is about 3.1 × 10³⁷. GJ/(c²r³) ≈ 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ rad/s ≈ 0.1 arcsec/yr (about 100 mas/yr). With the polar-orbit average factor of 1/2 and Earth's actual J ≈ 5.9 × 10³³, you get the ~39 mas/yr prediction.
5. frame-dragging.md, Core Idea: "This causes precession of gyroscopes (geodetic + Lense-Thirring precession)". Geodetic precession is not a frame-dragging effect, because it occurs for a non-rotating mass.
6. christoffel-symbols.md, Q4 answer: "In the Newtonian limit, the Γ^0_{00} component reduces to the gradient of the gravitational potential." It should be Γ^i_{00}. Γ^0_{00} vanishes for a static field.
7. christoffel-symbols.md, Explainer: "Γ^i_{00} ≈ (1/2) ∂_i g_{00}". With the (−,+,+,+) signature the course uses elsewhere (g_00 = −(1 + 2Φ/c²)), it is −(1/2)∂_i g_{00}. The sign is right only in (+,−,−,−).
8. christoffel-symbols.md, Q3 explanation: "how the 10 independent metric components vary across the 4 coordinate directions, minus the constraints". 10 × 4 = 40 equals the Christoffel count exactly, so nothing is subtracted.
9. gravitational-energy-pseudo-tensors.md, Core Idea: "∂_μ(√(-g)(T^μν + t^μν_LL)) = 0". The Landau-Lifshitz form uses (−g), not √(−g), as the Explainer and Q4 correctly write.
10. tests-of-general-relativity.md, Explainer: GW150914 tested "gravitational fields at the Planck curvature scale". Horizon curvature of ~30 solar-mass black holes is far below Planck curvature.
11. tests-of-general-relativity.md, Q2 explanation: it attributes the Shapiro delay to the spatial curvature (g_rr) alone. Both g_tt and g_rr contribute, giving the (1 + γ) factor.
12. singularity-theorems.md, Explainer: "The geodesics must therefore reach zero expansion (a caustic or focal point)". At a caustic the expansion diverges to −∞, not to zero.
13. singularity-theorems.md, Core Idea: Penrose's 1965 theorem also needs a non-compact Cauchy surface. Global hyperbolicity alone is listed. This is minor.
14. gravitational-lensing.md, Core Idea: it calls the 1919 eclipse "the first experimental validation of general relativity". Mercury's perihelion (1915) came first as a quantitative confirmation, and tests-of-general-relativity.md says so.
15. gravitational-waves.md, Explainer: "G/c⁵ ≈ 2.6 × 10⁻⁵³ W⁻¹". The value is about 2.76 × 10⁻⁵³ W⁻¹. This is minor.
16. kerr-solution.md, Q3 explanation: superradiance is called the Penrose process's "quantum analog". Superradiance is a classical wave effect. Its quantum version is spontaneous emission (Unruh-Starobinsky).
17. hawking-radiation.md, Q2 explanation: astrophysical black holes start evaporating "trillions of years from now". The CMB must cool by a factor of ~5 × 10⁷. With Λ-dominated expansion (Hubble time ~17 Gyr) that takes roughly 300 billion years. This is order-of-magnitude, so please check.
18. ricci-tensor-scalar-curvature.md, Q2 explanation: "For ordinary matter (ρ > 0, p ≥ 0), R can be negative. For a cosmological constant ... R can be positive." This is convention-dependent and muddled. In (−,+,+,+), R = 8πG(ρ − 3p/c²)/c² is positive for dust and zero for radiation. The answer (false) still stands.
19. riemann-curvature-tensor.md, Q3 answer: it labels side-by-side balls converging as "radial tidal compression". That is transverse compression.
20. friedmann-equations.md, Explainer: it says the curvature term "can accelerate or decelerate depending on sign". k does not appear in the acceleration equation. It affects H, not ä directly.
21. cosmological-constant-dark-energy.md and friedmann-equations.md: they write the Λ term as Λ/3 while also quoting Λ ~ 10⁻⁵² m⁻². With Λ in m⁻², the term is Λc²/3. This is a units inconsistency.
