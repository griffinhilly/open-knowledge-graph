## Flags

Suspected factual errors or overstatements in existing text:
- historical-difference.md, Explainer: "the past was, in the philosopher's phrase, a foreign country". The line comes from L. P. Hartley's novel *The Go-Between* (1953). Hartley was a novelist, not a philosopher.
- historical-difference.md, Explainer and q2: "Medieval Europeans did not distinguish between 'natural' and 'supernatural' as categories." This is overstated. Scholastic writers used a natural/supernatural distinction.
- marxist-historiography-theory.md, Explainer and q0/q2 explanations: these say Marx analyzed "the English Civil War". Marx left no major study of it; Engels wrote more on it. The Eighteenth Brumaire reference is fine.
- marxist-historiography-theory.md, Explainer: "primitive communism, slavery, feudalism, capitalism, socialism ... is a historical schema, not an inevitability". The fixed five-stage list was codified later, largely in Soviet Marxism. Marx's own texts also mention an Asiatic mode. Whether Marx thought the sequence inevitable is contested.
- memory-studies-theory.md, Explainer and q1 explanation: "remembered very differently in West Germany, East Germany ... in 1960 than in 1990 than in 2020". East and West Germany did not exist as separate states in 2020.
- memory-studies.md, q3 explanation and Explainer: these say trauma "resists narrative coherence", and q3 states this "drawing on psychological and neurological research". That is one strand of trauma theory. Psychological research on traumatic memory is mixed, and many studies find traumatic events well remembered. It should be hedged.
- global-history-approach.md, Explainer: "The collapse of Atlantic cod stocks affected New England fishing communities, Portuguese merchants, and West African diets." This is unclear and possibly wrong. The well-known cod collapse came in 1992, and the West African link is unsupported. Separately, "Silver ... drove price inflation in Ming China" is debated. The classic inflation case is Europe's price revolution.
- history-from-below-nonelites.md, Explainer: "The phrase itself comes from the socialist press tradition". This is unsupported. The phrase is usually traced to E. P. Thompson's 1966 TLS essay "History from Below", with earlier uses by Georges Lefebvre.
- history-from-below-concept.md, Explainer: Thompson's 1963 book is called "a rebuke to the structural Marxism dominant in social science at the time". Althusserian structural Marxism peaked later. Thompson's polemic against it, *The Poverty of Theory*, is from 1978. In 1963 his target was mainly economistic orthodox Marxism and economic history.
- herodotus-inquiry-method.md, Explainer: Herodotus "traveled extensively — Egypt, Persia, ...". Travel to Persia proper is doubtful. Even his visits to Babylon and Egypt have been questioned.
- historical-positivism.md, Explainer: this treats Ranke's program and "historical positivism" as one thing. Positivism in Comte's and Buckle's sense sought general laws, and Ranke rejected that. My misconception bullet notes the difference without contradicting the Explainer.
- hayden-white-linguistic-turn.md, historical-truth-and-representation.md, linguistic-turn-historiography.md: White's four emplotments are romance, tragedy, comedy, satire. Some of these files list "irony" as a plot type, but in *Metahistory* irony is one of the four tropes. The files are inconsistent with each other.
- history-science-debate.md, q3: this says the positivist model needs laws "confirmed by repeatable experiments". Hempel's covering-law model needs general laws but not experiments; observational sciences such as astronomy qualify.
- narrative-authority.md, Core Idea: this calls Hayden White a "post-structuralist thinker". He is usually classed as a narrativist or formalist. The "effect of the real" idea is mainly Roland Barthes's ("l'effet de réel"), not de Certeau's.
- historical-methodology-systems.md, Explainer: "Global history must find methods for comparing societies". This conflicts with global-history-approach.md, which separates global history (connections) from comparison.
- indigenous-historiography.md, q3: "Colonial archives are valuable ... because they contain detailed records of indigenous peoples' experiences and perspectives", marked false. This is overstated. Colonial archives do hold indigenous petitions and testimony, which can be read against the grain.
- historical-truth-theories.md, Explainer: "the Roman Empire fell in 476 CE". The conventional 476 date applies to the western empire only.
