## Flags

Factual / content issues in existing text:
- pulp-fiction-serialization-popular.md, Explainer: "The Shadow, Doc Savage, the early Spider-Man, and countless detectives appeared in issue after issue". Spider-Man is a 1960s Marvel comics character, not a pulp hero. The pulp hero was "The Spider."
- time-travel-narrative-mechanics.md, How It's Best Learned: "Primer [fixed], Back to the Future [branching], All You Need Is Kill [ontological]". These labels are doubtful. Back to the Future is usually read as one mutable timeline (Marty fades from the photo). All You Need Is Kill is a reset time loop, not an ontological (bootstrap) paradox. Primer's iterations are hard to call fixed.
- western-frontier-lawlessness.md, How It's Best Learned: lists Blood Meridian as a "canonical" western (it is usually called revisionist or anti-western). It also lists Butler's Parable series and Jemisin's Broken Earth as revisionist westerns; neither is generally classed as a western.
- urban-fantasy-magic-city.md, How It's Best Learned + Explainer: names Jemisin's Inheritance Trilogy and Novik's Scholomance as urban fantasy. Inheritance is secondary-world fantasy; Scholomance is a magic-school fantasy that is not city-based. (Jemisin's The City We Became would fit.)
- steampunk-industrial-fantasy.md, How It's Best Learned: Westerfeld's Leviathan is set in an alternate 1914 with diesel "Clanker" machines and fabricated "Darwinist" beasts; it is a loose fit for "steam power rather than electricity" and Victorian aesthetics. The Explainer also says airships matter "in ways they couldn't in our timeline", but real airships were used in exploration and war.
- romance-genre-emotional-centrality.md, Explainer: "Some end in recognition and respect between people who briefly loved each other". The same idea is in romance-emotional-fulfillment-arc.md, Explainer: "A relationship that ends with mutual recognition and respect can be emotionally satisfying". Both conflict with the common genre definition of romance, which requires an emotionally satisfying, optimistic ending (HEA/HFN). A story where the relationship ends is usually classed outside genre romance.
- romance-genre-emotional-centrality.md: the Core Idea defines romance by "two protagonists", but the Explainer lists polyamorous romance as a subgenre. This is an internal inconsistency.
- thriller-escalating-tension.md, question 3 (true-false, answer false): "Thriller protagonists typically have time to pause for character development and emotional reflection". This overgeneralizes, and so does the Explainer's "Thrillers aren't trying to explore ideas or character depth". Many thrillers use quiet breather scenes. The sibling file thriller-suspense-action-pacing.md says contemporary thrillers often develop character complexity.
- science-fiction-hard-soft.md, Explainer: describes Timescape's premise as "time-travel physics". The novel involves tachyon messages sent to the past, not time travel. It also says Gethenian sex "is fluid", which is a loose description of kemmer.
- western-frontier-morality-mythology.md, Explainer: typo "themaic" (should be "thematic").
