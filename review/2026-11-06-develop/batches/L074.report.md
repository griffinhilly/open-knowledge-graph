## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- gravity-surveys-and-data-inversion.md, Explainer: "0.01 milligal (about one ten-millionth of Earth's surface gravity)". g ≈ 980,000 mGal, so 0.01 mGal is about 1e-8 of g (one hundred-millionth).
- gravity-surveys-and-data-inversion.md, Core Idea: "resolution inversely proportional to depth". Resolution falls with depth, but "inversely proportional" is a stronger claim than the method supports.
- heat-flow-conduction-steady-state.md, Core Idea: "Continental heat flow (~60 mW/m²) exceeds oceanic heat flow (~82 mW/m² ...)". This contradicts itself. Average oceanic heat flow is higher than continental.
- heat-flow-conduction-steady-state.md, Explainer: "heat flow increases with depth because each layer of rock adds its own radiogenic contribution to the upward flux". Backwards. With heat production, flux increases toward the surface (decreases with depth). Q1's explanation states this correctly.
- heat-flow-conduction-steady-state.md, Explainer: "decreases predictably with the square root of crustal age". It should be the inverse square root (q ∝ 1/√t).
- heat-flow-conduction-steady-state.md, Q4 explanation: "when a layer is generating internal heat, the gradient reverses within the layer". In general it does not reverse. The gradient only gets gentler with depth.
- heat-flow-measurement-geothermal.md, Q2 explanation: it implies multi-decadal and longer climate signals stay within the shallow tens of meters. Climate changes over centuries to millennia reach hundreds of meters or more.
- isostasy-and-crustal-balance.md, Explainer: "still rising today, centuries after the ice sheets melted". It should be thousands of years (Q0 says thousands).
- joint-inversion-gravity-seismics.md, Core Idea: "Gravity adds depth sensitivity beyond seismic surveys". Gravity has poor depth resolution. Its value is density information and lateral coverage.
- joint-inversion-gravity-seismics.md, Q3 and Explainer: cross-gradient coupling is described as needing "parallel" gradients. The cross-gradient condition is also met by antiparallel gradients, or where one gradient is zero.
- lithospheric-structure-and-strength.md, Core Idea: "young, hot lithosphere is weak and thick". Young lithosphere is thin.
- lithospheric-structure-and-strength.md, Q0 explanation: "the Hawaiian chain shows progressively stronger subsidence signatures on older lithosphere as the Pacific Plate moves northwest". This is unsupported and likely garbled.
- magnetic-dipole-anomalies.md, Q2 explanation: "magnetic field strength decreases as the square of distance". A dipole field falls as 1/r³.
- magnetic-dipole-anomalies.md, Explainer, Q0 and Q4: these describe the equatorial anomaly as antisymmetric with an offset peak. For an induced compact source at the magnetic equator, the total-field anomaly is a central low with weaker highs to the north and south (symmetric N-S). The offset, asymmetric pattern belongs to intermediate inclinations.
- magnetic-field-reduction-pole.md, Core Idea: "displaced from their sources in non-equatorial regions". It should say non-polar. Displacement is absent only where the field is vertical.
- magnetic-field-reduction-pole.md, Q0 explanation ("peak offset in the poleward direction") and Q4 answer ("toward the pole"). For induced sources the positive peak shifts toward the equator. Q0's own stem (peak displaced south at northern mid-latitude) agrees with the equatorward shift.
- magnetic-field-reduction-pole.md, Q2: the stem is phrased as an open question ("What is the most likely explanation?") but typed true-false with answer false. The item is malformed.
- magnetic-field-reduction-pole.md, Q3 explanation: a vertically magnetized source gives a positive anomaly "with no negative lobe". A compact dipole at the pole has a weak negative ring around it. magnetic-dipole-anomalies.md Q3 says the opposite.
- magnetostratigraphy-dating.md, Core Idea: "valuable for dating sediments too young for radiometric methods". Very young sediments lie within the Brunhes chron and record no reversals. Radiometric methods such as radiocarbon cover young material. The claim is doubtful.
- mantle-adiabat-temperature.md, Core Idea: "potential temperature of the mantle is approximately 1300 K". It should be about 1300 °C (~1600 K). The Explainer says 1300–1350 °C.
- mantle-adiabat-temperature.md, Explainer and Q1 explanation: "same thermal energy per unit mass". Parcels on one adiabat share specific entropy, not internal energy.
- mantle-convection-and-dynamics.md, Explainer: "Where hot mantle material rises and spreads laterally, oceanic crust rifts apart to form mid-ocean ridges". This implies active upwelling drives ridges. Ridge upwelling is largely passive, and slab pull is a main plate-driving force.
- mantle-rheology-flow.md, Explainer: "spans roughly two orders of magnitude, from about 10¹⁹–10²⁰ ... to 10²²–10²³". That range is about 2–4 orders. The Core Idea range (10²¹–10²³) also conflicts with the asthenosphere value.
- moment-tensor-inversion.md, Q2 explanation: "null axis perpendicular to the fault plane". The null axis lies in the fault plane, perpendicular to slip (the intersection of the two nodal planes).
- paleomagnetic-poles-and-plate-reconstruction.md, Q0: South America–Africa paths "diverge starting in the Jurassic ... as the Atlantic opened" and "matches precisely when ... the South Atlantic began opening". The South Atlantic opened in the Early Cretaceous. The Central Atlantic opened in the Jurassic.
- paleomagnetic-dating-and-stratigraphy.md, Core Idea: "Phanerozoic and Cenozoic strata". This is redundant, since the Cenozoic is part of the Phanerozoic.
- radioactive-heat-production.md, Core Idea: "~10–50 mW/m³ in continental crust". The unit and magnitude are wrong. Granite heat production is about 1–5 μW/m³ (the Explainer agrees).
- radioactive-heat-production.md, Q1 and Explainer: neglecting heat production gives "temperatures too low in the upper crust". When the model is constrained by measured surface heat flow (the usual case), leaving out crustal heat production overestimates crustal temperatures at depth. The claim holds only if basal flux is fixed. As written it is at best ambiguous.
- reflection-seismic-survey-design.md, Q1 explanation: "only long-offset rays reach deep reflectors". Near-offset rays reach deep reflectors too. Long offsets are needed for measurable moveout and velocity analysis.
- rock-magnetism-hysteresis.md, Q2 and Explainer: every grain below ~80 nm is called single-domain. Very fine grains (below a few tens of nm) are superparamagnetic and carry no stable remanence.
- rock-rheology-elastic-plastic-deformation.md, Core Idea: "yield strength decreases with temperature and strain rate". Ductile strength increases with strain rate.
- rock-rheology-elastic-plastic-deformation.md, Core Idea: "brittle-ductile transition ... defines the upper boundary of the seismogenic zone". It is the lower boundary.
- rock-rheology-elastic-plastic-deformation.md, Explainer: "strong 'jelly sandwich' or 'crème brûlée' structure". These are competing models. Crème brûlée has a weak mantle, so it is not a structure with strength just above the BDT.
- saturation-and-remanence-in-rocks.md, Core Idea and Q4: TRM is described as acquired at, and reset only above, the Curie temperature. Remanence is blocked at grain blocking temperatures at or below the Curie point. Reheating above the blocking temperatures resets it.
