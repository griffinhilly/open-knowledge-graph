## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:
- band-structure-density-of-states.md, Explainer: "a step discontinuity at band edges in 3D, logarithmic divergences at saddle points". In 3D the band edge gives a square-root onset and saddle points give square-root kinks. Steps are the 2D band-edge case and log divergences are the 2D saddle-point case. (The Q1 explanation in the same file correctly says 3D gives kinks.)
- band-structure-density-of-states.md, Explainer: "the gradient must vanish at the band minimum, the band maximum, and at least two saddle points". Morse theory on the 3-torus requires at least 3 saddle points of each type, 8 critical points in total. Q1 explanation's "a minimum of four energies" is also doubtful.
- band-structure-density-of-states.md, Q3 (true-false): the item is a "how does this change" question with answer false, and its explanation admits it "is not true-false". Needs a rewrite.
- ferromagnetism-heisenberg-model.md, Q3 (true-false): the question is a "how does this affect" prompt, not a statement. Its explanation says "This is not a true-false question as stated". Needs a rewrite.
- ferromagnetism-heisenberg-model.md, Q4 answer: "the 3d band is narrow ... and less than half-filled". In Fe, Co and Ni the 3d band is more than half filled.
- ferromagnetism-heisenberg-model.md and antiferromagnetism-spin-waves.md, Explainers: Bloch law written as M(0)[1 - (T/T_C)^{3/2}]. The Bloch law is M(0)[1 - aT^{3/2}]; the coefficient a is not 1/T_C^{3/2}.
- hubbard-model.md, Q3 (true-false): "Why can't it be solved exactly?" with answer true. This is not a true-false statement.
- hubbard-model.md, Explainer: "strong numerical and analytical evidence that the doped system develops d-wave superconductivity" overstates the case. Q3's own explanation says methods "give conflicting predictions", and some large-scale numerics find stripes without superconductivity in the pure model.
- metals-insulators-semiconductors.md, Q3 explanation: "Larger inter-atomic spacing means more orbital overlap". Larger spacing gives less overlap and a smaller bonding-antibonding splitting, which is why the gap shrinks.
- electron-phonon-interaction.md, Explainer: "drives phonon-mediated thermal conductivity in metals (the Wiedemann-Franz law)". The Wiedemann-Franz law concerns the electronic thermal conductivity; phonon scattering limits it rather than driving it.
- electron-phonon-interaction.md, Q3 explanation: "In most metals the dressing is mild (mass enhancement of a few percent)". The electron-phonon mass enhancement 1 + lambda has lambda of roughly 0.1 to 1.5 in common metals, often tens of percent or more.
- heavy-fermion-systems.md: Core Idea says the bandwidth is ~k_BT_K "(typically 1-10 meV)", while Q1 explanation says T_K is "typically 1-10 K" with bandwidth "~1 meV". 1-10 K is 0.1-1 meV, so these do not agree.
- heavy-fermion-systems.md, Explainer: "Kadowaki-Woods ratio gamma^2/A". The ratio is conventionally A/gamma^2.
- integer-quantum-hall-effect.md, Q1: the correct option says that without disorder sigma_xy "would change continuously with B", while the explanation says it "would jump discontinuously at each level crossing". They contradict each other. (In a clean, translation-invariant 2DEG sigma_xy = ne/B varies continuously.)
- boltzmann-transport-equation-cm.md, Explainer: lists "de Haas-van Alphen oscillations in the semiclassical regime" as BTE magnetotransport. dHvA comes from Landau quantization and lies outside Boltzmann theory (Q2 in the same file says quantized orbits need a quantum treatment).
- semiconductor-physics-doping.md, Q2: "np = n_i^2 ... regardless of doping" and an explanation built on Boltzmann expressions, while the option calls them Fermi-Dirac. The law of mass action fails for degenerate doping, which Q1 of the same file discusses.
- superfluidity-cm.md, Q4 answer: second-sound formula "c₂ = c₁√(ρ_s T s²/(ρ_n c_p))". The standard result is c₂² = (ρ_s/ρ_n) T s²/c_p with no c₁ factor.
- superfluidity-cm.md, Q3 explanation: "the B-phase is fully gapped but anisotropic". The B-phase (BW state) gap is isotropic in magnitude.
- superfluidity-cm.md, Core Idea: "a linear phonon-roton excitation spectrum". Only the phonon part is linear; the roton part has a minimum.
- mott-insulators.md, Q1: calls NiO "the textbook Mott insulator" with a U-vs-W gap. In the Zaanen-Sawatzky-Allen classification NiO is a charge-transfer insulator (O 2p to Ni upper Hubbard band). The Q4 explanation in the same file uses this classification for cuprates.
- mott-insulators.md, Q4 answer (2): "a band gap is robust against these [pressure, doping]". Doping a band insulator also introduces mobile carriers (doped semiconductors), and pressure changes band gaps.
- greens-functions-many-body.md, Explainer: lists "transport, and optical absorption" among experiments described by the single-particle Green's function. Those are two-particle response functions.
- bcs-theory-detailed.md, Q1 explanation and Explainer: calls Delta ~ ħω_D exp(-1/N(0)V) the Cooper-pair binding energy. The single-pair binding energy is about 2ħω_D exp(-2/N(0)V); the exp(-1/N(0)V) form belongs to the BCS gap.
- bloch-theorem.md, Explainer: "reduces a many-body problem in infinite space". Bloch's theorem applies to the single-particle problem.
- berry-phase-topological-invariants.md, Core Idea: the Berry phase and curvature formulas lack the factor i (the Explainer has A = i<u|∇u>).
- anderson-localization.md, Core Idea and Q2: "In 1D and 2D, all states are localized for any amount of disorder". This holds for the orthogonal class; 2D systems with strong spin-orbit coupling (symplectic class) have a metal-insulator transition, which the file's own weak anti-localization text hints at.
- anderson-localization.md, Q3 explanation: "observed for microwaves in random media (1997)". The date is doubtful (microwave localization work is usually cited from 1991 and 2000; 1997 is the disputed optical result). Worth checking.
- kondo-effect.md, Core Idea: "solved exactly by Wilson's numerical RG (1975)". NRG is a numerical, non-perturbative solution; the exact analytic solution came from the Bethe ansatz (Andrei, Wiegmann, 1980).
- ginzburg-landau-theory.md, Q2 explanation: "When ξ > λ ... the condensation energy gain dominates and the surface energy is positive". The positive surface energy comes from the condensation energy lost over ξ exceeding the magnetic energy saved over λ, so "gain" is the wrong word.
