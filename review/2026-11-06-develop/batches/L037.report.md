## Flags

Question numbers (Q0, Q1, ...) are 0-based item indices in each file's Questions block.

### Suspected factual errors in existing text (highest priority first)

- ca-noether-normalization.md, Q0: answer 1 says k[x, y]/(xy - 1) is an integral extension of k[x]. It is not: y = 1/x is not integral over k[x]. The explanation contradicts itself ("wait, this isn't monic..."). A correct normalization is k[x + y] (x satisfies x² - (x+y)x + 1 = 0). The question needs a rewrite.
- ca-regular-sequences.md, Q0: option 1 "(xy, z)" is also a regular sequence in k[x, y, z]. xy is a non-zero-divisor in the domain, z is a non-zero-divisor on (k[x,y]/(xy))[z], and the quotient is nonzero. The explanation's reason ("xy is a zero divisor on k[x,y,z]/(xy)") makes no sense. So the question has two correct answers.
- ca-regular-sequences.md, Explainer: "(x, y(1-x)) is regular in k[x, y] but (y(1-x), x) is not". Both orders are regular in k[x, y]. x is a non-zero-divisor modulo y(1-x), since x, y and 1-x are coprime. The standard example uses three variables: x, y(1-x), z(1-x) in k[x, y, z].
- ca-krull-dimension.md, Q1: the statement assumes f is a non-unit non-zero-divisor. Under that hypothesis every minimal prime over (f) in a Noetherian ring has height exactly 1, so the statement is TRUE, not false. The explanation's example uses x in k[x,y]/(xy), which is a zero divisor and so breaks the question's own hypothesis. Suggest dropping "non-zero-divisor" from the question.
- ca-associated-primes.md, Explainer: "The embedded associated primes are strictly contained in some other associated prime". This is backwards. An embedded prime strictly contains another associated prime.
- ca-associated-primes.md, Explainer ("different primary decompositions of the same ideal can produce different embedded primes"), Q2 explanation ("Embedded primes depend on the particular decomposition") and Q3 explanation ("the embedded ones may vary"): all wrong. The set Ass(M), embedded primes included, is unique (first uniqueness theorem). Only the embedded primary components can vary.
- ca-local-rings.md, Core Idea: "the ideal structure collapses to a single chain above the maximal ideal". This is false. In k[x, y]_(x,y), the ideals (x) and (y) are incomparable. Totally ordered ideals is the property of valuation rings.
- ca-local-rings.md, Explainer: lists "being free, being finitely generated" as properties that can be checked locally. Neither is local. A nonprincipal ideal of ℤ[√-5] is locally free but not free. ⊕_p ℤ/pℤ is locally finitely generated but not finitely generated.
- ca-flatness.md, Q3 and Explainer: the local criterion "M flat iff Tor_1(R/m, M) = 0" is stated for any module over any local ring. The standard form needs R Noetherian and M finitely generated (or a suitable separatedness condition).
- ca-flatness.md, Q4 explanation: "Localizations S^{-1}R are ... faithfully flat only if every prime of R meets S or is 'seen'". Wrong. S^{-1}R is faithfully flat iff no maximal ideal meets S, which happens iff S consists of units.
- ca-flatness.md, Q2 explanation: calls flat going down "strictly more general than" going down for integral extensions of integrally closed domains. It is not more general: such integral extensions need not be flat.
- ca-flatness.md, Explainer: "Localization, completion, and extension of scalars are all flat operations". Extension of scalars is not flat in general (R → R/I).
- ca-completion.md, Q4 explanation: "faithful flatness of R → R-hat carries Noetherianness upward". This is wrong reasoning. R-hat is Noetherian because, for example, it is a quotient of a power series ring over R. Flatness of R-hat is usually proved after that.
- ca-going-up-going-down.md, Explainer: "When going down also holds, the dimensions are equal for integral extensions". Equality dim S = dim R holds for every integral extension, by lying over, going up and incomparability. Going down is not needed.
- ca-going-up-going-down.md, Q0 explanation: the example "Z[√5] over Z[2√5] can fail going down" is vague and unverified.
- ca-spec-and-zariski-topology.md, Q1 explanation: "V(∩I_α) = ∪V(I_α)" is wrong for infinite families. The correct rules are V(ΣI_α) = ∩V(I_α) and V(I ∩ J) = V(I) ∪ V(J) for finitely many ideals.
- ca-spec-and-zariski-topology.md, Explainer: "R is Noetherian if and only if Spec R is a Noetherian topological space". Only one direction holds. k[x₁, x₂, ...]/(x₁, x₂, ...)² has a one-point spectrum but is not Noetherian.
- ca-noetherian-rings.md, Q0 explanation: "Having all primes principal is neither necessary nor sufficient". It is sufficient: by Cohen's theorem, a ring whose prime ideals are all finitely generated is Noetherian.
- ca-noetherian-rings.md, Explainer: typo "Iₙ = Iₙ for all n ≥ N" (should be Iₙ = I_N). Q3 explanation has a non sequitur ("More concretely, ℤ is Noetherian, but ...").
- ca-graded-rings.md, Q1 explanation: says every nonzero ideal contains a homogeneous element. False: (x + 1) in k[x] contains no nonzero homogeneous element.
- ca-integral-extensions.md, Q4 explanation: "Integrally closed domains are the 'non-singular' rings ... normal (no self-intersections or cusps)". Normal does not mean nonsingular in dimension ≥ 2. The cone k[x,y,z]/(z² - xy) is normal but singular.
- ca-homological-dimension.md, Q2 explanation: "The formula also proves that regular local rings have finite global dimension". This is circular, because Auslander-Buchsbaum assumes pd(M) < ∞.
- ca-noether-normalization.md, Explainer: "every compact manifold admits a finite-sheeted covering map to a simpler space" is not a true general fact.
- ca-valuation-rings.md, Q3: "A valuation ring that is not a DVR is never Noetherian" is answered true, but fields are Noetherian valuation rings that are not DVRs (the explanation admits this). Explainer: the claim that valuation extension is "used in the proof of the going down theorem" is doubtful. The usual proof of going down does not use valuations.
- ca-primary-decomposition.md, Q0 explanation: contains an unfinished fragment "(since we need a²... actually)".
- ca-tensor-products.md, Q0 explanation: garbled false starts ("·something", "1̅ ⊗ (3·1̅)b̅..."). The short-answer Q4 gives the clean argument.
- ca-chain-conditions.md, Q0 explanation: "The other rings all have infinite descending chains or infinite Krull dimension". ℤ, k[x] and k[[x]] all have Krull dimension 1. The infinite-chain half is correct.
- ca-ideal-operations.md, Q3 explanation and Explainer: call the fact "P ⊇ I ∩ J implies P ⊇ I or P ⊇ J" prime avoidance. Prime avoidance is a different lemma (an ideal inside a finite union of primes lies in one of them). Q2 explanation: the justification "no smaller positive integer has this property" for √(12) = (6) is muddled.
