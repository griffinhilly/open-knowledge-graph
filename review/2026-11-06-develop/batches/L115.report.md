### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- mediterranean-medieval-merchant-networks.md, Explainer: "Spices ... were not mere luxuries but preservatives". The idea that spices were used mainly to preserve food is a widely repeated myth that food historians reject.
- mediterranean-medieval-merchant-networks.md, Explainer: "these innovations amounted to the first modern commercial finance system". Overstated; partnership and credit forms existed earlier in the Islamic world and antiquity.
- monastic-scriptoria-knowledge-preservation.md, Core Idea: "Monastic scribes were responsible for transmitting nearly all surviving Greek and Latin literature". Most surviving Greek literature was transmitted by Byzantine copyists, not Western monastic scriptoria.
- monastic-scriptoria-knowledge-preservation.md, Explainer: "Virgil, Cicero, Pliny, and later Aristotle survived the medieval period primarily because monks thought them ... useful". Much of Aristotle reached the Latin West in the 12th-13th centuries through translations from Arabic and Greek, not continuous Western monastic copying. Also "Irish monasteries like Clonmacnoise and Iona": Iona is in Scotland (an Irish foundation). Also "without scriptoria, nearly everything written before 1450 ... would have been lost" is overstated.
- mongol-administration-governance.md, Explainer: "A message could travel from eastern China to Persia in days rather than weeks." Very unlikely over that distance; the yam was fast, but this figure is exaggerated. Core Idea's "standardized weights and measures" across the empire is doubtful as stated. "The Silk Road reached its peak traffic under Mongol rule" is stated as fact but is hard to measure.
- normans-norse-settlement-emergence.md, Question 2 (index 1): a true/false-style stem used as multiple-choice; the correct option's wording "though they intensified rather than preserved Viking culture" is garbled. Q3 explanation says the Normans "sponsor[ed] the First Crusade"; Normans took part (Bohemond, Robert Curthose) but did not sponsor it. Explainer links the 9th-10th-century power vacuum to "the fall of Western Rome" when the relevant context is Carolingian fragmentation.
- papal-authority-and-reform.md, Core Idea: "Reform movements in the 11th–12th centuries led to the Gregorian Reform and the Cluniac movement". Cluny was founded in 910, so the Cluniac movement preceded and did not result from 11th-century reform.
- peasant-revolt-social-resistance.md, Explainer: "Wat Tyler was killed at that meeting" conflates the Mile End meeting with the later Smithfield meeting where Tyler died. Minor.
- pilgrimage-and-devotion.md, Core Idea: "The pilgrimage tradition eventually evolved into the Crusades." Overstated; the file's own Explainer says pilgrimage "didn't cause the Crusades". Explainer/Q3: Chaucer's pilgrims ride, not walk.
- sacred-art-iconography-theology.md, Q3 explanation: hierarchical scale called "a consistent feature of medieval religious imagery" is too absolute. The "books for the illiterate" framing is presented as settled; many scholars note that complex programs such as typological windows needed learned viewers.
- scholastic-inquiry-logical-method.md, Explainer: "medieval thinkers had recovered Aristotle's logical works through Arabic intermediaries". Boethius's Latin translations of the old logic were always available; the new logic was translated mainly from Greek (e.g., James of Venice). Also claims Aquinas's five ways are "each syllogistic chains", which is contestable.
- scholasticism-medieval-thought.md, Explainer and Q1: "Through Arabic translations ... the full corpus of Aristotle's works ... arrived". Many key translations were made directly from Greek (James of Venice, William of Moerbeke). Explainer's description of an article ("every article poses an objection, lists counterarguments, states...") misdescribes the order (objections, sed contra, responsio, replies).
- serfdom-medieval-unfree-peasantry.md, Q1, Q2, Q5 and Explainer: "serfs were bound to the land, not to the person of the lord" is stated as universal. In English law villeins "in gross" could be transferred apart from land, and in later Russia serfs were sold without land.
- subsistence-agriculture-famine-crisis.md, Q4 (true): that famine-weakened immunity is part of why the Black Death killed a third of Europe is contested and presented as settled. Explainer: "flagellant movements" in the Great Famine (major flagellant movements were 1260 and 1349); "infanticide and abandonment ... widespread; cannibalism was documented" rests on chronicle reports historians treat cautiously. Core Idea "Poor harvests caused immediate famine" is overstated.
- troubadour-courtly-love-literature.md, Core Idea: "maintaining feudal hierarchy and married fidelity" conflicts with the genre's typical devotion to a married woman not the poet's wife. Q1/Q5/Explainer present non-consummation as the norm; many cansos openly desire consummation and the alba depicts lovers parting after a night together. Explainer/Q3: the Albigensian Crusade "ended" troubadour culture; it declined, but troubadours were active to the late 13th century.
- urban-burgher-merchant-class.md, Explainer: "'Town air makes you free' was a contemporary saying." The German "Stadtluft macht frei" is a 19th-century legal formulation of an older principle. Q3 explanation refers to "The Explainer explicitly states", which reads oddly on a public page.
- women-agency-medieval-society.md, Explainer: coverture is described as a condition "canon lawyers called coverture". Coverture is an English common-law doctrine, not a canon-law term. Also "supervised large communities of men and women" applies only to double houses. Q3 explanation refers to "the explainer".
