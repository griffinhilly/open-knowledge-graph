## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:

1. neutrino-masses-and-oscillations.md, Q2 answer: "An overall shift of all masses by the same amount does not change any Delta m^2 ... m_1 = 1.0 eV, m_2 = 1.0005 eV give the same Delta m^2 = 10^{-4} eV^2." Wrong. 1.0005² − 1.0² ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻³ eV², not 10⁻⁴. Shifting the masses changes Δm². Shifting the squared masses does not. The example needs m₂ chosen so that m₂² − m₁² stays 10⁻⁴.
2. neutrino-masses-and-oscillations.md, Explainer: "the first and so far only confirmed phenomenon requiring physics beyond the minimal Standard Model". Dark matter and the baryon asymmetry also require new physics. The sibling file bsm-overview.md says so. It could be read as "only laboratory phenomenon", but as written it contradicts the course.
3. neutrino-masses-and-oscillations.md, Q2 explanation: "The unknown absolute mass scale also means we don't know the 'mass ordering'". These are separate unknowns. The ordering is the sign of Δm²₃₂, not a consequence of the unknown scale.
4. effective-field-theory-particle-physics.md, Q1 explanation: "For Lambda ~ 10^{14} GeV and C_5 ~ 1, this gives m_nu ~ 0.05 eV". v²/Λ with v = 246 GeV and Λ = 10¹⁴ GeV is about 0.6 eV. 0.05 eV needs Λ ~ 10¹⁵ GeV. Also, Q1 stem and Explainer: "59 operators for one generation". 59 counts operator types. The one-generation count of independent parameters is larger (76).
5. grand-unification.md, Explainer: "In SU(5), the electron charge equals minus three times the down quark charge". −1 = 3 × (−1/3), so it is plus three times, not minus.
6. extra-dimensions.md, Q1 correct option: "constraining but not yet ruling out n = 2 with M_D = 1 TeV". With R ~ 1 mm and inverse-square tests down to ~50 μm, n = 2 with M_D = 1 TeV is excluded. Astrophysical bounds for n = 2 are stronger still.
7. detector-physics-basics.md, Explainer: "Hadronic calorimeters use denser materials (iron, steel, brass...)". Iron and brass are less dense than the lead and tungsten used in EM calorimeters. Also: "minimum-ionizing energy (~2 MeV/cm)". That is ~2 MeV cm²/g, so ~2 MeV/cm only at unit density. In iron it is about 11 MeV/cm. Q2 stem: resolution "proportional to p_T / (B * L^2 * N)". The standard Gluckstern scaling goes as 1/√N, not 1/N.
8. collider-physics-methods.md, Q1 explanation: "every 5-sigma discovery in particle physics has been confirmed". Not true. Pentaquark Θ⁺ claims and the 2011 OPERA superluminal-neutrino result (about 6σ, later traced to an equipment fault) were not confirmed.
9. jets-and-jet-algorithms.md, Q1 explanation: "Both [k_T and anti-k_T] are IRC safe and give the same inclusive cross sections". Different algorithms give different jet cross sections. Q1 correct option: "perfectly circular". This holds only for isolated hard jets. Explainer: JES relates measured energy to "the true parton energy". Calibration is usually to particle-level jets.
10. electron-positron-annihilation.md, Q3 option and explanation: the Z couplings "are larger than the photon's electromagnetic coupling" / "greatly exceeds the electromagnetic coupling". The jump at the Z pole comes from the resonance. The Z couplings are not much larger than e. The phrase "including qqbar pairs with charge 0 total" is also confused.
11. electroweak-precision-measurements.md, Explainer: "agreement ... typically at the level of a few standard deviations". This reads as a claim of disagreement. Most pulls are under 2σ.
12. luminosity-and-event-rates.md, Explainer: "For precision measurements (such as the W mass ...), the luminosity uncertainty is often the dominant systematic". The W mass is a shape measurement and does not depend on luminosity.
13. lepton-flavor.md, Q2 stem: "The most precise test [of LFU] comes from R(D(*))". R(D(*)) is far less precise than the τ, π, and W tests. Explainer: R(K(*)) is listed among measurements with "tensions ... at the 2-3 sigma level". The 2022 LHCb R(K), R(K*) results agree with the SM. Explainer: W → ℓν universality "to 0.3%" looks too precise for LEP W branching ratios, which reach about 1%.
14. lattice-qcd-basics.md, Explainer: muon g−2 is "a major focus due to the tension between the experimental measurement and the Standard Model prediction". This is outdated. With lattice HVP, the 2025 SM prediction agrees with experiment. Q3 answer: the cost scaling "(1/a)^4 * (L/a)^4" double-counts the lattice spacing.
15. deep-inelastic-scattering.md, Q2 explanation and Explainer: the observed scaling violations "earned Gross, Politzer, and Wilczek the 2004 Nobel Prize". The prize was for the theory of asymptotic freedom. Q1: the stem asks about "three free quarks", but the keyed answer describes bound quarks and adds a confusing claim that the integral of F₂ would account for all the momentum.

The boost is what gives a measurable ~250 μm separation (βγ ≈ 0.56). Without it the separation would be tiny. The stem's "decay length of approximately 450 micrometers at the B factories" conflates cτ with the lab flight length.
