## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- ian-hamilton-finlay-garden-text.md, Explainer: "Little Sparta in the Scottish Highlands" (twice). Little Sparta is at Stonypath, Dunsyre, in the Pentland Hills southwest of Edinburgh. That is not the Highlands.
- ergodic-literature-aarseth.md, Core Idea and Explainer: they frame ergodic literature as separating digital forms from print. Aarseth's Cybertext (1997) says the category does not depend on medium. His examples include the I Ching, Queneau's combinatorial sonnets and other print works. The Q1 option "always digital and never print" is correctly marked wrong, so the prose contradicts its own quiz. Listing kinetic poetry as ergodic is also doubtful, since watching an animation is not nontrivial traversal in Aarseth's sense.
- ergodic-reading-effort-interpretation.md, Explainer: "The term 'ergodic' comes from physics (ergo = work, ode = path)". Aarseth builds it from Greek ergon (work) and hodos (path). "ergo" and "ode" are wrong forms. The physics term shares the roots, but these are not its components.
- hypertext-theory-narrative-paths.md, Core Idea and Explainer: they credit "lexia" and "multilinearity" to Bolter and Joyce. Both terms are mainly George Landow's (Hypertext, 1992), and Landow took "lexia" from Barthes's S/Z. The Q4 explanation cites "ergodic reading" and "nontrivial traversal" as hypertext-theory concepts. Those are Aarseth's terms, and Aarseth criticized that theory.
- hypertext-lexia-fragmented-structure.md, Core Idea: "Hypertext theory developed the lexia concept". The term comes from Roland Barthes (S/Z, 1970), and Landow adapted it. The Q4 explanation's afternoon example ("a lexia about the protagonist's childhood ... family conflict") looks invented.
- afternoon-a-story-lexical-paths.md, Core Idea: it calls "you have not read this" a "famous declaration" of afternoon, and the Explainer calls "lexical paths" Joyce's term. I could not confirm either attribution. Please check them.
- afternoon-a-story-joyce.md, Explainer: it sums up the plot as "a complex romantic entanglement involving the protagonist and two women". That leaves out the central thread: Peter fears he saw his ex-wife and son die in a car accident that morning. This is an incomplete summary, not strictly false.
- digital-born-literature-preservation.md, Explainer: "Storyspace ... is now obsolete". Eastgate still sells Storyspace (version 3 runs on current macOS). Older Storyspace files and runtimes are the obsolete part.
- bs-johnson-typographic-experiment.md, Core Idea and Q1: "form must be an extension of content" is a close variant of Robert Creeley's line "form is never more than an extension of content", which Charles Olson quoted in "Projective Verse". Please check that Johnson used the phrase himself. The Explainer calls Johnson "a pivotal figure in modernist literature", but he was a 1960s-70s postwar experimentalist. It also says House Mother Normal "uses columns", which looks wrong: it is a run of parallel monologues of equal length. The Explainer never mentions The Unfortunates (the boxed novel), which is his clearest "loose arrangement".
- apollinaire-calligrammes.md, Explainer: "poets were breaking free from Victorian constraints". Apollinaire was French, and "Victorian" is a British label. The Q4 true-false explanation ("Before calligrammes, poetry worked through language") ignores much older shaped poems, such as George Herbert's "Easter Wings" and the Greek technopaegnia.
- audio-drama-serialized-performance.md: the Q2 explanation says the gap between episodes is "absent from novels". Serialized novels, such as Dickens's in monthly parts, used it. The Explainer's "None of these possibilities exist in written literature" (about silence and sound) is overstated. The Q4 explanation says listening is "linear in real-time, unlike novels where readers control pace". Podcast listeners can pause, rewind and change playback speed.
- ai-generated-literature-neural.md, Explainer: it says the model "predicts the most likely next token" and repeats. Most generation samples from the predicted distribution, and always picking the top token is greedy decoding. The training is better called self-supervised than "unsupervised". The claim that output is "not copied from training data" ignores memorization, since models can reproduce training text word for word.
- electronic-poetry-digital-forms.md, Core Idea: it says algorithmic generation "cannot exist in print". Combinatorial print works exist, such as Queneau's Cent mille milliards de poèmes (1961).
- generative-poetry-algorithms-text.md, Q3 true-false: "requires human-machine co-authorship in a way that traditional poetry never does" is answered true. That ignores generative procedures carried out without computers, such as Queneau's sonnets and Tzara's cut-up method.
- conceptual-poetry-flarf.md, Explainer: its sample conceptual poems (words starting with one letter, alphabetized passages) are Oulipo-style constraints, not typical conceptual poetry (appropriation, e.g. Goldsmith's Day). It also calls Flarf "a specific instantiation" of conceptual poetry. The two are usually treated as parallel movements, and Flarf leans into taste and "badness" more than concept over result.
- collaborative-fiction-community-networks.md, Explainer: it describes fan fiction as typically multi-author ("other writers add chapters, revise earlier work"). Most fan fiction has one author. Collaborative forms exist, such as round-robins, shared worlds and wikis, but they are not the norm.
- fan-fiction-transformative-culture.md, Q4 explanation: "Derivatives (fan fiction) are legally prohibited" and "original author owns the work absolutely" are overstated. The legal status of fan works is unsettled, fair use may apply, and copyright has limits.
- archive-of-our-own-participatory.md, Explainer: "millions of works created by thousands of authors" undercounts. AO3 has millions of registered users and a very large author base.
- inform-language-fiction-authoring.md, Explainer: it says Twine enables "branching narrative in a way Inform does not". Inform can build branching narratives. Twine just makes link-based branching the default.
- code-poetry-aesthetic.md, Explainer: it says the text "must be meaningful to the machine (syntactically and semantically correct)". That contradicts the same file's point that code poetry need not execute.
- constrained-writing-formal-systems.md, Explainer: "A free-verse poem has no constraints" is overstated. There is also a typo, "a intuitive".
- YAML typos (each block is still valid YAML, but the question text shows a stray quote): question strings end in `?"` in bs-johnson-typographic-experiment.md (q1), ergodic-reading-effort-interpretation.md (q1), flarf-poetry-search-engine-found-text.md (q1), generative-poetry-algorithms-text.md (q1), house-of-leaves-form-materiality.md (q0, q1), hypertext-lexia-fragmented-structure.md (q1), hypertext-theory-narrative-paths.md (q1), ian-hamilton-finlay-garden-text.md (q0, q1) and inform-language-fiction-authoring.md (q0, q1).
