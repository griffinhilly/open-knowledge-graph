## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- feudal-social-estates-stratification.md, Core Idea: "expressed in texts like Aelred of Rievaulx's writings". The classic statements of the three orders are Adalbero of Laon and Gerard of Cambrai (early 11th c.), not Aelred.
- feudal-social-estates-stratification.md, Core Idea: "despite being increasingly descriptive rather than prescriptive". Reversed; the file's own argument is that the model became increasingly prescriptive (ideological) and less descriptive.
- feudal-social-estates-stratification.md / medieval-early-middle-ages-periodization.md Q3: "Dark Ages" bias attributed to "19th-century ... Renaissance and Enlightenment scholars". Inconsistent; the term originates with Petrarch and Renaissance humanists (14th c.).
- classical-knowledge-preservation-transmission.md, Explainer: "Cassiodorus and Boethius had been crucial bridges, translating Aristotle's logic into Latin". Boethius translated the logic; Cassiodorus did not. Also "the largest organized translation effort the ancient world had seen" for an 8th-10th c. movement.
- classical-knowledge-preservation-transmission.md and byzantine-preservation-classical-texts.md Core Idea: refugees after 1453 "igniting"/"sparked" the Renaissance. The Italian Renaissance and Greek teaching in Italy (e.g. Chrysoloras, 1397) predate 1453; the byzantine-preservation Explainer itself says emigres came earlier.
- crusade-movement-motivations.md, Explainer: younger-sons/primogeniture as a main crusading motive is contested in modern scholarship (crusading was very costly; many leaders were eldest sons or lords). Also Crusader-state contact presented as "a significant channel" for Greek texts; Spain and Sicily were the main channels. Q4 explanation "ONLY major crusade to achieve its primary objective" ignores Frederick II's recovery of Jerusalem by treaty in 1229. "holy war inherited from Augustine": Augustine wrote on just war, not holy war.
- feudal-decline-late-medieval.md, Q2 explanation: "Option B is incorrect: the Church's position..." The Church option is C (index 2). Core Idea: the Wars of the Roses did not demonstrate cavalry's obsolescence.
- late-antiquity-medieval-transition.md, Q2 explanation: "Option B reverses the chronology". The chronology option is C (index 2); B is the correct answer.
- feudal-land-tenure-fiefs.md: Core Idea "transformed land from a commodity to be bought and sold into a hereditary holding" is confused/backwards. Q1 explanation calls normal reversion at death "escheat"; escheat is reversion for lack of heir or felony. Q5 answer "it became politically awkward and then customary to deny a son his father's land" says the opposite of what is meant.
- medieval-church-monastic-reform.md, Explainer: lords using monastic offices to reward allies is called "simony"; simony is the buying or selling of church offices or spiritual goods. Q4 explanation reduces Luther to a corruption critique; his doctrine of justification was new.
- medieval-cluniac-cistercian-orders.md: Cluny's 910 charter put it under papal protection; full exemption from episcopal jurisdiction came later (11th c.). Wool-export wealth dated "by the 12th century" here but "by the 13th century" in medieval-church-monastic-reform.md. Q2 keyed answer (labor surpluses) over donations is debatable; Cistercians also received large donations.
- investiture-controversy.md / church-secular-power-tensions.md: spiritual investiture under the Concordat of Worms is said to be "performed by the Pope". It was performed by Church authorities (consecrating bishops), not personally by the pope. investiture-controversy Explainer: Gregory's doctrine stated as "only the Pope could invest bishops"; he banned lay investiture. Also ties Gregorian reform to "centered at Cluny", which is contested.
- medieval-cosmology-aristotelian-universe.md: Copernicus presented as achieving "equivalent accuracy with greater mathematical simplicity". His system still used epicycles and was not clearly simpler or more accurate; Kepler, telescopic evidence and Newton did more to tip the balance. "Internally consistent" Aristotelian-Ptolemaic system glosses the known conflict between Aristotle's spheres and Ptolemy's epicycles.
- medieval-diplomacy-statecraft.md: resident ambassadors dated to 13th-14th c. Italian states; the standard date is the 15th century (some 14th-c. precursors). "When a Carolingian princess married a Frankish rival's son" (Carolingians were Franks).
- medieval-charlemagne-empire-expansion.md and medieval-feudal-fragmentation-power.md: the division "after his death" at Verdun (843) skips Louis the Pious, who ruled the whole empire 814-840.
- feudal-decline-late-medieval-centralization.md, Explainer: Ferdinand and Isabella "expelled Muslims and Jews, and created a unified bureaucratic monarchy". Jews were expelled in 1492, but forced conversion or expulsion of Muslims came 1502 (Castile) and 1520s (Aragon); Castile and Aragon kept separate institutions.
- heresy-medieval-religious-dissent.md: Q2 and Q4 explanations quote "The Explainer notes/states", which is meta text in learner-facing content. Torture in inquisitorial procedure was authorized in 1252 (Ad extirpanda), not with the 1231 formalization. "first crusade directed against Christians" is debatable (e.g. the 1204 sack of Constantinople came earlier, though not called against Christians).
- hunting-forest-management-medieval.md: Q2 states as settled that lordly forest control prevented deforestation (Q3 hedges it). Commoners held customary rights in many forests, so "monopolization" is overstated.
- manorial-system-village-agriculture.md, Q5 answer: "no land market". Peasant land markets are well documented in 13th-c. England. The fair-shares explanation for scattered strips is one of several debated theories.
- medieval-english-monarchy-development.md, Q1 explanation: the "no free man" clause "applied primarily to the nobility". "Free man" covered free non-nobles too (unfree villeins were excluded).
- castle-architecture-and-fortification.md: "castles were built before roads and towns" is doubtful. "Enfilading fire" is used to mean inner walls firing over outer walls; enfilade is fire along the length of a line (the flanking-tower point is closer). Q5 dates bastion forts to the "late medieval period"; they are 15th-16th c. early modern.
- chivalry-code-and-practice.md, Core Idea: the Song of Roland (c. 1100) is cited as romanticizing the courtly ideal "by the 12th century"; it is a martial epic, not a courtly romance.
- islamic-medieval-science-and-medicine.md, Core Idea: al-Ghazali grouped with authors of "systematic medical texts, optical theories"; he was a theologian. "Million-word encyclopedia" for the Canon is unverified.
