## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- phylogenetic-inference.md (Explainer and Questions item 4 answer): "over 34 million for 10". The number of unrooted trees for 10 taxa is 15!! = 2,027,025. About 34.5 million is the count of rooted trees.
- molecular-evolution-rates.md (Questions item 4 answer): "For slowly evolving genes like histones, a universal rate would dramatically overestimate divergence times; for rapidly evolving genes like fibrinopeptides, it would underestimate them." The direction is reversed. Applying a faster average rate to a slow gene's few differences underestimates time, and the reverse holds for fast genes.
- mutation-rate-evolution.md (Explainer): "Bacteria and large-population viruses have extremely low per-base error rates". RNA viruses have very high per-base mutation rates, so "large-population viruses" is wrong as stated.
- mutation-rate-evolution.md (Questions item 0 explanation): "only down to the point where the fitness benefit of further improvement exceeds the noise introduced by genetic drift". This is inverted; the limit is where the benefit falls below drift noise. The Explainer states it correctly.
- mutation-selection-balance.md (Explainer and Questions item 1): sqrt(mu/s) = 0.001 is an allele frequency, but the text calls it a "carrier frequency" and compares it to an observed carrier frequency of about 1 in 25. The carrier frequency would be about 2q. Also, heterozygote advantage as "the leading explanation" for CF allele frequency is contested.
- mutation-selection-balance.md (Questions item 2): "will eventually eliminate it mostly from a large population" is marked false, but with "mostly" the statement is arguably true. The wording needs fixing.
- purifying-selection.md (Questions item 1 explanation): it calls drift accumulation of deleterious mutations in small populations "Muller's ratchet". Muller's ratchet refers specifically to asexual or non-recombining genomes.
- hamilton-rule.md (Explainer): "0.5 x 2 = 1 > 1... actually that is exactly equal" is a leftover self-correction. Questions item 0 explanation says first cousins share "one of eight possible shared great-grandparent alleles". First cousins share grandparents, not great-grandparents.
- hamilton-rule.md and kin-selection-theory.md (Questions item 1): both present the haplodiploidy (r = 0.75) explanation of eusociality as settled. It is widely questioned, because a worker's relatedness to brothers is 0.25 and eusociality also occurs in diploid taxa.
- hawk-dove-game.md (Questions item 1 explanation): it contains rambling self-correction ("wait... No — ... Actually"). The Core Idea says "neither pure strategy is stable" without the needed C > V condition.
- heterochrony-development-evolution.md (Explainer): "The opposite pattern, acceleration or hypermorphosis" treats acceleration (faster rate) and hypermorphosis (later offset) as one process; they are distinct. The Irish elk antler case is often explained as allometry. Snake limb reduction is mainly attributed to Hox and Shh regulatory changes, not heterochrony.
- hox-genes-body-plan.md (Core Idea): "homologs shared across all animals". Sponges and ctenophores lack Hox genes. The Explainer's "3' end ... expressed in the head" is loose, since the anterior head (forebrain) lacks Hox expression.
- major-evolutionary-innovations.md (Explainer): "The vertebrate limb is built on the same Hox-gene patterning toolkit that segments arthropod appendages" is doubtful. The shared appendage toolkit involves genes like Distal-less, not Hox segmentation of appendages.
- nearly-neutral-evolution.md (Questions item 1 explanation): "drift can fix beneficial mutations faster". Drift lowers the fixation probability of beneficial mutations. The genome-size explanation (Questions item 4) is a hypothesis presented as settled.
- slightly-deleterious-mutations.md (Explainer): "the observation that most amino acid substitutions between species are slightly deleterious led Tomoko Ohta to propose" presents a theoretical proposal as an observation. "Confirmed across many taxonomic comparisons" (also in Questions item 1) overstates mixed evidence. Questions item 2 says "the same genome change can be adaptive, neutral, or deleterious in different population contexts". Population size changes how effective selection is, not the sign of the effect.
- regulatory-evolution.md (Questions item 0 explanation): "Option C overstates the claim — coding mutations do drive some evolution". That argument fits option D, not C. Questions items 2 and 3 state that human-chimp differences are largely due to cis-regulation as established; the proportion is not established.
- reproductive-isolation.md (Explainer): "a hybrid forms with genotype aabb (or AaBb)". The F1 hybrid of aaBB x AAbb is AaBb; aabb appears only in later generations.
- recombination-evolution.md (Explainer): "recombination rates are higher near genes under strong selection" and the immune-region claim are doubtful generalizations. Questions item 2 wording ("acts cleanly on most locus") is garbled.
- polyploidy-speciation.md (Core Idea): "cannot produce viable hybrids with diploid parents". Triploid hybrids are often viable but sterile. Explainer: "30-80% of flowering plant species" is an older estimate. "Tetraploids can undergo meiosis normally" ignores the reduced fertility of many autotetraploids.
- parapatric-speciation.md (Questions item 0 explanation and Core Idea): Anthoxanthum on mine tailings is called "the textbook example of parapatric speciation". It is a case of local adaptation with partial isolation; the populations are not separate species. Questions item 2's statement ("reproductive isolation is present before geographic separation occurs") is garbled for a true-false item. Polyploidy is usually classed as sympatric, not parapatric.
- peripatric-speciation.md (Explainer and Questions item 4): it presents founder-effect speciation as well established and attributes the honeycreeper and Galapagos finch radiations to it. Founder-effect speciation is debated, and those radiations are usually explained by ecological adaptive radiation.
- punctuated-equilibrium.md (Explainer and Questions item 3): "Studies ... have found support ... establishing punctuated equilibrium as a major framework". Evidence is mixed; for example, several foraminifera series show gradual change.
- sexual-selection.md (Questions item 3 explanation): "Sexual dimorphism is a direct readout of sexual selection intensity ... not other forces". This overstates the case, since fecundity selection and ecological divergence also produce dimorphism.
- phylogenetic-inference.md (Questions item 3): it is marked true that parsimony "makes no assumptions", but its own explanation ends "assumption-light is not the same as being assumption-free".
- heterozygote-advantage.md (Questions item 4 and Explainer): it calls overdominance a "frequency-dependent dynamic". Genotype fitnesses are constant here; only marginal allele fitness depends on frequency, which may confuse it with frequency-dependent selection.
- positive-selection.md: MHC binding-site dN/dS > 1 is usually read as balancing or diversifying selection, not directional selection as the title framing implies.
