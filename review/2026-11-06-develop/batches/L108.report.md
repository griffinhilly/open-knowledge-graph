## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- noncompetitive-enzyme-inhibition.md, Core Idea: "Both Km and Vmax are reduced by equal fractional amounts." That describes uncompetitive inhibition. Pure noncompetitive inhibition leaves Km unchanged, as the file's own Explainer and questions say.
- noncompetitive-enzyme-inhibition.md, q3 explanation: "If the inhibitor bound only free enzyme and not ES ... (this is mixed inhibition)." Binding only E is not mixed inhibition. Mixed inhibition is unequal binding to E and ES.
- proton-gradient-and-chemiosmotic-coupling.md, Core Idea: "a ~4-unit pH gradient." The real gradient is about 0.5–1 unit, as the Explainer and q2 say. The Core Idea also contradicts the file's claim that the electrical term dominates.
- pyrimidine-metabolism-degradation.md, Core Idea: "either alanine (from cytosine) or methylalanine (from thymine)." The end products are β-alanine (from cytosine and uracil) and β-aminoisobutyrate (from thymine), as the Explainer says.
- pyrimidine-metabolism-degradation.md, q0 explanation: "5-FU is active as administered." 5-FU is a prodrug. It acts after conversion to FdUMP and other metabolites. The pyrimidine biosynthesis file says so itself.
- pyrimidine-metabolism-degradation.md, Explainer: cytosine is said to be freed as a base and then deaminated. In humans, deamination happens mainly at the nucleoside level (cytidine to uridine, by cytidine deaminase).
- pyrimidine-metabolism-biosynthesis.md, Core Idea: "Unlike purine synthesis (which is de novo)." Both pathways are de novo. The difference is ring-first versus sugar-first. The q4 answer's last sentence ("blocking early purine steps prevents sugar attachment") is also muddled.
- pentose-phosphate-pathway.md, Core Idea: it calls the non-oxidative phase "a reductive phase". That phase has no reduction step. The same sentence also says it converts "glucose-6-phosphate" into 3–7-carbon sugars. It acts on pentose phosphates and glycolytic intermediates, not on G6P.
- pyruvate-metabolic-hub.md, Core Idea: "carboxylated to form fatty acids and amino acids." Fatty acids come from pyruvate through PDH, acetyl-CoA and citrate export, not through carboxylation.
- receptor-signaling-pathways.md, Core Idea: "activate downstream kinases (Ras, MAPK/ERK cascade)." Ras is a GTPase, not a kinase. Explainer: "One RTK phosphorylates many Ras." RTKs activate Ras through Grb2/SOS nucleotide exchange, not by phosphorylating it.
- protein-quaternary-structure.md, q3 and Explainer: cooperativity and allostery are said to be "impossible" in single-chain proteins. That is too strong. Monomeric enzymes such as glucokinase show kinetic cooperativity, and many monomers are allosterically regulated.
- phospholipid-biosynthesis.md, q0, q4 and Explainer: phosphatidylserine is said to come from the CDP-diacylglycerol branch. That is true in bacteria and yeast. In mammals, PS is made mainly by head-group base exchange from PC and PE.
- nucleotide-synthesis.md, Explainer: "5-fluorouracil and methotrexate block this step [thymidylate synthase]." Methotrexate inhibits DHFR and affects TS only indirectly. The Core Idea's "(folate, B12)" is also loose: B12 matters only indirectly, through methionine synthase.
- oxidative-deamination.md, q0 explanation: losing GDH would block ammonia release, yet the explanation says this leads to "ammonia toxicity". That contradicts itself. Also, the Explainer says the reaction "occurs in the mitochondria of the liver". GDH is also active in kidney and brain.
- pyruvate-dehydrogenase-complex.md, q1 explanation: "This is why athletes can't run on fat alone for high-intensity exercise — fat can't be converted back to glycogen." This is a non sequitur. The limit at high intensity is the rate of fat oxidation.
- nad-nadh-structure-and-function.md, Core Idea: "low NADH/NAD+ indicates oxidative stress." A low ratio is an oxidized redox state. Oxidative stress refers to ROS, and the two are not the same.
- metabolic-hormones-and-gluconeogenesis.md, q4 explanation: "PKA ... inhibits acetyl-CoA carboxylase." ACC is inhibited mainly by AMPK phosphorylation, and the PKA effect is indirect or minor.
- post-translational-modifications.md, q3: the answer is false for "Ubiquitination typically marks a protein for destruction by the proteasome." Proteasomal targeting is arguably the typical outcome, so the item is ambiguous. Explainer: a phosphorylation cascade relays a signal "in milliseconds". That usually takes seconds to minutes.
- protein-primary-structure.md, Core Idea lists post-translational modifications as changes in primary structure. That is debatable. Also q2 (true) and the Explainer say every copy from one gene has the same sequence and fold. Alternative splicing, disordered proteins and misfolding make this too absolute.
- protein-targeting-and-subcellular-localization.md, q0 explanation: it uses I-cell disease (secretion of lysosomal enzymes) to illustrate "default destination is the cytosol". That example shows a different default, secretion. Explainer: all ER-bound proteins "begin with" an N-terminal signal. Many membrane proteins use internal signal-anchors, and tail-anchored proteins insert after translation.
- ribosome-structure-and-peptidyl-transferase.md: q1 (proteins cross-linked, catalysis "continues") overstates the evidence. In bacterial ribosomes, protein L27 approaches the PTC. The claim that rRNA deprotonates the α-amino group is contested; positioning and the P-site tRNA 2'-OH are the current emphasis. Erythromycin binds the exit tunnel next to the PTC.
- purine-metabolism-degradation.md, q4 explanation: "keeping peripheral joints warm can reduce attack frequency" and the claim about nighttime temperature are unsupported clinical claims. q0 says xanthine is "more soluble". Hypoxanthine is, but xanthine is itself poorly soluble, and xanthine stones can occur.
- purine-metabolism-biosynthesis.md, Explainer: GMP synthesis is said to need ATP "via IMP dehydrogenase". The ATP is used by GMP synthetase (XMP to GMP), not by IMPDH.
- phenylketonuria-and-metabolic-disease.md, Core Idea, q1 and q2: LNAA competition at the blood-brain barrier is presented as the settled primary cause of brain damage. The mechanism is multifactorial and partly unresolved. The Core Idea also implies that lower serotonin causes light skin; the cause is low tyrosine for melanin.
- nucleotide-salvage-pathways.md, q0 and Explainer: neuronal purine starvation is presented as the established cause of Lesch-Nyhan neurology. The mechanism is not settled, and dopaminergic dysfunction is implicated.
- protein-denaturation-and-renaturation.md, q4 explanation: prion and amyloid states are said to persist "despite having sequences that thermodynamically favor a different native state". Amyloid can be lower in free energy than the native state at high concentration.
- one-carbon-metabolism.md, q0: "symptoms identical to folate deficiency." B12 deficiency also causes neurological symptoms, as q2 says. Its "Folate supplementation alone cannot fix B₁₂ deficiency" needs a qualifier: folate corrects the anemia but not the nerve damage.
