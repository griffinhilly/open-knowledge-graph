## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- exaptation.md (Explainer and q3 explanation): "Swim bladders in fish, originally selected for buoyancy control, were co-opted in the lineage leading to tetrapods as primitive lungs." The direction is reversed. Lungs are ancestral in bony fishes, and the ray-finned swim bladder came from them. The same lines say "The bones of the mammalian middle ear (malleus, incus, stapes) evolved from jaw bones in reptilian ancestors." Only the malleus and incus come from jaw-joint bones (articular, quadrate). The stapes comes from the hyomandibula and was already an ear bone. The ancestors were synapsids, not reptiles. q3 explanation also says "The Explainer makes clear", which refers to the page itself. The Core Idea states the thermoregulation origin of feathers as fact; it is one hypothesis (display is another).
- convergent-evolution.md (q0 explanation and Explainer): sharks, dolphins and ichthyosaurs "last shared a common ancestor ... long before any of them entered the water." Their common ancestor was an aquatic jawed vertebrate. Dolphins and ichthyosaurs returned to the water.
- directional-stabilizing-selection.md (q0 stem): "mice with very dark fur absorb less heat." Dark fur absorbs more solar heat. Also q4 and the Explainer call stabilizing selection "the most common mode in nature". This is contested: field meta-analyses detect directional selection at least as often.
- directional-selection.md (q0 correct option and Explainer): "most copies are hidden in heterozygotes" is applied to a bacterial population. Bacteria are haploid.
- bayesian-phylogenetics.md (q1 explanation and Explainer): "for 50 species, more [unrooted trees] than the number of atoms in the observable universe." (2n−5)!! at n = 50 is about 2.8×10^74, below the usual ~10^80 estimate for atoms. The Explainer also says MCMC "accepts or rejects each proposal based on whether it increases the posterior probability." Metropolis-Hastings also accepts some downhill moves, with probability equal to the posterior ratio. My How section uses the correct rule.
- developmental-constraints.md (q0 stem): "vertebrates have never evolved a six-fingered standard limb." Early tetrapods had more than five digits (Acanthostega 8, Ichthyostega 7, Tulerpeton 6). The Explainer also states as settled that insects never evolved lungs because their "developmental architecture ... cannot produce them". That is a hypothesis.
- evolutionary-constraints.md (q0 explanation): limbs "established four limbs as the basic tetrapod blueprint over 500 million years ago." Tetrapod limbs arose in the Late Devonian, roughly 375–360 million years ago. Also inconsistent: q3 says the giraffe nerve detour is "up to a meter", while the Explainer says "meters".
- evolutionary-game-theory.md (Explainer): "neither pure strategy dominates, and the population settles at a mixed equilibrium". This holds only when C > V; when V ≥ C, pure Hawk is the ESS. q4 explanation says "tit-for-tat is stable in iterated games". TFT is not strictly an ESS, because unconditional cooperators can drift in.
- evolutionary-stable-strategy.md (Explainer): "so pure Hawk is not an ESS either". This holds only when C > V.
- coalescent-theory.md (q4 explanation and q0 explanation): "the coalescent is not an approximation — it is the exact probability distribution". The Kingman coalescent is a large-population approximation, valid when the sample is much smaller than Ne.
- fixation-probability.md (q3 explanation): "approximately 2s regardless of how large s is ... s = 0.20 has ~40%." The 2s rule holds only for small s; for s = 0.2 the better approximation 1 − e^(−2s) gives about 0.33. The Explainer says diffusion models yield "exact solutions"; they are approximations.
- efficacy-selection-finite-populations.md (q0 explanation): at 2Ne|s| = 2, "drift could easily fix it". At that value the fixation probability is only about 7.5% of the neutral value. The Explainer's "Most mutations in protein-coding genes are mildly deleterious" overstates; the distribution includes many strongly deleterious mutations.
- codon-bias-and-selection.md (q1 explanation and Explainer): s is given as "~10⁻⁶ to 10⁻⁸" with the rule s > 1/Ne at Ne ~10⁶. At 10⁻⁸ selection would be ineffective even in Drosophila, so the range contradicts the argument. The Core Idea attributes codon bias to selection only and omits mutational (GC) bias. The q4 answer ("not random drift") overclaims, because codon optimization shows an effect on translation, not the genome-wide cause.
- allopatric-speciation.md (Explainer and q2 explanation): "smaller populations diverge faster through drift" and "the same barrier duration produces more speciation in small populations". The neutral substitution rate does not depend on population size, so this overstates the case.
- altruism-evolution.md (q1, q2, Explainer): the argument from r = 0.75 to sisters ignores brothers (r = 0.25) and queens that mate several times. "Shares 75% of her genes" confuses relatedness with genes shared. The haplodiploidy hypothesis is generally considered insufficient.
- adaptive-landscape-crossing.md (q0 explanation): "Increasing mutation rate ... does not help". This ignores stochastic tunneling: large populations with enough mutation can cross valleys without fixing the intermediate.
- adaptive-radiation-molecular-basis.md (Explainer): "duplications in opsin genes allowed different species to tune their color vision ... in Lake Victoria." The cichlid opsin duplications predate the radiation. Differences among Lake Victoria species are mainly allelic or in expression. "Mutations that would otherwise be lethal" overstates; "deleterious" is accurate. The olfactory-receptor claim reads as speculative.
- adaptive-radiation.md (q3 explanation): "sometimes called a 'pulled-down' tree shape" is not a standard term, and "pull of the present" is a different artifact. The q1 and q3 explanations present early bursts as typical, but comparative studies find them less common than claimed. The q4 explanation ("finches existed for millions of years on continents ... Genetics was identical") is unsupported.
- chromosomal-evolution.md (q1 stem, q0 explanation): inversion heterozygotes are said to "typically show reduced fertility". In many taxa (for example Drosophila females with paracentric inversions), unbalanced products go to polar bodies and fertility loss is small. q2: Robertsonian fusions can lose short-arm material.
- effective-recombination-rate.md (Explainer): a sweep "locally eliminates diversity and linkage disequilibrium". This contradicts q1's explanation that sweeps create strong LD.
- balancing-selection.md (q3 explanation): trans-species polymorphism "impossible under neutral evolution". It is very unlikely, not impossible.
- evolutionary-trade-offs.md (q2 explanation): "There is no evolutionary pathway to simultaneously maximizing both". Genetic correlations can themselves evolve, and variation in resource acquisition can produce positive correlations.
- evolvability.md (q4 explanation): conflates the quasispecies "error threshold" with "mutational meltdown", which is a small-population process.
- extinction-and-recovery.md (q3 explanation): current rates are "consistent with the scale of past mass extinction events". Rates are elevated, but the total fraction of species lost is so far far below the 75% that defines past mass extinctions.
- gene-flow-migration.md (q2 explanation): the stem says "a few migrants per generation". This is the Nm ≈ 1 result against drift, but the explanation argues from m versus s. "Selection strengths rarely exceed a few percent per generation" is questionable; strong selection is often measured in the field.
- genealogical-methods-phylogenetics.md (q1 explanation): "For distantly related taxa ... gene trees and species trees are effectively identical". This fails for rapid ancient radiations with short internal branches.
- evo-developmental-modules.md (q4 explanation): segmentation in arthropods and vertebrates is offered as co-option of the same modules. That homology is contested.
