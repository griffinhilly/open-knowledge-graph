## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- african-independence-wave.md, Core Idea: "Between 1960 and the early 1970s, virtually all sub-Saharan Africa gained independence". Portugal's colonies became independent in 1974–75, Zimbabwe in 1980, Namibia in 1990. My new bullet names 1974–75 for Portugal's colonies, which sits awkwardly beside "early 1970s".
- african-independence-wave.md, Explainer: "Ghana ... the first sub-Saharan African nation to do so". Sudan became independent in 1956; the "first" claim needs "colony in West Africa" or similar qualification.
- african-independence-wave.md, imperialism-european-expansion-africa.md, decolonization-independence-movements.md (Q1 explanation): "borders drawn at the Berlin Conference". The 1884–85 conference set rules for claims; most colonial borders were fixed in later bilateral treaties. This is a standard misconception that the corpus repeats.
- algerian-war-colonial-violence.md, Core Idea and Q2: "an estimated one million Algerian deaths". This is Algeria's official figure; many historians give much lower estimates (often a few hundred thousand). Should be presented as contested.
- anarchism-no-state.md, Q0 explanation: "Bakunin's prediction ... was, he felt, vindicated by 20th-century socialist regimes". Bakunin died in 1876.
- anticolonial-liberation.md, Q1: attributes the term "neocolonialism" to Fanon and Cabral; it is most associated with Nkrumah (1965 book). Explainer/Q2 also say Ho Chi Minh studied in European universities; he lived and worked in France and trained in Moscow, not at a European university in the usual sense.
- berlin-blockade-crisis.md, Q3 explanation: "NATO was created the same month the blockade ended". NATO was founded 4 April 1949; the blockade was lifted 12 May 1949. Explainer "around 200,000 flights" is low (common figure is about 278,000). Q0 explanation cites "nuclear stakes" for Soviet restraint, but the USSR had no bomb until August 1949.
- chinese-communist-revolution.md, Explainer: "600 million people and a nuclear-armed state". China's first nuclear test was in 1964. Core Idea "Led by Mao ... (1927–1949)" overstates his leadership before the mid-1930s.
- communist-state-dictatorship.md, Explainer: "Collectivization ... forcing peasants onto state farms". Most peasants were forced into collective farms (kolkhozy), not state farms (sovkhozy).
- conservative-traditionalism.md: "Burke himself supported the American Revolution" (Explainer, Q1, Q2) oversimplifies; Burke opposed the government's coercive policy and urged conciliation. Q3 says Burke's defense of hierarchy was "not primarily theological"; Burke gave religion and the established church a large role, so this is at least contestable. The plant-versus-clock analogy is presented as Burke's own; I could not confirm it.
- cuban-missile-crisis.md, Explainer and Q1: "a U-2 ... was shot down over Cuba without Kennedy's authorization". It was shot down by Soviet forces, reportedly without Moscow's authorization; Kennedy's authorization is irrelevant. Explainer: "strayed into Soviet airspace in Alaska" should read "from Alaska".
- d-day-normandy-amphibious-invasion.md, Explainer: "where Allied armies stopped became the boundary between Western and Soviet spheres". Occupation zones were agreed in advance; U.S. forces withdrew from parts of the Soviet zone in 1945.
- economic-imperialism.md, Explainer: "Nkrumah coined the term neo-colonialism in 1965". The term was in use earlier (for example, at the 1961 All-African Peoples' Conference); Nkrumah's 1965 book popularized it. Explainer's "a barrel of oil ... bought fewer manufactured goods" is a poor example given the oil price surges of the 1970s. Q3 option text says "generally" but the explanation rebuts "always". Core Idea states neo-colonial exploitation as settled fact; this is a contested thesis.
- environmental-movement.md, Explainer: "the first photographs of Earth from space (1968)". Earthrise was 1968; earlier photos of Earth from space exist (1946 rocket images, 1966 Lunar Orbiter). Also "a new legal concept: that the environment had rights" overstates the 1970s U.S. laws. "Teddy Roosevelt's national park system": the National Park Service was created in 1916 under Wilson.
- fascism-and-authoritarianism-rise.md, Explainer and Q1: antisemitism and violence "became visible as the regimes radicalized". Squadristi and SA violence was highly visible before either party took power. Attributing this reading to Arendt is loose. Q3 "assigned sole war guilt": Article 231 does not use the word "sole".
- fascism-ethno-state.md, Q1 explanation: "fascism won elections and referenda before it consolidated authoritarian control". The Nazis never won a majority in a free election; Mussolini's 1924 majority came under the Acerbo law and violence. Q2 presents ethnic/racial hierarchy as core to all fascism; scholars disagree.
- global-social-movements-1968.md, Explainer: "installing a decade of 'normalization'". Normalization lasted until 1989. Tlatelolco "hundreds" killed: the death toll is disputed; estimates vary widely.
- globalization-economic-integration.md, Explainer: "rare earths mined in the Democratic Republic of Congo". The DRC is a major source of cobalt; rare earths come mostly from China. Q4 states contested voting-behavior findings (backlash voters "disproportionately" from deindustrialized areas) without hedging.
- holocaust-systematized-genocide.md, Explainer: "Gas chambers using Zyklon B" follows a list that includes Treblinka, Sobibór, Belzec and Chełmno, which used carbon monoxide. Zyklon B was used at Auschwitz and Majdanek. "Over 1.5 million Jews" killed by Einsatzgruppen by January 1942 may be high for that date. Q1 says Arendt's concept came from "observing Nazi war crimes trials"; it came from the 1961 Eichmann trial specifically. Core Idea lists political prisoners among genocide victims; they were persecuted but are not usually counted within the genocide.
- imperialism-asia-opium-wars.md: Core Idea "British imperial expansion in Asia focused on China"; Britain's Asian empire centered on India. Explainer: extraterritoriality came in the supplementary Treaty of the Bogue (1843), not the Treaty of Nanking itself. "Mao Zedong's 'Century of Humiliation' framing": the term predates Mao and was used by the Nationalists. Q4/Explainer: the East India Company sold opium at auction in Calcutta; private traders smuggled it into China.
- imperialism-european-expansion-africa.md, Explainer: "The Belgian Congo under Leopold II". Under Leopold it was the Congo Free State (1885–1908); it became the Belgian Congo in 1908. Q2 answer treats Berlin borders as "a primary cause" of postcolonial instability as settled; scholars debate this.
- indian-partition-independence.md, Explainer and Q3: "India and Pakistan have fought four wars over Kashmir". They fought four wars in total, but the 1971 war was over East Pakistan/Bangladesh. Core Idea: "over a million deaths" vs Explainer "between 200,000 and 2 million". Core Idea: "Pakistan as an Islamic republic"; it became one in 1956.
- industrial-labor-and-working-class.md, Explainer: "the Social Democratic Party became the largest political party in Europe by 1912". In 1912 it became the largest party in the Reichstag; "largest in Europe" is ambiguous.
- cold-war-bipolar-conflict.md, Q0 explanation lists U.S. intervention in "Angola and Afghanistan" alongside Korea and Vietnam; U.S. involvement in Angola and Afghanistan was covert aid, not direct military intervention.
- civil-rights-activism-american.md, Q2: answer `false` to "nonviolence chosen primarily because leaders believed violence is morally wrong". King's commitment was strongly moral as well as strategic; the either/or framing is contestable.
