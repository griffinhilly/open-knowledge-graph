## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. em-waves-in-conductors.md, Q2 explanation: "Shielding works by absorption, not just reflection", with the reflection option marked wrong. For good conductors, reflection loss from impedance mismatch is a major and often dominant part of shielding. Explainer: "Coaxial cables use a thin conducting shell rather than a solid rod because the interior is wasted". Coax inner conductors are commonly solid, and the outer shell is required by the geometry.
2. evanescent-waves-total-reflection.md, Core Idea: "When the wave vector becomes imaginary (above the cutoff frequency for the medium)". It should say below cutoff (plasma, waveguide), which is what the Explainer says. Explainer: "the requirement that k² = (n·ω/c)² forces k² to be negative". In TIR only the normal component k_z² is negative. The total k² stays positive.
3. ferromagnetism-microscopic-view.md, Explainer: "the spontaneous magnetization vanishes discontinuously". Wrong. The transition is continuous (mean field gives M ∝ (T_C − T)^½). This conflicts with magnetization-and-temperature.md.
4. field-tensor-lorentz-covariance.md, Explainer: "F^{0i} = E_i/c and F^{ij} = −ε^{ijk}B_k". The relative sign is inconsistent. With the standard boost matrix this gives E'_y = γ(E_y + vB_z), the wrong sign. Griffiths pairs F^{0i} = E_i/c with F^{ij} = +ε^{ijk}B_k. Also: "If B² > E²/c² in one frame, a boost exists in which E vanishes entirely". That also requires E·B = 0. The same omission appears in lorentz-transformations-em-fields.md, Explainer paragraph 3.
5. gauge-transformations.md, Q index 3 (true-false): the statement says the gauges "can seldom both be satisfied simultaneously" and is keyed false. The explanation then says they "can't both be imposed simultaneously ... in general". The key contradicts its own explanation.
6. group-velocity-and-dispersion.md, Q0 explanation and Explainer: "ripples travel faster than the wave group". For capillary ripples v_g > v_p. Crests outrun the group in deep-water gravity waves (v_g = v_p/2). Q1 (index 1) puts pulse broadening down to "slightly different phase velocities". Broadening comes from group-velocity dispersion, or modal dispersion in multimode fiber.
7. higher-multipole-radiation.md, Q index 4 answer: "roughly 10⁶ times longer than an E1 state — on the order of seconds to hours rather than nanoseconds". 10⁶ × ns is ms, so the numbers do not agree.
8. laplace-poisson-equations-electrostatics.md, Q0 premise: "She guesses φ = A + B·r² and verifies it satisfies ∇²φ = 0". In fact ∇²(r²) = 6, so this holds only for B = 0. Explainer: "G = 1/(4πε₀|r − r′|)" used with φ = ∫ G ρ/ε₀ dV′ counts ε₀ twice.
9. larmor-formula.md, Q index 4 explanation: "Synchrotron radiation scales as a⁴". It scales as γ⁴ at fixed radius. Same explanation: protons have "far less centripetal acceleration for the same momentum". At equal momentum and radius both particles are ultrarelativistic with a ≈ c²/R. The suppression comes from the proton's much smaller γ. Q index 3 explanation: "atoms collapse in nanoseconds". The classical estimate is about 10⁻¹¹ s.
10. lienard-potential-applications.md, Q index 2 explanation: velocity-field flux "goes as 1/r² × r² = constant over r". It should be 1/r⁴ × r² = 1/r². Explainer: "At relativistic speeds (v ≈ c), the radiation is no longer emitted isotropically". This implies slow charges radiate isotropically, but they radiate as sin²θ.
11. lienard-wiechert-potentials.md, Explainer and Q index 4: uniform motion not radiating is "consistent with the equivalence principle". It follows from Lorentz invariance (the field is static in the rest frame). Q0 and Q3 explanations say κ is "a feature of the velocity (bound) fields only". κ also appears, as κ³, in the acceleration field.
12. lorenz-gauge.md, Explainer: it defines □² = ∂²/∂t²/c² − ∇² and then writes □²φ = −ρ/ε₀ and □²A = −μ₀J. With that definition the signs should be +ρ/ε₀ and +μ₀J. Griffiths uses the opposite definition of □².
13. lorentz-covariance-em.md, Explainer paragraph 2: "∂_μ F^μν = J^ν" is missing μ₀ (paragraph 3 has it). "Every index is contracted" is also wrong, since ν is a free index (it is a 4-vector equation).
14. lorentz-transformations-em-fields.md, Core Idea and Explainer call frame mixing "electric-magnetic duality". That term usually names the symmetry E → cB, B → −E/c.
15. magnetic-dipole-radiation.md, Core Idea: "suppressed by factors of (a/c)²". This is dimensionally wrong. It should be (a/λ)² or (v/c)². The same error is in multipole-expansion-radiation.md Core Idea. Q1, keyed option: "electric dipole radiation involves static charge separation". E1 needs an oscillating dipole. Explainer attributes the green [O III] lines to "the slow quadrupole channel". I believe these lines are mainly magnetic dipole. Please verify.
16. maxwell-stress-tensor-forces.md (Explainer paragraph 2) and maxwell-stress-tensor.md (Explainer and Q index 4 answer) say T_ij "is the flux of the i-th component of momentum" and also F = ∮ T·dA. With the convention that makes F = ∮ T·dA correct (Griffiths), the momentum flux is −T. maxwell-stress-tensor-forces.md Explainer: "Electric fields push along field lines (tension)" is muddled. Field lines pull along their length (tension) and push sideways (pressure).
17. multipole-expansion-radiation.md, Q index 3: "two equal and opposite charges oscillating symmetrically about the origin has no net electric dipole moment". This is false: ±q at ±x(t) gives p = 2q x(t). Explainer: "expanding the retarded potential in powers of (a/r)". The radiation expansion is in ka. Q0 explanation and Explainer: "(ka)² = (a/λ)²" drops a factor (2π)².
18. multipole-expansion-fields.md, Explainer: "Because most neutral objects have non-zero dipole moments". This is doubtful, since atoms and many symmetric molecules have zero permanent dipole.
19. multipole-static-fields.md, Q0 and Explainer: a "perfectly symmetric" neutral atom is given a leading quadrupole term. If the distribution is spherically symmetric, every moment beyond Q vanishes, and the outside potential is zero.
20. faraday-law-advanced.md, Explainer: "A conducting loop placed anywhere in this region would experience an EMF". Only loops that encircle the solenoid do. The Q0 premise ("placed outside the solenoid") never says the loop encircles it.
21. magnetization-and-temperature.md, Core Idea: "(Tₓ - T)^β". This is a typo for T_c.
