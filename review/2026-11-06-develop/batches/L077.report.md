## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- ramsey-cass-koopmans-model.md, Q3 explanation and Explainer: "The long-run capital stock falls, not rises" / "A permanent increase in government spending crowds out capital accumulation because households, facing lower lifetime wealth, reduce saving." In the standard RCK model with lump-sum financing, steady-state k* is pinned by f'(k*) = rho + delta (the modified golden rule) and does not change; consumption falls one-for-one with G. The true-false answer (false) is right, but the stated reason is wrong.
- ramsey-cass-koopmans-model.md, Q4 explanation: "push capital past the golden rule, where the net marginal product of capital falls below zero". Past the golden rule, the MPK net of depreciation falls below n (+g), not below zero.
- phillips-curve-new-keynesian.md, Q2 explanation and Explainer: a supply shock "raises marginal costs directly, pushing inflation up through the kappa x_t term ... the output gap may turn negative even as inflation rises." This is internally inconsistent: if kappa x_t pushes inflation up, x_t is positive. In the baseline NKPC with x measured from flexible-price output, stagflation needs a cost-push term (or a wedge between natural and efficient output).
- wage-dynamics-labor-frictions.md, Core Idea, Q1, Q5 and Explainer: says Nash bargaining explains downward wage stickiness. In the standard DMP model, period-by-period Nash bargaining makes wages quite flexible; that is the source of the well-known unemployment-volatility puzzle. Sticky wages need departures such as wage norms or alternating-offer bargaining. Also, with efficient bargaining, wage rigidity inside the bargaining set does not cause separations, so "firms ... laying off marginal workers" because of sticky wages does not follow from the model as described.
- wage-setting-equilibrium.md, Core Idea: "the firm's productivity minus its hiring cost" and "increase workers' bargaining power". The standard Pissarides wage is w = (1-beta)b + beta(p + c*theta), with plus c*theta. Bargaining power beta is a fixed parameter; tightness raises wages through outside options, as the file's own Q1 explanation says. Q2 option 1 (the keyed answer) repeats "productivity minus its vacancy-posting cost". Also, Q1, Q4, Q5 and the Explainer say the firm's outside option worsens in a tight market; under free entry the value of a vacancy stays at zero, and tightness enters through the worker's outside option (the hiring cost the firm saves by agreeing).
- search-matching-unemployment.md, Q1 explanation: attributes the benefit effect to workers "holding out longer" and says option D "misidentifies which side of the market is affected". In the baseline Pissarides model every meeting forms a match; higher benefits raise the bargained wage, so firms post fewer vacancies. The vacancy channel is the main one.
- romer-growth-model.md, Q1 explanation: "doubling all rival inputs (labor, capital) while holding the stock of knowledge constant more than doubles output". By the replication argument, it exactly doubles output; output more than doubles only if knowledge also doubles. Core Idea also mentions "improve existing ones" and "duplication", which belong to quality-ladder models rather than the Romer (1990) expanding-variety model.
- romer-growth-model-research.md, Q3 explanation: "the exogenous rate of technological progress (n + g)". The technology growth rate is g; n + g is aggregate output growth. Q4 explanation has the same mix-up.
- neoclassical-growth-steady-state.md, Core Idea: "a steady state where capital stock is constant". Capital per effective worker is constant; the total capital stock grows at n + g (the file's own Q4 says this). Also the file has two `## Questions` YAML blocks.
- overlapping-generations-models.md, Q2 explanation: "reducing the capital stock ... would actually increase per-capita output". Lowering capital lowers output; it raises consumption, because less output is needed for break-even investment.
- steady-state-analysis-growth.md, Explainer: off-saddle paths lead to "zero consumption or infinite capital". With diminishing returns, capital rises toward a finite maximum (where f(k) = delta k), not infinity.
- real-business-cycle-theory.md, Explainer: "Stable eigenvalues inside the unit circle generate the hump-shaped impulse responses that RBC models use to match GDP". The baseline RBC model is known for not producing hump-shaped output responses; output largely mirrors the TFP process.
- technology-shocks-rbc.md, Q3 explanation and Explainer: capital accumulation as "the internal amplifier that converts a transient productivity improvement into a prolonged expansion". This overstates it; the baseline model's internal propagation is weak, and most output persistence comes from the assumed TFP persistence.
- quantitative-easing-unconventional-policy.md, Q1 explanation says option B (signaling) needs portfolio frictions, and Q4 says QE's impact "hinges entirely" on imperfect substitutability. The signaling channel works through expectations of the policy-rate path and does not need imperfect substitutability. Q3 explanation, "bond sellers receive reserves credited to their bank accounts at the central bank": non-bank sellers receive bank deposits, and their banks receive the reserves.
- zero-lower-bound-monetary-policy.md, Core Idea: "cannot make lending less attractive". Rate cuts make saving less attractive and borrowing cheaper; the wording is muddled.
