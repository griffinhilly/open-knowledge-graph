## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- multidimensional-poverty-measurement.md, Q0 explanation: "This household's deprivations in water, cooking fuel, and education likely clear the breadth threshold." Under global MPI weights (water 1/18, cooking fuel 1/18, years of schooling 1/6), the score is 5/18 ≈ 0.28, below the 1/3 cutoff. So the household would NOT be MPI-poor, and keyed answer 2 is wrong as written. The new How section works this case correctly, which makes the conflict visible. Suggest changing the scenario (e.g., add a nutrition deprivation) or the key.
- multidimensional-poverty-measurement.md, Q0 and Explainer: "$2.15/day" international poverty line. The World Bank moved to $3.00/day (2021 PPP) in mid-2025; the figure may be out of date. Explainer: "India's deprivations concentrate in nutrition and sanitation, while the DRC's concentrate in education and electricity". Unverified; DRC is deprived across most indicators.
- labor-migration-development.md, Q3 (true-false, answer false): "migration will slow and stop once urban unemployment rises high enough to equalize the expected urban wage with the rural wage." That is the Harris-Todaro equilibrium condition, so the statement is essentially true. The explanation contradicts itself ("migration continues until EXPECTED urban wages equal rural wages"). Answer should likely be true, or the statement reworded.
- inequality-in-developing-economies.md, Explainer: "Latin American countries like Brazil and South Africa". South Africa is not in Latin America.
- informal-financial-sectors.md, Explainer: "Sub-Saharan Africa (where they are called chit funds, tontines, or susus)". Chit funds are South Asian (India); tontines and susus are African/Caribbean names. Also "ROSCAs ... charge no interest" holds for basic ROSCAs; bidding ROSCAs carry implicit interest.
- human-capital-accumulation-development.md, Q1 explanation: "CCTs work on the supply side of investment, not the demand side." CCTs are standard demand-side interventions; the sentence inverts the usual terms. Q3 (false) and Q1 also state that families "consistently" know the returns to schooling; some evidence (e.g., Dominican Republic information experiment) found students underestimated returns and information raised schooling. Overstated.
- randomized-experiments-development.md, Q0: "A deworming RCT in Kenya found a 25% increase in school attendance." The Kenya study is usually reported as reducing absenteeism by about a quarter (roughly 7 percentage points more attendance, which school-attendance-and-incentives.md states). The two files are inconsistent.
- mobile-technology-and-leapfrogging.md, Explainer: M-Pesa poverty reduction "primarily by enabling better risk-sharing". The study behind the 2% figure attributes the effect mainly to changes in saving and occupational choice (notably women moving out of farming); risk-sharing was a separate earlier finding.
- technology-adoption-developing-countries.md, Q1: Green Revolution seeds "roughly tripled yields in South Asian countries". Likely overstated; national cereal yields rose substantially (roughly doubled over decades), not tripled.
- technology-adoption-constraints.md, Q2 explanation: "The same 'tradition-bound' farmers adopt eagerly once the rational constraints are addressed." Overstates the evidence; several field experiments find modest uptake even with subsidies or information. Also uses "poverty trap dynamic" for plain risk aversion.
- structural-transformation-economic-development.md, Q3 and Explainer: "If agricultural productivity stays low ... There is no labor surplus." This conflicts with the Lewis model file, where surplus labor exists because farm marginal product is near zero. It also ignores food imports in an open economy. Worth reconciling across the two topics.
- inequality-kuznets-curve.md, Q0 explanation: "The Kuznets curve was derived from mid-20th-century cross-sectional comparisons". Kuznets (1955) relied mostly on limited time series for the US, UK, and Germany; the cross-country evidence came later. Imprecise.
- health-and-economic-development.md, Core Idea and Q0/Q3: "a 1% increase in life expectancy correlates with 0.3–0.4% long-run growth". Source and units unclear (common estimates are stated per added year of life expectancy, as a percent change in output). Unverified.
- labor-markets-developing-economies.md, Q1 explanation: "banks use formal employment as collateral for loans". Formal employment helps as proof of income, not as collateral.
