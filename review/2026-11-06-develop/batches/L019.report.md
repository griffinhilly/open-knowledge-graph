## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:

1. alphafold-and-ml-prediction.md, Explainer: "for proteins without evolutionary homologs (de novo designed proteins, orphan sequences), the MSA provides no useful information, and prediction accuracy degrades." Also Q2 explanation point (4) lists "de novo designed proteins" as lacking what AlphaFold relies on. De novo designed proteins are generally predicted accurately by AlphaFold2, often even from a single sequence. The claim holds for natural orphan proteins, not designed ones.
2. diffraction-and-fourier-transforms.md, Q0 explanation: "A crystal with a larger unit cell has more closely spaced lattice planes (more d-values), which according to Bragg's law diffract at more closely spaced angles." A larger cell has larger d-spacings for its low-index planes; the correct point is that reciprocal-lattice points (spots) are closer together. The phrase "more closely spaced lattice planes" is confused.
3. diffraction-and-fourier-transforms.md, Explainer last sentence: refinement improves the model "until the calculated diffraction pattern matches the observed pattern to within experimental error." Protein R-factors of ~0.15-0.25 are well above experimental error; overstated.
4. macromolecular-assemblies.md, Explainer: "proteasomes use dihedral symmetry (two stacked rings of 7 subunits each)." The 20S core particle is four stacked heptameric rings (alpha7-beta7-beta7-alpha7) with D7 symmetry.
5. macromolecular-assemblies.md, Q0 option and Explainer: ribosome "~2.5 MDa, ~80 proteins + 3-4 rRNAs" / "~2.5 MDa, 80+ components". This mixes the bacterial 70S (~2.5 MDa, ~55 proteins, 3 rRNAs) with the eukaryotic 80S (~4 MDa or more, ~80 proteins, 4 rRNAs).
6. molecular-dynamics-simulations.md, Core Idea: "Modern simulations routinely reach microsecond to millisecond timescales." Microseconds are routine; milliseconds are reached mainly on special hardware (Anton) and are not routine. Explainer also says simulations "reveal the catalytic mechanism", which classical MD cannot do for bond-making steps (contradicts the file's own Q1 explanation).
7. intrinsically-disordered-proteins.md, Q2 answer: "structured proteins with the same interaction capacity would crystallize rather than forming liquid phases." Folded multivalent modules (e.g. SH3 domains with proline-rich partners) do phase separate; overstated.
8. nucleic-acid-structures.md, Q0 explanation last sentence: "the wider major groove of A-form RNA is less accessible." The A-form major groove is narrow and deep (as the file's own option text states); "wider" is wrong.
9. nucleic-acid-structures.md, Explainer final paragraph opens with protein-nucleic acid structural biology "revolutionized by cryo-EM" and then cites the 2009 ribosome Nobel, which was for crystallography. Misleading juxtaposition.
10. nmr-for-proteins.md, Q1 explanation and noesy-and-distance-constraints.md, Explainer: ensemble spread is equated with genuine flexibility ("regions that vary (high RMSD) are genuinely flexible"; convergence "directly reveals ... which are flexible"). Ensemble spread mainly reflects restraint density; flexibility must be measured with relaxation data.
11. structure-validation-model-quality.md, Q2 explanation: "a 2019 study found that roughly 10% of ligand-bound structures in the PDB have significant problems." Unsourced, possibly invented figure; please verify or remove.
12. structure-validation-model-quality.md, Explainer: R-factor defined as "the fractional difference between the observed diffraction intensities and those calculated." R is computed on structure-factor amplitudes, not intensities.
13. mass-spectrometry-structural.md, Explainer: structural MS works "with no size limit"; and CID reveals "which are core (released last)". Overstated; CID typically ejects single (often peripheral) subunits and does not cleanly release a core.
14. time-resolved-structural-methods.md, Explainer: "the pump laser illuminates the crystal stream microseconds to seconds before the XFEL pulse", which conflicts with the same file's femtosecond pump-probe delays. Q3 explanation also merges Nango 2016 (ns-ms) and Nogly 2018 (fs-ps) into "13 time points from femtoseconds to milliseconds"; the 13 points belong to Nango's ns-ms series.
15. homology-modeling.md, Explainer: "AlphaFold's multi-sequence alignment input is effectively an automated version of homology modeling's template search." MSAs and structural templates are separate inputs to AlphaFold2; loose analogy.
16. crystallographic-symmetry-space-groups.md, Core Idea: unit cell described as "the smallest box that tiles space by translation." The conventional (e.g. C-centered) cell is not the smallest; the primitive cell is. Minor.
17. x-ray-crystallography.md, Explainer: "engine of structural biology for nearly a century." Protein crystallography dates from the late 1950s (about 70 years). Minor.
