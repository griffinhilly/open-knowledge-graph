## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- bacterial-flagella-and-chemotaxis.md, Core Idea: "tumbling (counterclockwise rotation) for random reorientation and smooth runs (clockwise)". Reversed. In E. coli, CCW gives runs and CW gives tumbles; the file's own Explainer and questions say so.
- flagellar-motor-rotation.md, Core Idea: "a rotor (membrane-embedded proteins) and stator (FliG, FliM, FliN proteins)". Swapped. FliG/FliM/FliN form the rotor C ring; MotA/MotB are the stator. The Explainer has it right.
- flagellar-motor-rotation.md, Explainer: principles that "have no equivalent in the eukaryotic motor protein repertoire" / "far exceeding any human-engineered rotary motor". Mitochondrial F1Fo ATP synthase is an ion-driven rotary motor found in eukaryotes. The efficiency claim is overstated.
- bacterial-flagella-pili-motility-adhesion.md, Q0 explanation: "rotation of the rotor at up to 1,000 rpm". Should be revolutions per second (the Explainer says 1,000 rps).
- antimicrobial-agents-and-mechanisms-of-action.md, Q3 explanation and Explainer: azoles target lanosterol 14α-demethylase, "which is present in fungi but not in mammals" / "targets absent in human cells". Humans have this enzyme (CYP51) for cholesterol synthesis. Selectivity comes from different affinity, as the same explanation goes on to say.
- antibiotic-targets-and-resistance-development.md, Explainer: "Folate synthesis is absent in humans ..., making the enzymes dihydropteroate synthase and dihydrofolate reductase vulnerable". Humans have DHFR. Trimethoprim is selective for the bacterial form of the enzyme.
- fungal-dimorphism-morphology.md (Explainer), fungal-pathogenesis-and-mycosis.md (Explainer), fungal-reproduction-and-life-cycles.md (Q0 explanation): Coccidioides is listed as converting to a yeast at 37°C. In tissue it forms spherules with endospores, not yeast.
- fungal-dimorphism-morphology.md, Q1/Q4 and Explainer: "α-glucan replaces β-glucan". In Histoplasma yeast, an outer α-1,3-glucan layer masks β-glucan. It does not replace it.
- fungal-cell-wall-polysaccharides.md, Explainer: "Echinocandins have no effect on human cells because we do not synthesize glucans." Humans make glycogen, an α-glucan. The claim should say β-1,3-glucan.
- fermentation-pathways-and-end-products.md, Core Idea: mixed-acid fermentation produces "butyrate, methane". Methane comes from methanogenic archaea, not mixed-acid fermentation. Butyrate is from butyric (Clostridium) fermentation. The Explainer also calls Clostridium a mixed-acid fermenter.
- fermentation-pathways-and-end-products.md, Q0 explanation and Explainer: "Fermentation itself produces no additional ATP" and "sole biochemical purpose is NAD+ regeneration". This is true for lactate and ethanol fermentation. It is false for mixed-acid and butyrate fermentations, where acetate kinase / butyrate kinase make extra ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation.
- bacterial-ribosomes-and-translation-prokaryotes.md, Q1, Q4 and Explainer: mitochondria "contain 70S ribosomes". Mammalian mitoribosomes sediment at about 55S. They are bacterial-like, but not 70S. Separately, Core Idea says tetracycline binds "prokaryotic but not eukaryotic ribosomes". Tetracycline can bind eukaryotic ribosomes; much of its selectivity comes from active uptake into bacteria.
- bacterial-ribosomes-and-translation-prokaryotes.md, Q1 explanation: attributes aminoglycoside nephrotoxicity to mitochondrial ribosome damage. Kidney toxicity is largely driven by proximal-tubule uptake and accumulation of the drug. The mitochondrial mechanism is mainly cited for ototoxicity.
- antimicrobial-susceptibility-testing.md, Q0: ranks susceptibility to two different antibiotics by raw zone diameter (28 mm vs 12 mm). Zone sizes are not comparable across drugs, because diffusion and breakpoints differ for each drug. Each zone has to be read against that drug's own chart. Explainer also says AST "determine[s] which of those resistance mechanisms are actually present". AST measures phenotype, not mechanism.
- antibiotic-resistance-mechanisms-and-evolution.md (Q2 explanation) and antibiotic-resistance-mutations-downregulation.md (Q0 explanation, Explainer): "Antibiotics do not cause resistance mutations". This is overstated. Fluoroquinolones and other drugs can raise mutation rates through SOS-induced error-prone repair. Q0 of the mutations file marks that option wrong, and the file's own Q2 note admits this. The selection framing is fine as the main mechanism.
- antibiotic-resistance-mutations-downregulation.md, Explainer: "A resistance mutation arising in one cell can be copied onto a plasmid and transferred". Chromosomal point mutations such as gyrA usually stay on the chromosome. Plasmid-borne resistance is mostly acquired genes. Also "a new generation every 20 minutes" generalizes E. coli's lab optimum.
- antimicrobial-resistance-epidemiology-and-spread.md, Core Idea and Explainer: treat "incomplete treatment" / "incomplete courses" as a settled driver of resistance. The evidence that finishing a course prevents resistance is contested, and shorter courses are often recommended now. Q4 answer says one gene can spread "to thousands of unrelated species in a single generation". That is overstated.
- bacteriophage-and-lysogenic-cycles.md, Explainer and Q2: prophage replicates "without any phage proteins being produced". The prophage expresses CI repressor, a phage protein. Q1 says RecA "cleaves the CI repressor". Activated RecA stimulates CI self-cleavage (co-protease activity).
- bacterial-conjugation-plasmid-transfer.md, Q0 explanation: cites "Option 3" twice for what is option index 2. It also claims new transconjugants "can conjugate immediately". There is usually a short establishment lag.
- bacterial-pili-fimbriae-types.md, Q2: says DNA does not pass through the F pilus. Some evidence shows transfer can occur through an extended F pilus at a distance. The claim is contested, not settled.
- bacterial-aerobic-respiration-electron-transport.md, Q4 answer: mitochondria "can only function aerobically". Explainer says flexible chains are "something mitochondria cannot do". Plant and fungal mitochondria have alternative oxidases and NADH dehydrogenases, and some eukaryotes have anaerobic mitochondria.
- chemotaxis-signaling-phosphorylation.md, Q3 explanation: "the default state is tumbling, not running". This conflicts with the file's own statement that the motor defaults to CCW (run) when CheY-P is low.
- bacterial-transcription-and-operon-regulation.md, Q0/Q2/Q4 and Explainer: glucose effects on lac are explained only through cAMP/CAP. Inducer exclusion (glucose-PTS EIIA inhibiting LacY) is a major mechanism in E. coli. The cAMP-only account is a textbook simplification.
- aminoglycoside-ribosome-inhibition.md, Core Idea: "translation termination" and "truncated, non-functional proteins". The Explainer and questions center on misreading and membrane-inserted mistranslated proteins. The Core Idea wording is imprecise.
- fungal-nutrition-osmotrophy-degradation.md, Explainer: cellulases break down polysaccharides "that almost no other organisms can efficiently degrade". Many bacteria degrade cellulose efficiently, for example rumen bacteria and cellulolytic Clostridia.
- fungal-reproduction-and-life-cycles.md, Explainer: ascomycete ascospores "are actively discharged". Many are, but not all; truffles, for example, do not discharge them.
