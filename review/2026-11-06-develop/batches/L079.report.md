## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:
1. mechanism-design-basics.md, Q3 explanation: "Classic impossibility results (like the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem) show that no mechanism can be simultaneously efficient, individually rational, and budget-balanced". That result is Myerson-Satterthwaite. Gibbard-Satterthwaite concerns strategy-proof social choice functions.
2. mechanism-design-basics.md, Q0 explanation: "making truth-telling individually rational". This misuses the term. The property is dominant-strategy incentive compatibility.
3. mechanism-design-basics.md, Q4 answer: "misreporting can only leave them worse off". Strategy-proofness means misreporting cannot help. It can leave the student equally well off.
4. information-asymmetry.md, Q0 explanation: "(Deductibles and co-pays are screening tools designed to mitigate this moral hazard.)" This mixes up the two problems. As a moral-hazard device a deductible is a cost-sharing incentive, not a screening tool.
5. information-asymmetry.md, Explainer: "Now the average quality falls, say to $4,000". With two types and every good seller gone, the average is $2,000. The $4,000 figure needs partial exit, which the example does not set up.
6. integrability-revealed-preference.md, Core Idea: "determines when demand functions can be recovered from underlying preferences through integration". This is reversed. Integrability recovers preferences from demand.
7. integrability-revealed-preference.md, Q1/Q2 explanations: they call symmetry plus NSD "necessary and sufficient". The usual statement also needs homogeneity and budget balance (Walras' law).
8. labor-market-signaling.md, Explainer: "education can function purely as a **screening device**". In Spence's model education is a signal. Screening is when the uninformed side moves first.
9. market-for-lemons-unraveling.md, Q2 (true-false, answer true): it says unraveling can make ALL mutually beneficial trades fail, but its own example still has lemons trading at $6k. The explanation's sentence about lemon trades is muddled.
10. market-for-lemons-unraveling.md, Q4 answer: "an iterative collapse that cannot reach a stable mixed equilibrium". Partial-trade equilibria do exist. With a peach share of 2/3 or more, the explainer's own numbers pool stably.
11. mixed-strategies-probability.md, Q2 (true-false, answer false) and its explanation: "If a player were mixing to maximize their own payoff, they would play a pure strategy". A mixed equilibrium strategy is a payoff-maximizing best response. Every strategy in its support also maximizes, so the question's premise is arguably true, not false.
12. mixed-strategy-equilibrium.md, Core Idea: "Strategies outside the support yield strictly lower payoffs". The Explainer says the same ("verify that strategies outside the support yield strictly lower expected payoffs"). The correct condition is "no higher" (weakly lower).
13. moral-hazard.md, Q2 explanation: "The optimal contract with moral hazard always involves some pay-for-performance". This is false when the principal finds it optimal to implement low effort; a flat wage is then optimal. The Q4 explanation and the Explainer also say hidden action "always" gives a strictly worse outcome. That fails for a risk-neutral agent without wealth limits, who can buy the output for a fixed fee and reach first-best. I did not contradict the Explainer in the new text.
14. nash-refinements-and-trembling-hand.md, Q0 explanation: it says Firm A trembling into Accommodate shifts Firm B to Enter. The usual argument is the reverse: Firm B trembles into Enter, which makes A's Fight (weakly dominated) strictly worse. A small chance of Accommodate need not change B's best response.
15. nash-refinements-and-trembling-hand.md, Explainer coordination example (Firm A enters, Firm B stays out, "sustained by Firm B's belief that Firm A will always enter") is muddled. It never says why B's staying out is fragile.
16. repeated-games-and-trigger-strategies.md, Core Idea: "any outcome achieving each player's maximin payoff can be sustained". The Folk Theorem uses feasible payoffs strictly above the minmax, not the maximin. The Explainer glosses minmax as "the worst they can guarantee themselves", which mixes up minmax and maximin.
17. repeated-games-and-trigger-strategies.md, Explainer: "tit-for-tat recovers after one round of mutual retaliation". After one accidental defection, two tit-for-tat players fall into alternating defections. They do not recover.
18. principal-agent-contracting.md, Q4 explanation: "The information rent arises from the participation constraint of the high type plus the incentive compatibility constraint". It arises from the low type's participation constraint plus the high type's IC. The Q3 explanation also says a single contract "cannot simultaneously satisfy both" IC constraints. A single pooling contract satisfies IC trivially; the problem is that it does not separate types.
19. screening-contracts.md, Explainer paragraph 4: "that generosity is what prevents low-risk types from mimicking high-risk types". The direction is reversed. The rent stops the high type (high-risk) from mimicking the low type. This also conflicts with the file's next paragraph.
20. screening-and-self-selection.md, Q4 answer: "The only way to eliminate the rent entirely is to make both types' contracts identical". Excluding the low type (shutdown) also removes the rent.
21. second-price-auction.md, Explainer: "Google's ad auctions use a variant ... precisely because the dominant-strategy property". The generalized second-price auction is not truthful, so the dominant-strategy reason does not carry over. The Core Idea's mention of spectrum sales is also loose; those mostly use ascending or clock formats.
22. prospect-theory.md, Q0 explanation: "the pain of a $100 loss is weighted about twice as heavily as the pleasure of a $200 gain". Loss aversion compares equal-sized gains and losses. With a pure 2:1 kink and linear value, the +200/−100 coin flip gives exactly zero, so the explanation's reasoning does not support declining.
23. quasi-linear-preferences.md, Q2 and Explainer: "demand for x is independent of income" holds only at interior solutions. At low income the consumer is at a corner (y = 0), and demand for x depends on income.
24. revealed-preference-axioms.md, Explainer: "(at the same or lower price)" in the WARP sentence is garbled. Also, with two goods WARP implies SARP, so the Q2 claim that WARP is not sufficient holds only with three or more goods.
25. individual-rationality-mechanism.md, Q4 answer: "IC requires that high types earn strictly more". IC needs only weakly more.
26. incentive-compatibility-constraints.md, Explainer paragraph 1: "healthy people might claim to be sick to get extra coverage at a subsidized rate". This is backwards for the standard model. The binding concern is high-risk types taking the cheap low-coverage plan.
27. pooling-separating-equilibrium.md, Q1 explanation and Explainer: they say a pool survives when the cost of a separating signal exceeds the gain "from being identified as high-type". Whether a deviator is identified as high-type depends on off-path beliefs, which the explanation leaves out.
