## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. reception-history-and-transmission.md, Explainer and Q4 answer: "When those texts returned to Europe via Latin translation ... they arrived double-refracted" / "The Aristotle available to Aquinas arrived through a chain ... translated into Arabic ... then translated into Latin". Aquinas relied heavily on Latin translations made directly from Greek, notably William of Moerbeke's. Many twelfth-century translations (e.g., James of Venice) were also Greek-to-Latin. The Arabic route was real for some texts and for commentaries (Averroes), but "double-filtered" overstates it for Aquinas.
2. reception-history-and-transmission.md, Explainer: "The Greek concept of demokratia ... becomes the Roman res publica". Res publica was not a successor of demokratia; Roman writers mostly treated democracy with suspicion. The chain is misleading.
3. temporality-and-time.md, Q4 and Explainer: Braudel "relegates Philip II's wars and diplomacy to the third volume". *The Mediterranean* is in three parts; the editions are one or two volumes. "Third part" is accurate.
4. temporality-and-time.md, Q2 explanation: the longue durée factors listed for the French Revolution include "geographical constraints on Mediterranean trade". These are borrowed from Braudel's Mediterranean and do not fit the French Revolution example.
5. social-history-movement.md, Explainer: "The Annales school, which you have encountered through Ibn Khaldun's structural lineage". The Annales school does not descend from Ibn Khaldun, and Ibn Khaldun is not a prerequisite here.
6. social-history-movement.md, Core Idea: Thompson reconstructed working-class culture "through ... oral testimony". *The Making of the English Working Class* covers c. 1780-1832 and rests on printed and archival sources, not oral testimony.
7. poovey-statistics-and-facts.md, Q0, Q4 and Explainer: census categories "employed/unemployed" are presented as Poovey's examples. *A History of the Modern Fact* ends around the 1830s, and "unemployment" as a census category is later in the century. These examples look like extrapolation, not Poovey's own argument.
8. zemon-davis-cultural-history.md, Q3 and Explainer: "her concept of 'historical fiction'". Davis described parts of *Martin Guerre* as "invention" held in check by the sources, and *Fiction in the Archives* uses "fiction" for the crafting of narratives. "Historical fiction" as a named concept of hers is doubtful.
9. ranke-school-legacy.md, Explainer: "His seminar model at the University of Berlin (from 1825 onward)". Ranke arrived in Berlin in 1825, but his historical exercises (the seminar) are usually dated to the early 1830s. Worth checking.
10. ranke-school-legacy.md, Core Idea: "Ranke's positivism". The label is contested; Ranke sought no general laws and his view of history was religious. The Explainer's "implicit positivism" is more defensible.
11. vico-new-science.md, Core Idea: verum factum is described as argued "in Scienza Nuova". Vico first stated it in *De antiquissima Italorum sapientia* (1710); the New Science applies it to the civil world. The framing of Vico as reacting against "Enlightenment historiography" is also partly anachronistic, since his main target was Cartesian rationalism.
12. vico-new-science.md, Explainer: age of men described as "democratic societies". Vico's age of men includes popular commonwealths and monarchies.
13. progress-and-decline.md, Explainer: Gibbon grouped with Spengler as imposing "organic metaphors (birth, maturity, decay)". Gibbon offered no cyclical or organic theory of civilizations comparable to Spengler's.
14. pierre-nora-lieux-memoire.md, Q2 stem: "how accurately and mostly they preserve" is garbled wording (probably "completely").
15. rosenfeld-intellectual-concepts.md, Core Idea lists "democracy, nature, dignity" as her concepts, and Q1 says populists of the "19th century". Her main books treat common sense (2011), democracy and truth (2019) and language in the French Revolution (2001); "nature" and "dignity" may not be hers. Her common-sense book centers on the eighteenth century, with modern populism in its epilogue.
16. structures-mentalities.md, Explainer: the medieval peasant "experienced time as cyclical rather than linear". This is a contested generalization; liturgical and linear salvation time coexisted.
17. thucydides-scientific-history.md, Explainer: Thucydides ranked "contemporary documents (decrees, treaties, speeches)" above memory. Book I.22 speaks of eyewitness testimony and cross-checking; it gives no explicit ranking of documents.

### Suspected wrong or missing prerequisites
1. herodotus-inquiry-method -> prosopography-collective-biography: weak link. Prosopography grew out of Roman and medieval studies, not Herodotus.
2. enlightenment-historiography -> vico-new-science (hard): questionable as a hard prerequisite. Vico (New Science 1725) was reacting mainly to Descartes, and much of the Enlightenment historiography came after him. A Cartesian-rationalism or early modern philosophy topic may fit better.
3. Missing: ancient-historiography or herodotus-inquiry-method -> thucydides-scientific-history. The Explainer leans on the Herodotus contrast; ancient-historiography is present, so this is minor.
4. Missing: microhistory or carlo-ginzburg-microhistory -> zemon-davis-cultural-history (soft). The file lists Ginzburg as builds-toward, but Davis's Martin Guerre is usually taught beside Ginzburg as microhistory.
