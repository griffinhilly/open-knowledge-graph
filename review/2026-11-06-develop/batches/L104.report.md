### Suspected errors in existing text
1. commutative-property-addition-1st.md, Explainer: "Put 4 blocks on the left and 2 blocks on the right. Count them all: 6. ... The total is always 8 (or whatever the sum is)". The example sums to 6, so "always 8" is wrong.
2. collecting-data-tally-marks.md, Question 3 (true-false): "You should write down most your tally marks first, then go back and make the diagonal lines through most fourth mark." Garbled wording; it probably meant "all ... every fourth mark". Also, the diagonal is the fifth mark drawn across four, so "through every fourth mark" is ambiguous.
3. equal-groups.md, Question 1 (true-false): "that tells you the total number of objects in most of the groups." Garbled wording; it probably meant "in all of the groups". This looks like the same automated "all" -> "most" swap as item 2.
4. fact-families.md, Question 2 (true-false, answer true): "A fact family always contains exactly three different numbers and four related equations." This is false for doubles: 4, 4, 8 has two different numbers and only two facts (4 + 4 = 8, 8 − 4 = 4). The explainer says "No more, no fewer," which is too strong.
5. comparing-two-digit-numbers.md, Explainer: "The > symbol points left toward the bigger number ... the < symbol points right toward the bigger number". The point of > aims right, at the smaller number. The open side faces left. The sentence mixes up "points" and "opens".
6. fractions-halves-and-fourths-1st.md, title: "Introduction to Halves, Thirds, and Fourths". The Core Idea, Explainer and questions cover only halves and fourths. Either the title or the content is wrong.
7. identifying-two-dimensional-shapes.md, Question 4 answer: "might call a tilted rectangle a 'parallelogram' because it doesn't look 'upright'". Every rectangle is a parallelogram, so this example does not show a misidentification.
8. addition-subtraction-word-problems-1st.md, Question 0 explanation: "the collection shrank. That always signals subtraction". This is too strong. The same story can also be solved as a missing addend (9 + ? = 15).
9. building-and-decomposing-shapes.md, Explainer: "take two triangles. If you flip one over and slide it next to the other so their long edges touch, you get a rectangle!" This is true only for two matching right triangles. Composing-shapes.md, Explainer: "Or cut two triangles out of paper and slide them around until they click into a square" has the same issue (it needs two matching isosceles right triangles).
10. identifying-three-dimensional-shapes.md, Question 4 (short-answer): the question asks about a cube, sphere, cylinder and cone, but the answer also sorts a "rectangular prism". This is minor.
