## Flags
1. substitution-rates.md, Core Idea: "At neutral sites, substitution rate K equals twice the mutation rate (K = 2μ)." This is wrong. The neutral substitution rate per site per generation equals the mutation rate, K = μ, which is what the file's own questions derive. The factor 2 belongs to pairwise divergence between two lineages (about 2μt). The Explainer repeats the error ("for a diploid, K = 2μ when counting per-site rates across both alleles"), and so does question 1's explanation ("K_synonymous ≈ 2μ"). My sections use the correct relationship. I added a misconception bullet on pairwise divergence that explains where the factor 2 comes from.
2. Prerequisite cycle: molecular-clock -> substitution-rates is listed as a hard prerequisite, and molecular-clock is also in substitution-rates' builds-toward. One of the two links should go.
3. sympatric-speciation.md, Explainer: "far too many species in too small and uniform a lake for geographic isolation to explain" (Lake Victoria). Lake Victoria is Africa's largest lake by area, so "too small" is wrong. Whether its cichlid radiation was sympatric is also debated: hybrid origins and micro-allopatry within the lake have been proposed. Crater-lake cichlids are the usual textbook cases of sympatric speciation. The short-answer explanation (item 4) repeats the claim that Lake Victoria "is small".
4. synonymous-nonsynonymous-substitutions.md, Explainer: "almost all amino acid changes are lethal and removed" (histones). This is overstated. Most such changes are deleterious and removed by purifying selection; lethality is not required.
5. synonymous-nonsynonymous-substitutions.md, question 4 answer: "If mutation rate were elevated at nonsynonymous sites specifically, it would elevate dS at those positions too". This reasoning is muddled. Mutation biases (for example transition/transversion bias or CpG effects) can shift dN/dS somewhat, and standard methods model them for that reason. Question 0 compares histone genes between humans and yeast. At that divergence synonymous sites are saturated, so a dN/dS of 0.005 could not be estimated reliably. This is minor, but the scenario is unrealistic.
6. transposable-elements.md, Explainer: "'jumping genes,' a term coined by Barbara McClintock". This attribution is doubtful. McClintock called them "controlling elements"; "jumping genes" was a later popular term.
7. Prerequisite: horizontal-gene-transfer -> synonymous-nonsynonymous-substitutions (soft). Nothing in this topic depends on HGT, so the link looks spurious.
