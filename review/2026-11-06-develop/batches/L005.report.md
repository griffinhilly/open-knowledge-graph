## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text

High priority:
- sandwich-compounds-metallocenes.md, Q4: the stem says "chromocene Cr(Cp)₂ (15 valence electrons)", and the answer contains draft self-correction text: "(Cr: 6 + 2×Cp: 10 = 16? Actually using Cr⁰ count: 6 + 10 = 16. Let me recalculate — ...)". Chromocene has 16 electrons. The stem and answer need a rewrite.
- sandwich-compounds-metallocenes.md, Explainer: "Only ferrocene and its cation hit the 18-electron sweet spot." Ferrocenium is 17 electrons. The 18-electron cation in the series is cobaltocenium.
- metal-carbonyls.md, Q3 (true-false, answer true): "All stable binary metal carbonyls ... obey the 18-electron rule." V(CO)₆ is a stable, isolable 17-electron binary carbonyl, so the answer should be false. The Explainer repeats this: "Every known stable binary carbonyl satisfies it." The Q3 explanation of the Co₂(CO)₈ count is also garbled.
- chelate-effect-stability-constants.md, Explainer: "Ethylenediamine (en), oxalate, and acetylacetonate all form five-membered rings". acac forms six-membered chelate rings. Also, Q1 option B says "three reactant particles produce seven product particles", while the explanation says 4 (1 complex + 3 en). These disagree.
- cluster-compounds.md, Q3 explanation: "Os₆(CO)₁₈ (an octahedral cluster) has the same 7 SEP as B₆H₆²⁻". Neutral Os₆(CO)₁₈ is a bicapped tetrahedron with 6 SEP. The octahedral closo cluster is [Os₆(CO)₁₈]²⁻.
- inorganic-photochemistry.md, Q4 answer: "the excited Ru²⁺* oxidizes water ... while being reduced to Ru⁺. A sacrificial oxidant regenerates Ru²⁺." At +0.84 V, *Ru(bpy)₃²⁺ cannot practically oxidize water. The standard scheme quenches *Ru²⁺ oxidatively with a sacrificial acceptor (e.g. persulfate) to give Ru³⁺ (~+1.26 V). Ru³⁺ then oxidizes water at a catalyst such as IrO₂. The text as written has the redox roles backward.
- jahn-teller-effect.md, Q4 answer: "values for d³ (Cr²⁺ is anomalous due to JT)". Cr²⁺ is d⁴; the d³ divalent ion is V²⁺. The answer also lists "d⁶ low-spin" among the hydration-enthalpy humps. The hexaaqua M²⁺ ions are high-spin, and Fe²⁺(aq) is high-spin d⁶.
- metal-metal-bonding.md, Q3: typed true-false with answer false, but the stem asks "What is the effective bond order?" This is malformed. It should be short-answer (answer: 2), or the stem should be recast as a claim.
- group-theory-applications-inorganic.md, Explainer: "Only the t₂g component has a metal orbital counterpart". The t₁u pi SALCs also match metal p orbitals. The claim holds only for the d orbitals.
- homogeneous-catalysis-mechanisms.md, Q1 explanation: "rate-limiting step (usually oxidative addition for electron-poor aryl halides, transmetallation for electron-rich substrates)". This is backwards. Electron-poor aryl halides undergo fast oxidative addition. Oxidative addition tends to limit the rate for electron-rich or less reactive halides (e.g. aryl chlorides).
- mo-theory-transition-metal-complexes.md, Q3: "[Co(CO)₆]³⁺". This is not a known complex. An isoelectronic real example would be Cr(CO)₆ or [Mn(CO)₆]⁺.
- hsab-theory.md, Explainer: "platinum anticancer drugs coordinate through soft nitrogen donors". N donors are hard or borderline, not soft. Also "EDTA ... poor for soft ones" is questionable, because Hg²⁺-EDTA has a large formation constant.
- main-group-chemistry-overview.md, Q2 explanation: Li and Mg "carbonate and fluoride salts that decompose on heating". The carbonates (and nitrates) decompose on heating; the fluorides do not. The usual parallel for the fluorides is low solubility.
- materials-chemistry-zeolites-mofs.md, Explainer: "Zn₄O paddle wheel for octahedral coordination". Zn₄O(CO₂)₆ is an octahedral SBU, not a paddlewheel. The paddlewheel is the Cu₂(CO₂)₄ unit. Also unsourced: "Over 60 billion dollars of petroleum products".
