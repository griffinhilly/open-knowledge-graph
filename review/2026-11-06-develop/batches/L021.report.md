## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- defect-chemistry.md, Q0 and Q2: "V''_O represents ... an oxygen vacancy with an effective charge of 2+" and "the two dots (sometimes written as superscript primes or dots)". In Kroger-Vink, dots mean positive and primes mean negative; the oxygen vacancy should be written with two dots (V_O^••). Primes and dots are not interchangeable. This matters most: the file teaches the wrong convention.
- x-ray-powder-diffraction.md, Q0 explanation: "This pattern matches aluminum (a = 4.05 Angstroms)." Peaks at 38.2, 44.4, 64.5 and 77.5 degrees (Cu K-alpha) match gold or silver (a about 4.08 A). Aluminum's peaks sit near 38.5, 44.7, 65.1 and 78.2 degrees. The FCC answer is still right.
- crystal-symmetry-and-space-groups.md, Q2 answer: "The slash means a mirror plane perpendicular to that screw axis." P2_1/c has no mirror. The slash means the plane named after it (the c-glide) is perpendicular to the 2_1 axis.
- crystal-symmetry-and-space-groups.md, Q3 (true-false, answer true): "Two crystals have the same chemical composition and unit cell dimensions but different space groups. They must have different physical properties." "Must" is too strong. For example, enantiomorphic pairs (P3_1 vs P3_2) have identical scalar properties. Also, diamond and graphite, the example given, do not share unit cell dimensions.
- thin-film-deposition-cvd-pvd.md, Q2 correct option and Explainer: "Each ALD cycle deposits exactly one monolayer" / "ALD deposits exactly one atomic layer per cycle". Growth per cycle is usually a fraction of a monolayer. The file's own figures (0.5-1.5 A; 1.1 A for Al2O3) show this. The Explainer's "not on ... temperature variations" also overstates; growth per cycle varies with temperature, and is steady only inside the ALD window.
- metal-organic-frameworks-extended.md, Explainer: "A 6-connected octahedral node ... gives a cubic net regardless of whether the node is Zn4O, Cu2(COO)4, or Zr6O4(OH)4." The Cu2(COO)4 paddlewheel is a 4-connected square node. The Zr6 cluster in UiO-66 is 12-connected (fcu net). Only Zn4O fits the claim as written.
- semiconductor-materials-chemistry.md, Q0 answer: "binding energy ~45 meV, far less than kT at room temperature". kT at 300 K is about 26 meV, so 45 meV is larger than kT. Donors still ionize almost fully, because of the large conduction-band density of states (entropy), not because the binding energy is below kT.
- magnetic-materials-chemistry.md, Q3: "Single-domain nanoparticles of magnetite (below approximately 80 nm) exhibit superparamagnetism." Roughly 80 nm is the single-domain limit. Room-temperature superparamagnetism in magnetite needs much smaller particles, about 20-25 nm or less.
- superconducting-materials-chemistry.md, Q0: "loses superconductivity when delta exceeds about 0.5". YBCO keeps T_c of roughly 60 K near delta = 0.5 (the 60 K plateau). Superconductivity vanishes near delta of about 0.6-0.65.
- superconducting-materials-chemistry.md, Q1 explanation: "The theory predicts a maximum T_c around 30-40 K". BCS does not set a firm ceiling. That was an empirical expectation, and the file's own Explainer (H3S at 203 K, phonon-mediated) contradicts it.
- electronic-band-theory-of-solids.md, Q1 (true-false, answer true): "a half-filled band always produces metallic behavior". The explanation itself names Mott insulators as an exception, and Peierls distortion is another. With "always" in the stem, the answer is arguably false.
- conducting-polymers-chemistry.md, Q0 answer: describes I2-doped polyacetylene carriers as polarons, then bipolarons. trans-Polyacetylene has a degenerate ground state, and its characteristic carriers are solitons. Polarons and bipolarons describe non-degenerate polymers (polythiophene, polypyrrole). I kept my misconception general so it does not contradict the file.
- nanomaterials-synthesis.md, Q0 answer: "Bulk gold appears gold-colored because the flat surface supports only non-resonant reflection". Bulk gold's color comes from interband (5d to 6sp) absorption of blue light. In the Explainer, plasmon resonance is grouped under "the high fraction of under-coordinated surface atoms"; LSPR is a collective conduction-electron effect, not a coordination effect.
- glass-and-amorphous-materials.md, Q3 explanation: "cooled at rates exceeding 10^5-10^6 K/s". This holds for early metallic glasses. Bulk metallic glasses (for example Zr- and Pd-based) form at about 1-100 K/s or less.
- ceramic-materials-chemistry.md, Explainer: "colossal magnetoresistance materials (LaMnO3)". Undoped LaMnO3 is an antiferromagnetic insulator. CMR appears in hole-doped La1-xAxMnO3 (A = Ca, Sr).
- crystal-structures-and-unit-cells.md, Explainer: the radius ratio rule "correctly predicts the structure of most simple ionic compounds". Its success rate is known to be modest, often cited as about half. Also, the Core Idea ("smallest repeating unit that captures the full symmetry") and the Explainer ("the smallest parallelepiped") blur the conventional cell and the primitive cell.
- mechanical-properties-microstructure.md, Q1 option/explanation: "In compression ... the flaw population is mechanically irrelevant" / "entirely a consequence of flaw sensitivity". Compressive failure of ceramics still starts at flaws (wing cracks). Flaws matter less in compression, but they are not irrelevant.
- biomaterials-chemistry.md, Q2 explanation: calls fast protein adsorption "the Vroman effect". The Vroman effect is the later displacement of early-adsorbed abundant proteins by higher-affinity ones.
- biomaterials-chemistry.md, Explainer: "releasing Ca^2+ and Si^4+ ions". Bioactive glass releases soluble silica (Si(OH)4); free Si4+ ions do not exist in water.
- biomaterials-chemistry.md, Q1 explanation: "higher glycolic content = faster degradation". This is not monotonic. 50:50 PLGA usually degrades fastest, because crystallinity rises toward either end of the composition range.
- zeolite-chemistry-and-applications.md, Q0: presents ZSM-5 as the methanol-to-olefins catalyst. Commercial MTO for ethylene and propylene mainly uses SAPO-34 (CHA). ZSM-5 is used for methanol-to-gasoline and methanol-to-propylene. Also, "molecules up to about C10" in the option has no clear source.
- catalytic-materials-design.md, Q0 answer: "Pd binds CO too weakly, W binds it too strongly". For CO methanation, Pd binds CO strongly but dissociates it poorly. In descriptor plots it sits on the weak-dissociation side, not the weak-binding side. Low confidence; check against the source volcano.
