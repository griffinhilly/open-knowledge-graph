- bifurcation-in-pdes.md, Q3 explanation: "Away from onset, a band of wavenumbers near k_c is unstable (the Eckhaus band)". The linearly unstable band is bounded by the neutral curve. The Eckhaus band is the narrower sub-band of stable periodic patterns.
- bifurcation-theory-saddle-node.md, Q4 explanation: "Think of slowly loading a beam until it buckles (saddle-node: the straight configuration disappears)". Euler buckling is a pitchfork, and the straight state persists as an unstable state. The sibling file bifurcation-theory-transcritical-pitchfork.md Q2 correctly calls it a pitchfork. Snap-through of an arch would be a correct saddle-node example.
- bifurcation-theory-transcritical-pitchfork.md, Q4 answer: "the cubic term ... requires the quadratic term x² to vanish, which only happens when the system has the x → -x symmetry". This is overstated. The quadratic term can vanish without symmetry, but that case is non-generic.
- chaos-definition-and-properties.md, Q2: this item treats sensitive dependence as the condition the other two must add to. For maps on infinite metric spaces, transitivity plus dense periodic orbits already imply sensitive dependence (Banks et al.). Separately, option 1's text "Without dense periodic orbits, the aperiodic behavior might be trivial (like trajectories escaping to infinity)" is dubious.
- chaos-definition-and-properties.md, Explainer: "compressed in others (the system is dissipative — volumes contract)". This is stated as if all chaos is dissipative. Hamiltonian chaos is volume-preserving.
- feigenbaum-constants.md, Explainer and Q3 explanation: "the constants δ and α are eigenvalues of the linearized operator". δ is the unstable eigenvalue. α is the rescaling factor in the fixed-point equation, not an eigenvalue.

I believe the four-doubling result with δ ≈ 4.4 is Libchaber, Laroche and Fauve (1982), in mercury. Libchaber and Maurer's helium work was c. 1980. Please verify.
- hamiltonian-chaos.md, Explainer: "Yet the KAM tori confine the chaos (in 2 degrees of freedom per planet pair), preventing catastrophic orbital instability". The solar system has many degrees of freedom, so KAM confinement does not apply. The same file's Q3 and kam-theorem.md Q3 say this.
