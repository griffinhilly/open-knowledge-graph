## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:

1. expert-problem-and-hedge-algorithm.md, Q2 (true-false, answer true): "If all N experts have identical cumulative loss after T rounds, the Hedge algorithm's regret is zero." Equal totals do not mean equal per-round losses. Example: two experts with alternating losses (1,0),(0,1),... Hedge moves weight toward the expert that loses next, so its loss exceeds L. The answer should be false (or the premise should be "identical loss every round").
2. information-theoretic-lower-bounds.md, Q0: answer is 0 ("the colleague's algorithm must have a bug"), but the explanation itself gives two possibilities, the second being extra structure outside the lower-bound class, which is option 1. Answer key likely should be 1.
3. information-theoretic-lower-bounds.md, Explainer: "The total variation between the n-fold products ... is bounded by n times the chi-squared divergence or KL divergence." The standard route is KL(P^n||Q^n) = n·KL(P||Q) plus Pinsker, giving TV ≤ sqrt(n·KL/2), not n·KL.
4. causal-inference-ml.md, Explainer do-calculus rules: Rule 1 condition "if Z is not a descendant of X" is wrong (it is a d-separation condition in the graph with edges into X removed). Rule 2 is the action/observation exchange rule, not "ignore interventions". Rule 3 described as "complex rule".
5. causal-inference-ml.md, Explainer: "Instrumental Variables: Variables that affect treatment but only through treatment's effect on outcome" is garbled. An instrument affects the outcome only through the treatment.
6. causal-inference-ml.md, Q3 (index 3): a "why" question typed as true-false with answer true. Malformed. Also Q1 explanation says confounding makes P(Y|T) "inflated"; bias can go either direction.
7. contrastive-learning-theory.md, Explainer: credits Gutmann and Hyvärinen's NCE with showing the contrastive objective lower-bounds mutual information. NCE was for density estimation; the InfoNCE/MI bound is from van den Oord et al. (CPC, 2018).
8. contrastive-learning-theory.md, Explainer: SimCLR step "(3) projecting the embeddings to a high-dimensional space" — SimCLR's projection head maps to a lower-dimensional space.
9. contrastive-learning-theory.md, Explainer: "SimSiam removes the memory bank requirement through redundancy reduction." Redundancy reduction is Barlow Twins; SimSiam uses a predictor and stop-gradient.
10. contrastive-learning-theory.md, Explainer "Scaling properties": "number of negatives required ... scales roughly logarithmically with dimensionality and task difficulty" appears unsupported.
11. contrastive-learning-theory.md, Core Idea: says the representation should "minimize" mutual information with negative pairs; the standard InfoNCE view maximizes a bound on MI between positive views.
12. diffusion-models-theory.md, Explainer: "This forward process is deterministic given the noise schedule" — the forward process is stochastic.
13. diffusion-models-theory.md, Explainer sampling formula mixes alpha_t and the cumulative ᾱ_t: DDPM uses (1 − α_t)/sqrt(1 − ᾱ_t), and x_t = sqrt(ᾱ_t)x_0 + ... uses the cumulative product.
14. diffusion-models-theory.md, Explainer: "Tractable Likelihood: The likelihood is tractable via importance weighting, unlike VAEs" is doubtful (likelihood is bounded by the ELBO or computed via the probability flow ODE). "The training objective (predicting noise) is a lower bound on the log-likelihood" — the simplified loss is a reweighted ELBO, not the bound itself.
15. diffusion-models-theory.md, Q3 (index 3): "What advantage do diffusion models have..." typed true-false. Malformed.
16. double-descent-phenomenon.md, Explainer: underfitting regime "Both bias and variance are high" — variance is low when underfitting.
17. double-descent-phenomenon.md, Explainer: formula "error = noise * sample_complexity / (1 - underparameterization_factor)" is garbled. Also "preference for solutions with small margin" should be large margin. "Test error decreases monotonically in this region" is too strong.
18. double-descent-phenomenon.md, Q3 (index 3): "How does the ratio ... relate" typed true-false. Malformed.
19. fairness-ml-theory.md, Explainer heading "Equalized Odds (Equal Opportunity)" and Q1 explanation "also called 'equal opportunity'": equal opportunity (Hardt et al. 2016) requires only equal true positive rates; equalized odds requires equal TPR and FPR. Q1 explanation also says equalized odds is "more compatible with calibration", which conflicts with Q2.
20. fairness-ml-theory.md, Explainer: "Calibration: P(Y=1 | pred=1, group=A) = ..." is predictive parity; calibration is usually defined for scores, P(Y=1 | S=s, A) = s.
21. fairness-ml-theory.md, Q3 (index 3): "What is the difference..." typed true-false. Malformed.
22. graph-neural-network-theory.md, Explainer "Universal Approximation: GNNs can approximate any permutation-invariant function" — false for message-passing GNNs, which are bounded by 1-WL (as the same file says).
23. graph-neural-network-theory.md, Explainer over-squashing chain example (a chain has no bottleneck in the usual sense; over-squashing is about exponentially growing receptive fields squeezed into fixed-size vectors), and "Addressing over-squashing requires higher-order GNNs" (graph rewiring is a common fix). Spectral convolution formula is garbled. Q0 answer: "GNNs cannot solve graph isomorphism (which is known to be hard for the WL test)" is muddled. Q3 malformed true-false. Typo "1-WL-equivocal", "polyomial".
24. implicit-regularization.md, Explainer: min-norm solution w* = X^T(XX^T)^{-1}y holds only from zero initialization (or init in the row space); not stated. "smaller LR = stronger regularization" is questionable (larger step sizes are usually linked with stronger implicit regularization).
25. implicit-regularization.md, Q2: question calls early stopping "explicit regularization", then the answer says it "directly implements implicit regularization". Inconsistent.
26. information-bottleneck-theory.md, Explainer and Q1 answer present the fitting-then-compression phases as established. This was contested (Saxe et al. 2018: compression depends on activation, and I(T;X) is ill-defined/infinite for deterministic networks on continuous inputs).
27. information-bottleneck-theory.md, Q3 explanation: VIB uses "tractable lower bounds on I(T; X)". VIB uses a variational upper bound on I(T;X) (KL to a prior) and a lower bound on I(T;Y). Q3 is also a "Can it..." question typed true-false. Explainer says IB "applies most cleanly to deterministic mappings"; for continuous X, stochastic encoders are the clean case.

"edge rewinding" should probably be "weight/late rewinding". ">99%" failure threshold is unsourced.

Also "with 1 million experts, the regret only grows by a factor of sqrt(ln(10^6)) ≈ 3.7 compared to 2 experts": the ratio is sqrt(ln 10^6 / ln 2) ≈ 4.5. Q1 explanation "used in cryptographic protocols" is unsupported.
