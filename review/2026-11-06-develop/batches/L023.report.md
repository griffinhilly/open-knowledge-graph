## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. semidefinite-programming-relaxation.md, Q2 (true-false, answer true): "for any combinatorial optimization problem, the SDP integrality gap is at most the LP integrality gap." This holds only when the SDP contains the specific LP being compared. Strong LP hierarchies (for example Sherali-Adams at high levels) can beat a basic SDP. The universal claim is false as stated.
2. sketching-data-structures.md, Q4 explanation: "Linearity is why count-min and min-hashing are so practical." Min-hash is not linear (it is a coordinatewise minimum). It is mergeable by taking minima, not by addition.
3. sketching-data-structures.md, Q3 answer and Explainer: min-hash with k = O(1/ε²) gives "(1 ± ε)-multiplicative" error. The standard bound is additive ε error (with a log(1/δ) factor for high probability), not multiplicative.
4. sketching-data-structures.md, Explainer: JL distances "concentrate by Chebyshev's inequality." Chebyshev does not give O(log n / ε²) dimensions. The proof uses exponential (Chernoff-type) tail bounds.
5. sketching-data-structures.md, Explainer: "the union bound over d rows bounds the overestimation." The failure bound uses independence across rows (product of per-row probabilities), not a union bound. Also t-digest and HyperLogLog are cited as sketches that "preserve ... random projections + linearity"; neither is a linear random projection.
6. streaming-algorithms.md, Q1 correct option: "All d counters are unbiased estimators that can only OVERCOUNT." Counters that only overcount are biased upward, so "unbiased" is wrong. The explanation's "(1 - 1/e)^d" and "union bound" are also off: Markov gives at most 1/e per row with w = e/ε, so independence gives at most e^(-d).
7. streaming-algorithms.md, Q2 answer: harmonic mean "reduces variance by a factor of sqrt(m)." It is the standard error that shrinks by sqrt(m); variance shrinks by m.
8. smoothed-analysis.md, Explainer: "sigma is the noise variance." Sigma is the standard deviation (Core Idea and Q2 correctly say variance sigma^2).
9. smoothed-analysis.md, Q2 is typed true-false but its stem is an open question ("what happens to the smoothed complexity?"), so "answer: true" does not fit.
10. smoothed-analysis.md, Core Idea lists "SAT solvers" among smoothed-analysis applications. I could not confirm a standard smoothed-analysis result for SAT solvers; may be inaccurate.
11. spectral-graph-algorithms.md, Q4 stem: spectral clustering uses "the top-k eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian." It uses the eigenvectors of the smallest k eigenvalues. Same question and Explainer write the eigengap as "lambda_k - lambda_{k+1}", which is negative under the file's ascending ordering; it should be lambda_{k+1} - lambda_k.
12. spectral-graph-algorithms.md, Explainer: "spectral methods cannot find the exact minimum cut (the lower bound has a square root)." The square root is in the upper bound. Also the Cheeger form lambda_2/2 <= h(G) <= sqrt(2 lambda_2) is for the normalized Laplacian and conductance, while the Explainer pairs it with L = D - A and "cut edges to subset size."
13. spectral-graph-algorithms.md, Explainer: near-linear max-flow "(Kelner et al., 2014)" is described as interior-point methods with Laplacian solves. The Kelner-Lee-Orecchia-Sidford result is approximate undirected max-flow by a different (oblivious-routing / gradient) approach. Worth checking.
14. sublinear-algorithms.md, Q3 (true-false, answer true): "no deterministic sublinear-time algorithm can provide useful approximate answers." False: binary search on a sorted array is deterministic and O(log n), and other deterministic sublinear algorithms exist.
15. sublinear-algorithms.md, Explainer: "Estimating the median requires Omega(n) queries in the worst case." An ε-approximate median (in rank) needs only O(1/ε² · log(1/δ)) samples. Only the exact median needs Omega(n).
16. sublinear-algorithms.md, Core Idea and Explainer: mean estimation with O(1/ε²) samples "works for any array." It needs values in a bounded range (for example [0, 1]).
17. sublinear-algorithms.md, Explainer: connected components estimated "in O(1/epsilon) time." The Chazelle-Rubinfeld-Trevisan bound is roughly O(d/ε² · log(1/ε)) for additive εn error and depends on the degree bound.
18. sublinear-algorithms.md, Q1: the naive degree-sampling estimator with s = O(sqrt(n)/ε²) is presented as giving ε-relative error. Known sqrt(n)-type results (Feige; Goldreich-Ron) need either a (2 + ε) factor with degree queries only or neighbor queries for (1 + ε). The naive estimator fails on a star graph.
19. submodular-optimization.md, Q2 explanation: Cunningham's algorithm is cited as polynomial; it is pseudo-polynomial. Strongly polynomial combinatorial algorithms are due to Schrijver and Iwata-Fleischer-Fujishige. Core Idea calls minimization "the dual problem," which is loose wording.
20. universal-and-perfect-hashing.md, Q1: the stem asks why universality is "strictly stronger" than a random function, but the keyed answer is about compact representation, and distractor option 0 ("a truly random function automatically satisfies the universal property") is a true statement.
21. universal-and-perfect-hashing.md, Explainer: (ax + b) mod p and (ay + b) mod p are "uniformly distributed and independent." That holds when a ranges over all of Z_p; with a ≠ 0 (the usual Carter-Wegman choice) the two values are distinct, not independent. The 1/m bound still holds.
