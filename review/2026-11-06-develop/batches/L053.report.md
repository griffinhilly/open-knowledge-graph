## Flags
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/non-equilibrium-basics-statmech.md, Explainer: "Onsager's minimum entropy production principle". The minimum entropy production theorem is due to Prigogine (it builds on Onsager's reciprocal relations).
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/non-equilibrium-basics-statmech.md, Explainer: "which your prerequisite on the fluctuation-dissipation theorem established". The fluctuation-dissipation theorem is not a listed prerequisite. Suggested: fluctuation-dissipation-theorem -> non-equilibrium-basics-statmech (if the graph direction allows it).
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/pair-distribution-function.md, Explainer: "compute the equation of state ... through the **virial expansion**". The g(r) route to pressure is the virial (pressure) equation, which differs from the virial expansion in density; both formulas assume pairwise-additive potentials.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/percolation-critical-phenomena.md, Core Idea: "critical exponents that match those of equilibrium phase transitions". Percolation has its own universality class (q → 1 Potts limit). Its exponents differ from Ising (2D: β = 5/36 vs 1/8).
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/percolation-critical-phenomena.md, Question 1 (index 1): premise "percolation shares critical exponents with equilibrium phase transitions like the Ising model" is false, and the keyed answer says Ising and percolation have "identical exponents".
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/percolation-critical-phenomena.md, Explainer: "random graphs (Erdős–Rényi networks) ... share the same critical exponents when they have the same spatial dimension". Erdős–Rényi graphs have no spatial dimension; they show mean-field percolation exponents, matching lattice percolation only above six dimensions. Also "mean finite cluster size diverges as ξ ~ |p − p_c|^{−γ}" reuses ξ, the usual symbol for correlation length.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/percolation-critical-phenomena.md, Core Idea: "The order parameter (cluster size)". The order parameter is P_∞, the probability that a site belongs to the infinite cluster, as the Explainer says.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/phase-diagrams.md, Question 0 explanation: "This anomalous negative slope is why ice skating works". Pressure melting is far too small to explain skating at typical rink temperatures; friction heating and the surface premelted layer are the accepted explanations.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/phase-diagrams.md, Explainer: "Near the critical point, the Maxwell equal-area rule is needed". The equal-area construction applies at every temperature below T_c, not only near the critical point.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/phase-diagrams.md, Question 2: typo "where most three phases can coexist".
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/phase-space-density-evolution.md, Question 4 explanation: "why Maxwell's demon cannot work ... must violate the incompressibility of Hamiltonian flow" and "the incompressibility ensures that long-time averages and ensemble averages are connected". The demon is resolved by the entropy cost of information erasure (Landauer); and incompressibility does not imply ergodicity.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/phase-transition-equilibrium.md, Explainer: "the free energy has a single minimum whose location shifts continuously to zero as T approaches the critical temperature T_c from below". Below T_c (Ising symmetry) there are two symmetric minima ±M that merge at M = 0.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/phase-transitions-first-and-second-order.md, Core Idea: "second derivatives diverge". In the Ehrenfest sense and in mean-field (Landau) theory a second derivative can jump by a finite amount, as the heat capacity does; divergence is typical but not required.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/phase-transitions-first-and-second-order.md, Question 1 explanation: "superfluid density for a superconductor". The superconducting order parameter is usually the gap or condensate wavefunction.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/photon-gas-thermodynamics.md, Explainer: "Ω = −k_BT Σ_k ln(1 − e^{−ℏω_k/k_BT})". Sign error: Ω = +k_BT Σ ln(1 − e^{−βℏω}), which is negative and gives P = −Ω/V > 0.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/photon-gas-thermodynamics.md, Explainer and Question 4 explanation: the grand partition function "factors into independent mode contributions because μ = 0". It factorizes for any ideal Bose gas with any μ; μ = 0 removes the number constraint, not mode coupling.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/photon-gas-thermodynamics.md, Explainer: radiation pressure "dominant in the interior of massive stars (where T ~ 10⁷ K)". Gas pressure usually dominates at those temperatures; radiation pressure becomes dominant only in the most massive stars. Question 1 explanation repeats "radiation pressure dominates in stellar interiors".
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/planck-distribution-blackbody.md, Explainer: "iron glows red then white then blue as it heats"; Question 3 explanation: "then white ... then blue-white". Iron melts near 1800 K and boils near 3100 K, so it cannot reach a blue-white color temperature.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/radial-distribution-function.md, Explainer: "for liquid metals, a simple single-peak structure like noble-gas liquids". Simple liquids and liquid metals both show a first peak followed by several damped oscillations; "single-peak" misdescribes them.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/renormalization-group-intro.md, Explainer: "The Ising model in two dimensions, liquid-gas systems, polymer collapse — all belong to the same universality class because they share the same symmetry (Z₂) and dimensionality (2D)". Liquid-gas systems are three-dimensional (3D Ising class), and polymer collapse at the theta point is a tricritical point of a different class.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/renormalization-group-intro.md, Explainer and Questions 3–4: mean-field is "the fixed point of a hypothetical infinite-dimensional system". Mean-field exponents are those of the Gaussian fixed point and are exact above the upper critical dimension (d > 4 for Ising), not only at infinite dimension.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/renormalization-group-scaling.md, Question 0 and Explainer: iron and CO₂/water "have nearly identical critical exponents"; "both are described by the same symmetry (scalar order parameter, Z₂ symmetry)". Iron is an isotropic ferromagnet in the 3D Heisenberg class (O(3)), whose exponents differ from 3D Ising (β ≈ 0.37 vs 0.326). Question 4 explanation repeats "iron and water".
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/response-functions-definition.md, Explainer: "The fluctuation-dissipation theorem, which you studied as a prerequisite". It is not a listed prerequisite. Possible missing prereq: fluctuation-dissipation-theorem -> response-functions-definition.

- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/response-functions-susceptibilities.md, Question 3 explanation and Explainer: the heat capacity "also diverges" and "all response functions diverge together". When α < 0 (3D XY, 3D Heisenberg) the heat capacity stays finite with a cusp.

Iron is 3D Heisenberg. Core Idea "binary fluids, ferromagnets, superconductors have identical exponents" has the same problem: these fall into different classes (Ising, Ising or Heisenberg, XY).
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/scaling-invariance-universality.md, Question 2: "The critical exponents of the 3D Ising model are the same for a square lattice as for a triangular lattice." Square and triangular are 2D lattices; the statement needs 2D Ising or 3D lattices (simple cubic, bcc).
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/second-virial-coefficient.md, Explainer: "PV/NkT = 1 + B₂(T)/V + B₃(T)/V² + …" with B₂ = −½∫f 4πr² dr (a per-molecule volume). These are inconsistent: with that B₂ the series is 1 + B₂N/V + …, or V must be the molar volume with B₂ per mole. Also "obeys PV = NkT to first order regardless of density" holds only at low density.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/spin-glasses-quenched-disorder.md, Question 4 answer: "qEA is non-zero in the spin glass phase and zero in both the paramagnetic and (trivially, in the opposite sense) ferromagnetic descriptions". Earlier in the same answer qEA is non-zero for a ferromagnet, which is correct; the closing sentence contradicts it. Core Idea "with only short-range correlations" is also questionable: the spin-glass phase has long-range Edwards-Anderson (squared) correlations.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md, Core Idea: "can develop a state with lower symmetry when the free energy cost is outweighed by entropy gain". This is backwards: ordering lowers energy and costs entropy; the ordered state wins when the energy gain outweighs the entropy loss (low T).
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/static-structure-factor.md, Question 0: a peak at k₀ = 2π/(2.8 Å) is read as an interparticle spacing ≈ 2.8 Å. For dense liquids the main peak sits at k₀ ≈ (1.1–1.2)·2π/d, so 2π/k₀ underestimates the neighbor spacing; "a peak in g(r) at r ≈ 2π/k₀" is only rough.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/statistical-ensembles-intro.md, Question 3 explanation: "The ensemble that makes the calculation tractable is always the right one to use"; Explainer says both "This is not a matter of taste; it reflects the actual physical situation" and "most convenient mathematically is therefore the right one to use regardless of the physical setup". Ensemble equivalence fails for fluctuations, small systems, long-range interactions (e.g. self-gravitating systems) and at first-order transitions.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/superconductivity-bcs-theory.md, Core Idea "excitations cost finite energy, yielding zero resistance" and Question 2 (keyed true). Zero resistance comes from phase rigidity of the condensate; gapless superconductors also have zero resistance, so the gap is not the reason.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/superconductivity-bcs-theory.md, Explainer: "by the time electron 2 arrives ... the lattice has relaxed and the local positive charge density is still elevated". Self-contradictory; should read "has not yet relaxed".
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/superconductivity-bcs-theory.md, Explainer and Question 3 explanation: heavier isotopes lower T_c by "weakening the phonon coupling". In BCS the coupling N(0)V is independent of M; T_c falls because the energy scale ω_D ∝ M^(−1/2) falls. Explainer also says both predictions "provided strong evidence ... before the full BCS theory was published", but a prediction cannot precede the theory; the isotope effect (1950) was an observation that preceded it.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/superconductivity-bcs-theory.md, Question 4 explanation: "The analogy to a superfluid (like helium-4) is exact". BCS pairs overlap strongly and are not a dilute Bose gas; the analogy is qualitative.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/superfluidity-bosons.md, Explainer: "This lattice of vortices — the Abrikosov lattice — appears in superfluid ⁴He". "Abrikosov lattice" names the flux-line lattice in type-II superconductors; in superfluids it is called a vortex lattice. Also "For irrotational flow, ∇φ is uniform" (irrotational does not imply uniform).
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/superfluidity.md, Explainer: "enough energy to break a Cooper-pair analog or create a quantized vortex". ⁴He has no pairs; the relevant excitations are phonons, rotons and vortices. Also Ψ = √(ρ_s)e^{iθ} and Question 4 explanation "the superfluid component (the condensate)" conflate superfluid density with condensate density (in ⁴He the condensate is roughly a tenth of the superfluid at T → 0).
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/symmetry-breaking-phase-transitions.md, Explainer: Goldstone modes include "the spin waves in a magnet, sound waves in a crystal, and the photon itself". The photon is a gauge boson, not a Goldstone boson of a broken symmetry.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/thermal-conductivity-kinetic.md, Explainer and Question 1 explanation: "κ = (5/2)(k_B / σ_eff) √(k_B T / πm)". The standard first Chapman-Enskog result for hard spheres of diameter d is κ = (75/64)(k_B/d²)√(k_BT/πm), i.e. κ = (15/4)(k_B/m)η; the 5/2 prefactor does not match. Question 0 explanation credits Maxwell with the pressure-independence of κ; Maxwell's 1860 prediction was for viscosity.
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/thermodynamic-limit-statmech.md, Explainer says the limit "is an extremely good approximation" and later "The thermodynamic limit is not an approximation"; Question 2 is keyed false while its explanation calls observed sharpness "an extreme approximation". Also "the average energy in the canonical ensemble fluctuates" should be "the energy fluctuates".
- domains/physics/statistical-mechanics/time-correlation-functions.md, Core Idea "relax to zero for ergodic systems" and Question 1 explanation. Decay of correlations requires mixing, which is stronger than ergodicity, and C(t) tends to ⟨A⟩², which is zero only for zero-mean observables.
