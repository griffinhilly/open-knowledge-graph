### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- almost-sure-convergence.md, Core Idea: "This is the strongest form of convergence". Almost sure convergence and L^p convergence do not imply each other, so neither is "strongest". (I avoided writing a misconception that would contradict this line.)
- convergence-in-lp.md, Explainer: "L^p convergence is stronger than convergence in probability but weaker than almost sure convergence". Almost sure convergence does not imply L^p convergence (n·1_{[0,1/n]} → 0 a.s. with E|Xₙ| = 1). The file's own Q3 explanation says the two modes do not imply each other. Also Q3 stem "for almost most ω" is garbled.
- borel-cantelli-lemmas.md, Q3 explanation: "counterexamples where Σ P(A_n) = ∞ but P(lim sup A_n) = 0 (e.g., if A_n = A for all n ... the lim sup equals A)". That example gives P(lim sup) = P(A) > 0, not 0. A correct example is A_n = [0, 1/n] on [0, 1].
- borel-sigma-algebra.md, Q3 explanation and Explainer: "constructing any non-Borel set requires the Axiom of Choice" and "every set you can explicitly describe using a rule or formula is Borel". False: analytic non-Borel sets (e.g., Suslin's examples) are explicitly definable and need no choice. Choice is needed for non-Lebesgue-measurable sets such as the Vitali set. Q3 stem "Most subset of ℝ is a Borel set" is garbled.
- cramer-rao-lower-bound.md, Core Idea: "equality holds iff T is the UMVUE". Attaining the bound implies UMVUE, but a UMVUE need not attain it (s² for σ² with μ unknown has variance 2σ⁴/(n−1) > 2σ⁴/n). Explainer's "This happens precisely in exponential family distributions" is also too strong (needs the right function of the parameter).
- confidence-intervals-rigorous-theory.md, Core Idea, Q4 explanation and Explainer: "Shortest confidence intervals use the critical region from the UMP test". Inverting UMP tests gives uniformly most accurate confidence bounds (smallest false-coverage probability), which is not the same as shortest length in general.
- confidence-intervals-rigorous.md, Q1 explanation: pivot and test-inversion constructions "are mathematically equivalent and produce the same intervals". Test inversion is more general and need not produce the pivot interval; the claim is overstated.
- independence-of-sigma-algebras.md, Q1 explanation: "Option A covers only CDF-level events". A factored joint CDF is equivalent to independence (π-λ argument), so option A is also correct. Q3 (answer false) is also wrong: independence holds iff the joint law is the product of the marginal laws, for every distribution type.
- convergence-in-probability.md, Q0 explanation: for Xₙ = n w.p. 1/n, "Var(Xₙ) = n·(1/n) = 1". Actually E[Xₙ] = 1, E[Xₙ²] = n, Var = n − 1. Q3 explanation: SLLN "requiring the same conditions plus finite variance (or just finite first moment under certain formulations)" is misleading; finite first moment suffices for i.i.d. (Etemadi/Kolmogorov).
- conjugate-priors.md, Explainer: "β like α−1 prior failures" should read β−1.
- bayesian-inference-foundations.md, Q0 explanation "The prior always 'smooths' the estimate relative to the MLE" and Q4 explanation "Even a uniform prior shifts the posterior mean from the MLE in finite samples". With 5 heads in 10 flips the uniform-prior posterior mean equals the MLE (6/12 = 0.5).
- method-of-moments.md, Q1 explanation: MOM = MLE for the exponential is called "a coincidence specific to the exponential distribution". It also holds for Poisson, Bernoulli and normal, since in exponential families with T(x) = x the first-moment equation is the likelihood equation.
- moment-generating-functions.md, Q3 explanation: "showing that MGFs converge is equivalent to showing that distributions converge". Only one direction holds. Explainer: MGF as "the practical, computable tool for distributions with finite moments" is misleading; the lognormal has all moments but no MGF for t > 0.
