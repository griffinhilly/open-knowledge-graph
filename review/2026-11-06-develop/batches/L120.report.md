## Flags

Factual or framing problems in existing text:
- genre-evolution-publishing-markets.md, Explainer: "the printing press's affordances for serialization" as a cause of the Gothic novel's emergence. The printing press was centuries old by the 1760s. Early Gothic is more usually tied to circulating libraries and a growing reading public than to serialization.
- jackson-domestic-horror-uncanny.md, Explainer: "that a mother is poisoning her son". I know of no Jackson work with this plot. In We Have Always Lived in the Castle, a younger sister poisons the family. Also "There are no external threats in Jackson" is too absolute (Hill House leaves a supernatural reading open). The Core Idea calls her settings "suburban", but "The Lottery" is set in a village.
- king-everyday-evil-supernatural.md, Explainer: lists "a rabid dog in Cujo" as an example of "supernatural horror". Cujo's threat is natural (rabies).
- hard-sf-extrapolation-rigor-scientific.md, Explainer: gives "faster-than-light travel" as an example premise for hard SF. This conflicts with the file's own claim that every physical law must follow established science. FTL is a standard marker of softer SF.
- locked-room-mystery-impossible-crime.md, Core Idea and Q1 options: list "outright fantastic elements" as a solution type, and the Explainer says secret passages are "fair game". Both conflict with the fair-play tradition the Explainer leans on (Knox's rules bar supernatural solutions and limit secret passages).
- mystery-genre-puzzle-structure.md, How It's Best Learned: suggests And Then There Were None as a model of fair-play clue tracking. Its solution arrives through a written confession and is hard to deduce. It also presents "contemporary unreliable narrator mysteries" as the fair-play violation, though the classic case is Christie's The Murder of Roger Ackroyd (1926).
- psychological-horror-mind-dissolution.md, How It's Best Learned: suggests "The Lottery" for tracking uncertainty about the protagonist's sanity. That story has no such uncertainty. "The Yellow Wallpaper" or The Haunting of Hill House would fit.
- paranormal-romance-supernatural-love.md, How It's Best Learned and Explainer: call Anita Blake and Psy-Changeling deconstructions of the subgenre set against Twilight. Anita Blake (from 1993) started as urban fantasy, years before Twilight (2005). Psy-Changeling is generally read as straight paranormal romance.
- le-guin-anthropological-thought-experiment.md, Explainer: "They're not dystopias ... nor utopias". The Dispossessed is subtitled "An Ambiguous Utopia". Also has a grammar slip: "inhabitants are ambisexually".
- grimdark-nihilism-decay.md, Core Idea: "all actors pursue power, survival, or advantage" overgeneralizes. Many grimdark works include characters with real loyalties and moral struggle. Q3 (true-false, answer false) depends on this claim.
- literary-fiction-vs-genre-debate.md, Explainer: "Irish literatures have always mixed genre elements with literary prestige" is vague and unsupported. Typo "Magaret Atwood".
- genre-fiction-vs-literary.md, How It's Best Learned: lists Blood Meridian as "genre fiction acclaimed as literary", but the Explainer says it was published and treated as literary fiction. Q3's explanation attributes the Atwood example to the Core Idea, but it appears in How It's Best Learned.
- mystery-technique-misdirection-clues.md, Core Idea: "guiding attention toward the true culprit" reads as the opposite of misdirection. It probably means guiding attention away until the reveal.
- noir-moral-ambiguity-corruption.md, Explainer: says "individual virtue is irrelevant" and later "individual choice and personal code matter". This is internally inconsistent. The topic also largely duplicates noir-aesthetic-moral-corruption.
- gothic-romance-desire-danger.md, How It's Best Learned: calls The Thirteenth Tale (2006) "canonical" beside Jane Eyre and Rebecca, which is a stretch.
- high-fantasy-secondary-world.md, Q4 explanation: describes dark fantasy as "Earth-like", which is not a defining trait.
