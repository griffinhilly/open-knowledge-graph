## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. gabriel-garcia-marquez-solitude-cyclical.md, Explainer: "A grandmother floats up to heaven while folding laundry". In the novel it is Remedios the Beauty, a young woman, who ascends while folding sheets. She is not a grandmother.
2. dante-alighieri-divine-comedy.md, Core Idea: "the first major European work written in the vernacular rather than Latin". Wrong. Major vernacular works came before it, for example the Song of Roland, the Nibelungenlied, Chrétien de Troyes and troubadour lyric.
3. chaucer-canterbury-tales.md, Explainer: "Before Chaucer, serious literature was written in French or Latin; English was provincial". Also chaucer-vernacular-narrative-Canterbury.md, Explainer and q0 explanation, which say the same. Overstated. Old English poetry such as Beowulf came centuries earlier. Langland and the Gawain/Pearl poet wrote serious English verse at the same time as Chaucer.
4. classical-antiquity-literature-overview.md, Core Idea and Explainer: Roman literature developed "new forms like satire and philosophical dialogue". The Romans did claim satire as their own. Philosophical dialogue is Greek (Plato), though.
5. classical-antiquity-literature-overview.md, Explainer: "A medieval writer could adapt Greek dramatic structure to a religious mystery play". Doubtful. Greek drama was little known in the medieval Latin West, and mystery plays grew out of liturgical drama.
6. enlightenment-satire-social-critique.md, Core Idea: satire was "creating forms like the mock-heroic poem". Mock-epic is much older; the ancient Battle of Frogs and Mice is one example. The Explainer also calls "A Modest Proposal" a "satirical tale". It is a pamphlet or essay.
7. flaubert-madame-bovary-style.md, Core Idea and Explainer: free indirect discourse is presented as "Flaubert's innovation". Overstated. Austen and others used it earlier, though Flaubert used it in a famously systematic way.
8. franz-kafka-alienation-absurdity.md, Explainer: "Earlier literature had separated realistic narration from surreal content. Kafka unified them." Overstated. Gogol's "The Nose", for one, tells an absurd event in a deadpan way.
9. existentialist-literature-philosophy.md, Explainer: lists Camus and Heidegger as existentialist philosophers. Both rejected the label. Calling them existentialists is common but disputed.
10. aestheticism-movement-art-autonomy.md, q3 (true-false): "Modernism's rejection of traditional form and structure in favor of realism". Modernism did not favor realism. The explanation says Aestheticism did not anticipate Modernism's "rejection of tradition", which muddles the item. Rewrite it.
11. dickens-novels-urban-society.md, Explainer: typo "populally".
12. contemporary-literary-fiction-21st-century.md and gabriel-garcia-marquez-solitude-cyclical.md: these pages frame García Márquez as "non-Western" narrative tradition. That framing is contested. He wrote in Spanish within Western literary history. This is a judgment call, not a clear error.
