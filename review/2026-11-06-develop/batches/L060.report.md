## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
1. conservative-fields-potential.md, Core Idea: "Magnetic fields are irrotational models of conservation." Magnetic fields generally have nonzero curl (Ampere's law, curl B = mu0 J). They are irrotational only in current-free regions. The sentence should be removed or replaced (gravity or electrostatic fields are the standard conservative examples).
2. conservative-fields-potential.md, question 1 explanation: "A region that does not *encircle* the origin is simply connected (no holes)." A region can avoid encircling the origin and still have holes elsewhere. The answer choice is fine for this field, but the stated reason is false in general.
3. conservative-vector-fields-potential.md, Core Idea: "If F is conservative and curl-free ..., then ∫ F · dr around any closed path is zero." Conservative alone is enough; the "and curl-free" clause is redundant and suggests an extra condition is needed.
4. conservative-vector-fields-potential.md, Explainer: lists "magnetic forces" among non-conservative fields where "work dissipated or injected depends on how you travel." The magnetic force on a moving charge does no work, so it is a poor example of path-dependent work.
5. contour-maps.md, Explainer and question 2: "contour lines never cross" / "crossing contours are logically impossible for any well-defined function." Contours for different values cannot cross, but a single level set can cross itself at a saddle (x² − y² = 0 is two crossing lines). The same Explainer then says saddles show "curves crossing without closing," which contradicts its own rule.
6. curl-divergence.md, Explainer: "A field with ∇×F = 0 everywhere is called irrotational or conservative" and the following sentence treat zero curl as equivalent to conservative with no simply-connected condition. Same issue in question 2 ("Which of the following must be true?" with answer "F can be written as the gradient"), which is not guaranteed on a domain with holes (⟨−y, x⟩/(x² + y²)).
7. curl-divergence.md, Explainer and short-answer: curl's "magnitude gives the angular speed." For rigid rotation ⟨−ωy, ωx, 0⟩ the curl is ⟨0, 0, 2ω⟩, so the magnitude is twice the local angular speed.
8. curl-and-divergence-operators.md, Explainer: "the line integral of F is path-independent if and only if curl(F) = 0." The "if" direction needs a simply connected domain; the sentence just before states that caveat, but this sentence drops it.
9. curvature-and-torsion.md, Core Idea: "oscillating plane" should be "osculating plane."
10. curvature-and-torsion.md, short-answer (question 5): says |r′ × r″| "scales with the square of speed." Under a constant-factor speedup c, r′ scales by c and r″ by c², so the cross product scales by c³, which is why the denominator is |r′|³. The stated reasoning (square, plus an extra arc-length factor) is wrong.
11. cross-product-geometry-3d.md, Explainer: "The cross product also produces an outward normal vector to a surface." r_u × r_v is a normal, but whether it points outward depends on the parametrization order.
12. differentiability-multivariate.md, question 1 explanation: "The hierarchy is: differentiable ⟹ continuous ⟹ (partial derivatives may exist)." Garbled: continuity does not lead to partials. The correct chain is continuous partials ⟹ differentiable ⟹ continuous (as the Explainer states).
13. applications-multivariable.md, Explainer: "The condition for conservatism is curl F = 0." Needs a simply connected domain for sufficiency.
