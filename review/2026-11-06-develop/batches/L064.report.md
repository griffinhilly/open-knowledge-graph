## Flags

Suspected factual errors in existing text:
- coordinate-systems-projections.md, question 1 explanation: "UTM keeps maximum scale distortion below 0.04%". The 0.9996 scale factor gives -0.04% at the central meridian. At zone edges near the equator the scale factor is about 1.0010, so the maximum error is about 0.1%.
- multispectral-imaging.md, question 1 (true-false, answer false): the explanation says band ratios and indices make "information content exceed the raw band count". Indices are functions of the 4 measured values and add no independent information. They only make some information easier to use. The keyed answer is doubtful as written.
- multispectral-imaging.md, question 2 answer: says the 1.6 um band "reveals clay mineral absorption features". Clay Al-OH absorptions sit near 2.2 um (Landsat band 7), not 1.6 um.
- gis-fundamentals.md, Explainer: "expensive desktop software (ArcGIS, QGIS)". QGIS is free and open source.
- remote-sensing-oceans.md, Explainer: "the upcoming PACE mission". PACE launched in February 2024. Same paragraph group: "SAR altimetry (Sentinel-3, SWOT)". SWOT's main instrument is a Ka-band radar interferometer (KaRIn), so "SAR altimetry" is loose for SWOT.
- remote-sensing-atmosphere.md, Explainer: "Cloud-profiling radar (on CloudSat and GPM)". GPM carries the Dual-frequency Precipitation Radar. Only CloudSat carries the Cloud Profiling Radar.
- remote-sensing-atmosphere.md, question 1 (true-false, answer true): "geostationary weather satellites can directly measure atmospheric temperature profiles". Profiles are retrieved, not measured directly, and the current GOES-R series has no dedicated sounder. The keyed answer is arguable.
- optical-remote-sensing.md, Explainer: "a single Landsat scene covers 185 x 185 km at 30 m resolution in seven spectral bands". Landsat 8/9 OLI has eight 30 m bands (including cirrus) plus a 15 m panchromatic band. Minor.
