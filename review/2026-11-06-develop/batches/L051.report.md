## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- bogoliubov-transformation.md, Explainer: "In a weakly interacting Bose gas near its condensation temperature". Bogoliubov theory applies well below T_c (near T = 0), where nearly all atoms are in the condensate. Q5 explanation also calls the linear dispersion an "energy gap". It is gapless.
- bose-einstein-condensation-statmech.md, Core Idea: "the hallmark of a quantum phase transition". BEC at finite T_c is a thermal transition. A quantum phase transition happens at T = 0. True-false Q3 says "|ψ|² equal to the fraction of particles". Its explanation (correctly) says density n₀. Short-answer Q5 implies phase coherence alone gives superfluidity. The ideal Bose gas condenses but is not superfluid (Landau critical velocity zero); interactions are needed.
- bose-einstein-condensation.md, Core Idea: the T_c formula "(2π)^{2/3}(ℏ²n/mk_B)^{2/3}/k_B" is dimensionally wrong. Correct: k_BT_c = (2πℏ²/m)(n/ζ(3/2))^{2/3}. Core Idea also says "finite density of states at k=0". The density of states vanishes there (as Q2 says). Explainer and true-false Q4: condensate fraction "1 − (T/T_c)³". For a uniform 3D gas it is 1 − (T/T_c)^{3/2}; the cube applies to a harmonic trap. Explainer's superfluidity sentence ("costs a finite energy even at arbitrarily small flow speeds") is garbled. It also implies the ideal gas is superfluid.
- bose-einstein-distribution-statistics.md, true-false Q4: "Below T_c, most particles ... occupy the ground state" is marked false. For the 3D ideal gas, most particles are in the ground state below about 0.63 T_c, so the item is wrong or ambiguous. Short-answer Q5: "exp(...) is bounded below by 1" does not explain why the excited-state sum is finite. Finiteness depends on the density of states (it fails in 2D).
- brownian-motion.md, Explainer: "Drop a grain of pollen into still water ... it jitters". Brown observed small particles released from pollen grains, not whole grains. Whole grains are too large to show visible Brownian motion.
- canonical-partition-function.md, Explainer: "N-state system with equally spaced levels ... Z = 1/(1 − e^(−βε))". That is the infinite ladder, not N states. Its claim "⟨E⟩ saturates" at high T is wrong for the ladder, where ⟨E⟩ ≈ kT grows.
- chapman-enskog-expansion.md: "η ∝ √T ... confirmed experimentally". Real gases show η rising roughly as T^0.6–0.8. Only the qualitative rise is confirmed; √T is the hard-sphere result.
- chemical-potential.md, MC Q2 explanation and Explainer: "pressure increase lowers μ of the liquid phase". Raising P raises both μ values (∂μ/∂P = v). The vapor's rises more, so the liquid becomes relatively lower.
- clausius-clapeyron-equation.md, Core Idea: "explains why ice melts at higher temperatures under pressure (small ΔV gives positive dP/dT)". This is wrong. Ice's ΔV is negative, so pressure lowers the melting point. The file's own Q1 and Explainer say so.
- collective-excitations-phonons.md, short-answer Q5 explanation lists "Cooper pairs" with normal-mode quasiparticles. Cooper pairs are bound pairs, not collective normal modes.
- correlation-functions-statmech.md, Core Idea: "Near criticality, G(r) ~ exp(-r/ξ)". This should be the connected function G − m², and the Ornstein-Zernike form has a power-law prefactor. Q5 explanation credits renormalization group with explaining opalescence. Ornstein-Zernike theory explained it first; RG explained the exponents.
- critical-exponents.md, Q1 and Explainer; critical-phenomena-critical-exponents.md, Q2 and Explainer; critical-phenomena-statmech.md, Q1 and Explainer: iron is said to share β ≈ 0.326 / 3D Ising exponents with liquid-gas systems. Iron is an isotropic (3D Heisenberg) ferromagnet with β ≈ 0.34–0.37. Uniaxial magnets are in the Ising class, not iron. critical-phenomena-critical-exponents.md Explainer also implies helium (XY class) and an Ising magnet have "mathematically identical" fluctuations.
- critical-phenomena-statmech.md, Core Idea: "heat capacity ... all diverge". With α < 0, C shows a cusp, not a divergence. The Q2 explanation repeats this. "Long-range order correlations" at the critical point is misleading.
- debye-model-lattice-dynamics.md, Core Idea: "C_V → 12π⁴/5 (Nk) at T ≪ T_D" is missing the factor (T/T_D)³. Explainer: "The Einstein model (which this topic builds toward)". Einstein is a prerequisite, not a successor.
- debye-model-solids.md, Explainer: "lower frequencies crowd together more densely in frequency space". This is wrong: g ∝ ω² means higher frequencies are denser. MC Q2 explanation: "accessible modes grow as (k_BT/ℏ)² ... total energy U ∝ T³ and C_V = dU/dT ∝ T³". This is inconsistent. The mode count goes as T³, U ∝ T⁴ and C ∝ T³.
- debye-temperature.md, Q1 and Q4: diamond at 300 K (T/Θ_D ≈ 0.13–0.15) is said to "follow the T³ law". That ratio is far outside the T³ regime.
- density-of-states-fermi-gas.md, Explainer defines g(E) "per unit volume", but then g(E_F) = 3N/(2E_F) is a total, not per volume.
- distribution-functions-moments.md, MC Q2: zero skewness plus zero excess kurtosis is said to imply a Gaussian "uniquely". This is false: all cumulants beyond the second must vanish. Short-answer Q5 says the "mean and variance vary smoothly" near a critical point. The variance of the order parameter (susceptibility) diverges there.
- einstein-model-solids.md, Explainer: the Einstein model "introduced the idea of phonons". Einstein quantized lattice vibrations; the phonon concept and name came later.
- ergodic-hypothesis.md, MC Q2 and Explainer: the 2D oscillator "explores only a 1D curve on the 2D energy surface". The energy surface is 3D (4D phase space). With either frequency ratio the motion stays on a 2-torus, because E₁ and E₂ are separately conserved, so neither case is ergodic. The Explainer frames the irrational case as filling "the surface". Q4 explanation: "mixing timescale is the molecular collision time (~10⁻¹² s)" for gases. Gas collision times at STP are nearer 10⁻¹⁰ s.
- ergodicity-breaking.md, Explainer: a glass is "a metastable state that will never relax". Glasses age and relax slowly; "never" overstates this. "Replica symmetry breaking" is established for mean-field spin glasses; for finite-dimensional ones it is debated.
- fermi-dirac-distribution-statistics.md, Explainer: the distribution comes from "maximizing the grand partition function". Z_G is evaluated, not maximized. "(3/2 Nk per electron)" mixes per-electron and total.
- fermi-gas-finite-temperature.md, Q1 explanation: "baffled 19th-century physics". The electronic heat-capacity puzzle arose with the Drude model (1900) and was resolved in 1927–28. Short-answer Q5 explanation: the correction "(kT/E_F)² ~ 0.003" is wrong; (π²/12)(kT/E_F)² is about 10⁻⁵ at room temperature. Its claim that the shift "becomes important near absolute zero" is backwards, since the shift vanishes as T → 0.
- fermi-gas-ideal-quantum.md, Q1, Q5 and Explainer: "about 200 times smaller". With C_V = (π²/2)(kT/E_F)Nk and kT/E_F ≈ 0.005, the ratio to 1.5 Nk is about 1/60. Explainer also says heat capacity at low T is "dominated by lattice vibrations (phonons ∝ T³)". At the lowest temperatures the linear electronic term dominates. "n ~ 10²⁸ m⁻³ giving E_F ~ 5 eV" is loose: 10²⁸ gives about 1.7 eV.
