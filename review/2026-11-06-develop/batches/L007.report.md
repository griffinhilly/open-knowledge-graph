## Flags

Factual or internal-consistency concerns in existing text:

1. present-bias.md, question 0 explanation and Core Idea: "sophistication can also lead to 'preproperation' — giving up on plans prematurely". In O'Donoghue and Rabin (1999), preproperation means doing something too early, typically when rewards are immediate and costs are delayed. It does not mean giving up. The question 2 answer ("a naive agent ... may actually start the task and ... eventually make partial progress") also does not match their result. Their result is that sophistication can make things worse through preproperation in immediate-reward tasks.
2. neuroeconomics.md, Explainer paragraph 4: "accepting an unfair offer recruits the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex ... suggesting that accepting unfairness requires actively overriding an emotional rejection response". The next sentence says disrupting dlPFC by TMS lowers rejection rates. That is the Knoch et al. (2006) finding. It implies the opposite: dlPFC supports rejecting by overriding self-interest. The two sentences conflict.
3. intertemporal-choice-behavioral.md, question 3 answer: "beta between 0.6 and 0.9, meaning people treat the present as 10-40% more valuable". 1/0.9 ≈ 1.11 and 1/0.6 ≈ 1.67, so the range is about 11-67%. The "10-40%" figure is 1 − beta, not the premium. The beta range also differs from hyperbolic-discounting.md ("0.5 to 0.8"). The course gives two different ranges.
4. behavioral-public-policy.md (Core Idea) and nudge-theory.md (Explainer): both list the US Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA) as a nudge unit. OIRA is the White House regulatory review office. The dedicated US behavioral unit was the Social and Behavioral Sciences Team.
5. status-quo-bias.md, question 2 explanation: "opt-out systems ... have donation rates near 90%, while opt-in countries have rates near 15%". These are consent or registration rates, not donation rates. default-effects.md uses "consent rates" correctly.
6. ultimatum-dictator-games.md, question 2 answer: "Lamelara" should be spelled "Lamalera".
7. choice-architecture.md, Explainer: the jam study is presented as settled. Later meta-analyses (for example Scheibehenne et al., 2010) found a small and highly variable average choice-overload effect. Suggest a hedge.
8. social-preferences.md, question 0 explanation: "This pattern is remarkably stable across cultures". The same file's Explainer reports large variation across 15 small-scale societies (Henrich et al.). Minor internal tension.
9. endowment-effect.md, Explainer: "one of the most well-replicated" findings. The WTA-WTP gap is debated: Plott and Zeiler (2005) argued it shrinks under certain procedural controls. Consider a hedge.
