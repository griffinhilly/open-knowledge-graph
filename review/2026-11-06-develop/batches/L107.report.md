## Flags

Factual errors in existing text:
- aromatic-amino-acid-catabolism.md, Core Idea: "All three aromatic amino acids are exclusively glucogenic". Phe, Tyr and Trp are both glucogenic and ketogenic (fumarate + acetoacetate; Trp gives acetyl-CoA). The file's own Explainer says tyrosine is "both glucogenic and ketogenic".
- aromatic-amino-acid-catabolism.md, Explainer: "regenerated by dihydrobiopterin reductase". The enzyme is dihydropteridine reductase.
- branched-chain-amino-acid-catabolism.md, Q2 statement (answer true) and Explainer ("This makes leucine unique among the common amino acids"): "Leucine is the only purely ketogenic common amino acid". Lysine is also purely ketogenic.
- branched-chain-amino-acid-catabolism.md, Q3 explanation: "Muscle expresses high BCAT and substantial BCKDH activity". Muscle BCKDH activity is relatively low and mostly in the inactive phosphorylated form; liver has high BCKDH. Muscle is the main site of the BCAT step.
- carbohydrate-homeostasis.md, Q2 (index 2) explanation: "The liver ... is the only organ that can export glucose". The kidney also expresses glucose-6-phosphatase and releases glucose, especially in prolonged fasting.
- cholesterol-synthesis.md, Core Idea and Q2 (index 2) explanation: "allosteric feedback inhibition (cholesterol inhibits HMG-CoA reductase)". Sterols act mainly by SREBP retention and by Insig-mediated degradation of HMG-CoA reductase. Direct allosteric inhibition by cholesterol is not the standard mechanism. Q4 (index 4) answer builds a "milliseconds" timescale on this.
- cholesterol-synthesis.md, Q3 (index 3), answer true: "cells cannot synthesize cholesterol when they are in a low-energy state". This overstates the case. AMPK reduces HMG-CoA reductase activity; it does not abolish synthesis.
- cholesterol-metabolism-and-regulation.md, Core Idea: "esterification by ACAT also suppress synthesis". ACAT lowers free cholesterol, which would relieve SREBP suppression, not add to it. Cholesterol taken up from LDL is what suppresses synthesis.
- citric-acid-cycle-regulation.md, Core Idea: "Citrate synthase ... is inhibited by ... acetyl-CoA". Acetyl-CoA is citrate synthase's substrate, not an inhibitor.
- competitive-enzyme-inhibition.md, How It's Best Learned: "competitive inhibition shows parallel lines with the same y-intercept". Competitive lines intersect on the y-axis; parallel lines indicate uncompetitive inhibition. The Explainer is correct.
- competitive-enzyme-inhibition.md, Core Idea: competitive inhibitors "can be reversible (weak binding) or irreversible (covalent modification)". Irreversible inhibitors do not follow competitive kinetics (Vmax unchanged, overcome by substrate).
- dna-polymerase-structure-and-fidelity.md, Core Idea: geometry + proofreading reduce error rates "to ~10⁻10 per base". The Explainer says the polymerase alone reaches about 10⁻⁷, and 10⁻¹⁰ requires mismatch repair.
- dna-replication-machinery.md, Core Idea: "Pol I and Pol III removing primers". Pol III lacks 5'→3' exonuclease and does not remove primers; Pol I (and RNase H) does.
- enzyme-classification-nomenclature.md, Core Idea and Explainer: "six major categories". Since 2018 the EC list has seven classes (EC 7, translocases). The Core Idea also says names are "assigned systematically once the mechanism is characterized"; EC assignment is based on the overall reaction, not the mechanism.
- fad-fadh2-and-other-redox-carriers.md, Core Idea: FAD "accepts two electrons and one proton, forming FADH₂". FADH₂ formation takes two electrons and two protons, as the Explainer says. The Core Idea also implies all FADH₂ enters at Complex II; β-oxidation FADH₂ enters via ETF/ETF-QO.
- fatty-acid-synthesis.md, Explainer: "four reactions per cycle, each consuming NADPH". Only the two reductions use NADPH (2 per cycle, 14 per palmitate).
- glycogen-metabolism.md, Q2 (index 2) explanation: "A linear polymer of the same mass would present only two ends". Phosphorylase acts only at non-reducing ends, and a linear chain has one.
- lipolysis-and-fatty-acid-mobilization.md, Q0 explanation is garbled: "because glucagon activates phosphodiesterase would degrade cAMP". Also, glucagon's direct lipolytic role in human adipocytes is debated. Catecholamines and falling insulin are the better-established drivers.
- genetic-code-wobble-pairing.md, Q4 (index 4) answer ends "rather than 61 — one for each sense codon", which reads garbled. Its explanation says a glycine tRNA with inosine reads GGU/GGC/GGA. Inosine-containing tRNAs in most organisms do not include glycine.
- fatty-acid-structure-and-classification.md, Q2 (index 2): the statement "Most unsaturated fatty acids have lower melting points..." is arguably true as worded (most are cis). The "false" key hinges only on the word "any".
- irreversible-enzyme-inhibition.md, Core Idea equates "Irreversible" with "(mechanism-based)". Mechanism-based (suicide) inhibitors are a subset; aspirin, for example, is not mechanism-based.
- lipoproteins-structure-and-transport.md, Q0 and Q3 explanations treat raising HDL as protective. Trials of HDL-raising drugs have not shown event reduction, so this is overstated.
