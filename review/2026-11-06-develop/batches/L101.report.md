## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- planetary-albedo-temperature-feedback.md, Q4: "An increase in a planet's average albedo typically leads to a lower equilibrium surface temperature." answer false. With all else equal, higher albedo lowers equilibrium temperature; the answer should be true. The Venus comparison changes the greenhouse at the same time.
- planetary-cloud-physics.md, Q4: "A planet with very high albedo will generally have a lower surface temperature than a similar planet with lower albedo." answer false. Same error. Also Q1 and Q4 attribute Venus's hot surface mainly to its clouds trapping infrared; the CO₂ greenhouse is the main cause, and Venus's clouds are thought to have a net cooling effect.
- planetary-system-stability.md, Q3 + Explainer: "systems with spacing greater than about 3.5 mutual Hill radii tend to be long-term stable." 2√3 ≈ 3.46 is the Hill-stability limit for two planets on circular orbits. Systems of three or more planets need much wider spacing (around 10 mutual Hill radii or more) to last billions of years. The answer "true" is misleading. The same 3.5 figure appears in multi-planet-system-architecture.md (Q2 explanation, Q5 answer, Explainer).
- n-body-planetary-dynamics.md, Q4 + Explainer: attributes Mercury's roughly 1% instability chance to Jupiter and Saturn's near 5:2 "Great Inequality". Published work ties it to a secular resonance between the perihelion precession of Mercury and of Jupiter (g1–g5).
- planetary-accretion-chronology.md, Q4 explanation: "their concentrations in modern samples are zero and undetectable." Meteorites contain measurable cosmogenic Al-26 made by cosmic-ray exposure. Primordial Al-26 is gone, but the statement as written is false. Q5 answer also treats a uniform initial Al-26/Al-27 as fact, and that point is debated.
- planetary-magnetic-field-evolution.md, Core Idea: "Most planetary bodies (Moon, Mercury, Mars) lost their magnetic fields early." Mercury has a present-day field, as the file's own Explainer says. Q3 also links Earth's 3.5+ Gyr dynamo to inner-core crystallization, but the inner core may be under about 1.5 Gyr old.
- planetary-magnetospheres-and-solar-wind.md, Explainer: the standoff distance "depends on the cube root of the ratio of magnetic field strength to solar wind pressure". The correct scaling is r ∝ B^(1/3) P^(−1/6). Also: "Jupiter's magnetic field is 20,000 times stronger than Earth's". It is Jupiter's magnetic moment that is about 20,000 times larger; its surface field is about 10–20 times stronger. And "large enough to engulf the Sun if it were visible" is garbled; the usual claim is that it would look larger than the full Moon in our sky. Q5 says Earth "does not suffer the same fate". Earth also loses atmosphere (polar wind, ion outflow), and the protective role of magnetic fields is debated.
- planetary-habitability-and-biosignatures.md: Core Idea lists a magnetic field as required, and Q4 calls Mars's field loss the cause of its atmospheric loss. The magnetic field's role in keeping an atmosphere is debated; Venus has no field and a thick atmosphere. Q3 says Venus "orbits at the inner edge of the Sun's habitable zone". Most estimates put Venus inside the inner edge. Explainer: methane "requires a continuous biological source to persist". This contradicts the same file's later point about serpentinization.
- satellite-formation-and-orbital-mechanics.md, Explainer: "the Moon orbits close to Earth's equatorial plane". The Moon's orbit is inclined about 5° to the ecliptic, so 18–29° to Earth's equator. Q5 says the impact sends debris from both bodies' mantles and calls the compositional match strong evidence. In standard simulations the Moon forms mostly from impactor material, so the Earth-like isotopes are an open problem for the model, not clean support.
- planetary-migration-mechanisms.md, Core Idea: "Type III migration (high-mass planets)". The Explainer correctly describes an intermediate, partial-gap regime. Q1 calls in-situ formation of hot Jupiters impossible and migration "observationally confirmed". In-situ formation and high-eccentricity migration are both still argued.
- orbital-resonance-capture.md, Core Idea: says the lock is due to "gravitational coupling through the disk". The planets' mutual gravity holds the resonance; the disk drives the migration.
- ring-particle-dynamics.md, Explainer: thickness ratio "comparable to a sheet of paper the size of a football field". 280,000 km / 20 m ≈ 1.4 × 10⁷. A 0.1 mm sheet at that ratio is about 1.4 km across, roughly 10 times larger than a football field.
- planetary-seismology.md: Mars core radius "about 1,830 km" (Q3 and Explainer) is the 2021 InSight value. Later analyses that include a molten silicate layer above the core revised it to about 1,650–1,700 km. The Q2 explanation sentence "The Moon's crust having no liquid core to speak of" is garbled.
- planetary-tectonics-comparative.md: the Core Idea calls the Moon and Mercury "no-tectonics systems", but the Explainer describes Mercury's thrust faults. The Explainer also says the Moon has been "tectonically dead for billions of years", but young lunar lobate scarps (under about 50 Myr) and shallow moonquakes point to recent contraction.
- planetary-thermal-modeling.md, Explainer and Q2: say Mars "lost its plate tectonics early". It is unclear whether Mars ever had plate tectonics. Q2 option A refers to "Planet B", which the stem never defines.
- mineral-physics-high-pressure.md: Q5 explanation describes "intensive variables (pressure and temperature per unit volume)", which is wrong. P and T are intensive, but they are not quantities per unit volume. Q1 explanation says a compositional boundary "would produce a gradual velocity change". A compositional boundary can be sharp. Q5 answer says phase transitions are "governed by pressure-temperature conditions alone"; composition also shifts them.
- surface-weathering-planetary-comparison.md: calls Venus's gas-solid weathering "negligible". Many studies argue those reactions are fast in geological terms. This also conflicts with regolith-and-surface-weathering.md, which describes active high-temperature chemical weathering on Venus. The Explainer gives Earth "a CO₂-rich atmosphere", which is wrong. It also says Martian chemical weathering "essentially ceased", which conflicts with the ongoing peroxide oxidation described in regolith-and-surface-weathering.md.
- volcanic-processes-and-landforms.md, Q1 + Explainer: "Mars's lower gravity allows lava to flow farther". The usual explanation is that lower gravity and a thick lithosphere support taller edifices, and that high effusion rates produce long flows. Lower gravity by itself slows flow. "Flood basalts ... cover thousands of square kilometers" understates them by orders of magnitude.
- transit-timing-variations-exoplanets.md, Explainer: claims TTVs "can distinguish between a massive planet on a circular orbit and a lighter planet on an eccentric one". Near-resonant sinusoidal TTVs suffer exactly that mass-eccentricity degeneracy; only extra signal such as "chopping" breaks it. "Kepler measured masses of hundreds of planets" by TTV is generous; it is closer to about 150 with good precision.
- tidal-orbital-evolution-long-term.md: Core Idea says migration is "typically inward" and links tides to "Venus hot runaway"; neither is standard. Q1 calls Io's eccentricity "enormous" (it is about 0.004). Q4 says the Moon's eccentricity is "slowly decreasing", but tides raised on Earth tend to increase it, and the net trend is debated.
- planetary-atmospheres-composition-structure.md, Core Idea: "primary outgassing during formation". Primary atmospheres are captured nebular gas, not outgassed.
- resonance-heating-icy-bodies.md and tidal-heating-moon-interiors.md: "the tidal bulge becomes fixed in space" is wrong; the bulge is fixed relative to the moon's surface. tidal-heating Q4 says "A circular orbit at any distance produces zero tidal heating", which ignores obliquity tides and non-synchronous rotation.
