## Flags

Suspected factual errors or problems in existing text:
- historical-semantics-and-terminology-change.md, Q0 option A: "Historicism — judging the past by the standards of the present". That definition is presentism. Historicism means close to the opposite. It is a distractor, but the label teaches a wrong definition. The Core Idea also says "abolitioner" (should be "abolitionist").
- historical-rhetoric-analysis.md, Explainer: "A Enlightenment philosophe" (typo, should be "An").
- identifying-causation-in-history.md, Explainer: says structural causes "are necessary but rarely sufficient", then says "Most historical causes are contributing rather than necessary". These two statements pull against each other. The fire analogy ("Remove any one layer and the fire may not start") also sits badly with the claim that the trigger was not necessary.
- intellectual-genealogies.md, Q3 explanation: calls privileging an origin "a form of anachronism (the 'genetic fallacy' in interpretation)". The genetic fallacy means judging an idea by its origin; it is not a kind of anachronism. Explainer: gives "Confucius anticipated virtue ethics" as an example of anachronism. Many scholars treat Confucian ethics as a form of virtue ethics, so the example is contested.
- literature-as-historical-evidence.md, Explainer: "reading for the grain of a text rather than against it". The paragraph then describes reading for silences and assumptions, which is usually called reading against the grain.
- local-community-history.md, Q1 explanation: the question is about American industrialization, but the explanation cites Lancashire and Bombay. Explainer: "trace land ownership through probate inventories". Inventories mostly list movable goods; land is usually traced through deeds and wills.
- longue-duree-analysis.md, Explainer: "the sea as connector and connector" (typo). It also gives event-time as "years to decades". Braudel's short time is usually described as days to years. Q0 premise says Mediterranean cities developed "rather than large continental agricultural empires", which sits oddly with Rome being one.
- material-culture-historical-interpretation.md, Q2 explanation and Q4 answer: claim that material culture "bypasses" elite agendas and that objects "are produced by the people who used them". Both overstate the case. Survival, excavation choices and supply (for example, goods issued to enslaved people) filter the material record too.
- material-symbolic-integration.md, Q0: frames sumptuary laws as "18th-century". Such laws were most common from the 14th to the 17th century. Q2 explanation: "The laws' existence proves..." overstates the point, since sumptuary laws also had economic and moral motives.
- network-analysis-and-relationship-mapping.md, Explainer: defines centrality as "one through whom many connections pass". That is betweenness centrality, and Q0 separately uses degree centrality. The Medici example centers on Lorenzo, but the well-known network study of Medici marriage and business ties concerns Cosimo's rise in the 1430s. It also calls the Medici "political clients" of other groups; they were mainly patrons.
- numismatic-analysis-and-coins.md, Q0 and Explainer: "approximately 90% silver under Augustus". The Augustan denarius is usually put at about 95–98% silver. By the later 3rd century the main silver coin was the antoninianus, not the denarius. Q1 explanation: says hoards were buried "typically in a moment of crisis". Savings and votive hoards are also common, and savings hoards build up over time rather than capturing one moment.
- periodization-and-historical-division.md, Q1: "Two historians are both studying 15th-century Europe" but one uses 1517, which is in the 16th century.
- photographic-evidence-analysis.md, Q2 explanation: treats "Migrant Mother" as the caption guiding the reading. That title was attached later; the original FSA captions were different.
- radiocarbon-and-scientific-dating.md, Q3 explanation: says OSL dates "when sediment or pottery was last heated or exposed to light". OSL measures the last exposure to light; heating is measured by TL. It also states that fired pottery has no datable organic material, which is too strong because organic residues and temper can sometimes be dated.
- research-design-and-historical-inquiry.md: the Explainer calls handling contradictory evidence "the most important design decision", but Q4 says the research question is the most important design decision.
- secondary-source-mining.md, Q0 explanation: ends "not new evidence or better access to archives", which is more absolute than the correct option ("not necessarily better evidence").
- source-selection-strategy.md, Q3 question text is garbled: "When a research question can seldom be answered by examining most available source". It probably meant "cannot be answered by examining all available sources".
- source-synthesis-and-triangulation.md, Q2 (true-false): the statement says "most alternative ... interpretations ... are effectively ruled out", but the explanation argues against "all alternatives". The item is ambiguous as keyed. Explainer: "The analogy to surveying is exact" overstates.
- historiographical-positionality-and-bias.md, Q0: keys a single-cause answer ("the most important explanation") for 19th-century topic choice. This is contestable. The Explainer's line that a Cold War-trained historian "applied assumptions" generalizes about a whole cohort.
