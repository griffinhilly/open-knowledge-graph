## Flags

- welfare-analysis-advanced.md, Core Idea and Explainer: "deadweight loss is approximately half the distortion squared times quantity" / "one-half times the tax (or distortion) squared times the quantity affected". The Harberger approximation is ½·t²·|dQ/dP| (the demand/supply slope), or ½·ε·τ²·PQ in elasticity form. "Times quantity" is dimensionally wrong.
- welfare-analysis-advanced.md, Explainer para 3: says CV/EV handle the fact that "a dollar lost by a poor household is more consequential than a dollar lost by a wealthy one". CV and EV are individual money measures that handle income effects; they do not weight dollars across people. That is a social-welfare-weight question.
- vcg-auction-mechanism.md, Explainer: "Google's original ad auction and the FCC's spectrum auctions drew heavily on VCG principles." Google used a generalized second-price auction (not VCG), and FCC spectrum auctions used simultaneous multiple-round ascending auctions. The Core Idea's "VCG mechanisms are used in combinatorial auctions and spectrum allocation" also overstates how often pure VCG is used in practice.
- vcg-auction-mechanism.md, true-false question 3 ("payment depends in part on their own reported valuation" → false) and Explainer "the payment depends only on others' reports". Agent i's report changes the chosen allocation, which changes i's payment (in a Vickrey auction, bidding below the second-highest bid drops the payment to 0). The correct claim is that i's report does not affect the payment except through the allocation. The keyed answer "false" is debatable.
- strategic-form-games.md, Core Idea: "Nash's existence theorem guarantees that mixed-strategy equilibria exist under mild continuity conditions." Nash's theorem covers finite games. Explainer: existence "requires only that payoff functions are continuous and strategy sets are compact and convex". For pure-strategy existence in infinite games, quasi-concavity of payoffs in own strategy is also needed (Debreu-Glicksberg-Fan).
- strategic-form-games.md, multiple-choice question 0: option D ("Both players are playing dominant strategies, and dominant strategies always produce Nash equilibria") is also a true statement for the Prisoner's Dilemma, and the explanation admits it. The item has two defensible answers.
- strong-axiom-revealed-preference.md, Core Idea and question 1: "SARP is necessary and sufficient for consistency with neoclassical consumer theory." That holds for single-valued demand. For general rationalization by a locally nonsatiated utility function, the condition is GARP (Afriat). Explainer also lists "downward-sloping demand" as implied by the theory, but utility maximization does not rule out Giffen goods.
- strong-axiom-revealed-preference.md, true-false question 3: "satisfy WARP in most pairwise comparison" is garbled wording (should be "every pairwise comparison").
- subgame-perfect-equilibrium.md, true-false question 2: "guarantees that most player's strategy is rational at nearly every decision node" is garbled hedging; the intended claim is "every player's strategy ... at every decision node". Explainer: "backward induction always yields at least one SPE" in perfect-information games needs the game to be finite.
- walrasian-equilibrium.md, true-false question 1: "so that most markets clear simultaneously" is garbled (should be "all markets"). Existence of equilibrium does not guarantee that decentralized price adjustment (tâtonnement) converges to it; the explanation's "competitive price adjustment ... can achieve general equilibrium" overstates this.
- social-welfare-optimization.md, true-false question 2 explanation: "less concave utility can yield unequal optimal distributions even under utilitarianism". With identical, strictly concave utility, any degree of concavity gives equal consumption in the first-best problem; inequality needs linear utility, differing preferences, or incentive effects. Multiple-choice question 1 explanation calls the utilitarian equal split egalitarian "for efficiency reasons"; it follows from maximizing the utility sum, not from efficiency.
- signaling-games.md, Explainer last sentence: "Without differential cost, signals are cheap talk and cannot credibly separate." A uniformly costly signal is not cheap talk (cheap talk is costless), and cheap talk can transmit information when interests are partly aligned. The How It's Best Learned text ("Draw payoff matrices") fits an extensive-form game poorly.
- signaling-games-and-pooling-separating.md, true-false question 2 explanation: says a separating signal moves the posterior to "nearly 100%" high-ability; in a separating equilibrium Bayes' rule gives exactly 100%.
- slutsky-equation.md, multiple-choice question 0 explanation: writes the own-price income effect as −xⱼ·(∂xᵢ/∂m); for an own-price change it should be −xᵢ·(∂xᵢ/∂m).
