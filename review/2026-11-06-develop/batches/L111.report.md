## Flags

### Suspected factual errors in existing text
- molecular-dating-phylogenetic-clocks.md, Q2 (question + explanation): "estimates for rodents are systematically younger ... while whale estimates are too old" / "overestimating the time needed for slowly-evolving lineages (making whale dates too old)". Direction is reversed. A shared compromise rate is slower than the true rodent rate, so rodent dates come out too OLD; it is faster than the whale rate, so whale dates come out too YOUNG. High priority.
- functional-response-predation-types.md, Q1 explanation: "As prey become scarce, the time predators spend searching shrinks". Search time grows as prey become scarce. Q3 explanation: "at low prey density ... handling time dominates total time (search time is already short)". Wrong: at low density search time dominates. The higher proportion eaten at low density follows from f(N)/N = a/(1+ahN) falling with N.
- lotka-volterra-predation-cycles.md, Q4 explanation: "With logistic prey growth, the equilibrium typically becomes either a stable spiral ... or a limit cycle". With logistic prey and a linear (Type I) functional response the coexistence equilibrium is stable; limit cycles need something extra such as a saturating response (Rosenzweig-MacArthur). Q5 answer: "the system is infinitely sensitive to initial conditions" is the wrong term (that describes chaos); neutral cycles depend on initial conditions but small changes give small changes.
- metapopulation-connectivity-patches.md, Q2 explanation: "The 60% conversion threshold is consistent with percolation thresholds". The site-percolation threshold on a square lattice is about 59% habitat REMAINING (about 41% lost), not 60% converted. Also Core Idea and Explainer equate "rescue effect" with recolonization; the rescue effect is immigration that prevents a local extinction, distinct from recolonizing an empty patch.
- landscape-ecology-and-spatial-heterogeneity.md, Explainer: "Think of it like a chessboard versus a checkerboard: both have the same number of black and white squares, but the pattern differs". Chessboards and checkerboards have the same 8x8 pattern, so the analogy fails.
- evidence-for-evolution.md, Explainer: Lenski E. coli "ability to metabolize citrate, which no E. coli ancestor could do". E. coli can use citrate anaerobically; the evolved novelty was aerobic citrate use (Cit+).
- microevolution-and-macroevolution.md, Explainer: "the origin of mammals from reptilian ancestors". Mammals descend from synapsids, which are not reptiles in current classification.
- molecular-evolution-phylogenetics.md, Q1 explanation: treats a rate five times higher than a constrained metabolic gene as "five times higher than a neutral benchmark" and so as evidence of positive selection. A faster rate than a constrained gene can come from weaker constraint; positive selection is usually inferred from dN/dS > 1.
- local-adaptation-genotype-by-environment.md, Explainer: "If each population performs better in its home environment than the foreign one, local adaptation is confirmed." The home-versus-away comparison is confounded by site quality; the standard diagnostic is local-versus-foreign at each site (as Q1 and Q5 state). Q1 explanation's claim that plasticity "would show one population performing well in both environments" is also muddled.
- extinction-vortex-populations.md, Explainer: defines a "demographic Allee effect" as per-capita growth becoming negative below a critical density. That is a strong Allee effect; a demographic Allee effect can be weak (growth reduced but still positive).
- habitat-fragmentation-connectivity-corridors.md, Q2 explanation: "The SLOSS debate ... showed that for most species, one large patch outperforms several small ones". SLOSS is unresolved, and several reviews find several small patches often hold as many or more species. Q3 explanation: "Less total area often has less impact than this structural change" conflicts with a substantial literature finding habitat amount has larger effects than fragmentation per se.
- facilitation-mechanisms-ecology.md, Q2 explanation and Explainer: stress gradient hypothesis "well-supported" / "confirmed across ecosystems worldwide". Support is mixed, with refinements (for example facilitation weakening at extreme stress). Overstated.
- invasive-species-establishment-mechanisms.md, Q1 explanation and Explainer: the brown tree snake on Guam is used as the example of enemy release. Its impact is mainly predation on naive prey; it is a weak fit for enemy release.
- modes-of-speciation-...md, Explainer: crater-lake cichlids "where dozens of species have arisen within a single small lake". Lake Barombi Mbo has about 11 endemic cichlids; "dozens" is overstated.
- extinction-rates-phylogenetic-patterns.md, Explainer: coelacanth given as "the last species in an ancient, species-poor lineage". Two living coelacanth species exist.
- evolution-through-natural-selection.md, Explainer: "say the bark becomes lighter due to pollution". Pollution darkens bark; bark lightens when pollution falls.
- inbreeding-depression-genetic-rescue.md, Explainer: Florida panther "within a generation the population tripled". The roughly threefold increase took about a decade or more; "within a generation" is likely overstated.
- evolutionary-comparative-anatomy.md, Q5 explanation: Pax6 "was co-opted independently in each lineage". The usual deep-homology reading is that Pax6 had an ancestral role in photoreceptor development, which the sister file evolutionary-developmental-biology.md also states.
- molecular-dating-fossil-calibration.md, Q2 explanation: "a statistical distribution (e.g., lognormal or uncorrelated)". "Uncorrelated" describes the model type, not a distribution.
- molecular-dating-phylogenetic-clocks.md, Q1: option B ("twice as long ago if divergence is twice as large") is also defensible under a strict clock, so the item is ambiguous.
