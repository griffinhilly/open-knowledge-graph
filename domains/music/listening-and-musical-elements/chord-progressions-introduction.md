# Introduction to Chord Progressions

## Core Idea
A chord progression is a series of chords played in sequence. Certain chord progressions appear in thousands of songs because they create satisfying patterns of tension and resolution. The I-IV-V-I progression is one of the most common in Western music, creating a sense of departure and return. Learning to hear chord progressions helps you understand why music moves the way it does.

## How It's Best Learned
Play a I-IV-V-I progression in C major (C-F-G-C) and listen to how it creates a sense of journey and arrival. Identify the same progression in several familiar songs. Try playing a melody over a simple chord progression and notice how the chords affect the melody's feeling.

## Common Misconceptions
- Thinking chord progressions are unique to each song; the same progressions appear across many songs and genres.
- Believing chord progressions must be complex to sound good; some of the most beloved songs use only two or three chords.
- Assuming the Roman numeral system is confusing math; it simply labels each chord by its position in the scale.

## Explainer

A **chord progression** is a sequence of different chords played one after another. Instead of playing just one chord repeatedly, compositions use multiple chords in a planned order to create harmonic movement and shape the emotional journey of the music. Chord progressions are the harmonic "storyline" of a piece.

Chord progressions work because of the concepts of **tension** and **resolution**. Some chords feel stable and grounded (resolution), while others feel unstable or like they're pulling toward somewhere else (tension). As a chord progression moves through different chords, it creates a sense of musical journey—moving away from home and back again, or creating climax and release. A skilled composer uses this tension and resolution to guide the listener's emotions throughout the piece.

Certain chord progressions appear in countless songs because they create satisfying emotional effects. The progression I-IV-V-I (using Roman numerals to represent scale degrees) is extremely common in popular music and folk songs because it feels natural and complete. However, there are infinite possible progressions, and different progressions create different moods. A progression with darker chords (minor chords) will feel different from one with bright chords (major chords). Understanding chord progressions helps you recognize patterns in songs you love and eventually create your own expressive harmonic structures.
