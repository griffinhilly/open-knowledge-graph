# Harmonic Rhythm

## Core Idea
Harmonic rhythm refers to the rate at which chords change within a piece. Fast harmonic rhythm—chords changing every beat or every two beats—creates forward momentum and urgency. Slow harmonic rhythm creates stability, grandeur, or a sense of suspension. Strategically varying harmonic rhythm within a passage is one of the most powerful tools for shaping musical energy and directing listener attention toward climactic moments.

## How It's Best Learned
Transcribe the harmonic rhythm of contrasting passages (a Baroque prelude vs. a Romantic adagio) and graph how chord-change rate correlates with perceived energy before composing your own exercise that deliberately manipulates harmonic rhythm for expressive effect.

## Common Misconceptions
- Harmonic rhythm and tempo are independent — a slow tempo with chord changes every beat creates different energy than a fast tempo with chord changes every four bars.
- Root position chords and inverted chords contribute differently to perceived harmonic weight even at the same rhythmic rate.
