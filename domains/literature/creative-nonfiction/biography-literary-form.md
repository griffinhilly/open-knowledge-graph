# Biography as a Literary Form

## Core Idea
Biography as a literary form moves far beyond factual chronology to construct a compelling portrait of a life. Literary biography employs narrative techniques, stylistic craft, and interpretive judgment to create psychological depth, cultural significance, and engaging narrative while remaining grounded in research and evidence.

## Explainer

Biography is one of the oldest literary forms, yet the relationship between literary craft and historical accuracy is still debated. What makes biography a literary form, rather than just a historical record?

Literary biography asks: how do we construct a compelling, meaningful portrait of a human life? This is different from compiling facts. A literary biography of someone might discover the same historical facts as a scholarly biography, but will arrange them, interpret them, and present them in ways designed to create insight and engagement. It might use vivid description of settings, reconstruct scenes from letters and documents, explore psychology and motivation, and create narrative momentum.

This doesn't mean literary biography is less factual. It means biography employs literary tools in service of truth rather than imagination. A literary biographer might reconstruct what a character experienced in a particular moment using sensory detail and psychological language, grounded in what we know from documents and evidence about that moment. The language might be more vivid than scholarly biography, but the facts aren't distorted—they're illuminated.

One of the powers of literary biography is that it can create psychological depth. By using narrative insight, internal monologue derived from diaries and letters, and careful characterization, a biographer can help readers understand not just what someone did but why they did it, what inner conflicts they faced, what motivated them. This understanding comes from careful reading of evidence, not imagination. But it's the literary skill of the biographer that makes these depths visible and compelling.

Literary biography also has responsibilities. It must be clear about the difference between documented fact and inference or interpretation. It cannot invent dialogue that was never recorded or attribute thoughts to someone without evidence. The craft serves accuracy rather than replacing it. The best literary biography is rigorous in research and transparent about method while also offering the literary engagement and psychological insight that make a life come alive for readers.
