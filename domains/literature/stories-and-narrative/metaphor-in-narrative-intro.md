# Metaphor in Narrative: Comparisons That Reveal Meaning

## Core Idea
A metaphor is a figure of speech that compares two unlike things without using 'like' or 'as' (e.g., 'Life is a journey' or 'Her voice was honey'). In narratives, metaphors can be woven throughout—used in descriptions, dialogue, and even plot structure—to deepen meaning and create emotional resonance.

## How It's Best Learned
Read a passage with metaphors and identify them. Ask: What two things are being compared? What does this comparison reveal or suggest? How does the metaphor make the writing more vivid or meaningful than a literal description would?

## Common Misconceptions
- Confusing metaphor with simile; metaphors say something 'is' something, while similes use 'like' or 'as'.
- Thinking metaphors are only decorative; they can carry central meaning and theme.
- Assuming readers will interpret metaphors the same way; metaphors invite multiple interpretations.
