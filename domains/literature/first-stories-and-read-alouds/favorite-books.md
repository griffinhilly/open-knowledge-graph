# Favorite Books

## Core Idea
Everyone has favorite books -- the ones you want to hear over and over again. It is completely normal to ask for the same story many times. Each time you hear a favorite book, you might notice something new, remember more of the words, or enjoy it in a different way. Having favorites is a sign that you are becoming a person who loves stories.

## How It's Best Learned
Keep your favorite books where you can reach them easily. Talk about why certain books are your favorites. Listen to a favorite story again and see what you notice this time that you missed before. Start a collection of books you love.

## Common Misconceptions
- Thinking it is babyish to want the same story again; rereading favorites is something lifelong readers do at every age.
- Believing you should only have one favorite book; you can have many favorites for different reasons and different moods.
- Assuming a book you liked once will always be your favorite; tastes change as you grow, and that is a good thing.

## Explainer

Everyone has **favorite books** -- the ones you cannot get enough of. They are the books you ask to hear again and again. You might beg your grown-up to read the same story before bed every single night! That is wonderful and completely normal. Having favorite books is a sign that you are becoming someone who **loves stories**.

Here is something special about favorite books: each time you listen to one, something new can happen. Maybe the first time you did not notice a silly detail in the pictures. Maybe the second time you remembered some of the funny words and said them out loud with the grown-up. Maybe the third time you noticed how the character felt scared but then became brave. **Every reading** brings something different, even though you know what happens.

**Rereading** your favorites is not babyish at all. People of all ages do it! A grown-up might read their favorite book five times because it makes them happy. Kids learn SO much by reading favorites over and over. Your brain gets stronger, you remember more **words**, and you understand the **characters** and the story deeper each time.

You can have **many** favorite books, and they can be favorites for different reasons. Maybe one is your favorite when you want to laugh, and another is your favorite when you want to feel cozy and calm. Maybe one is your favorite because a character reminds you of someone you know. It is **wonderful** to have lots of favorites!

One more cool thing: as you grow, your favorite books might change. A book you loved when you were three might not be your favorite when you are five, and that is perfectly okay. Your tastes **change and grow**. That is part of becoming a reader.
