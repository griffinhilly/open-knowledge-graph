# Picture Books

## Core Idea
Picture books tell stories with both words and pictures working together. The pictures are not just decoration -- they show you what the characters look like, where the story happens, and how the characters feel. A picture book is a team effort between words and art.

## How It's Best Learned
Read a picture book with a grown-up and look carefully at each illustration before turning the page. Talk about what you see in the pictures that the words did not say. Try covering the words and telling the story using just the pictures.

## Common Misconceptions
- Thinking the pictures are just there to look pretty; in good picture books, the illustrations carry important parts of the story.
- Believing picture books are only for babies; many picture books tell complex stories that older children and adults enjoy.
- Assuming the words tell you everything; sometimes the pictures show details the words leave out.

## Explainer

A **picture book** is a special kind of book where **words and pictures work together** as teammates to tell a story. The words are not just labels for the pictures, and the pictures are not just decoration. Together, they create the **whole story**. Each one needs the other to tell the complete **tale**.

When you read a picture book, look carefully at the **illustrations**. They show you what the **characters** look like, what they are doing, and where the story takes place. If the words say, "The girl opened the door," the picture might show what is on the other side of the door. Or the picture might show that the girl is smiling or scared, even if the words do not say her feeling out loud. The **picture adds information** that the words did not give you.

Picture books are for **all ages**. Yes, little toddlers enjoy simple picture books with just a few words. But older kids and **grown-ups** also love picture books because they are beautifully illustrated and tell amazing stories. Some picture books have complicated ideas, funny jokes, or beautiful **artwork** that appeals to everyone.

A cool skill to learn is to **cover the words** and see if you can tell the story using just the pictures! Or look at the pictures **before** you hear the words and imagine what the story might be. Then hear the actual words and see what you guessed right and what surprised you. This helps you understand how powerful the **illustrations** are.

**Picture books** teach us that reading is not just about words. **Art** and **pictures** are part of reading too. When you read a picture book, you are reading with your eyes in two ways: reading the **words** and reading the **pictures**. That makes you a really smart reader!
