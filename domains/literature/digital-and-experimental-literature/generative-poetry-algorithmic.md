# Generative Poetry: Algorithmic Composition

## Core Idea
Generative poetry emerges from algorithmic systems—rule-based composition using randomness, constraint, or neural networks. Rather than fixed text, generative systems produce variation; each instantiation is unique. This practice questions authorship and determinism by treating literature as combinatorial exploration rather than singular creation.

## Explainer

Generative poetry might seem like a contradiction in terms. Poetry is personal expression, individual voices crafted with care. How can a poem generated by an algorithm be poetry?

The answer requires rethinking what poetry is and where creativity resides.

Traditional poetry is composed by a person. The poet writes lines, revises them, shapes them toward completion. The final poem is fixed: the poet has determined its exact form. Reading a poem means encountering the poet's determined choices. Authorship is clear: the poet is the sole source of creative decision.

Generative poetry inverts this. Instead of writing poems, the poet designs a system that generates poems. This system might use randomness (choose random words from banks and combine them), constraints (follow formal rules), or algorithms (apply transformations to text). Each time the system runs, it produces a unique poem. The poet does not determine individual poems; the system does.

Where is creativity in this arrangement? The answer is: in system design. The poet's creativity goes into designing rules that generate meaningful poems. The rules might be sophisticated—constraining which words can follow which words, enforcing thematic coherence, respecting formal structures. The generative poet's skill lies in designing systems that produce aesthetically coherent, thematically resonant poems, not in crafting individual poems.

This distributes authorship differently. Conventionally, the author determines the final form. In generative poetry, the author determines the system; the system determines individual poems. Agency is distributed. This raises unsettling questions: who is the author? The person who designed the system, or the system itself? Is the generative poet responsible for all poems the system produces, or only the system design?

Generative poetry also challenges the assumption that literature must be singular. Conventional poems are fixed: identical on every reading. Generative poems are variable: unique on each instantiation. This questions whether a poem can be a *thing* (fixed object) or must be understood as a *system* (producing variations).

Philosophically, generative poetry reveals that literature is not exclusively bound to individual consciousness and intentionality. Aesthetic coherence can emerge from systematic rules. Meaningful poems can be generated without human composition of individual lines. This expands what counts as literature and questions what authorship fundamentally is.
