# Genre Conventions in Fiction

## Core Idea
Genre is a contract between writer and reader, establishing shared expectations about setting, character types, plot structures, and tonal registers before a single page is read. Genres are not rigid boxes but fluid conventions that evolve historically — what counts as 'science fiction' in 1920 differs substantially from 2020. Literary critics distinguish between genre fiction (which foregrounds genre conventions) and literary fiction (which tends to subvert or transcend them), though many important works occupy both spaces. Understanding genre means recognizing both the rules and the creative possibilities that arise from breaking them.

## How It's Best Learned
Compare two books from the same genre written decades apart to trace how conventions shift. Read a genre parody alongside its source genre to make implicit conventions explicit. Ask: what would a reader expect, and how does this text deliver, delay, or deny those expectations?

## Common Misconceptions
- Genre fiction is not inherently lower quality than literary fiction; this hierarchy reflects cultural biases, not craft.
- Genres are not mutually exclusive; most contemporary fiction blends multiple genre conventions.
- Genre conventions are not formulas — skilled genre authors use conventions to create meaning, not just satisfy checklists.
