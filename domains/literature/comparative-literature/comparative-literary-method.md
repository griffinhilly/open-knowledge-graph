# Comparative Method in Literary Analysis

## Core Idea
The comparative method involves systematic analysis of literary works, movements, or traditions across cultures, languages, and periods to identify patterns, differences, and relationships. Rather than reading texts in isolation, comparative literature asks: what does text A in Culture X share with text B in Culture Y, and what do those similarities reveal about how literature works? This method requires attention to both universal patterns and irreducible cultural specificity.

## How It's Best Learned
Start by comparing two closely related works (same period, adjacent traditions) to see concrete differences and similarities. Then expand to more distant pairs (different periods, languages, regions) to develop sensitivity to both pattern and particularity.

## Common Misconceptions
- That comparison implies equivalence or that all traditions must be equally developed.
- That finding similarities means traditions are the same or that differences mean they're incomparable.
- That comparison requires reading all source languages (translation is legitimate for many purposes).
