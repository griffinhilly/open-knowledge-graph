# Dialogism and Bakhtin's Polyphonic Novel

## Core Idea
Mikhail Bakhtin argues that all utterance is fundamentally dialogic—responding to, anticipating, and incorporating multiple voices and perspectives. The novel reaches the fullest expression of dialogism by allowing multiple characters and ideological positions to coexist in unresolved tension, refusing authorial synthesis or hierarchy.

## How It's Best Learned
Analyze a novel (Dostoyevsky, García Márquez, or Morrison) focusing on how different characters' voices and ideological positions exist without the narrator resolving them. Examine how the form itself enacts polyphony through dialogue and narrative technique.

## Common Misconceptions
Polyphony does not mean the author is absent or that the novel lacks coherence. The author orchestrates the voices and creates space for dialogue. Dialogism is not simply multiple perspectives; it is unresolved tension where worldviews confront each other.
