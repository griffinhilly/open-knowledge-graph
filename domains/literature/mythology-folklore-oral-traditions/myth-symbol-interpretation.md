# Myth and Symbol: Interpretation and Meaning-Making

## Core Idea
Mythic symbols—objects, creatures, actions—carry cultural meanings that shift with context and interpreter. A serpent symbol represents fertility in one context, chaos in another, and wisdom in a third, depending on cultural values and narrative position. Interpreting mythic symbols requires understanding cultural context; symbols are not universal but culturally inflected.

## How It's Best Learned
Track a single symbol across different myths within a culture and across cultures. Document how its meanings vary and why different contexts activate different symbolic associations.

## Common Misconceptions
A symbol has one correct meaning that must be discovered. (Symbols are radically polysemic; multiple meanings coexist.) Symbols in myths have the same meanings as in other contexts. (Mythic symbols draw power from their narrative context.)
