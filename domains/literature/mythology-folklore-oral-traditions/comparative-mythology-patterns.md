# Comparative Mythology: Cross-Cultural Patterns and Variation

## Core Idea
Comparative mythology examines recurring patterns across cultures (creation myths, flood narratives, culture heroes) to identify universal narrative structures and cultural specificity. Comparison can illuminate what questions all cultures address and how different societies answer them, but risks flattening cultural difference by emphasizing similarity. Responsible comparative work acknowledges both deep patterns and irreducible cultural difference.

## How It's Best Learned
Select a mythic pattern and map its variants across cultures, documenting similarities and noting where variation becomes meaningful. Reflect on whether comparison reveals understanding or imposes false universalism.

## Common Misconceptions
Comparing myths across cultures proves they're all expressing the same underlying truth. (While patterns recur, cultural specificity remains; comparison and cultural particularity are both necessary.) Identification of cross-cultural patterns exhausts any myth's meaning. (Patterns contextualize; cultural context and historical moment remain essential.)
