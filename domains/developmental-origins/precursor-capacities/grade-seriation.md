# Grading and Seriation

## Core Idea

Ordering several things along a single continuous dimension — smallest to largest, lightest to
heaviest, quietest to loudest. Where discernment judges two things same or different, grading
arranges many along a gradient. This is the substrate beneath comparison language, measurement, and
ordinal number, and it is the experience that makes the endpoints of a scale ("big" and "small") nameable.

## Emerges Through

Built through nesting and stacking that only work in order: nesting cups, a ring-stacker that grades
by size, lining up family members by height. Montessori grading materials (the Pink Tower, graded
rods, color shades) isolate one dimension so a child can build the full series by hand.

## Observable Signs

Illustrative, not a checklist:

- Nesting or stacking graded pieces in order, adjusting when one is out of sequence.
- Lining things up from small to big.
- Picking "the bigger one" reliably across many pairs, then the biggest of several.

Developmental markers vary widely; this is an enrichment map, not a screening tool. For concerns
about a child's development, consult a pediatrician.
