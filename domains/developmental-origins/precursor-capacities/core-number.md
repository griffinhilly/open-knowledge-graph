# Approximate Number and Small-Set Tracking

## Core Idea

An innate core-knowledge system for quantity, made of two parts: an approximate sense of "more versus
fewer" for large amounts, and the ability to track one, two, or three things exactly without counting.
This is the pre-verbal foundation that later counting, comparison, and arithmetic are built onto — the
reason "more" and "a lot" mean something long before any number words are learned.

## Emerges Through

Strengthened by everyday encounters with quantity: noticing which plate has more crackers, that two
shoes pair with two feet, that a handful is more than one. Sharing, snack time, and filling and
emptying containers all give repeated, embodied experience of amount.

## Observable Signs

Illustrative, not a checklist:

- Reaching for the larger of two clearly different piles of treats.
- Noticing when one thing is taken from a small group — that something is missing.
- Reacting to "more" as meaningful long before knowing any number words.

Developmental markers vary widely; this is an enrichment map, not a screening tool. For concerns
about a child's development, consult a pediatrician.
