# Agency and Goal-Directed Action

## Core Idea

An innate core-knowledge system: the recognition that some things in the world are agents — they move
on their own, act toward goals, and respond to circumstances — as distinct from inert objects pushed
around by contact. This is the seed of cause and intention: the understanding that an action can be
*for* something, which later grounds reasoning about why things happen and what others want.

## Emerges Through

Sharpened by watching and interacting with people and animals: a hand reaching for a cup, a pet going
to its bowl, a caregiver responding to a cry. Simple back-and-forth — rolling a ball to a child and
having it rolled back — builds the sense of others as intentional partners rather than moving scenery.

## Observable Signs

Illustrative, not a checklist:

- Watching where a person is reaching or looking, expecting them to act on a goal.
- Treating people and animals differently from objects — expecting them to start moving on their own.
- Repeating an action to make a person respond, as if expecting a goal-directed reply.

Developmental markers vary widely; this is an enrichment map, not a screening tool. For concerns
about a child's development, consult a pediatrician.
