# Naming and Symbol–Referent Mapping

## Core Idea

The insight that a word or sign *stands for* something — that "cup" points at the cup. This is where a
felt experience becomes a shareable, named concept. Binary opposites live here: hot/cold, big/small,
up/down are the two endpoints of a gradient the child has already felt, and naming those endpoints is
the first and most natural use of words for qualities. Comparatives ("bigger," "biggest") come only
after the endpoints are named. Naming presupposes discrimination (you must tell the quality apart) and
grading (you must have felt the gradient whose ends you are labeling).

## Emerges Through

Built through being given words for things and qualities in context — especially the two extremes of a
dimension first: the hot bowl and the cold bowl, the big block and the little block. Pointing-and-naming
games, labeling opposites during everyday handling, and picture books all attach words to already-felt
contrasts.

## Observable Signs

Illustrative, not a checklist:

- Pointing to a thing to get its name, or naming it to share it.
- Using opposite words (hot/cold, big/little, up/down) for the ends of a felt scale.
- Understanding that a picture or word refers to a real thing that is not present.

Developmental markers vary widely; this is an enrichment map, not a screening tool. For concerns
about a child's development, consult a pediatrician.
