# Mathematical Thinking and Number Sense

## Core Idea
Mathematical thinking develops from infants' innate ability to discriminate small quantities (subitizing) through toddlers' one-to-one correspondence, to preschoolers' counting and cardinality understanding, to school-age children's understanding of place value and arithmetic operations. Number sense—intuitive understanding of quantity, magnitude, and relationships—develops before formal instruction and predicts mathematical achievement. Early mathematics learning depends on language, cognitive skills, and guided practice with manipulatives and real-world contexts.

## How It's Best Learned
Conduct mathematics interviews to understand children's informal strategies and reasoning; use manipulatives and visual representations to build conceptual understanding before procedural fluency.

## Common Misconceptions
Mathematical ability is innate and fixed. While some cognitive prerequisites are innate, mathematical competence develops through instruction and practice; early intervention is highly effective for struggling learners.
