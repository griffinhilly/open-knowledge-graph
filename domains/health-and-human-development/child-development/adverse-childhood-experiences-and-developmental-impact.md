# Adverse Childhood Experiences and Developmental Impact

## Core Idea
Childhood trauma including abuse, neglect, loss, or exposure to violence can alter neurodevelopment—particularly stress-response systems and prefrontal circuitry—leading to long-term impacts on emotion regulation, social relationships, learning capacity, and physical health through epigenetic and physiological mechanisms. However, responsive relationships and evidence-based therapeutic interventions can significantly mitigate these effects and support adaptive development.

## How It's Best Learned
Review developmental trauma cases with particular attention to timing of trauma and developmental domains affected. Design trauma-informed intervention protocols addressing neurobiological underpinnings of trauma symptoms.

## Common Misconceptions
- All children exposed to trauma develop PTSD and lasting problems; actually, individual differences in temperament, attachment, and resources significantly affect outcomes.
- Trauma effects are fixed and unchangeable; actually, neuroplasticity permits recovery through supportive relationships and targeted interventions.
