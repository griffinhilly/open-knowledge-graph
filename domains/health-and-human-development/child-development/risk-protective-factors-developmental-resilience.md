# Risk and Protective Factors: Developmental Resilience

## Core Idea
Child development is shaped by the balance between risk factors—conditions increasing developmental vulnerability (poverty, trauma, parental psychopathology, maltreatment)—and protective factors—conditions and relationships that buffer against adversity (secure attachment, cognitive ability, temperamental ease, supportive relationships, community resources, school success). Resilience refers to adaptive functioning despite significant adversity, emerging from protective processes that modify, ameliorate, or alter exposure to risk. Children show highly variable resilience depending on cumulative burden of risk, developmental timing of exposure, and availability of protective relationships and services. Early intervention targeting modifiable risk factors and strengthening protective processes can substantially alter developmental trajectories.
