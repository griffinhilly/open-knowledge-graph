# Coins and Their Values

## Core Idea
The four U.S. coins — penny (1¢), nickel (5¢), dime (10¢), and quarter (25¢) — each have a specific value that does not depend on the coin's size. The dime, though smaller than the nickel, is worth twice as much. Students learn to identify each coin by name, appearance, and value, and understand that coin value is a social convention, not determined by physical size or weight.

## How It's Best Learned
Handle real or plastic coins. Sort by type, then discuss values. Practice the rhyme 'a penny for one, a nickel for five, a dime for ten, a quarter for twenty-five.' Emphasize that the dime is the trickiest because it is the smallest coin but not the smallest value.

## Common Misconceptions
- Thinking the larger coin (nickel) is worth more than the smaller coin (dime).
- Confusing quarters (25¢) with nickels (5¢) based on size.
- Not knowing that 4 quarters make $1.
