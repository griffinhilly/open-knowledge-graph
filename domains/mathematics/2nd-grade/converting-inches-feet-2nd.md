# Converting Between Inches and Feet

## Core Idea
12 inches equals 1 foot. To convert, multiply feet by 12 to get inches, or divide inches by 12 to get feet. For example, 2 feet = 24 inches.

- question: "Jordan measures a bookshelf and finds it is 3 feet tall. How many inches tall is the bookshelf?"

  options:

    - "36 inches — because 3 × 12 = 36"

  answer: 1
  explanation: "To convert feet to inches, multiply by 12 because each foot contains exactly 12 inches. 3 feet × 12 = 36 inches. Option A (adding 12) is a common error — adding 12 only accounts for 1 foot's worth of inches, not all 3 feet. When you go from a larger unit (feet) to a smaller unit (inches), you need more of them, so the number gets bigger. Multiplication is the right operation when converting to smaller units."

- question: "A ribbon is 48 inches long. A student wants to write this length in feet. What should they do?"
  type: multiple-choice
  options:
    - "Multiply 48 by 12 to get 576 feet"
    - "Divide 48 by 12 to get 4 feet"
    - "Subtract 12 from 48 to get 36 feet"
    - "Add 12 to 48 to get 60 feet"
  answer: 1
  explanation: "To convert from inches to feet, divide by 12, because 12 inches make 1 foot. 48 ÷ 12 = 4 feet. Option A reverses the operation — multiplying gives you more of the smaller unit, which goes the wrong direction. When converting from a small unit (inches) to a large unit (feet), the count gets smaller, so you divide. You can always check: 4 feet × 12 = 48 inches confirms the answer is right."

- question: "To convert any measurement from feet to inches, you multiply by 12, because each foot is made up of 12 inches."
  type: true-false
  answer: true
  explanation: "This is always correct. Each foot 'unpacks' into 12 individual inches, so you multiply the number of feet by 12 to count all the inches. 1 foot = 12 inches, 2 feet = 24 inches, 5 feet = 60 inches. The same relationship works every time: feet × 12 = inches."

- question: "2 feet and 24 inches are different lengths — feet measure longer objects while inches measure shorter ones."
  type: true-false
  answer: false
  explanation: "They are exactly the same length. 2 feet = 2 × 12 = 24 inches. Feet and inches are just different-sized units for measuring the same thing. Choosing to say '2 feet' versus '24 inches' is like choosing to say you have 1 dollar versus 100 cents — the amount is identical, only the unit name changes. A door that is 2 feet wide and a door that is 24 inches wide are the same door."

- question: "If you know that 1 foot = 12 inches, how would you figure out how many inches are in 5 feet? Explain your thinking step by step."
  type: short-answer
  answer: "Since 1 foot = 12 inches, 5 feet means 5 groups of 12 inches. Multiply: 5 × 12 = 60. So 5 feet = 60 inches."
  explanation: "The key is to think of each foot as a group of 12 inches. If you have 5 feet, you have 5 such groups. To find the total number of inches, you count how many inches are in all 5 groups combined: 5 × 12. You could also add: 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 = 60. Both approaches work, but multiplication is faster once you know the basic fact. The rule holds for any number of feet: multiply by 12 to convert to inches."

## Explainer

You have already practiced measuring lengths using a ruler — you know that inches are small units and that a foot is a larger unit. The key fact that unlocks this entire topic is a single number: **12 inches = 1 foot**. Every conversion between inches and feet flows from this one relationship.

Think of a foot as a group, like an egg carton holds 12 eggs. Just as 2 cartons hold 24 eggs, 2 feet hold 24 inches. To go from feet to inches — from big units to small units — you **multiply by 12**. You are "unpacking" each foot into its 12 individual inches. So 3 feet = 3 × 12 = 36 inches.

To go the other direction — from inches to feet — you ask: "How many groups of 12 fit into this number?" That is **division**. If you have 36 inches and want to know how many feet that is, you divide: 36 ÷ 12 = 3 feet. The same relationship, used in reverse. A useful memory trick: going to **s**maller units means you **m**ultiply (both start with the same feeling — things get bigger in count), and going to larger units means you divide.

In everyday life, you might measure something with a ruler and get a number bigger than 12. Converting to feet makes large measurements easier to say and compare. A table that is 36 inches wide is easier to picture as 3 feet wide. Practice by measuring objects around you with a ruler and then writing the same length two ways — once in inches and once in feet and inches.
