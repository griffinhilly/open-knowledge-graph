# Sigma-Algebras (Formal Construction)

## Core Idea
A σ-algebra is a collection of subsets closed under countable unions and complements, forming the measurable sets on which measures are defined. It generalizes the notion of ''nice'' sets beyond simple intervals. The Borel σ-algebra generated by open sets is the standard example in analysis.

## How It's Best Learned
Start with finite algebras, then see how closure under countable operations creates σ-algebras. Verify that the Borel σ-algebra on ℝ contains all open/closed sets but is strictly larger than finitely-generated algebras.

## Common Misconceptions
- Confusing σ-algebra closure (countable ops) with algebra closure (finite ops). - Thinking all subsets form a σ-algebra (the power set does, but meaningful measures don't exist on it). - Assuming σ-algebra structure is unique (it depends on the generating sets).
