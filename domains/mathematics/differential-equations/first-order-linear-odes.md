# First-Order Linear Differential Equations

## Core Idea
A first-order linear differential equation has the standard form dy/dx + p(x)y = q(x), where p and q are functions of x alone. This form is crucial because it admits a systematic solution method via integrating factors, making it one of the most important classes of ODEs to master.

## How It's Best Learned
Practice converting equations into standard form, identifying p(x) and q(x). Then apply the integrating factor method consistently. Verify solutions by substitution back into the original equation.

## Common Misconceptions
- Confusing y' + p(x)y = q(x) with nonlinear forms like y' + p(x)y^2 = q(x). - Sign errors when rewriting equations in standard form. - Not recognizing that 'linear' refers to the function y and its derivative, not to the coefficients.
