# Decomposing Shapes

## Core Idea
Decomposing shapes means breaking a shape into smaller component shapes. A rectangle can be divided into two squares, or a hexagon into two trapezoids or six triangles. This is the reverse of composing and builds flexible spatial thinking. It also previews fraction concepts by showing that shapes can be split into equal parts.

## How It's Best Learned
Draw lines on paper shapes or physically cut them to reveal the component shapes. Encourage students to find multiple ways to decompose the same shape and discuss whether all decompositions are equally valid.

## Common Misconceptions
- Thinking there is only one correct way to decompose a given shape.
- Not recognizing standard named shapes within the resulting pieces.
- Confusing decomposing (splitting into recognizable shapes) with simply breaking an object.
