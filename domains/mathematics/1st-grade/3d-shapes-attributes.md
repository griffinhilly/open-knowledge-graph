# Attributes of 3D Shapes

## Core Idea
Three-dimensional shapes have faces (flat or curved surfaces), edges (where two faces meet), and vertices (corners where edges converge). A cube has 6 square faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices. Recognizing 2D shapes as the faces of 3D solids bridges flat and spatial geometry and helps students understand real-world objects.

## How It's Best Learned
Use actual 3D objects — wooden blocks, cans, spheres, cones — for hands-on exploration of faces, edges, and vertices. Have students trace faces onto paper to identify which 2D shapes are hiding on 3D figures. Sort objects by functional properties: does it roll? stack? slide?

## Common Misconceptions
- Calling a cube a 'square' (confusing a 3D solid with its 2D face).
- Not recognizing that a sphere has zero flat faces, edges, or vertices.
- Miscounting faces on a cylinder (2 flat circular faces plus 1 curved surface = 3 surfaces total).
