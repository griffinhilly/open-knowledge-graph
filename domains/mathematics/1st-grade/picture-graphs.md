# Picture Graphs

## Core Idea
A picture graph uses symbols or small pictures — each typically representing one item — to display data for different categories. Rows or columns of pictures allow viewers to compare categories visually and answer questions about totals, differences, and which category has more or fewer. Reading a picture graph requires understanding that each symbol stands for a count.

## How It's Best Learned
Begin with graphs where each picture equals exactly 1. Have students build their own picture graphs from data they've collected. Ask comparison questions ('how many more cats than dogs?') that require subtraction to reinforce data interpretation beyond simple reading.

## Common Misconceptions
- Not grasping that each picture represents a quantity, treating the graph as purely decorative.
- Misaligning pictures in rows, making visual comparison difficult.
- Difficulty with 'how many more' questions — students may not connect this to subtraction.
