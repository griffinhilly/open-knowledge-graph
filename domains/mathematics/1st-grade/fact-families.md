# Fact Families

## Core Idea
A fact family is a set of related addition and subtraction equations that use the same three numbers: for example, 3 + 5 = 8, 5 + 3 = 8, 8 − 3 = 5, and 8 − 5 = 3. Recognizing fact families shows that addition and subtraction are related operations, and that knowing one fact gives you three more for free.

## How It's Best Learned
Use triangle flashcards with one number at each corner; covering different corners prompts all four related facts. Part-part-whole mats and number bond diagrams help students see the whole-parts relationship visually.

## Common Misconceptions
- Not recognizing that addition is commutative (3 + 5 = 5 + 3) while subtraction is not (8 − 3 ≠ 3 − 8).
- Confusing the whole (the sum) with the parts when writing subtraction sentences from a fact family.
