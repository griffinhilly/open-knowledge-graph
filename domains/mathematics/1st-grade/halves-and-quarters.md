# Halves and Quarters of Shapes

## Core Idea
A half is one of two equal parts of a whole; a quarter (or fourth) is one of four equal parts. The essential condition is that the parts must be equal in size — cutting a circle into one big piece and one small piece does not produce halves. Students first apply equal-sharing to shapes, which is the earliest introduction to fractions as partitioning.

## How It's Best Learned
Use paper folding to create halves and quarters kinesthetically — students can see and feel equal parts. Use food models carefully and meaningfully. Emphasize the equality requirement explicitly by showing unequal divisions and asking whether they count as halves.

## Common Misconceptions
- Believing any division into two pieces creates halves, ignoring the equal-size requirement.
- Difficulty visualizing halves and quarters of non-rectangular shapes.
- Not realizing that two halves or four quarters together reconstruct the whole.
