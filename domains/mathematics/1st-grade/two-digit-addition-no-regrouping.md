# Adding Two-Digit Numbers (No Regrouping)

## Core Idea
Adding two two-digit numbers without regrouping means the ones digits sum to 9 or less and the tens digits sum to 9 or less, so no carrying is needed. For example, 23 + 14 = 37: add the ones (3 + 4 = 7), then add the tens (20 + 10 = 30), giving 37. This extends place value understanding into structured computation.

## How It's Best Learned
Use base-ten blocks to add ones to ones and tens to tens before recording with numerals. Arrange blocks in columns to match the written format. Emphasize that ones are added to ones and tens to tens — never mix the columns.

## Common Misconceptions
- Adding all digits together as a single number (23 + 14 = 2 + 3 + 1 + 4 = 10).
- Adding across rather than column-by-column.
- Reversing the tens and ones in the answer (writing 73 instead of 37).
