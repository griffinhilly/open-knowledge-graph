# Cartesian Product and Ordered Pairs

## Core Idea
The Cartesian product A × B is the set of all ordered pairs (a, b) where a ∈ A and b ∈ B. Unlike sets, ordered pairs are sensitive to order: (a, b) ≠ (b, a) unless a = b. If |A| = m and |B| = n, then |A × B| = mn. The concept generalizes to n-tuples for products of more than two sets. Cartesian products are the foundation for defining relations and functions as sets of ordered pairs.

## How It's Best Learned
Connect to the coordinate plane: ℝ × ℝ = ℝ² is the familiar xy-plane. Compute small explicit Cartesian products by listing all pairs. Emphasize that the product is a set of pairs, not a single number.

## Common Misconceptions
- Thinking A × B = B × A; they have the same size but different elements when A ≠ B.
- Confusing the Cartesian product with the cross product from linear algebra.
- Treating (a, b) as a set {a, b}, losing the notion of order.
