# Proof Structure and Mathematical Terminology

## Core Idea
Mathematical writing distinguishes precise roles: an axiom is an assumed truth, a definition assigns precise meaning to a term, a theorem is a proven statement, a lemma is a helper result proven on the way to a theorem, and a corollary follows easily from a theorem. A proof is a finite sequence of logically valid steps leading from assumptions to the conclusion. Understanding this vocabulary is prerequisite to reading and writing mathematics fluently.

## How It's Best Learned
Read published proofs and label each sentence by its role: 'This is a definition being unpacked.' 'This is a lemma being applied.' Begin writing structured proofs with explicit 'Proof.' and '□' (QED) markers. Emphasize that every claim in a proof must be justified.

## Common Misconceptions
- Confusing 'assume' with 'conclude' — assumptions are givens, not things to be proved.
- Writing 'obvious' or 'clearly' in place of a real justification.
- Thinking a proof by example is sufficient for universal claims.
