# Partitioning Shapes into Equal Parts

## Core Idea
Partitioning means dividing a shape into equal parts, where each part has the same area. Rectangles and circles can be partitioned into halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, and eighths. Each part is named by its fraction (one-sixth, one-eighth). Students practice drawing partition lines and shading specified fractions of the shape.

## How It's Best Learned
Use folding and cutting activities — fold paper rectangles and circles into equal parts. Comparing shapes partitioned into different numbers of parts reinforces that more parts means smaller parts.

## Common Misconceptions
- Students may draw unequal parts and still name them as fractions. Emphasize that equal area is required.
- Partitioning a circle into thirds is harder to draw than halves or fourths; students may not space the sections evenly.
