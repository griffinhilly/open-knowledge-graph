# Division as Equal Sharing

## Core Idea
Partitive (sharing) division answers the question: if we share a total equally among a known number of groups, how many are in each group? For example, 12 cookies shared equally among 4 friends means each gets 3. The number of groups is known; the group size is unknown. This is the most intuitive model of division for young learners.

## How It's Best Learned
Use physical objects that students actually distribute, one at a time, into cups or circles. Start with small totals and 2–3 groups. Recording the sharing process in a table helps students see the pattern.

## Common Misconceptions
- Students sometimes confuse the divisor (number of groups) with the quotient (size of each group).
- When objects cannot be shared equally, students are uncertain what to do — introduce remainders gently as 'leftovers'.
