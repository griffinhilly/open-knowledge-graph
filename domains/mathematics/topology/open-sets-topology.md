# Open Sets in Topological Spaces

## Core Idea
Open sets form the foundation of topology. A set U is open in a topological space if it belongs to τ. Unlike in ℝ where openness is defined by distance, openness here is purely by membership in τ—illustrating that 'openness' is a relative property depending entirely on the chosen topology.

## How It's Best Learned
Compare with intervals in ℝ: (0,1) is open in standard topology, [0,1) is not. Then see how open sets generate all topological structure; practice constructing different topologies on the same set.

## Common Misconceptions
- Assuming openness is an intrinsic property of a set (it's relative to τ).
- Confusing 'looks open' with 'is open in τ' (unusual topologies make familiar sets closed).
