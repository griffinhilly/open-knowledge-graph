# Equipotential Surfaces and Geometry

## Core Idea
Equipotential surfaces are regions of constant electric potential. The electric field is always perpendicular to equipotential surfaces and points toward lower potential. No work is required to move a charge along an equipotential. Equipotential surfaces and field lines form an orthogonal coordinate system.

## How It's Best Learned
Sketch equipotential surfaces for simple charge distributions and verify perpendicularity to field lines. Use E = -∇V to explain why the field must be perpendicular to constant-potential surfaces.

## Common Misconceptions
- Charges move along equipotential surfaces (they move along field lines, perpendicular to equipotentials).
- Equipotential surfaces are always spherical (only true for point charges).
- Field strength is uniform if equipotential spacing is uniform (closer spacing indicates stronger field).
