# Postulates of Quantum Mechanics

## Core Idea
Quantum mechanics rests on a small set of postulates: quantum states are vectors in Hilbert space, observables are represented by Hermitian operators, measurements yield eigenvalues of operators, and state evolution follows the Schrödinger equation. These postulates provide the complete mathematical framework for predicting quantum behavior.

## How It's Best Learned
Study each postulate separately with concrete examples like the position and momentum operators. Compare predictions with experimental outcomes from the double-slit experiment and Stern-Gerlach apparatus.

## Common Misconceptions
Postulates cannot be derived from deeper principles—they are axioms that define the theory. The postulates are distinct from specific interpretations of quantum mechanics. Postulates predict probabilities but do not explain why individual measurements yield specific outcomes.
