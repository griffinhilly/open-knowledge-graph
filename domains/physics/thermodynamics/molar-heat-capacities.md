# Molar Heat Capacities: Cp, Cv, and Their Relationship

## Core Idea
Cp (heat capacity at constant pressure) and Cv (heat capacity at constant volume) are intensive molar properties representing energy needed to raise one mole's temperature by one degree. For an ideal gas, Cp - Cv = R and γ = Cp/Cv depend only on molecular degrees of freedom. For real gases, these quantities depend on temperature and pressure.

## How It's Best Learned
Derive Cp - Cv = R from the first law for an ideal gas undergoing constant-pressure heating. Relate γ to molecular structure (monatomic, diatomic, polyatomic). Measure Cp and Cv experimentally for various gases and verify the ideal gas relationship and equipartition predictions.

## Common Misconceptions
- Assuming Cp and Cv are the same for all gases (they vary with molecular complexity and temperature).
- Thinking Cp - Cv is always R for real gases (this is exact only for ideal gases; real gases require corrections).
- Confusing specific heat capacity (per unit mass) with molar heat capacity (per mole).
