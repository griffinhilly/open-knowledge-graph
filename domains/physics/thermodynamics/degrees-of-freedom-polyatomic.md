# Degrees of Freedom and Equipartition in Polyatomic Molecules

## Core Idea
Each quadratic degree of freedom contributes (1/2)kT to average energy (equipartition). Monatomic gases have 3 translational degrees of freedom (f=3); diatomic molecules add 2 rotational (f=5, or f=7 with vibration at high T); polyatomic molecules add more. Cv = (f/2)R relates heat capacity directly to molecular structure.

## How It's Best Learned
Count degrees of freedom for simple molecular geometries. Use Cv = (f/2)R to predict γ and verify against experimental data. Understand why vibrational modes are 'frozen out' at low temperatures and activate at high T (when kT ≈ ℏω). Plot Cv versus temperature to observe transitions.

## Common Misconceptions
- Assuming all degrees of freedom are active at all temperatures (vibrational modes are frozen unless kT ≈ ℏω).
- Treating rigid molecules with all rotational modes always active (some rotational modes also freeze at low T).
- Forgetting that vibrations contribute both kinetic and potential energy (f_vib = 2 per vibrational mode).
