# Relational Aesthetics: Art as Social Practice

## Core Idea
Relational aesthetics, associated with theorist Nicolas Bourriaud, proposes that art's primary value lies in creating social encounters, relationships, and participatory experiences rather than in objects or formal properties. Relational art generates convivial situations, temporary spaces of exchange, and horizontal modes of engagement. This aesthetics challenges the autonomy of art and the commodity status of artworks by emphasizing process, community, and the social conditions of meaning-making. It reflects contemporary concerns with public space, collaboration, and democratic participation.

## How It's Best Learned
Encounter participatory and socially engaged art projects; reflect on what aesthetic value consists of if not sensory or formal properties.

## Common Misconceptions
- Relational art abandons aesthetics; it relocates aesthetics from object-properties to social and relational properties. - All participatory or social art is relational; relational aesthetics specifically theorizes aesthetic experience through relational form.
