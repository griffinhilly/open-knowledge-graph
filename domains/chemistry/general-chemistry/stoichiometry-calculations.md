# Stoichiometric Calculations

## Core Idea
Stoichiometry uses mole ratios from a balanced equation to convert between amounts of reactants and products. The limiting reagent is the reactant consumed first and determines the theoretical yield of product. Percent yield compares actual yield (measured experimentally) to theoretical yield: % yield = (actual/theoretical) × 100%. These calculations form the quantitative backbone of chemistry, from laboratory synthesis to industrial manufacturing.

## How It's Best Learned
Work problems systematically: write a balanced equation, convert all quantities to moles, apply mole ratios, and convert back to desired units. For limiting reagent problems, calculate theoretical yield from each reactant separately — the smaller answer identifies the limiting reagent.

## Common Misconceptions
- The limiting reagent is not necessarily the reactant present in smaller mass — it depends on the mole ratio from the balanced equation and the molar masses involved.
- Percent yield above 100% is impossible in principle; if calculated, it signals a measurement error, impure product, or an incomplete reaction being counted as complete.
