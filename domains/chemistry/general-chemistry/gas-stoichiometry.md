# Gas-Phase Stoichiometry

## Core Idea
Gas stoichiometry extends mole-based calculations to gaseous reactants and products using PV = nRT to convert between moles and volume at any specified temperature and pressure. At STP (0°C, 1 bar), one mole of any ideal gas occupies 22.4 L — the molar volume. Dalton's law of partial pressures states that total pressure in a gas mixture equals the sum of each component's partial pressure (Ptotal = ΣPi), which is essential for experiments involving gas collection over water.

## How It's Best Learned
Work problems that combine stoichiometry with gas law calculations: 'What volume of CO₂ at 25°C and 1.00 atm is produced from 10.0 g of CaCO₃?' Practice Dalton's law for gas collected over water, where water vapor pressure must be subtracted from total pressure to find the gas pressure.

## Common Misconceptions
- Molar volume of 22.4 L/mol applies only at standard conditions (0°C, 1 atm); at other conditions, always use PV = nRT.
- Real gases deviate from ideal behavior at high pressure and low temperature — ideal gas law is an approximation that works well under typical laboratory conditions.
