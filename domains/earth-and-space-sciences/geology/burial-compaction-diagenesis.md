# Burial, Compaction, and Diagenesis

## Core Idea
As sediments are buried, they lose water and consolidate through mechanical compaction (grain rearrangement) and chemical cementation. Temperature and fluid chemistry control mineral dissolution and precipitation, transforming sediment to rock. Diagenetic changes (grain contact pressure solution, stylolites) record burial depth and duration.

## How It's Best Learned
Track porosity loss with depth in well logs. Examine diagenetic textures in thin section to infer burial history.

## Common Misconceptions
- Cementation always requires external fluid source.
- Compaction is purely mechanical with no chemical change.
- Diagenesis stops at shallow depths.
