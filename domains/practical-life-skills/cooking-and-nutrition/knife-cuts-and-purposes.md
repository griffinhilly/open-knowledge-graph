# Common Knife Cuts and When to Use Them

## Core Idea
Different knife cuts (julienne: thin matchsticks; brunoise: tiny cubes; chiffonade: thin ribbons; mince: fine pieces) serve different purposes beyond aesthetics. Smaller, more uniform cuts cook faster and more evenly, hold sauce better, and feel better in the mouth. Large chunks cook slowly and unevenly. Matching cut size to cooking method and ingredient ensures even doneness.

## How It's Best Learned
Cut a carrot into thick chunks, thin sticks, and small cubes. Cook each by the same method (boiling or roasting) and notice how the time to tenderness and final texture differ based on size and shape.

## Common Misconceptions
- Knife cuts are only for fancy plating; they don't affect how food cooks. - All knife cuts are interchangeable; it doesn't matter which cut you use.
