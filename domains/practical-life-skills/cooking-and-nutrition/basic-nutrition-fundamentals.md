# Basic Nutrition Fundamentals

## Core Idea
Nutrition is the study of how food supplies the body with energy and building materials. Calories measure energy content, while nutrients — vitamins, minerals, proteins, fats, carbohydrates, and water — serve structural, regulatory, and metabolic functions. No single food provides everything the body needs, which is why dietary variety is the foundation of good nutrition.

## How It's Best Learned
Compare nutrient profiles of common foods side by side. Track meals for 3–5 days and identify gaps (e.g., consistently low fiber or calcium). Use visual guides like the USDA MyPlate to anchor proportional thinking before moving to detailed macronutrient analysis.

## Common Misconceptions
- Fat is inherently bad — dietary fat is essential for hormone production, vitamin absorption, and brain function.
- 'Natural' or 'organic' labels indicate nutritional superiority — these terms refer to production methods, not nutrient content.
