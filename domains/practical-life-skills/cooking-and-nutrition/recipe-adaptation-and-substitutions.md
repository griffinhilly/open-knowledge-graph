# Adapting Recipes and Substituting Ingredients

## Core Idea
Recipes are guides, not laws. Understanding the role of each ingredient lets you substitute intelligently—if a recipe calls for milk, you can choose cream, coconut milk, or broth depending on what you're making. Similarly, you can substitute vegetables if you match water content and texture. Understanding cooking science lets you adapt with confidence.

## How It's Best Learned
Make a simple recipe (like a soup or sauce) with exact ingredients first. Then make it again, deliberately substituting one or two ingredients based on what's in your kitchen. Observe what changes and what stays the same.

## Common Misconceptions
- Recipes must be followed exactly; any substitution will ruin the dish. - If an ingredient isn't in the recipe, you can't use it; recipes are rigid.
