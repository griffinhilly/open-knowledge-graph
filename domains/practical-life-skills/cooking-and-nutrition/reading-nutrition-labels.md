# Reading Nutrition Labels

## Core Idea
Nutrition Facts labels report nutrient content per serving, not per package — the first step is always checking serving size and servings per container. The % Daily Value (%DV) benchmarks each nutrient against a 2,000-calorie reference diet: 5%DV is considered low, 20%DV is high. The ingredients list, ordered by weight, reveals added sugars and sodium even when the Nutrition Facts panel seems favorable. Comparing labels between similar products is one of the most practical decision-support tools available while shopping.

## How It's Best Learned
Compare labels on three versions of the same product (e.g., regular, reduced-fat, and 'natural' yogurt) and identify which is genuinely healthiest across multiple dimensions. Practice spotting added sugar under alternate names (dextrose, corn syrup, maltose). Calculate total sodium in a full meal by summing multiple packaged components.

## Common Misconceptions
- Low fat means low calorie — fat-free products often add sugar to compensate for lost palatability.
- The first ingredient listed is the most prominent — for sugars added in multiple forms, the combined total may exceed the leading ingredient.
