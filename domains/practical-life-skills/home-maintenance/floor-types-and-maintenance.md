# Floor Types and Maintenance

## Core Idea
Different flooring materials require fundamentally different care routines, and using the wrong approach causes damage that ranges from cosmetic dulling to structural failure. Hardwood floors need dry or damp mopping with pH-neutral cleaners and periodic refinishing; excess water warps and stains them. Laminate resists scratches better than wood but its core swells irreversibly if water seeps into seams. Tile is nearly indestructible on the surface but its grout is porous and stains without sealing. Vinyl (LVP/LVT) is the most forgiving — waterproof and low-maintenance — but can be permanently dented by heavy furniture without proper padding.

## How It's Best Learned
Identify what flooring you actually have (many homeowners confuse laminate for hardwood or LVP for tile), then read the manufacturer's care guide for your specific product. Test any new cleaning product in a hidden area like a closet floor before using it on a visible surface.

## Common Misconceptions
- Steam mops are safe for all hard floors — steam forces moisture into seams and finishes, damaging laminate cores and clouding hardwood polyurethane; they are only truly safe on sealed tile and some vinyl.
- Vinegar is a universal natural floor cleaner — its acidity etches natural stone, dulls hardwood finish over time, and can degrade grout; pH-neutral cleaners are the safer default for most flooring.
- Scratched hardwood floors must be fully refinished — isolated scratches can be repaired with wood filler markers or spot-sanding and recoating, saving the cost and disruption of a full refinish.
