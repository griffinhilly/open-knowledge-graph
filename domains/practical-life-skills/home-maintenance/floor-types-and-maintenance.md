# Floor Types and Maintenance

## Core Idea
Different flooring materials require fundamentally different care routines, and using the wrong approach causes damage that ranges from cosmetic dulling to structural failure. Hardwood floors need dry or damp mopping with pH-neutral cleaners and periodic refinishing; excess water warps and stains them. Laminate resists scratches better than wood but its core swells irreversibly if water seeps into seams. Tile is nearly indestructible on the surface but its grout is porous and stains without sealing. Vinyl (LVP/LVT) is the most forgiving — waterproof and low-maintenance — but can be permanently dented by heavy furniture without proper padding.

## How It's Best Learned
Identify what flooring you actually have (many homeowners confuse laminate for hardwood or LVP for tile), then read the manufacturer's care guide for your specific product. Test any new cleaning product in a hidden area like a closet floor before using it on a visible surface.

## Common Misconceptions
- Steam mops are safe for all hard floors — steam forces moisture into seams and finishes, damaging laminate cores and clouding hardwood polyurethane; they are only truly safe on sealed tile and some vinyl.
- Vinegar is a universal natural floor cleaner — its acidity etches natural stone, dulls hardwood finish over time, and can degrade grout; pH-neutral cleaners are the safer default for most flooring.
- Scratched hardwood floors must be fully refinished — isolated scratches can be repaired with wood filler markers or spot-sanding and recoating, saving the cost and disruption of a full refinish.

## Explainer

Your household cleaning knowledge gives you the framework: different surfaces need different approaches, and the right approach for one surface can damage another. Flooring amplifies this lesson because the stakes are higher — inappropriate cleaning can permanently destroy a floor's finish, warp its structure, or void the warranty. The first step with any flooring is accurate identification, because the visual difference between hardwood, laminate, and luxury vinyl plank can be subtle, yet their care requirements diverge sharply.

**Hardwood floors** are solid or engineered wood with a protective polyurethane finish on top. That finish is your asset — it's what makes the floor cleanable and scratch-resistant. The enemy of hardwood is moisture: water that sits on the surface seeps into the wood grain, causing swelling, staining, and eventual warping. Clean hardwood with a barely damp mop — wrung out until almost dry — and a pH-neutral hardwood cleaner. Over years, the finish wears through in high-traffic areas and needs **refinishing**: sanding the surface down to bare wood and applying fresh polyurethane. This is expensive and disruptive, which is why protecting the finish day-to-day is worth the discipline.

**Laminate flooring** looks remarkably similar to hardwood from a distance, but it's fundamentally different: the surface is a photographic image layer protected by a hard wear layer, over a dense fiberboard (HDF) core. That core is the vulnerability — it's highly susceptible to moisture swelling. Once water seeps through the seams (from mopping too wet, spills left too long, or steam mops), the core swells and the planks buckle permanently. Laminate cannot be refinished. Care is simpler than hardwood — dryer cleaning methods and no special cleaners — but any water exposure to seams must be avoided and dried immediately.

**Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) and tile (LVT)** are the most forgiving modern flooring materials: 100% waterproof, scratch-resistant, and low-maintenance. You can mop them freely without worrying about moisture damage. Their weakness is dents from heavy, concentrated loads (refrigerators, piano legs) and edge lifting in areas with extreme temperature changes. **Tile** is essentially indestructible on the surface but its **grout lines** are porous and stain readily. Sealed grout resists staining; unsealed or old grout absorbs it. The care priority for tile is grout sealing every 1–3 years and prompt cleanup of anything that stains — coffee, wine, grease. When in doubt about what floor type you have, look at the edge of a plank in a closet, or check the property records or original construction documentation.
