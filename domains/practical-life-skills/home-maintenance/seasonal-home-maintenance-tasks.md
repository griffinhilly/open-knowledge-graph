# Seasonal Maintenance Tasks and Timing

## Core Idea
Different seasons bring different maintenance challenges. Spring focuses on cleaning gutters, checking the roof for winter damage, and inspecting the exterior. Summer includes lawn care, deck maintenance, and HVAC servicing. Fall requires weatherization, gutter cleaning, and heating system preparation. Winter focuses on monitoring for ice dams, protecting pipes, and checking heating efficiency. Following a seasonal schedule ensures that critical maintenance tasks happen at the right time and problems are caught before they worsen.

## Explainer

Basic home maintenance gave you a map of what systems exist in a house and what they require. Seasonal scheduling answers the question of *when* — because timing transforms maintenance from reactive (fixing what's already broken) to preventive (catching problems before they become expensive). A furnace filter changed in September catches airflow issues before the first hard freeze; one ignored until February means a strained system, higher bills, or a breakdown on the coldest night of the year.

**Spring** is primarily an inspection and recovery season. After winter's freeze-thaw cycles, examine the roof for lifted shingles and flashing separations — small openings that let in water. Clean gutters of winter debris so spring rains drain away from the foundation instead of overflowing against it. Walk the exterior perimeter and look for cracks in the foundation, lifted caulk around windows and doors, and any drainage that runs toward rather than away from the house. Spring is also the right time to service your **air conditioning system** before the heat season: change filters, clear debris from the condenser unit, and schedule a technician check if the system is aging.

**Fall** is the weatherization season — the last window to protect the home before cold weather arrives. Clean gutters again after leaves have fallen (doing it too early in fall is wasted effort). Have the **heating system serviced** while HVAC technicians are still available before the seasonal rush. Check weatherstripping around doors and windows; if you can feel a cold draft or see light around a closed door, warm air is escaping and cold air is entering constantly. Insulate exposed pipes in unheated spaces like crawl spaces, garages, and attics — a frozen pipe that bursts can cause tens of thousands of dollars in water damage. Drain exterior hose bibs and disconnect garden hoses.

**Summer and winter** are primarily monitoring seasons with targeted tasks. Summer is the time for exterior work: inspect and seal deck boards, check exterior paint for peeling, and address any drainage issues identified in spring while the ground is dry and workable. In winter, monitor for **ice dams** — ridges of ice at the eave line that form when heat escaping through a poorly insulated roof melts snow, which refreezes at the colder overhang. Ice dams force water under shingles and into the ceiling. The fix is attic insulation improvement; the short-term response is a roof rake to remove snow before it melts. The underlying logic of the seasonal calendar is this: each season creates specific stressors, and each transition between seasons is an opportunity to prepare for the next one.
