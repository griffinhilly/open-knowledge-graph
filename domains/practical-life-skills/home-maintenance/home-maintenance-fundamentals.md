# Home Maintenance Fundamentals

## Core Idea
Home maintenance is the regular care and repairs needed to keep a house safe, comfortable, and in good condition. It includes preventive tasks (like cleaning gutters to prevent overflow), corrective tasks (fixing problems when they happen), and emergency repairs. Understanding why we maintain our homes helps us plan for repairs and avoid costly damage.

## How It's Best Learned
Observe what needs care or breaks in your own home. Ask homeowners what seasonal tasks they do. Help with basic maintenance to understand the purpose of each task.

## Common Misconceptions
Maintenance is only needed when something breaks. (Preventive maintenance prevents most problems.) Only professionals can do home repairs. (Many basic repairs are safe for homeowners.)

## Explainer

A house is a machine made of subsystems — structural, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, roofing, exterior envelope — each of which degrades over time at a predictable rate. **Preventive maintenance** is the practice of servicing these systems on a schedule before they fail, analogous to changing a car's oil rather than waiting for the engine to seize. The economics are decisive: a $15 furnace filter replaced every three months prevents a $3,000 HVAC replacement. Gutters cleared twice a year prevent water infiltration that rots fascia boards, damages siding, and floods basements. In homeownership, deferred maintenance is not money saved — it is larger repair bills accumulating with interest.

Home maintenance divides into three categories with different urgency levels. **Preventive maintenance** runs on a schedule: changing air filters, testing smoke and CO detectors, flushing water heaters, cleaning dryer vents, checking caulk around tubs and windows. These tasks are cheap, quick, and boring — which is why they get skipped until the problem they were preventing becomes expensive. **Corrective maintenance** responds to failures: a leaking faucet, a stuck door, a cracked caulk line. The rule here is to fix small failures before they cascade — a small roof leak becomes a rotted rafter if ignored for one wet season. **Emergency repairs** are unplanned and urgent: burst pipes, power outages, HVAC failure in extreme weather. Knowing your home's systems means you can diagnose quickly and either fix the problem or describe it accurately to a contractor.

The seasonal rhythm of maintenance is the most practical mental model. **Spring**: inspect the roof and gutters after winter, service the AC before summer, check exterior caulk and paint for winter damage. **Summer**: monitor for pest activity, keep vegetation away from the foundation, trim trees away from the house. **Fall**: clean gutters after leaves fall, service the furnace, inspect weatherstripping on doors and windows, drain exterior hose bibs before freezing. **Winter**: check for ice dams, monitor humidity levels inside, test sump pumps before spring thaw. Following a seasonal checklist means maintenance happens proactively rather than reactively.

Learning to perform basic maintenance yourself builds the most valuable skill in homeownership: the ability to assess a problem and know whether it requires a professional or a weekend afternoon. Many homeowners default to calling contractors for tasks that take 30 minutes and $20 in materials — not from laziness but from unfamiliarity. This course covers the fundamentals that make the boundary between DIY and professional work legible, so you can make that judgment confidently rather than defaulting to expensive help for tasks that do not require it.
