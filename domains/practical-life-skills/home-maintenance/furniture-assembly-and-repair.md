# Furniture Assembly and Repair

## Core Idea
Furniture assembly and basic repair are practical skills that save money and extend the life of your belongings. Assembly — whether flat-pack furniture or items requiring partial construction — follows a consistent approach: inventory all parts and hardware first, read the full instructions before starting, work on a clean and spacious surface, and tighten fasteners in stages rather than fully torquing each one immediately. Common repairs include tightening loose joints with wood glue and clamps, fixing wobbly legs by shimming or re-drilling, touching up scratches with wood filler or markers, and reinforcing sagging shelves with additional brackets or supports.

## How It's Best Learned
Assemble a piece of flat-pack furniture following the instructions carefully, then practice a simple repair like re-gluing a loose chair joint — these two experiences cover the most common real-world scenarios.

## Common Misconceptions
- Power tools are necessary for furniture assembly — most flat-pack furniture is designed for hand tools, and over-tightening with a power drill is a leading cause of stripped holes and cracked particle board.
- Wood glue is a weak, temporary fix — modern wood glue, when properly clamped and cured, creates a bond stronger than the surrounding wood fibers.
- Wobbly furniture is ruined and needs replacing — most wobbles are caused by a single loose joint or uneven leg that takes minutes to fix with basic tools.
