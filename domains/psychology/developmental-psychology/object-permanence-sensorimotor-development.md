# Object Permanence and Sensorimotor Development

## Core Idea
Object permanence is the understanding that objects continue to exist even when out of sight—a milestone achieved in Piaget's sensorimotor stage around 8-12 months. This cognitive achievement enables infants to search for hidden objects and forms the foundation for mental representation. Without object permanence, the infant cannot conceive of an absent caregiver or hidden toy, limiting physical and social understanding.

## How It's Best Learned
Observe infants' behavior when a toy is covered or hidden; trace how searching behavior emerges over months. Use developmental screening tasks (e.g., A-not-B task) to see how infants update mental location of objects.

## Common Misconceptions
Object permanence is not all-or-nothing; it develops gradually across the sensorimotor stage. Infants may fail the A-not-B task not from lack of permanence but from perseveration (repeating previous successful action).
