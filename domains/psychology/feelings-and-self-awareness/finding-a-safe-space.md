# Finding a Safe Space

## Core Idea
A safe space is a place where you can go to calm down, be alone for a moment, or just feel peaceful. It might be a cozy corner, a special chair, or even a spot under a tree. Having a safe space is not about hiding — it is about giving yourself room to take care of your feelings.

## How It's Best Learned
Create a cozy calm-down corner in the classroom with soft cushions, feelings books, and calming tools. Let children help design the space so it feels like theirs. Practice going to the safe space during calm moments so it becomes a familiar, positive place — not just a place you go when you are upset.

## Common Misconceptions
- Thinking a safe space is the same as time-out; a safe space is something you choose, not a punishment.
- Believing you only need a safe space when you are upset; you can go there anytime you want to feel calm or peaceful.
- Assuming everyone's safe space has to look the same; some children like small, enclosed spaces while others prefer open, bright ones.

## Explainer

A **safe space** is a place where you can go to calm down, be alone for a moment, or just feel peaceful. It might be a cozy corner with soft cushions. It might be a special chair. It might even be a spot under a tree outside or a particular room in your house. **A safe space is YOUR special place** where you can take care of your feelings.

Here is something important: **a safe space is not about hiding.** It is not punishment. It is not time-out. A safe space is something **YOU choose** to go to when you want to feel calm. You are in charge. You decide when to go there and when to come back. It is your place of power and peace.

You can go to your safe space anytime — not just when you are upset. You might go there when you want to:
- Calm down from big feelings
- Rest and feel peaceful
- Be quiet and think
- Enjoy your favorite book
- Just have a moment to yourself
- Feel safe and comfortable

**Your safe space does not have to look like anyone else's.** Some children love small, cozy, enclosed spaces where they feel wrapped up and held. Other children prefer open, bright, quiet spaces where they can see everything. Some children like soft things. Others like movement or music. **Your safe space should feel right for YOU.**

What makes a good safe space?
- It feels calm and peaceful to you
- You can reach it when you need it
- It has things that help you calm down
- It feels safe and comfortable
- It is a place where you can take care of yourself

When you have a safe space and practice going there during calm moments, it becomes a wonderful tool. When feelings get big, you know exactly where to go to help yourself.
