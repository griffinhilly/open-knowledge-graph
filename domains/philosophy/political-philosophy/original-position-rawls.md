# The Original Position and Veil of Ignorance

## Core Idea
John Rawls's 'original position' is a hypothetical decision procedure: rational agents choose principles of justice from behind a 'veil of ignorance' that strips them of knowledge of their own place in society — class, wealth, natural talents, conception of the good. Because no one knows their position, no one can bias principles in their favor; the resulting principles reflect pure procedural fairness. Rawls argues that from behind the veil, rational agents would choose (1) equal basic liberties and (2) fair equality of opportunity plus the difference principle, because they would use maximin reasoning — protecting against the worst outcome. The original position is not a historical event but a model of what fairness requires.

## How It's Best Learned
Read A Theory of Justice §3–4, 24–26. Work through the maximin argument carefully: why would risk-neutral agents not choose utilitarianism? Engage the objections — Harsanyi argues expected utility maximization is more rational; Sen argues the veil distorts by stripping out morally relevant information.

## Common Misconceptions
- The veil of ignorance strips particular facts, not all knowledge — parties know general facts about psychology, economics, and human society.
- The original position is not an actual contract — it is a device for representing the conditions of fairness, not an account of how societies actually form.
- Rawls's maximin reasoning is not irrational risk-aversion per se; he argues the stakes (basic rights and life prospects) are too high for gambling.
