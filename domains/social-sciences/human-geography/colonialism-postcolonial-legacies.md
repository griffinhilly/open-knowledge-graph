# Colonialism and Postcolonial Geographies

## Core Idea
Colonialism remapped the world, drawing borders, extracting resources, imposing institutions, and subordinating peoples in service of European imperialism. Colonial legacies persist in current global inequalities, unequal trade relationships, geopolitical tensions, and political instability in formerly colonized regions. Postcolonial geographies examine how former colonies navigate, resist, and rebuild from colonial legacies.

## How It's Best Learned
Trace how colonialism created current territorial boundaries—which cut across ethnic groups and combine incompatible peoples. Examine how colonial extraction of resources shaped current poverty. Study postcolonial movements and independence struggles. Analyze how colonial institutions persist in law, education, and governance.

## Common Misconceptions
Colonialism is not merely historical; colonial relationships persist in current structures of inequality. Postcolonial states are not independent if they remain economically dependent on former colonizers. Blaming colonialism does not excuse postcolonial states' own oppression and exploitation of their populations.
