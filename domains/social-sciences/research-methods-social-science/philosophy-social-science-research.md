# Philosophy of Social Science Research

## Core Idea
Examines foundational questions about the nature of social reality, the observer-observed relationship, and how social knowledge differs from natural science. Explores debates between positivism, interpretivism, critical realism, and constructivism, establishing philosophical foundations for methodological choices.

## How It's Best Learned
Engage with classic debates (Durkheim vs. Weber), read contemporary philosophy of science texts, discuss how philosophical positions shape research design decisions in real projects.

## Common Misconceptions
- Assuming positivism is universally rejected in social science
- Conflating interpretivism with lack of rigor
- Treating methodology as separate from philosophical worldview
