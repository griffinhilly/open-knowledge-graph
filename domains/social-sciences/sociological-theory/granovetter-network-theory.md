# Granovetter and Network Theory

## Core Idea
Network theory analyzes society as patterns of connections between actors. Granovetter showed that weak ties (distant acquaintances) often transmit novel information better than strong ties (close friends), solving the embeddedness problem. Social networks shape opportunities (jobs, ideas, resources) and constraints; position in networks determines access and influence.

## How It's Best Learned
Map your own network: who are your strong ties? Weak ties? Through which ties do novel opportunities come? How does your position enable/constrain action?

## Common Misconceptions
Network theory isn't reducing society to connections—it's showing how structure of connections shapes material outcomes and social possibilities.
