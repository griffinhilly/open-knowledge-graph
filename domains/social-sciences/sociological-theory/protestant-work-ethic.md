# The Protestant Work Ethic and Capitalism

## Core Idea
Weber's thesis argues that Calvinist Protestant theology—particularly the doctrine of predestination and the concept of worldly success as a sign of salvation—created a cultural environment that encouraged systematic, rational profit-seeking and capital accumulation. This cultural shift contributed to the rise of modern capitalism in Northwest Europe, demonstrating that non-economic cultural and religious ideas can shape economic organization. The thesis remains debated but established the importance of analyzing cultural factors in economic history.

## How It's Best Learned
Compare Protestant and Catholic regions' early capitalism development. Examine whether religious doctrine's emphasis on salvation through good works correlates with business formation.

## Common Misconceptions
- Weber argued that Protestantism caused capitalism in a simple causal way. - The Protestant work ethic no longer exists in secular societies. - The thesis applies equally to all forms of Christianity.
