# Habermas and Communicative Action

## Core Idea
Habermas distinguishes instrumental action (strategic, means-to-ends) from communicative action (oriented to understanding and consensus). Legitimate authority derives from discourse where participants freely engage in argumentation. Modernity's crisis stems from instrumental logic colonizing domains (family, politics) where communicative action should prevail.

## How It's Best Learned
Examine a public debate or decision-making process. To what extent is it communicative vs. instrumental? What would ideal discourse require?

## Common Misconceptions
Communicative action isn't naive consensus-seeking—Habermas insists it includes conflict and power asymmetries that distort dialogue.
