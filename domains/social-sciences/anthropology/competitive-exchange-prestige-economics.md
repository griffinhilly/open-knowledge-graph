# Competitive Exchange and Prestige Economics

## Core Idea
In competitive exchange systems—exemplified by the potlatch of the Pacific Northwest and kula ring of Melanesia—prestige and status are gained through giving away or exchanging valuables, creating ongoing obligations and establishing status hierarchies. These systems combine economic exchange with social and political dimensions, demonstrating that non-capitalist economies structure exchange through social relationships and concepts of honor. Generosity and strategic giving, not accumulation, are the path to prestige and leadership.

## How It's Best Learned
Study the potlatch and kula ring in detail, tracing how exchange creates and reproduces social relationships and political authority. Compare these systems with other economies and discuss why some societies structure prestige through giving.

## Common Misconceptions
- Treating competitive exchange as economically irrational from a capitalist perspective.
- Assuming the goal is purely social status rather than also achieving political power and leadership.
- Overlooking the political and military implications of debt and obligation creation.
