# Ritual and Ceremony

## Core Idea
Rituals are formalized, symbolic actions repeated in prescribed ways that carry meaning beyond their literal content. Arnold van Gennep identified a tripartite structure in rites of passage — separation, liminality (threshold), and reincorporation — that Victor Turner elaborated with the concept of 'communitas': the egalitarian solidarity that arises among ritual participants in the liminal phase. Rituals do not merely reflect social reality; they actively create it, transforming identities, reinforcing hierarchies, resolving conflict, and establishing shared meaning. They range from life-cycle ceremonies to calendrical rituals to healing rites.

## How It's Best Learned
Analyze a familiar ritual (graduation, wedding, religious confirmation) using van Gennep's tripartite structure. Identify what social transformation the ritual enacts and what would happen socially if it were skipped.

## Common Misconceptions
- Ritual is not limited to 'primitive' or religious contexts — secular and modern societies are saturated with ritual (sporting events, state ceremonies, corporate onboarding).
- Ritual efficacy is social and psychological, not necessarily supernatural, even for participants who believe otherwise.
- Liminality describes a structural position, not merely a feeling of ambiguity.
