# Absolutism and the Age of Louis XIV

## Core Idea
Louis XIV of France (r. 1643–1715) represented the apogee of European absolutism — the doctrine that monarchs derived authority directly from God and held supreme, undivided power over their kingdoms. Louis centralized French administration, domesticated the nobility at the palace of Versailles, revoked the Edict of Nantes expelling Protestants, and waged expensive wars to extend French hegemony in Europe. Versailles became a model for European court culture and a symbol of royal magnificence. Yet absolutism carried its contradictions: the fiscal demands of Louis's wars burdened French society and contributed to the structural crisis that led, two generations later, to the French Revolution.

## How It's Best Learned
Analyze Versailles as a political and social instrument for controlling the nobility by keeping them dependent on royal favor. Trace the fiscal consequences of Louis's wars using contemporaneous budget documents. Compare French absolutism with constitutional monarchy in England.

## Common Misconceptions
- Louis XIV was not an all-powerful tyrant — he governed within constraints of law, privilege, and limited administrative capacity that no premodern state could fully overcome.
- 'L'état, c'est moi' (The state is me) may be apocryphal and certainly did not mean he governed without significant institutional and social resistance.
