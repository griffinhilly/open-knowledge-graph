# Voltage-Gated Sodium Channels and Fast Inward Current

## Core Idea
Voltage-gated sodium (Na+) channels open when membrane potential depolarizes, allowing rapid Na+ influx that depolarizes the cell. They exhibit fast inactivation—the channel quickly closes and becomes temporarily unresponsive even if the membrane remains depolarized—limiting action potential duration. The voltage-dependence and kinetics of these channels are fundamental to generating and terminating action potentials.

## How It's Best Learned
Perform voltage clamp recordings on isolated channels or whole cells to directly observe sodium currents at different membrane potentials. Plot current-voltage relationships and distinguish opening (activation) from closing (inactivation) phases.

## Common Misconceptions
- Sodium channels stay open as long as the membrane is depolarized; they actually inactivate within milliseconds despite continued depolarization.
- All sodium channels behave identically; there are many subtypes with different gating kinetics and tissue distributions.
