# The Nernst Equation: Calculating Ion Equilibrium Potentials

## Core Idea
The Nernst equation predicts the membrane potential at which there is no net electrochemical driving force for a given ion, determined entirely by the concentration gradient and temperature. This provides the quantitative framework for understanding ion-specific driving forces and how changes in ionic gradients affect neuronal excitability.

## How It's Best Learned
Calculate equilibrium potentials for Na+, K+, Cl−, and Ca2+ using typical neuronal concentrations. Verify experimentally using voltage clamp: observe reversal potentials where single ion currents change sign.

## Common Misconceptions
- The Nernst potential is the resting membrane potential; the Nernst potential applies to a single ion and equals actual potential only if the membrane is permeable only to that ion.
- Equilibrium potentials never change; they can shift during disease or development if intracellular or extracellular ion concentrations change.
