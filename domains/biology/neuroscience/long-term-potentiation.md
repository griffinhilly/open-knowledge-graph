# Long-Term Potentiation: Activity-Dependent Strengthening of Synapses

## Core Idea
Long-term potentiation (LTP) is a persistent increase in synaptic strength following high-frequency stimulation, lasting hours to days or longer. NMDA-receptor-dependent LTP requires both presynaptic glutamate release and postsynaptic depolarization to relieve Mg2+ block, allowing Ca2+ influx that triggers signaling cascades leading to increased postsynaptic receptor insertion and enhanced transmission.

## How It's Best Learned
Use slice electrophysiology to induce LTP with theta-burst stimulation and measure persistent increases in EPSCs. Block NMDA receptors to show that both presynaptic release and postsynaptic depolarization are required for induction.

## Common Misconceptions
- LTP means synapses become infinitely strong; LTP saturates at a maximum and can reverse through depotentiation.
- LTP is identical across all synapses and brain regions; different synapses express distinct LTP mechanisms with different molecular requirements.
