# Fitness Landscape and Adaptive Evolution

## Core Idea
A fitness landscape maps genotypes (or allele combinations) to their reproductive success; populations evolve by moving uphill toward local or global fitness peaks. The landscape's shape—with its peaks, valleys, and plateaus—constrains evolutionary trajectories and determines whether populations can reach optimal phenotypes. Adaptation is movement along the landscape constrained by current variation and history.

## How It's Best Learned
Visualize landscapes for simple genetic models (one or two loci). Simulate evolution on realistic rugged landscapes to see how populations get stuck at local optima.

## Common Misconceptions
- Fitness landscapes are fixed; landscapes change as the environment changes and as populations evolve (other organisms adapt).
- Populations always reach the peak; populations often get stuck at local maxima, especially on rugged landscapes.
