# Reproductive Isolation Mechanisms

## Core Idea
Reproductive isolation—the inability of populations to produce viable, fertile hybrids—is the basis of the biological species definition and the essence of speciation. Prezygotic barriers prevent mating or fertilization (geographic, behavioral, mechanical), while postzygotic barriers reduce hybrid viability or fertility. Multiple barriers can evolve independently, and their strengthening through reinforcement can accelerate speciation.

## How It's Best Learned
Survey reproductive barriers in species pairs (e.g., Drosophila, cichlids). Study how barriers evolve and strengthen during speciation.

## Common Misconceptions
- Reproductive isolation requires all barriers; even one strong barrier is sufficient for species status.
- Reproductive isolation evolves because it benefits species; it evolves as a byproduct of divergence and local adaptation.
